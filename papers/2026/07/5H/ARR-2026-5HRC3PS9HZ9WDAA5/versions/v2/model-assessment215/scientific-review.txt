Main mathematical assessment
1. Law, marginal input, and full conditionals (page 1).
For n>=2, summing chi_[n] over any omitted sign cancels it, so every proper marginal really is uniform and independent of t. Normalization, strict positivity for |t|<1, and conditional formula (2) follow directly. The literal labeled tuple M_r and factorization with the same observable f remove an otherwise serious ambiguity: a conditional involving one refreshed coordinate can depend on the whole remaining configuration and is not thereby a low-order marginal statistic.

2. Complete spectrum and arbitrary positive rates (page 2).
For i in S, conditional expectation replaces x_i by t times the product of the other coordinates. Character multiplication cancels the coordinates in S other than i, leaving t chi_(S complement). For i outside S the character is measurable with respect to the conditioning variables. Thus equation (6) is correct, including its rate a multiplying both terms. In the ordered character basis the columns are (a,-ta) and (-tb,b), as displayed in (7). The matrix is not Euclidean symmetric when rates differ; that does not invalidate self-adjointness in L2(mu_t).
The Gram identity (8) is correct because chi_S chi_T is the character of the symmetric difference. Its Gram blocks have eigenvalues 1+t and 1-t, positive in the stated interval. Distinct complement blocks are orthogonal. There are 2^(n-1)-1 proper complement pairs plus the empty/full pair, giving all 2^n dimensions. On the latter pair H1=0 and H chi_[n]=R(chi_[n]-t), so chi_[n]-t has eigenvalue R. The trace R and determinant (1-t^2)ab of every proper block give exactly (4); the eigenvalues are positive. Degeneracies at t=0 or equal subset sums do not affect completeness or diagonalizability because the full operator is self-adjoint.
For a proper nonempty subset, m<=a<=R-m and hence a(R-a)-m(R-m)=(a-m)(R-m-a)>=0. The lower root increases with this product; equality is attained by a minimum-rate singleton. The lower root is no greater than R/2, while upper roots are at least R/2 and the distinguished nonzero root is R. Therefore (5) is indeed the gap. Positivity uses m>0, R-m>0, and |t|<1. No hidden equal-rate assumption was found.

3. Optimal variance constant and limits (pages 2-3).
Each P_i is the conditional-expectation orthogonal projection, so its energy is exactly E Var(f|rest). The Poincare variational identity on the mean-zero subspace proves both the inequality and the optimal constant 1/gap. The exhibited eigenfunction is correct: its second coefficient is (m-lambda)/(t(R-m)); the first row of (7) vanishes after subtracting lambda, and the second follows from the characteristic polynomial. Its mean is zero since both characters are proper, and its variance is positive by the Gram matrix. At t=0 a minimum-rate coordinate character works, including all degeneracies.
Rationalization gives (10). As |t| tends to 1, its denominator tends to 2R, giving gap/(1-t^2) -> m(R-m)/R and hence the stated asymptotic of C. For unit rates the n=2 expression is 1-|t|. At t=0 the unit-rate gap is 1. At fixed |t|<1, divide numerator and denominator in (10) by n to obtain the n->infinity limit 1-t^2. The random-scan convention P=I-H/R gives gap(P)=gap(H)/R. Since a convex combination of orthogonal projections is positive semidefinite, there is also no negative-eigenvalue ambiguity in its relaxation gap.

4. Quantitative obstruction (page 3).
Theorem 4 follows with its exact quantifiers. Fixing n and all positive rates fixes the common tuple of every proper marginal along the whole family. Any finite value F at that tuple is contradicted by C(mu_t)->infinity. Each t strictly inside the interval defines an admissible strictly positive law; the singular endpoint is never required as an admissible law. The reciprocal lower-bound version follows as stated. Setting n=r+1 proves the advertised obstruction for each fixed marginal order r>=1. This says no finite bound based solely on the tuple works for the unrestricted positive class; it does not say every tuple has unbounded fibers or that bounds with additional structural information are impossible. The manuscript correctly excludes a uniform positivity lower bound and bounded interaction assumptions from its conclusion.

5. Exact-zero lemma, influence and scope (page 4).
Proposition 5 is a correct elementary consequence of the explicit factorization and zero condition. In its example Var(X_n)=1 and the only nonzero conditional-variance contribution comes from refreshing site n; that contribution is 1-t^2. Thus the stated defect t^2 is correct. This example alone does not manufacture a concrete certificate B, and the text appropriately retains the certification hypothesis instead of claiming an application to unspecified formulas.
Flipping one other coordinate reverses the conditional bias, giving total-variation distance |t| and influence row sum (n-1)|t|. These are genuine full-conditional data and vary while the marginal tuple stays fixed. For n>=3, ordinary distinct-coordinate covariances vanish. The block observables X_n and product_(i<n) X_i each have mean zero and product chi_[n], hence covariance t. These observations justify the text's distinctions but do not prove spatial decay, an entropy constant, or a physical mass gap. The withdrawn four-inequality application is not a theorem of this exact manuscript and was not evaluated by consulting an earlier version.

Concrete attempts to falsify the claims
I tested the vulnerable cases analytically, without executing scientific programs.
- n=2, unit rates: the proper block is [[1,-t],[-t,1]], with eigenvalues 1-t and 1+t. The gap is 1-|t|, including negative t. The extra mode has eigenvalue 2. This agrees with the claimed formula and shows that the obstruction itself already has a simple two-coordinate instance for one-coordinate input.
- n=2, unequal rates rho=(1,2), t=1/2: the block is [[1,-1],[-1/2,2]], trace 3 and determinant 3/2, giving roots (3 +/- sqrt(3))/2. This is the formula with R=3,m=1. Replacing t by -1/2 changes the off-diagonal signs, leaves the eigenvalues intact, and changes the extremizer sign correctly.
- t=0: the full character spectrum reduces to subset-rate sums. The complement-block roots are a and b; the smallest positive subset sum is m. The formula and the separate t=0 extremizer agree.
- Rates with ties or a=b and t=0: repeated roots do not create a missing mode or a Jordan block. The Gram positivity and self-adjointness handle these cases.
- As |t| approaches 1, positivity is lost only at the excluded endpoint. There is no uniform positive lower bound on configuration probabilities along the sequence, so compactness inside a uniformly positive class cannot defeat the counterexample.
No concrete counterexample or unresolved objection capable of invalidating a main claim was found. This is a bounded analytical assessment, not a guarantee against undiscovered errors.
