1. CENTRAL CONSTRUCTION AND QUANTIFIERS
For n=r+1>=2, the density 1+chi_[n] with respect to the uniform Boolean product is nonnegative, normalized, and is twice the indicator of positive parity. Its Fourier coefficient at a nonempty proper S is zero because both chi_S and chi_[n]\S have zero uniform expectation. Character moments determine the finite Boolean marginals, so all marginals on at most r sites match the product distribution. This correctly establishes (a). The support identity X_n=product_{i<n}X_i and the uniform one-coordinate marginal establish (b), including mean zero and variance one.

There is no exceptional failure at r=1: the two-coordinate measure is supported on (++),(--), each of probability 1/2. Each coordinate is uniform and the two are equal almost surely. For larger r the dependence becomes invisible to every proper coordinate marginal. The measure class really must contain the parity and product pair at the relevant dimension n=r+1. The theorem does not exclude certificates on structurally restricted classes that do not contain that pair, nor methods using marginal order growing with dimension.

If the total proposed right-hand side factors through the collection of <=r coordinate marginals and the identical fixed observable f=X_n, it has the same value at the two measures. Certification makes that value zero at the product; separation makes the left-hand side positive at parity. The contradiction is valid for any finite constant, even one depending on dimension, because the right-hand side is exactly zero. Thus uniformity is more than needed for this particular exact-zero obstruction. No sign hypothesis on individual Phi_b is necessary for this proof.

The variance-defect example in the proof is also correct. Under the product law, the term for b=n is one and all other single-site conditional variance terms are zero. Under the parity law, every coordinate is determined by the rest, and every term is zero. The defect is therefore zero and one respectively. This is a valid explicit separating functional.

2. MATERIAL OBJECTION: THE INPUT MODEL AND ITS APPLICATION BRIDGE
Definition 1 should be a literal factorization or invariance condition on a specified input map M_r(mu), with f fixed, rather than the current mixture of marginal data and 'conditional variance given order-<=r marginal information.' Conditioning on at most r observed coordinates is different from a conditional quantity recoverable from an r-coordinate marginal. For example with r=1 and f=X_2, Var(X_2|X_1) is zero under two-coordinate parity and one under the product. One conditioning coordinate does not make this an order-1 quantity. This is a demonstrated distinction; it is not a disproof if the intended definition is strict factorization through r-marginals.

The note correctly acknowledges on page 3 that true Dobrushin interdependence coefficients consume the conditional structure and lie outside its bounded-order input model. But page 2 groups Dobrushin row entries with the informal influence examples, and Corollary 1 says the audited cross-site influence expressions are order 1 without displaying or deriving them. These examples must be separated explicitly into standard full-conditional coefficients and genuinely marginal-only proxies. In the two-site example, changing X_1 changes the conditional law of X_2 from a point mass at -1 to a point mass at +1; the corresponding influence is one. For the product it is zero, despite identical order-1 marginals. Thus actual conditional influence cannot be inferred to be order 1 from 'cross-site' or single-site terminology.

The paper's own acknowledgment that the bridge remains natural-language is honest, but that bridge is where the claimed application may fail. A formal proof of equal inputs implies equal outputs does not verify that an actual analytic proposed inequality takes only those inputs. Supply the four audited formulas and prove, for each, the invariance, certification, and separation conditions. Without that, Corollary 1's historical application is unsupported by the self-contained note. I did not read the other audited manuscripts, in accordance with the one-artifact task, so I do not assert that their claims are either correct or incorrectly audited.

3. MATERIAL OBJECTION: 'COVARIANCE DECAY' REQUIRES OBSERVABLES AND GEOMETRY
For r>=2, parity has exactly the same two-site law as the product. Consequently Cov_pi(X_i,X_j)=0 for every i!=j, just as under the product law. This is a concrete analytic counterexample to using ordinary pair-coordinate covariance as the separating functional for that parity pair. The theorem's separation hypothesis prevents the central theorem from being false, but the unqualified covariance-decay application does not explain this limitation.

A valid block separation is f=X_n and g=product_{i<n}X_i: their covariance is one at parity and zero at the product. To obtain a spatial-decay statement, specify a geometry, block supports, distance, normalization, and allowed support dependence of constants. Merely calling the scalar dependence a failure of covariance decay leaves those matters open. Restrict the sentence to explicitly defined block-decoupling certificates or prove a suitable spatial formulation. Similarly, 'Dobrushin-type mixing' must identify a property and functional rather than rely on the label.

4. TENSORISATION: A VALID NEGATIVE EXAMPLE, WITH SCOPE TO STATE
The parity measure genuinely fails any finite-constant single-site variance tensorization: Var(X_n)=1 whereas all true single-site conditional variances vanish. The standard entropy analogue also fails. For h=1+X_n (a nonnegative observable), every conditional entropy is zero because all single-site conditionals are deterministic, while Ent_pi(h)=log 2>0. Thus the note's basic warning about single-site tensorization on a class containing parity is mathematically justified, independently of any abstract proxy classification.

This degeneracy is also an important limitation of the example: the single-site heat-bath dynamics is frozen. A class of strictly positive Gibbs measures excludes the exact parity law. Extending the warning to such a class requires a separate stability or approximation argument and the precise uniformity assumptions; the published theorem does not state that extension. As an analytic observation, mu_t=(1+t chi_[n])u for 0<t<1 has the same proper marginals, and for f=X_n the variance is one while E Var(X_n|rest)=1-t^2. Any variance tensorization constant must be at least 1/(1-t^2). This supports a possible strengthened statement as t tends to one, but it is not a scientific execution or a proof that every intended application contains these measures.

Page 3's formula 'Dobrushin uniqueness => tensorisation with constant (1-delta)^-1' needs a specified variance-versus-entropy notion, coefficient norm, and exact theorem citation. Dobrushin's 1968 foundational conditional-specification paper is not a substitute for specifying the modern tensorization statement. Caputo-Menz-Tetali give a precise entropy result with an ergodicity condition and conditional-law coefficients gamma,kappa, with constant (1-gamma-kappa)^-1. I am not claiming every possible variance version of the note's formula is false; the issue is that the unqualified formulation is underspecified.

5. CERTIFICATION CONDITION AND RHETORICAL LIMITS
Condition (1) is a substantial extra assumption, not a consequence of having a useful comparison bound. The manuscript recognizes the positive product-law right-hand side of an Efron-Stein variance proxy. A positive upper bound can still be useful without vanishing identically; the theorem rules out this particular exact-zero certification mechanism, not every use of bounded-order data. In particular, 'bounded-order data can falsify decoupling, never certify it' on page 3 is too broad without repeating the measure-class and exact-certification restrictions. Structural prior assumptions that exclude the parity pair can allow bounded-order data to certify properties.

Standard Efron-Stein statements assume independence in their classical setting; they should not be read as a true arbitrary-dependent-measure variance bound. The true conditional-variance proxy on general dependent measures itself uses full conditional structure. These distinctions need to accompany the informal 'true but non-certifying bounds' language. The note should be especially careful not to imply that the classical inequality is refuted by parity.

The stated connection to the irreducible analytic core of constructive Yang-Mills programmes is a motivation or conjectural methodological judgment. No class of gauge-field measures, limiting procedure, continuum estimate, or proof that such measures realize this obstruction is given. The note establishes no Yang-Mills mass gap theorem and does not establish a universal impossibility of fixed-order strategies supplemented by substantive model assumptions.

6. GENERALISATIONS
The finite-abelian-group density 1+Re chi has nonnegative values and is normalized when the character has a nontrivial component on each coordinate. Integrating out any coordinate then removes its perturbation, so the proper-marginal argument extends. However, the Boolean deterministic support identity and maximal unit-variance dependence need not extend verbatim to higher-order characters. State the nontriviality assumptions and provide the separating observable for the generalized claim. This is an omitted specification/proof detail, not a demonstrated failure of the qualified marginal-indistinguishability generalization. Coordinatewise extension to finite-dimensional vector observables preserves a scalar obstruction; a general vector norm inequality would require its own statement.

The literature check corroborates that restricted-marginal independence is a longstanding subject: Alon-Goldreich-Mansour explicitly study the relation between almost k-wise and k-wise independent laws. I do not infer priority or absence of prior formulations from this limited search.
