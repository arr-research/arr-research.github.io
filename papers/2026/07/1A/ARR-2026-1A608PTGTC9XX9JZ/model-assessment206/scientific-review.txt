CLAIM-BY-CLAIM ASSESSMENT
1. Assumptions and positivity. A real strictly positive finite square kernel on a nonempty type, with v>0 and Av=lambda v, necessarily has lambda>0: take any coordinate and divide the strictly positive sum (Av)_i by v_i. The nonempty assumption matters. Existence and uniqueness of v are imported from the companion paper, not proved here. The source-dependent specialisation is not made self-contained by naming it.

2. Lemma 2.1. Applying the companion existence theorem to A transpose gives phi>0 and A^T phi=nu phi. Pairing phi with Av gives (lambda-nu)<phi,v>=0, and the pairing is strictly positive, hence nu=lambda. This is correct.

3. Lemma 2.2. If z=Au-lambda u is componentwise nonnegative, pairing against phi gives zero. Since every phi_i>0, each z_i must vanish. Correct. In fact u>=0 is unnecessary for this step once z>=0 is assumed; it is harmless.

4. Theorem 3.1. Assume real w!=0 and Aw=-lambda w. Let u=|w|. The entrywise triangle inequality and lambda>0 give Au>=lambda u. Lemma 2.2 yields equality. Then p=u-w>=0 and q=u+w>=0 satisfy Ap=lambda q and Aq=lambda p. If p!=0, strict entrywise positivity of A forces Ap>0, so q>0. A negative or zero w_i would give q_i=0, so actually w>0; the weaker conclusion w>=0 already gives p=0, contradiction. Thus p=0; w>=0 and Au=lambda u then force both Aw=lambda w and Aw=-lambda w, whence w=0. There is no circular sign inference or hidden equality-case assumption. The prose's weaker sign conclusion is adequate.

5. Theorem 4.1. The imported domination |mu|<=lambda plus realness of mu and exclusion of both endpoints yields |mu|<lambda. Correct. This establishes separation of eigenvalues different from lambda. To interpret a second eigenvalue counted with multiplicity as separated, one also needs Perron simplicity/uniqueness. The companion's geometric uniqueness is stated in the abstract; in the coupled case similarity to a real symmetric matrix removes Jordan blocks, so geometric uniqueness suffices there. The explicit disclaimer that algebraic simplicity is not proved should not be read as a proof of algebraic simplicity for a general nonsymmetric kernel.

6. Theorem 5.1. Rescaling a positive nonzero eigenvector by its Euclidean norm yields unit norm and (A/lambda)Omega=Omega. Correct and routine. The result is an interface, not new model physics.

7. Theorem 5.2. For real symmetric K and positive w, D K D with D=diag(sqrt(w)) is real symmetric and entrywise strictly positive if K is. Correct conditionally. Entrywise positivity does not mean positive semidefiniteness. For instance [[1,2],[2,1]] is entrywise positive symmetric and has eigenvalues 3 and -1. The paper does not claim semidefinite positivity or a transfer-Hamiltonian logarithm, so this is a scope check, not an error.

8. Theorems 6.1-6.2. A real symmetric matrix is Hermitian on complex vectors. The Hermitian pairing yields mu*sum|y_i|^2 real, hence mu real. Similarity transfers eigenvalues without changing multiplicities. If K_w=D^2 K (source weighting), then D^{-1} K_w D=D K D; if K_w=K D^2, the opposite conjugation applies. Either usual convention works, but the PDF never states which convention defines K_w, so the model-specific claim is conditional on an omitted definition. Full real-spectrum separation also needs the routine step: for a real eigenvalue and complex eigenvector y=a+ib, at least one of a,b is a nonzero real eigenvector. That omission is repairable and is not a demonstrated failure.

9. Corollary 6.3. Follows from the preceding facts if all specialisation premises hold. The coupled kernel, configuration space, boundary conditions, beta domain, and extent-zero convention are absent from the PDF. Therefore I cannot check that positivity and similarity hold for every advertised beta and L. The paper calls the matrix 2^L by 2^L, which suggests a nonempty configuration type even for L=0; if dimension is one, separation for other eigenvalues is vacuous and a second-largest ratio needs a convention. This is a clarification request rather than a counterexample.

10. Quantitative interpretation. For a fixed finite spectrum, strict separation implies an existential epsilon_L>0 whenever a non-Perron eigenvalue exists: take the minimum of finitely many positive separations. The statement that the theorem contains no epsilon should mean that it supplies no explicit modulus or controlled dependence, not that no positive epsilon follows logically. For a fixed matrix there is qualitative gap information. Nothing here bounds its infimum over L.

ANALYTICAL ADVERSARIAL CHECKS (NO EXECUTION)
A. Let A_e=[[1,e],[e,1]] with 0<e<1. It is strictly positive and symmetric. Its eigenvectors (1,1) and (1,-1) have eigenvalues 1+e and 1-e. The ratio (1-e)/(1+e) tends to 1 as e tends to 0. Thus the generic assumptions cannot provide a universal gap over strictly positive matrices. This validates the paper's qualitative/quantitative distinction, but does not prove anything about the named model at fixed beta,gamma as L grows.
B. The nonnegative swap matrix [[0,1],[1,0]] has eigenvalues 1 and -1. It fails strict positivity and illustrates exactly why the hypothesis is essential.
C. For a one-point type A=[a] with a>0, the theorem is valid and the non-Perron assertion is vacuous. The paper should avoid treating an undefined second eigenvalue as automatically present.
D. The symmetric example with entries 1 and 2 above tests the distinction between entrywise positivity and operator positivity.
None is a counterexample to the stated generic theorem.

NUMERICAL SECTION
The four ratios in each family are author-reported observations, not independently reproduced. Four finite extents cannot establish an asymptotic limit or prove that a uniform gap fails. The paper explicitly admits this in Section 1.3 and states that no theorem depends on the probe, which is good. Nonetheless, the abstract and Section 7 say one family is collapsing and that the second does not; these should be phrased as finite-range trends rather than established limiting behaviour. The threshold comparison needs an explicit correspondence between this kernel and the anisotropic Ising transfer matrix, including boundary and coupling conventions, plus a direct citation. The reference list contains no Kramers-Wannier or Onsager primary paper. Provide the exact ratio definition (modulus or signed ordering), matrix construction, precision and residual/error diagnostics. A repository path and transcript are useful but do not substitute for defining the measured observable.
