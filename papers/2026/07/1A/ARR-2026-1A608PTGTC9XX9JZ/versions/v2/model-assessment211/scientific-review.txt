Mathematical assessment, with dependency and quantifier checks
1. Operator convention and similarity (pages 1-2).
A=diag(w)K is explicitly row/source weighted, with every w strictly positive and every beta finite real. D=diag(sqrt(w)) is invertible, S=DKD is symmetric, and DSD^{-1}=D^2K=A. These identities are correct entrywise. They prove real spectrum and diagonalizability for this specified class, not positive semidefiniteness for arbitrary beta. The paper correctly avoids that conflation. The temporal product has no hidden spatial bond; the example open-chain weight supplies such bonds separately. The index set remains nonempty at L=0, where the operator is one-dimensional and there is no second eigenvalue. No undefined gap is assigned there.

2. Lemma 2.1 (page 2).
The sole imported positive-matrix input is existence of positive right eigenvectors for A and A^T. With Av=lambda v and A^T phi=nu phi, the positive pairing forces lambda=nu. For z=Au-lambda u >=0, phi^T z=0 implies z=0 since each phi_i>0. The implication holds even for a sign-changing u, as claimed. There is no circular use of strict peripheral separation in this step.

3. Lemma 2.2 (page 2).
Choosing a maximizing coordinate in |x_j|/v_j is legitimate because the index set is finite, v_j>0 and x is nonzero. At that coordinate |x_i|>0, so division is legitimate. The complex triangle inequality and Av=lambda v give |mu|<=lambda with no assumption of real spectrum.

4. Theorem 2.3 (pages 2-3).
If Au=-lambda u, h=|u| satisfies Ah>=lambda h. Lemma 2.1 upgrades this to equality. With p=h-u and q=h+u the equations Ap=lambda q and Aq=lambda p are correct. If p is nonzero, strict entrywise positivity makes Ap>0 and hence q>0. This forces all u_i>0, contradicting p!=0. Thus p=0, and the positive and negative eigenvalue equations force u=0. Excluding -lambda plus domination proves the stated claim for other REAL eigenvalues of a general positive real matrix. This theorem alone would not eliminate a nonreal peripheral value; the paper uses symmetry of its specified operator before drawing the full-spectrum conclusion, which is appropriate.

5. Theorem 3.1 (page 3).
Similarity with a real symmetric matrix makes every eigenvalue real. The conjugate-inner-product argument requires a nonzero transferred eigenvector, guaranteed by invertibility of D. Real and imaginary parts recover a nonzero real eigenvector when needed. For the lambda eigenspace, c=min_i u_i/v_i makes y=u-cv nonnegative with a zero coordinate; positivity rules out y!=0. Symmetric diagonalizability then converts one-dimensional real eigenspace to algebraic simplicity. This avoids an unjustified general leap from geometric to algebraic multiplicity. For L>=1 at least two eigenvalues counted with multiplicity exist; simplicity of lambda ensures the non-Perron set is nonempty even when others repeat. Its finite maximum gives delta>0. At beta=0, A=w 1^T has rank one and nonzero eigenvalue sum w, so delta=1 for L>=1, correctly illustrating that gap collapse is not universal.

6. Theorem 4.1 and the all-L asymptotic (pages 3-4).
The quantifier is fixed q in (0,1), then every integer L>=1, with globally defined L-dependent weights. The two corner configurations are distinct for L>=1. Write z=q^L, R=(1+q)^L, t=z^2/R. The noncorner weight is t^2, so its square root really is t. H is the tensor power of [[1,q],[q,1]]. Separating the coordinate sums yields Walsh eigenvalues (1-q)^{|J|}(1+q)^{L-|J|}>0; congruence proves positive definiteness of S. This positivity justifies using the ordinary second-largest eigenvalue rather than a possibly negative eigenvalue of larger modulus.

The constant row and column sums of H are R. Weighted Cauchy-Schwarz establishes ||H||<=R, and the constant vector attains R. For orthogonal coordinate projections P onto the two corners and Q=I-P, D=P+tQ and S-B=t(PHQ+QHP)+t^2 QHQ with B=PHP. The corner block is [[1,z],[z,1]]. Consequently ||S-B|| <= (2t+t^2)R = 2z^2+z^4/R=eta. This is a valid, deliberately loose bound; no dimension factor is omitted.

The corner-sum Rayleigh quotient gives lambda1>=1+z, and the norm bound gives lambda1<=1+z+eta. The two-dimensional corner subspace has minimum quotient 1-z and intersects the top eigenvector's orthogonal complement, giving lambda2>=1-z. The corner-sum orthogonal hyperplane has B quotient <=1-z; the perturbation bound and min-max give lambda2<=1-z+eta. The ratio directions are correct:
delta >= 1-(1-z+eta)/(1+z) = (2z-eta)/(1+z);
delta <= 1-(1-z)/(1+z+eta) = (2z+eta)/(1+z+eta).
The upper-bound numerator 1-z is positive throughout the stipulated domain. A negative lower bound for small sizes or q close to one is merely uninformative, not false. Since eta/z=2z+z^3/R tends to zero at each fixed q<1, dividing these bounds by 2z gives the asserted limit one. Finally K_beta,L=e^{beta L}H for q=e^{-2 beta}, so eigenvalue ratios are unchanged. No step exchanges a limit in q or beta with L.
