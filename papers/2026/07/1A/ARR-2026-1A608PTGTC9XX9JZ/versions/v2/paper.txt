                                                  Abstract
         We specify a finite spin kernel and its source-weight convention, then distinguish strict
     separation at each finite size from a bound uniform over sizes and weights. For XL = P {−1, 1}L ,
     β ∈ R and w : XL → (0, ∞), the operator has entries A(σ, τ ) = w(σ) exp(β j σj τj ).
     It is similar to a real symmetric matrix. A classical positive-matrix argument, organized
     through a positive left eigenvector and the pair |u| − u, |u| + u, excludes the negative Perron
     eigenvalue and gives strict separation of the whole spectrum. We state the actual specialized
     historical formal premises rather than identify a theorem name with an unspecified physical
     operator. We also give a fully explicit positive source-weight sequence at each fixed β >
     0 whose relative modulus gap satisfies δL ∼ 2 exp(−2βL). The construction concentrates
     weight at the two constant configurations; its proof uses a rank-two compression and exact
     variational bounds. These weights depend globally on the configuration and on L. The
     result supplies no conclusion for a fixed local-interaction Ising family. The finite gap, the all-
     size analytic counterexample and earlier formal endpoints are distinguished; twelve rational
     diagnostic cases corroborate the finite construction but do not prove its limit. Originality of
     this particular specialization remains unestablished. No interacting or continuum Yang–Mills
     result is claimed.

2    Finite positive kernels and real peripheral separation
Let A be a real matrix on a finite nonempty index set, with Aij > 0. Classical positive-matrix
existence supplies v > 0 and Av = λv. Taking any coordinate shows λ > 0.
Lemma 2.1 (Left vector and subeigenvector equality). There is φ > 0 with AT φ = λφ. If
Au − λu ≥ 0 componentwise, then Au = λu.
Proof. Apply existence to AT , obtaining eigenvalue ν. Pairing its positive eigenvector with Av
gives (λ − ν)⟨φ, v⟩ = 0; the pairing is positive, so ν = λ. For z = Au − λu ≥ 0 the same pairing
gives ⟨φ, z⟩ = 0. All positive coefficients φi force zi = 0. No sign assumption on u is needed for
this implication.

Lemma 2.2 (Domination). Every complex eigenvalue µ of A obeys |µ| ≤ λ.
Proof. For a nonzero complex eigenvector x, let r = maxj |xj |/vj > 0 and choose i attaining it.
Then |xi | = rvi > 0 and

                              X              X                   X
                 |µ||xi | =       Aij xj ≤       Aij |xj | ≤ r       Aij vj = rλvi = λ|xi |.
                              j              j                   j

Division by |xi | proves the claim.

Theorem 2.3 (Excluding the negative Perron value). There is no nonzero real vector u with
Au = −λu. Consequently every real eigenvalue µ ̸= λ has |µ| < λ.
Proof. Suppose Au = −λu, and put h = |u| entrywise. The triangle inequality and λ > 0 give
Ah ≥ λh, so Lemma 2.1 gives equality. Set p = h − u ≥ 0, q = h + u ≥ 0. Then
                                       Ap = λq,          Aq = λp.
If p ̸= 0, strict positivity of A implies Ap > 0, and hence q > 0. But qi = |ui | + ui vanishes when
ui ≤ 0, so u > 0, which forces p = 0, a contradiction. Thus p = 0 and h = u ≥ 0. The two
equations Au = λu and Au = −λu imply u = 0. Finally, Lemma 2.2 and realness leave only ±λ
as possible equality cases, both excluded for a real eigenvalue different from λ.

Proof. Similarity (2) transfers an eigenpair of A to one of P the real symmetric matrix S. For
Sy = µy, pairing y with Sy and conjugating shows (µ − µ̄) i |yi |2 = 0, hence µ is real. For a
real eigenvalue and a nonzero complex eigenvector of the real matrix A, its real and imaginary
parts are real eigenvectors, and at least one is nonzero. Theorem 2.3 therefore gives the strict
modulus assertion for every non-Perron eigenvalue.
    To check simplicity, let Au = λu with u real, and choose c = mini ui /vi . Then y = u − cv ≥ 0,
one coordinate of y is zero, and Ay = λy. If y ̸= 0, strict positivity of A would imply Ay > 0
at that zero coordinate, a contradiction. Thus the real Perron eigenspace is one-dimensional.
A real symmetric matrix is diagonalizable, so similarity to S promotes this geometric assertion
to algebraic simplicity for this class. For L ≥ 1 there are 2L ≥ 2 eigenvalues counted with
multiplicity, and the maximum of the finitely many non-Perron moduli is strictly below λ.

    The existence of this positive finite gap is quantitative in an existential sense. The issue left
open by qualitative separation is an explicit controlled modulus as L, w and parameters vary.
Calling the finite theorem devoid of all quantitative content would be too strong. Likewise L = 0
gives a valid one-dimensional operator and a vacuous separation assertion,P not a defined second-
largest eigenvalue. For β = 0, A = w1T has sole nonzero eigenvalue σ w(σ), so δ(A) = 1 for
L ≥ 1. Not every positive-weight family loses its uniform relative gap.

                                     1 + z ≤ λ1 ≤ 1 + z + η.

Every unit vector in the two-dimensional range of P has Rayleigh quotient at least 1 − z. That
space intersects the orthogonal complement of a top eigenvector nontrivially, proving λ2 ≥ 1 − z.
On the orthogonal complement of the corner-sum vector, B has Rayleigh quotient at most 1 − z,
and S has quotient at most 1 − z + η. The finite-dimensional min-max principle consequently
gives
                                    1 − z ≤ λ2 ≤ 1 − z + η.
Dividing by the appropriate positive bounds on λ1 proves both inequalities in (3). Finally
ηL /zL = 2zL + zL3 /RL → 0, so both bounds divided by 2zL tend to 1. Since Kβ,L = eβL HL when
q = e−2β , the positive scalar factor changes no eigenvalue ratio and proves the claim for (1).

Scope of this counterexample. The source weights depend on L and globally distinguish
the two constant configurations. Their minimum tends to zero. They are not asserted to come
from one fixed local nearest-neighbour coupling γ. The theorem is an actual family in the source-
weighted kernel class, not merely a changing 2 × 2 example, but it does not settle the fixed-(β, γ)
Ising case or exclude uniform estimates under stronger separately proved weight restrictions. The
counterexample removes no hypothesis from the physical Yang–Mills problem.
