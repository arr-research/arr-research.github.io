    The weights (m−1, m, m+1) are not decorative: they arise from a specific surface expansion

(1) is a statement about a weighted second-order combination.

The second factor is strictly positive: P − (2m + 1)c > 0 is exactly the unit-step order-
monotonicity inequality 1/ρm − ρm > (2m + 1)/x of the companion note [8], which follows
from (3) by the exact calibration 1/Um − Um = (2a)/x = (2m + 1)/x together with the strict
decrease of t 7→ 1/t − t on (0, ∞).
   The first factor is strictly positive: since s > a in (3), Um < x/(2a) = x/(2m + 1), so
u < x/(2m + 1) ≤ x/3 for m ≥ 1, hence S > 1/u > (2m + 1)/x ≥ 3/x = 3c.
   The third term (2m + 1)c2 is positive. This proves Theorem 1.
Remark 3 (the calibration, for the third time). The bound Um of (3) is the positive root of
1/t−t = (2m+1)/x: it is the extremal function of the threshold that appears in the factorization.
In the companion note [8] this exact calibration was the engine of the unit-step monotonicity of
(log Iν )′ and was shown to be equivalent to the Amos bound; here the same calibrated threshold
reappears, multiplied against a second factor whose positivity is again a consequence of the
same bound. Within the audit programme from which these notes emerge, this is the third
lock opened by the same key, which suggests the calibration identity, rather than any particular
inequality, is the structural invariant of this family of problems.

exact in the rewriting and open in the dominance. Lemma 5 makes the displayed series well

error specifications were incomplete. Cancellation may make naive summation unreliable, but

(β, ε), cutoffs, working precision, tail bounds and error control. The problem remains open.

is context, not a derivation of a physical observable or a positivity theorem.
