1. MAIN THEOREM, QUANTIFIERS, AND DEPENDENCIES (PAGES 1-3)
Theorem 1 asserts a unit-step comparison for every real m>=1 and every x>0. It does not assert monotonicity between arbitrary real m<n with noninteger separation, and its proof does not imply that stronger statement. Corollary 4 only needs integer steps, so this distinction causes no gap.

The dependency chain is positivity of I_nu for nu>=0 and x>0, the Bessel three-term recurrence, the strict Amos upper bound, elementary ordered-field algebra, then integer iteration. NIST DLMF 10.29.1 gives I_(nu-1)-I_(nu+1)=(2nu/x)I_nu, confirming both ratio recurrences after shifting the order. Direct source: https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.29.E1 . Ruiz-Antolin and Segura, Theorem 2, give the upper bound for I_nu/I_(nu-1), nu>=1/2. Substitution nu=m+1 gives exactly x/[m+1/2+sqrt((m+1/2)^2+x^2)] and covers the stated m>=1 range with room to spare. Direct source: https://arxiv.org/html/1606.02008 (Theorem 2). Thus there is no apparent hidden integer-order restriction or citation-domain mismatch in the analytic input.

I checked the normalization and factorization manually. Substituting the two recurrences gives Delta=m(m+1)(phi_(m+1)-phi_m). The coefficients of u^(-2), u^2, the terms linear in c, and c^2 are respectively 2m(m+1), -2m(m+1), -4m(m+1)[(m-1)u+(m+2)/u], and 8m(m+1)(2m+1). Division by 2m(m+1) therefore yields P*S-c(2m+1)S-3cP+4(2m+1)c^2. Expansion of (S-3c)[P-(2m+1)c]+(2m+1)c^2 gives exactly that expression. In particular, the potentially easy factor-of-two error in the abstract is absent.

Calibration is exact: with a=m+1/2 and U=x/(a+sqrt(a^2+x^2)), 1/U-U=2a/x. Because u<U and t->1/t-t is strictly decreasing for t>0, P-(2m+1)c>0. Also sqrt(a^2+x^2)>a>0 implies u<x/(2m+1), so S>1/u>(2m+1)c>=3c. The residual term (2m+1)c^2 is strictly positive. Every denominator cleared is positive. This is a self-contained proof once the classical bound and recurrence are supplied; the companion note is explanatory rather than a necessary unproved dependency.

Analytical attempts to break the claim: at m=1 the zero backward weight does not create a zero denominator, and S>3c remains strict. At fixed m>1, the small-x leading term phi_m~4m(m-1)/x^2 makes the leading step difference ~8m/x^2 positive; at m=1, phi_1~x^2/8 whereas phi_2~8/x^2. At fixed m and large x, the factorization alone gives phi_(m+1)-phi_m>2(2m+1)/x^2, so the vanishing gap at infinity is not a sign reversal. These are consistency tests, not substitutes for the general proof. I found no demonstrated mathematical error in Theorem 1 or Lemma 2.

2. COEFFICIENT ARRAYS, DETERMINANTS, AND OPEN PROBLEM (PAGES 3-4)
For k>=2, A_k/B_k=[(k-1)r_(k-2)^2+(k+1)r_k^2]/[k r_(k-1)^2], and normalization by I_1 cancels, giving phi_k. At k=1, A_1=2r_1^2 and B_1=1, agreeing with phi_1=2(I_2/I_1)^2. Thus the boundary truncation is handled correctly without defining r_(-1). B_k>0, integer iteration proves phi_m<phi_n, and c_mn=B_m B_n(phi_m-phi_n)<0. This is a valid determinant ordering for the defined arrays.

I checked the parity sign analytically. With p=n-m, q=n+m, sin(q(pi-epsilon))=(-1)^(q+1)sin(q epsilon), and similarly for p. Since p and q have the same parity, substitution into the original summand, followed by c_mn=-|c_mn|, gives |c_mn|(-1)^(m+n)[q sin(p epsilon)-p sin(q epsilon)]. There is no sign flip missing in the rewritten identity. For q epsilon<=pi, 0<p epsilon<q epsilon<=pi makes the bracket positive by the strictly decreasing sinc function on that interval. This does not control all terms of the sum. Consequently determinant ordering alone establishes neither W<0 nor Griffiths-II positivity. The paper properly leaves this as Open problem 5; I do not treat failure to solve an expressly open problem as a theorem error.

Combining |c_mn|<=A_m B_n+A_n B_m with the at-most-linear trigonometric prefactor gives absolute convergence. The primary definition used here is NIST DLMF 10.25.2: https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.25.E2 .
