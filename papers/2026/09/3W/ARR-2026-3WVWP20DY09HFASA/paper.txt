Rank plus Weyl sparsity is at least p+1: an operator form of the
   Chebotarev-Tao uncertainty principle in prime dimension

                            Lluis Eriksson (Independent researcher)

                           9 September 2026 - Internal revision v004


Abstract
Let 𝑝 be prime and let 𝑊(𝑎,𝑏) = 𝑋 𝑎 𝑍 𝑏 be the cyclic Weyl operators on ℂ𝑝 . We prove that
every nonzero linear combination 𝐶 of ℓ Weyl operators satisfies dim ker 𝐶 ≤ ℓ − 1, i.e. rank 𝐶 +
𝑠𝑊 (𝐶) ≥ 𝑝 + 1, where 𝑠𝑊 counts the nonzero Weyl coeﬀicients. The collinear case is Tao’s
uncertainty principle on ℤ/𝑝 (Chebotarev’s theorem on Fourier minors). The general case follows
from the reduction-modulo-(1 − 𝜔) mechanism of Frenkel and Tao after two further observations:
Weyl combinations stay integral under the non-unimodular Clifford conjugations 𝐹 ′ 𝑄𝑠 , which
realise all 𝑝 + 1 line directions of 𝔽2𝑝 , and, in the nonvacuous sparsity range, a pigeonhole over
those directions produces a nonzero fibre sum. The bound is sharp for every ℓ ≤ 𝑝 and fails in
every composite dimension. As a self-contained corollary, for one use of the 𝑝2 Weyl channels
the minimal perfect list length over probes of Schmidt rank 𝑟 is exactly 𝑝 − 𝑟 + 1. An exact
certificate refutes a natural “line conjecture”.

1. Introduction
1.1 The result
Fix a prime 𝑝, 𝜔 = 𝑒2𝜋𝑖/𝑝 , and the Weyl basis 𝑊(𝑎,𝑏) = 𝑋 𝑎 𝑍 𝑏 of 𝑀𝑝 (ℂ), where 𝑋 is the cyclic
shift and 𝑍 = diag(𝜔𝑗 ). Every matrix 𝐶 has a unique expansion 𝐶 = ∑𝑔 𝑐𝑔 𝑊𝑔 over 𝑔 ∈ 𝔽2𝑝 ;
its Weyl sparsity 𝑠𝑊 (𝐶) is the number of nonzero coeﬀicients. The main result (Theorem 1,
Section 3) is


                  rank 𝐶 + 𝑠𝑊 (𝐶) ≥ 𝑝 + 1          for every nonzero 𝐶 ∈ 𝑀𝑝 (ℂ),

with equality attained at every sparsity 1 ≤ ℓ ≤ 𝑝 (Section 5.1). When the support lies on a
line of 𝔽2𝑝 , 𝐶 is (up to a Weyl translation and Clifford conjugation) a polynomial 𝑓(𝑍) with
ℓ monomials and the statement reduces to Tao’s principle “an ℓ-term polynomial has at most
ℓ − 1 zeros among the 𝑝-th roots of unity”, which is Chebotarev’s theorem on the minors of
the Fourier matrix. The new content is the non-collinear case. The proof (Section 4) is short:
it is the Frenkel-Tao reduction-modulo-𝑝 argument together with (i) the observation that the
non-unimodular Clifford operators 𝑈𝑠 = 𝐹 ′ 𝑄𝑠 (unnormalised Fourier matrix times a quadratic
phase) conjugate Weyl combinations to Weyl combinations with the same coeﬀicients up to 𝑝-th
roots of unity, so integrality is preserved along all 𝑝 + 1 parallel classes of lines of 𝔽2𝑝 , and (ii)
a pigeonhole over the 𝑝 + 1 lines through a coeﬀicient of minimal valuation, which guarantees
a direction in which the reduced matrix is a nonzero circulant. Theorem 1’ is the sharper
valuation-theoretic form. Section 5 shows that the bound is sharp for every ℓ ≤ 𝑝, that every
intermediate corank is attained at exact sparsity ℓ, that the multiplicative bound alone does not
imply it (the multiplicative bound rank 𝐶 ⋅ 𝑠𝑊 (𝐶) ≥ 𝑑 holds in every dimension), and exactly
where primality enters, matching the composite counterexamples of [0WE]. An exact certificate

1.2 Antecedents
Vector-level uncertainty principles. Chebotarev’s theorem (1926) states that every minor
of the 𝑝 × 𝑝 Fourier matrix (𝜔𝑗𝑘 ) is nonzero; see Stevenhagen and Lenstra [SL96] for the history
and Frenkel [Fr04] for a half-page proof. Tao [Ta05] observed that it is equivalent to | supp 𝑓| +
| supp 𝑓|̂ ≥ 𝑝 + 1 for 𝑓 ≠ 0 on ℤ/𝑝, proved it by reduction modulo the prime (1 − 𝜔) of ℤ[𝜔], and
showed the bound sharp. Frenkel’s proof and Tao’s are the same mechanism: after reduction,
𝑥𝑝 −1 = (𝑥−1)𝑝 , and an 𝑚-term polynomial cannot vanish to order 𝑚 at 𝑥 = 1 because the Euler
operator 𝑥 𝑑/𝑑𝑥 applied 𝑗 < 𝑚 times and evaluated at 1 yields a Vandermonde system in the
(distinct modulo 𝑝) exponents. The multiplicative bound | supp 𝑓| | supp 𝑓|̂ ≥ |𝐺| for every finite
abelian group is due to Donoho and Stark [DS89]; Meshulam [Me92, Me06] refined it for groups
with nontrivial subgroups and extended it to nonabelian groups (rank-support form, [WW21,
Thm. 3.9]); Ghobber and Jaming [GJ11] generalised Tao’s theorem to pairs of bases of ℂ𝑛 .
Later variants of Chebotarev’s theorem remain at the level of vectors: functions with symmetry
(Garcia, Karaali and Katz [GKK21]), cyclic groups of composite order 𝑝𝑟 (Loukaki [Lo25]), real
and finite-field Fourier matrices (Emmrich and Kunis [EK25]), and finite-field-valued functions
on ℤ/𝑝 (Quader, Russell and Sundaram [QRS19]).
Time-frequency shifts. Krahmer, Pfander and Rashkov [KPR08] proved uncertainty princi-
ples for the short-time Fourier transform on finite abelian groups; on ℤ/𝑝 their Proposition 4.4
gives ‖𝑉𝑔 𝑓‖0 ≥ 𝑝(𝑝 + 1) − ‖𝑓‖0 ‖𝑔‖0 when ‖𝑓‖0 + ‖𝑔‖0 > 𝑝, and ‖𝑉𝑔 𝑓‖0 ≥ 𝑝(𝑝 + 1) − (𝑝 + 1 −
‖𝑓‖0 )(𝑝 + 1 − ‖𝑔‖0 ) otherwise and their Theorem 4.5 gives ‖𝑓‖0 + ‖𝑉𝑔 𝑓‖0 ≥ 𝑝2 + 1 for a generic
window 𝑔. Since 𝑉𝑔 𝑓(𝜆) is, up to a phase, the Weyl coeﬀicient of the rank-one operator 𝑓 ⊗ 𝑔 ̄ at
𝜆, these are statements about the Weyl support of rank-one operators, stronger than Theorem
1 in that case and silent for rank ≥ 2. Lawrence, Pfander and Walnut [LPW05] proved that
generic Gabor systems in prime dimension are full spark, and Malikiosis [Ma15] extended this
to every dimension; these concern linear independence of the vectors 𝑊𝑔 𝑣, not the rank of the
operators ∑𝑔 𝑐𝑔 𝑊𝑔 .
Operator-level antecedents. A directly related published inequality between operator rank
and Weyl (Pauli) support is multiplicative: Bu’s “uncertainty principle for Pauli rank” [Bu24,
arXiv v1, Theorem 1, equation (2)] states, for an 𝑛-qudit state 𝜌 expanded in the Heisenberg-
Weyl basis, log rank 𝜌 + log 𝜒𝑃 (𝜌) ≥ 𝑛 log 𝑑, with equality exactly for stabiliser states, where
𝜒𝑃 counts the nonzero Weyl coeﬀicients. In Bu’s Section II normalization, Ξ𝜌 (𝑔) = Tr(𝜌𝑤(𝑔)∗ )
and 𝜌 = 𝑑−𝑛 ∑𝑔 Ξ𝜌 (𝑔)𝑤(𝑔). For 𝑛 = 1, 𝑑 = 𝑝, changing 𝑤(𝑔) to 𝑋 𝑎 𝑍 𝑏 changes only nonzero
phases and the indexing; our expansion coeﬀicients are 𝑝−1 Ξ𝜌 (𝑔) up to those phases. Thus
𝜒𝑃 (𝜌) = 𝑠𝑊 (𝜌), including the identity coeﬀicient, and exponentiating gives rank 𝜌 𝑠𝑊 (𝜌) ≥ 𝑝.
This is the field-free bound rank 𝐶 ⋅ 𝑠𝑊 (𝐶) ≥ 𝑑 of Section 5.2 restricted to positive operators
(the proof there is the same Cauchy-Schwarz argument), and it is exactly the kind of bound
that Section 5.2 shows cannot yield the additive law. Meshulam’s nonabelian rank-support
inequality, applied to a function on the Heisenberg group of order 𝑝3 supported on ℓ lifts of the
labels, bounds ℓ times the total rank of its convolution operator, summed over all irreducible

Calibration. We describe the contribution as a short, apparently new corollary of the Frenkel-
Tao reduction-mod-𝑝 method; the new ingredients are the integrality of Weyl combinations under
the non-unimodular Clifford conjugations 𝐹 ′ 𝑄𝑠 and the pigeonhole over the 𝑝+1 directions. The
literature searches (above and Appendix A) found no prior statement of the operator inequality,
but a bounded negative search does not certify priority. Appendix A records how the manuscript
was produced.

2. Setting
Throughout, 𝑝 is a prime, 𝜔 = 𝑒2𝜋𝑖/𝑝 , and 𝑋, 𝑍 act on ℂ𝑝 by 𝑋|𝑗⟩ = |𝑗 + 1 mod 𝑝⟩, 𝑍|𝑗⟩ = 𝜔𝑗 |𝑗⟩.
They satisfy 𝑍𝑋 = 𝜔𝑋𝑍. The cyclic Weyl basis is 𝑊(𝑎,𝑏) = 𝑋 𝑎 𝑍 𝑏 , (𝑎, 𝑏) ∈ 𝔽2𝑝 ; it is orthogonal
for the trace form, Tr(𝑊𝑔∗ 𝑊ℎ ) = 𝑝 𝛿𝑔ℎ , and it is the fixed basis of [0WE] and [3H0] (a tensor
product of qubit Pauli bases is a different setting and is not considered). For 𝐶 ∈ 𝑀𝑝 (ℂ)
write 𝐶 = ∑𝑔 𝑐𝑔 𝑊𝑔 ; the Weyl support is 𝑆(𝐶) = {𝑔 ∶ 𝑐𝑔 ≠ 0} and the Weyl sparsity is
𝑠𝑊 (𝐶) = |𝑆(𝐶)|. Products of Weyl operators are Weyl operators up to powers of 𝜔: 𝑊𝑔 𝑊ℎ =

8. Open questions
   1. Exact maximal nullity per support. For a support 𝑆 let 𝜈(𝑆) be the maximal nullity
      over full-support coeﬀicient vectors, an AGL(2, 𝑝)-invariant. Known: ℓ − 1 for collinear 𝑆;
      ≤ 1 for non-collinear trinomials ([0WE] Cor. 2.1) and for “line plus one point” (Proposition
      4); 2 for the parabola in 𝔽25 (Proposition 5).√ A dimension count (rank ≤ 𝑝 − 𝑛 has
                      2      ℓ−1
      codimension 𝑛 in ℙ ) suggests 𝜈(𝑆) ≤ ⌊ ℓ − 1⌋ in general position, consistent with
      Section 7(d) (ℓ = 4: 1; ℓ = 5: 2), but this is a heuristic, not a conjecture we can support
      beyond 𝑝 ≤ 11.
   2. A Newton-polygon refinement. Theorem 1’ is a first-order statement (residues modulo
      𝔪). The first-order bound already settles non-collinear trinomials and the parabola of
      Proposition 5 (Section 5.5). Can higher-order information sharpen the bound for other
      supports and determine more values of 𝜈(𝑆)?
   3. Prime powers. For 𝑑 = 𝑝𝑘 the reduction step survives and only the Vandermonde step
      fails (Section 5.3). The 𝑠𝑊 ≤ 3 data of [0WE] Theorem 3 (coranks 𝑝𝑗 , 2𝑝𝑗 ) are consistent
      with dim ker 𝐶 ≤ (ℓ − 1)𝑝𝑘−1 , which for collinear supports is the question of how many
      𝑝𝑘 -th roots of unity an ℓ-term polynomial can annihilate; we have not investigated it.
   4. Real and orthogonal analogues. Chebotarev-type statements exist for real Fourier
      matrices and over finite fields [GKK21, EK25]; is there a rank-sparsity law for real Clifford-
      covariant operator bases, or for the Weyl operators of other finite groups (for instance
      groups of order 𝑝𝑞, where Meshulam’s inequality replaces Tao’s)?
   5. Quantitative versions. The finite-search residuals of Section 7(d) motivate explicit
      quantitative bounds on 𝜎𝑝−ℓ+1 (𝐶)/‖𝑐‖ in terms of 𝑝 and ℓ. Theorem 1 and compactness
      give a qualitative positive minimum for fixed 𝑝, ℓ in the nonvacuous range, including sub-
      supports, but do not identify it with an experimental residual.

repro/, and fresh v003 execution logs in verification/. The final workshop run used Python
