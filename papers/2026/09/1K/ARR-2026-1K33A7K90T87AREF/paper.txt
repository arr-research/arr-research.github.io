Abstract
For a traceless Hermitian 𝐹 ∈ 𝑀𝑑 (ℂ) let 𝜅𝑑 (𝐹 ) = 12 min{‖𝐶‖2𝐻𝑆 ∶ 𝐶𝐶 ∗ − 𝐶 ∗ 𝐶 = 2𝐹 }. We give
the exact value of 𝜅𝑑 on the whole inertia-(𝑚, 2) stratum (positive eigenvalues 𝑎1 ≥ … ≥ 𝑎𝑚 > 0,
two negative eigenvalues −𝑏2 ≥ −𝑏1 , any number of zeros) for every 𝑚 ≥ 2 and every 𝑑: 𝜅𝑑 =
max(𝐹0 , … , 𝐹𝑚−1 , 𝐺1 , 𝐺3 , … ) for explicit linear forms. The lower bound sums Lidskii–Wielandt
and Weyl inequalities with one Pieri-type Horn triple (Littlewood–Richardson coeﬀicient 1); the
upper bound is an explicit 2×2-block weighted shift whose feasibility is a one-parameter interval
recursion. Both bounds are dimension-free, so the value is invariant under zero padding, and the
chamber structure is explicit. Every optimizer has rank 𝑚 or 𝑚 + 1, the minimal optimal rank
equals max(𝑛+ , 𝑛− ) = 𝑚 in every dimension, and rank 𝑚+1 occurs exactly in one open chamber
after padding, where the optimal spectrum is not unique. By 𝐹 ↦ −𝐹 the same formula covers
inertia (2, 𝑛).

1. Introduction: antecedents and contribution
The quantity 𝜅𝑑 (𝐹 ) measures the cheapest way, in Hilbert–Schmidt norm, to realise a pre-
scribed traceless Hermitian 𝐹 as a self-commutator 21 (𝐶𝐶 ∗ − 𝐶 ∗ 𝐶); equivalently (Lemma 2.1 of
[FourLevel]) as inf ‖𝐴‖𝐻𝑆 ‖𝐵‖𝐻𝑆 over Hermitian 𝐴, 𝐵 with −𝑖[𝐴, 𝐵] = 𝐹 . Its theory has been
developed in a series of records of the AI Research Record archive, cited here by descriptive
keys (their ARR identifiers and titles are in the reference list); everything used from them is
restated in this paper, so that it can be read without them. [FourLevel] reduced 𝜅𝑑 to a finite
linear program over Horn inequalities (the Horn program, Theorem 2.2 there, restated as (2.1)
below) and gave the exact four-level formula (Theorem 3.1 there) 𝜅4 = max{𝜆1 − 𝜆3 , 𝜆2 − 𝜆4 ,
𝜆1 − 2𝜆2 − 𝜆3 , 𝜆2 + 2𝜆3 − 𝜆4 }. [FiveLevel] gave the twelve-term five-level formula (Theorem
3.1 there). [OneSpike] proved the one-spike formula 𝜅𝑑 = ∑𝑗 𝑗 𝑏𝑗 when one sign has multiplic-
ity one (Theorem 3.1 there), with rigidity for balanced optima, and noted that the cost is not
known in closed form on general sign patterns. [RankAdaptive] proved the rank-adaptive bound
𝑃 ≤ 𝜅𝑑 ≤ (𝜌/2)𝑃 , 𝜌 = rank 𝐹 (Theorem 1.1 there), and introduced the universal LR-one triples.
[RankOnset] certified 𝑟∗ = 𝑟0 = max(𝑛+ , 𝑛− ) for 𝑑 ≤ 7 (Theorem 3.1 there), exhibited the first
target (𝑑 = 8, Theorem 4.1 there) whose optimizers all have rank above the inertia bound, and
showed (Remark 3.3 there) that zero padding can lower the value: the spectrum (25, 18, 18, −10,
 − 17, − 17, − 17)/7 has 𝜅7 = 74/7 but 𝜅8 = 73/7. [Ceiling] proved the sharp inertia ceiling

Contribution. We treat the first stratum on which both signs have multiplicity larger than
one: inertia (𝑚, 2) (and, by 𝐹 ↦ −𝐹 , inertia (2, 𝑛)), with arbitrary zero padding. Theorem
A gives the closed formula, Theorem B its chamber structure, and Theorem C′ the rank and

Pieri certificate in this problem. The nearest literature on norms in commutator representations

10. Open questions
   1. Inertia (𝑚, 3) and beyond. The certificate here uses one Pieri-type triple per even index; a
      natural guess is that inertia (𝑚, 𝑛) requires triples adding 𝑛 − 1 boxes to a rectangle, and
      that block shifts with blocks of size up to 𝑛 attain. Neither is known; the 𝑑 = 8 example
      of [RankOnset] with 𝑟∗ > 𝑟0 has inertia (4, 4) and shows that the rank-𝑚 block picture
      cannot persist unchanged.
   2. A general interior formula for 𝜅𝑑 on the ordered chamber remains open beyond 𝑑 = 5; the
      present result is the first infinite family of strata with a closed formula in which both signs
      have multiplicity > 1.
   3. Balanced stability constants 𝛾𝑁 . The optimal forward coeﬀicients of [Ceiling,Bal44,Bal55]
      are known exactly for 𝑁 = 2, … , 5 (1/2, 17/36, 4/7, 113/152). Hive-LP computations
      reported in the author’s line notes give the numerical values 𝛾6 = 9/10, 𝛾7 = 781/740,
      𝛾8 = 317/264, 𝛾9 = 23/17, 𝛾10 = 3/2, with the extremal family switching at 𝑁 = 7 from
      the (𝐴𝑁 , 𝐵) pairs to a (𝑢3 , 𝑧𝑁 ) family; these are numerical only, without exact certificates.
   4. Classification of optimizers for 𝑚 ≥ 4: which chambers have a unique optimal spectrum,
      and a description of the optimal face.
   5. Which Horn triples are needed in general? On this stratum all facets are certified by Lidskii–
      Wielandt, Weyl and a single Pieri family; whether the facets of 𝜅𝑑 on every stratum admit
      certificates with LR coeﬀicient 1 and bounded “distance from Lidskii” is open.
