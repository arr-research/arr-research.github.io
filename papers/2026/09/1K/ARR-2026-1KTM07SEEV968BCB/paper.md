# Sparse cyclic Weyl operators in prime-power dimension: an operator extension of the scalar uncertainty law through sparsity $2p-1$

Lluis Eriksson (Independent researcher) — 13 September 2026.

## Abstract

Let $d=p^k$ and let $W_{(a,b)}=X^aZ^b$ be the cyclic Weyl operators on $\mathbb C^d$. Write $w_p(r)$ for the base-$p$ digit weight $\prod_t(r_t+1)$ and $\Omega_k(m)=\max\{r<p^k:w_p(r)\le m\}$. We give a valuation proof of the classical Delvaux–Van Barel scalar law: (Z) a polynomial with at most $m$ terms and degree below $d$ annihilates at most $\Omega_k(m)$ of the $d$-th roots of unity, and this is sharp. Our operator extension is: (B) every nonzero combination $C$ of $\ell\le2p-1$ Weyl operators satisfies $\dim\ker C\le\Omega_k(\ell)$, i.e. $\operatorname{rank}C\ge p^k-(\ell-1)p^{k-1}$ for $\ell\le p$ (sharp) and $\operatorname{rank}C\ge p^{k-1}$ for $p\le\ell\le2p-1$. The commutative ingredient is the Massey–Costello–Justesen weight-retaining property; the operator ingredient is a Radon-type lemma showing that first-order obstructions have weight at least $2p$. For composite $d$ the maximal nullity is $d/p_{\min}$ at sparsity two and, conditionally on a published trinomial rank rule, $\max(2d/r_3,\,d/2\,[d\text{ even}])$ at sparsity three. The conjecture $\dim\ker C\le\Omega_k(s_W(C))$ for all sparsities is proved for $d\in\{4,8,9\}$ and for torsion cosets; its first open case is $d=16$, $\ell=6$.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The results

Fix $d=p^k$, $\omega=e^{2\pi i/d}$, the cyclic Weyl basis $W_{(a,b)}=X^aZ^b$ of $M_d(\mathbb C)$ indexed by $G=(\mathbb Z/d)^2$, and for $C=\sum_gc_gW_g$ the Weyl sparsity $s_W(C)=\#\{g:c_g\ne0\}$. The published prime law [3W] states that in prime dimension $\dim\ker C\le s_W(C)-1$. Its Open question 3 asked what replaces this in prime-power dimension, where the coranks $p^j,2p^j$ of [0WE] Theorem 3 already show that $\ell-1$ is wrong, and suggested the candidate $(\ell-1)p^{k-1}$. This paper answers the question in the range $\ell\le2p-1$ and identifies the function that governs the problem. With $w_p(r)=\prod_t(r_t+1)$ for $r=\sum_tr_tp^t$ and
$$\Omega_k(m)=\max\{\,r<p^k:\ w_p(r)\le m\,\},$$
the scalar antecedent and the operator results are:

* **Theorem Z (Delvaux–Van Barel scalar law, in digit-weight notation).** The maximal number of $p^k$-th roots of unity annihilated by a nonzero polynomial of degree $<p^k$ with at most $m$ nonzero coefficients is exactly $\Omega_k(m)$. The product construction used in our proof is certified exactly in $\mathbb Z[\omega_d]$ for $d\in\{4,8,9,16,25,27,32,49,81\}$.
* **Theorem B (operators).** If $d=p^k$ and $1\le s_W(C)=\ell\le2p-1$, then $\dim\ker C\le\Omega_k(\ell)$. Explicitly, $\dim\ker C\le(\ell-1)p^{k-1}$ for $\ell\le p$, sharp at every such $\ell$, and $\dim\ker C\le(p-1)p^{k-1}$ for $p\le\ell\le2p-1$ ($k\ge2$). The candidate $(\ell-1)p^{k-1}$ of [3W] is therefore right exactly up to $\ell=p$; beyond it the true envelope is $\Omega_k$, which grows much more slowly (for example $\Omega_k(p^a)=p^k-p^{k-a}$).
* **Theorems T2 and T3 (composite $d$).** For every $d\ge2$ the maximal nullity at sparsity two is $d/p_{\min}(d)$; for every $d\ge3$ the maximal nullity at sparsity three is $\max(2d/r_3(d),\,\tfrac d2[d\text{ even}])$, where $r_3(d)$ is the smallest divisor of $d$ that is at least $3$; the second branch is attained by an anticommuting pair when $d\equiv2\pmod 4$. Theorem T3 is conditional on the trinomial rank rule [0WE] Theorem 2, used as published. Both laws refute every additive law in the largest prime or prime-power factor of $d$.

The commutative ingredient (Theorem A, Section 3) is a characteristic-$p$ statement: a polynomial with $m$ nonzero terms vanishes at $x=1$ to order $r$ only if $w_p(r)\le m$. This is a special case of the weight-retaining property of Massey, Costello and Justesen [MCJ73], and we say so plainly; a short self-contained proof is included because the form needed here (arbitrary fields of characteristic $p$) is the one used in the valuation argument. The operator ingredient is new relative to [3W]: the pigeonhole over $p+1$ directions of [3W] is replaced by Lemma N, which shows that a residue vector with vanishing fibre sums in all $p^{k-1}(p+1)$ primitive directions has at least $2p$ nonzero entries, via a Radon-type lemma on $\mathbb F_p^2$ (Lemma R).

Beyond $2p-1$ the first-order method genuinely stops (two parallel lines of a $p$-torsion coset form an obstruction of weight $2p$), and we state Conjecture C: $\dim\ker C\le\Omega_k(s_W(C))$ for all $C$. It is a theorem for $d\in\{4,8,9\}$, for every support inside a coset of the $p$-torsion, for every support inside a coset of the $4$-torsion when $d\in\{16,32\}$, and wherever the field-free bound $\operatorname{rank}C\cdot s_W(C)\ge d$ suffices; the first open case is $d=16$, $\ell\in\{6,7\}$ (Section 6).

### 1.2 Antecedents

The prime case is [3W] (Sections 2–5 there: the setting, the valuation reduction modulo $(1-\omega)$, the explicit Clifford conjugations $F'Q_s$, the pigeonhole, and Frenkel's lemma), which in turn rests on Tao's uncertainty principle [Ta05] and Frenkel's proof of Chebotarev's theorem [Fr04]. The three-term record [0WE] proves the trinomial rank rule (Theorem 2 there, with its Lemma 1 on central sectors), the classification of coranks for $s_W\le3$ in prime-power dimension (Theorem 3), and exhibits composite counterexamples to any $\ell-1$ law: $2^{1/4}I+X^3+Z^3$ in $d=12$ (nullity $3$, Proposition 4), $I+X^3+iX^{-3}Z^{18}$ in $d=36$ (nullity $6$, eq. (6a)), and the binomial $X(I+Z^6)$ in $d=12$ (nullity $6$, eq. (6c)). The origin of the Weyl rank problem is quantum list decoding: [3H0] determines exact list thresholds for Weyl channels with consecutive and arithmetic probes, [0WE] eq. (8) reduces list decoding to a rank problem, and [3W] Corollary 2 closes the prime case. The list-decoding consequences of the present rank bounds are not pursued here.

On the commutative side, Meshulam's uncertainty inequality for finite abelian groups [Me06] is the general tool (used in Section 6 for torsion cosets); Delvaux and Van Barel [DVB08] prove the prime-power Fourier Hamming-number law, which is exactly the monotone scalar zero-count envelope of Theorem Z in equivalent notation (Definition 7 and Theorem 10, equation (23), in their author-hosted report [DVB06]); Lam and Leung [LL00] classify the lengths of vanishing sums of roots of unity, the phenomenon behind the "smallest divisor" laws of Section 5; Bíró and Lev [BL18] give the sharp uncertainty inequality on $\mathbb F_p^2$, relevant to the $p$-torsion cosets of Section 6; Loukaki [Lo25] treats principal Fourier minors of composite order $pq$. Lucas' theorem [Lu78] is what makes $w_p$ the right weight.

### 1.3 Calibration

Theorem A is classical [MCJ73], and the scalar envelope Theorem Z is the known Delvaux–Van Barel theorem [DVB06, DVB08]; Section 4.2 supplies a self-contained proof through valuation reduction and digit weight. Lemma R is the classical minimum-distance statement for the dual incidence code of the affine plane of prime order, stated here over any characteristic-$p$ field with an elementary proof; see [KMM08, Section 2, Result 1]. The contribution proposed here is the extension to arbitrary complex combinations of cyclic Weyl operators through sparsity $2p-1$, the valuation formulation B′, and the primitive-direction/torsion-coset mechanism N. The search for prior operator results is bounded and does not certify worldwide priority. Theorems T2 and T3 are short consequences of trace orthogonality and of the imported [0WE] rank rule, respectively; T3 remains conditional. Verification coverage is itemised in Section 7. The supplied history includes author-only runs, separately implemented checks and unrepeated computations, not a universal independent replay.

## 2. Notation and setting

**Weyl operators.** For $d\ge2$ let $\omega=\omega_d=e^{2\pi i/d}$, $X|j\rangle=|j+1\bmod d\rangle$, $Z|j\rangle=\omega^j|j\rangle$, so $ZX=\omega XZ$, and $W_{(a,b)}=X^aZ^b$ for $(a,b)\in G=(\mathbb Z/d)^2$. Then $W_gW_h=\omega^{\langle g,h\rangle}W_{g+h}$ with $\langle(a,b),(a',b')\rangle=ba'$, $\operatorname{Tr}W_g=0$ for $g\ne0$, and the $W_g$ form a trace-orthogonal basis of $M_d(\mathbb C)$. For $C=\sum_gc_gW_g$ the support is $S(C)=\{g:c_g\ne0\}$ and $s_W(C)=|S(C)|$. Left multiplication by $W_h^{-1}$ translates the support by $-h$, multiplies coefficients by roots of unity and preserves the kernel; conjugation by an invertible matrix $U$ with $UW_gU^{-1}\in\mathbb C\,W_{Mg}$ ($M\in SL_2(\mathbb Z/d)$) maps the support to $MS$ and preserves rank. Two families of such $U$ are made explicit in Section 4.3.

**Digit weight.** For $d=p^k$ and $0\le r<p^k$ write $r=\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}r_tp^t$ with $0\le r_t<p$ and put $w_p(r)=\prod_t(r_t+1)$. By Lucas' theorem, $\binom ri\not\equiv0\pmod p$ exactly when every digit of $i$ is at most the corresponding digit of $r$, so $w_p(r)$ is the number of nonzero terms of $(x-1)^r$ in characteristic $p$. For $m\ge1$ let $\Omega_k(m)=\max\{r<p^k:w_p(r)\le m\}$; it is nondecreasing in $m$, $\Omega_k(1)=0$, $\Omega_k(p^k)=p^k-1$, and
$$\begin{aligned}\Omega_k(m)&=(m-1)p^{k-1}&&(1\le m\le p),\\ \Omega_k(m)&=(p-1)p^{k-1}+(\lfloor m/p\rfloor-1)p^{k-2}&&(p\le m<p^2,\ k\ge2),\\ \Omega_k(p^a)&=p^k-p^{k-a}&&(0\le a\le k).\end{aligned}$$
(For $m\le p$: a number above $(m-1)p^{k-1}$ has top digit $\ge m$, or top digit $m-1$ and a further nonzero digit, hence weight $>m$. The other two formulas are checked the same way; all three were also verified by computer for every $p^k\le2401$.) For example, for $d=16$ the values $\Omega_4(1..16)$ are $0,8,8,12,12,12,12,14,\dots,14,15$; for $d=27$, $\Omega_3(m)$ is $0,9,18$ for $m=1,2,3$, then $18$ ($m=4,5$), $21$ ($6\le m\le8$), $24$ ($9\le m\le17$), $25$ ($18\le m\le26$), $26$.

**Torsion and directions.** For $d=p^k$ let $T_j=p^{k-j}G$ be the $p^j$-torsion, so $T_1\cong\mathbb F_p^2$. A *primitive direction* is a surjective linear form $\lambda:G\to\mathbb Z/d$ up to units; there are $|\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Z/p^k)|=p^{k-1}(p+1)$ of them, and the fibres of $\lambda$ are the cosets of $\ker\lambda$, a cyclic subgroup of order $p^k$; we call these cosets *primitive lines*. Every cyclic subgroup of order $p^k$ is the kernel of exactly one primitive direction. A support contained in a primitive line is called *collinear*.

**Valuation rings.** Let $K$ be a subfield of $\mathbb C$ containing $\omega_d$. A valuation ring $\mathcal O\subset K$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, residue field $\Bbbk=\mathcal O/\mathfrak m$ and valuation $v$ is *admissible* if $1-\omega_d\in\mathfrak m$. Since $1-\omega_{p^k}$ generates the unique prime of $\mathbb Z[\omega_{p^k}]$ above $p$ and $p$ is a unit multiple of $(1-\omega_{p^k})^{\varphi(p^k)}$, admissibility forces $p\in\mathfrak m$, so $\operatorname{char}\Bbbk=p$ and every $d$-th root of unity reduces to $1$. Admissible valuation rings exist on every finitely generated $K\supseteq\mathbb Q(\omega_d)$ by Chevalley's extension theorem ([St, Tag 00IA]; this is the route of [3W], Step 1). Two rank facts are used: for $A\in M_d(\mathcal O)$, $\operatorname{rank}_KA\ge\operatorname{rank}_\Bbbk\bar A$; for $A\in M_d(K)$, $\operatorname{rank}_KA=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb C}A$.

**Root-of-unity counts.** For any $d$ and $m\ge1$ put $Z(d,m)=\max\#\{\zeta\in\mu_d:f(\zeta)=0\}$ over polynomials $f$ with exactly $m$ nonzero coefficients and exponents distinct modulo $d$, and $Z_\le(d,m)=\max_{m'\le m}Z(d,m')$. Since $\{f(\zeta)\}_{\zeta\in\mu_d}$ is the spectrum of $f(Z)$, $Z(d,m)$ is the maximal nullity of a polynomial in $Z$ with exactly $m$ terms, i.e. of a collinear Weyl combination on the $Z$-axis with exact sparsity $m$.

## 3. Statements

**Theorem A (order and digit weight in characteristic $p$; [MCJ73]).** Let $\Bbbk$ be any field of characteristic $p$ and $f\in\Bbbk[x]$ nonzero with exactly $m$ nonzero monomials. Then $w_p(\operatorname{ord}_{x=1}f)\le m$. If moreover $\deg f<p^k$, then $\operatorname{ord}_1f\le\Omega_k(m)$, and $\max\{\operatorname{ord}_1f:\deg f<p^k,\ \#\text{terms}\le m\}=\Omega_k(m)$, attained by $(x-1)^{\Omega_k(m)}$.

Theorem A is the weight-retaining property of Massey, Costello and Justesen ([MCJ73], Theorem 1.1 for $\operatorname{GF}(2^r)$ and Section III-B, Theorem 6.1 for $\operatorname{GF}(q)$, with their Lemma 1 giving $W[(x-c)^i]=\prod_j(i_j+1)$): writing $f=\sum_{i\ge r}b_i(x-1)^i$ with $b_r\ne0$ and $r=\operatorname{ord}_1f$, their theorem states that the Hamming weight of $f$ is at least that of $(x-1)^r$. They state it over finite fields; the proof in Section 4.1 is written for an arbitrary field of characteristic $p$, which is the form the valuation argument needs. The degree restriction is needed only for the reformulation through $\Omega_k$ (note $x^{p^k}-1=(x-1)^{p^k}$ has two terms).

**Theorem Z (Delvaux–Van Barel scalar law in equivalent notation).** For $d=p^k$ and $m\ge1$, $Z_\le(d,m)=\Omega_k(m)$. Equivalently, a polynomial in $Z$ with at most $m$ terms has nullity at most $\Omega_k(m)$, and for every $r<p^k$ the polynomial
$$f_r(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\ \prod_{i=1}^{r_{j-1}}\big(z^{p^{j-1}}-\omega_p^{\,i}\big),\qquad \omega_p=\omega^{p^{k-1}}=e^{2\pi i/p},\quad r=\sum_{j=1}^kr_{j-1}p^{j-1},$$
has exactly $w_p(r)$ nonzero coefficients, degree $r$, and exactly $r$ zeros in $\mu_d$. In particular $Z(d,m)=\Omega_k(m)$ whenever $w_p(\Omega_k(m))=m$ (for instance $m\le p$), and $Z(d,m)\le\Omega_k(m)$ always.

**Equivalence with the classical Hamming-number formula.** Let $1\le m<p^k$, and write $cp^a\le m<(c+1)p^a$, with $1\le c\le p-1$ and $0\le a<k$. Then
$$\Omega_k(m)=p^k-(p-c+1)p^{k-a-1},\qquad H_{F_{p^k}}(m)=p^k-\Omega_k(m).$$
The first integer has $a$ leading base-$p$ digits $p-1$, then $c-1$, then zeros, so its weight is $cp^a$. Any larger integer has digit weight at least $(c+1)p^a$: either an earlier digit increases, or a lower nonzero digit at least doubles the weight, and $2c\ge c+1$. The second identity is [DVB06, Definition 7 and Theorem 10, equation (23)]: their support budget is at most $m$. Fourier values are polynomial evaluations on roots of unity, so minimum nonzero Fourier support equals $p^k$ minus maximum zero count. For $m\ge p^k$, separately, $\Omega_k(m)=p^k-1$ and the minimum Fourier support is $1$.

**Theorem B′ (valuation form, all sparsities).** Let $d=p^k$, let $K\subseteq\mathbb C$ contain $\omega_d$ and, if $p=2$, also $\omega_{2d}$; let $\mathcal O\subset K$ be admissible. Let $C=\sum_{g\in S}c_gW_g$ with all $c_g\in K^\times$, scaled so that $\min_gv(c_g)=0$, and let $\bar c_g\in\Bbbk$ be the residues. For a primitive direction $\lambda$ put $\gamma^\lambda_t=\sum_{\lambda(g)=t}\bar c_g$ ($t\in\mathbb Z/d$) and $m_\lambda=\#\{t:\gamma^\lambda_t\ne0\}$. Then for every $\lambda$ with $m_\lambda\ge1$,
$$\dim\ker C\ \le\ \Omega_k(m_\lambda)\ \le\ \Omega_k(|S|).$$

**Lemma R (Radon kernel on $\mathbb F_p^2$).** Let $\Bbbk$ have characteristic $p$ and let $f:\mathbb F_p^2\to\Bbbk$ be nonzero with zero sum on every affine line. Then $|\operatorname{supp}f|\ge2p$. The bound is attained by $1_{L_1}-1_{L_2}$ for two parallel lines.

**Lemma N (first-order obstructions have weight at least $2p$).** Let $d=p^k$ and let $\bar c:G\to\Bbbk$ be nonzero with zero sum on every primitive line. Then $|\operatorname{supp}\bar c|\ge2p$; two parallel lines inside a coset of $T_1$ attain the bound.

**Theorem B (operator law in prime-power dimension, $\ell\le2p-1$).** Let $d=p^k$ and $C\ne0$ with $s_W(C)=\ell\le2p-1$. Then $\dim\ker C\le\Omega_k(\ell)$; explicitly
$$\operatorname{rank}C\ \ge\ p^k-(\ell-1)p^{k-1}\quad(\ell\le p),\qquad \operatorname{rank}C\ \ge\ p^{k-1}\quad(p\le\ell\le2p-1,\ k\ge2).$$
For $\ell\le p$ the bound is attained by $C_\ell=\prod_{i=0}^{\ell-2}(Z^{p^{k-1}}-\omega_p^iI)$, which has $s_W=\ell$ and nullity $(\ell-1)p^{k-1}$. For $k=1$ this is [3W] Theorem 1.

**Corollary (coranks at sparsity $\le3$).** $\{r:w_p(r)\le3\}=\{0\}\cup\{p^j,2p^j:j<k\}$ for odd $p$ and $\{0\}\cup\{2^j:j<k\}$ for $p=2$, so Theorem B′ bounds the nullity of every $C$ with $s_W(C)\le3$ by the largest element of this set, $2p^{k-1}$ resp. $2^{k-1}$, in agreement with [0WE] Theorem 3, whose corank set $\mathcal N_p$ is exactly this set without $0$.

**Theorem T2 (binomials, every $d\ge2$).** $\max\{\dim\ker C:s_W(C)=2\}=d/p_{\min}(d)$, where $p_{\min}(d)$ is the smallest prime factor of $d$.

**Theorem T3 (trinomials, every $d\ge3$; conditional on [0WE] Theorem 2).** Let $r_3(d)$ be the smallest divisor of $d$ that is $\ge3$. Then
$$\nu_3(d):=\max\{\dim\ker C:s_W(C)=3\}=\max\Big(\frac{2d}{r_3(d)},\ \frac d2\,[d\text{ even}]\Big),$$
$$\max\{\dim\ker C:s_W(C)\le3\}=\max\Big(\frac d{p_{\min}(d)},\ \frac{2d}{r_3(d)}\Big).$$
The second branch of $\nu_3$ is strictly larger than the first exactly when $d\equiv2\pmod4$ and $3\nmid d$; it is attained by the anticommuting pair $Z^{d/2},X^{d/2}$.

**Conjecture C (prime powers, all sparsities).** For $d=p^k$ and every nonzero $C$, $\dim\ker C\le\Omega_k(s_W(C))$.

**Conjecture U (every $d$).** For every $d$ and every nonzero $C$, $\dim\ker C\le Z_\le(d,s_W(C))$: the monotone collinear maximum bounds all operators. (The exact-sparsity version $\dim\ker C\le Z(d,s_W(C))$ is false: $d=10$, $\ell=3$, Section 5.)

## 4. Proofs

### 4.1 Theorem A

*Frenkel's lemma* ([Fr04]; [3W] Step 6): a nonzero $q=\sum_{i=1}^na_ix^{t_i}\in\Bbbk[x]$ with distinct exponents $t_i\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}$ has $\operatorname{ord}_1q\le n-1$. Indeed, with $\theta=x\,d/dx$, $\theta$ maps $(x-1)^a\Bbbk[x]$ into $(x-1)^{a-1}\Bbbk[x]$, so $\operatorname{ord}_1q\ge n$ would give $(\theta^sq)(1)=\sum_ia_it_i^s=0$ for $0\le s<n$, a Vandermonde system in the $t_i$, which are distinct in $\mathbb F_p\subseteq\Bbbk$; hence all $a_i=0$.

We prove $w_p(\operatorname{ord}_1f)\le m$ by induction on $k$, where $\deg f<p^k$ (every polynomial has such a $k$; the degree restriction is only used to run the induction). For $k=0$, $f$ is a nonzero constant, $\operatorname{ord}_1f=0$ and $w_p(0)=1\le m$. For $k\ge1$ write $f=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}x^jg_j(x^p)$ with $\deg g_j<p^{k-1}$; the exponents $pj'+j$ are distinct, so $m=\sum_j\#\text{terms}(g_j)$. Put $x=1+y$. Since $(1+y)^p=1+y^p$,
$$f(1+y)=\sum_{j}(1+y)^j\,g_j(1+y^p)=\sum_{n\ge0}y^{pn}P_n(y),\qquad P_n(y)=\sum_jh_{j,n}(1+y)^j,$$
where $h_{j,n}$ is the coefficient of $Y^n$ in $g_j(1+Y)$ (the $n$-th Hasse derivative of $g_j$ at $1$) and $\deg P_n\le p-1$. The block $y^{pn}P_n$ occupies degrees $[pn,pn+p-1]$, disjoint for different $n$. Hence, if $r=\operatorname{ord}_1f=pr'+r_0$ with $0\le r_0<p$, then $P_n=0$ for $n<r'$ and $\operatorname{ord}_yP_{r'}=r_0$. Because $\{(1+y)^j\}_{0\le j<p}$ is a basis of the polynomials of degree $<p$, $P_n=0$ if and only if $h_{j,n}=0$ for all $j$. Therefore $\operatorname{ord}_1g_j\ge r'$ for every $j$, with equality exactly on $J_0=\{j:h_{j,r'}\ne0\}\ne\emptyset$. The polynomial $q(x)=P_{r'}(x-1)=\sum_{j\in J_0}h_{j,r'}x^j$ is nonzero, has $|J_0|$ terms with distinct exponents in $\{0,\dots,p-1\}$, and $\operatorname{ord}_1q=r_0$; Frenkel's lemma gives $|J_0|\ge r_0+1$. For $j\in J_0$ the polynomial $g_j$ is nonzero, has degree $<p^{k-1}$ and order $r'$, so by induction $\#\text{terms}(g_j)\ge w_p(r')$. Altogether
$$m\ \ge\ \sum_{j\in J_0}\#\text{terms}(g_j)\ \ge\ |J_0|\,w_p(r')\ \ge\ (r_0+1)\,w_p(r')\ =\ w_p(r).$$
If $\deg f<p^k$ then $r<p^k$, so $w_p(r)\le m$ means $r\le\Omega_k(m)$; conversely $(x-1)^{\Omega_k(m)}$ has $w_p(\Omega_k(m))\le m$ terms (Lucas) and order $\Omega_k(m)$. $\square$

*Remark.* The function $m\mapsto\max\{\operatorname{ord}_1f:\text{exactly }m\text{ terms}\}$ is not monotone (for $d=16$ it takes the value $8$ at $m=2$ and $4$ at $m=3$); the clean statement is the "at most $m$" one.

### 4.2 Alternative proof of the classical scalar law Z

*Upper bound.* Let $f\in\mathbb C[x]$ be nonzero, $\deg f<d$, with at most $m$ terms. Let $K=\mathbb Q(\omega)(\text{coefficients of }f)$, finitely generated over $\mathbb Q(\omega)$, and let $\mathcal O$ be an admissible valuation ring of $K$; divide $f$ by a coefficient of minimal valuation, so $f\in\mathcal O[x]$ and the reduction $\bar f\in\Bbbk[x]$ is nonzero with at most $m$ terms and degree $<p^k$. The number of zeros of $f$ in $\mu_d$ is the nullity of the diagonal matrix $f(Z)$; conjugating by the unnormalised Fourier matrix $F'=(\omega^{jl})$, for which $F'^{-1}ZF'=X$ (Section 4.3), it is also the nullity of the circulant $f(X)\in M_d(\mathcal O)$. (Reducing $f(Z)$ itself would only give $\bar f(1)I$; the circulant form is what makes the reduction informative.) Now $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb C}f(X)=\operatorname{rank}_Kf(X)\ge\operatorname{rank}_\Bbbk\bar f(\bar X)$, where $\bar X$ is the cyclic shift over $\Bbbk$. As a $\Bbbk[x]$-module, $\Bbbk^d\cong\Bbbk[x]/(x^{p^k}-1)=\Bbbk[x]/(x-1)^{p^k}$, and $\bar f(\bar X)$ is multiplication by $\bar f=(x-1)^{r}u$ with $u(1)\ne0$, $r=\operatorname{ord}_1\bar f\le\deg\bar f<p^k$; $u$ is a unit modulo $(x-1)^{p^k}$ and the kernel of multiplication by $(x-1)^r$ has dimension $r$. Hence $\#\{\zeta\in\mu_d:f(\zeta)=0\}\le r\le\Omega_k(m)$ by Theorem A.

*Lower bound.* Fix $r<p^k$ with digits $r_{j-1}$ and let $f_r$ be as stated. For $n\le p-1$ the $q$-binomial theorem with $q=\omega_p$ gives $\prod_{i=1}^{n}(u-\omega_p^i)=\sum_{t=0}^n(-1)^t\omega_p^{t(t+1)/2}\binom nt_{\omega_p}u^{n-t}$, and every Gaussian binomial $\binom nt_{\omega_p}=\prod_{i=1}^t(1-\omega_p^{n-t+i})/(1-\omega_p^i)$ is nonzero because $\omega_p$ is a primitive $p$-th root of unity and all exponents are in $[1,p-1]$. So the $j$-th inner product has exactly $r_{j-1}+1$ nonzero coefficients, at the exponents $ip^{j-1}$, $0\le i\le r_{j-1}$. The exponents of the full product are the numbers $\sum_ji_jp^{j-1}$ with $0\le i_j\le r_{j-1}$, which are pairwise distinct base-$p$ expansions below $p^k$; no cancellation occurs and $f_r$ has exactly $\prod_j(r_{j-1}+1)=w_p(r)$ terms and degree $r$. The factor $z^{p^{j-1}}-\omega_p^i$ ($1\le i\le p-1$) vanishes at the $z\in\mu_d$ with $z^{p^{j-1}}=\omega_p^i$: the map $z\mapsto z^{p^{j-1}}$ from $\mu_{p^k}$ onto $\mu_{p^{k-j+1}}$ has fibres of size $p^{j-1}$ and $\omega_p^i\in\mu_p\subseteq\mu_{p^{k-j+1}}$, so there are exactly $p^{j-1}$ such $z$, all of exact order $p^j$. These sets are disjoint over $(j,i)$, so $f_r$ has at least $\sum_jr_{j-1}p^{j-1}=r$ zeros in $\mu_d$, and exactly $r$ since $\deg f_r=r$. Taking $r=\Omega_k(m)$ gives $Z_\le(d,m)\ge\Omega_k(m)$. $\square$

Note that modulo $1-\omega$ each factor becomes $(z-1)^{p^{j-1}}$, so $\bar f_r=(z-1)^r$: the construction lifts the extremal polynomial of Theorem A.

### 4.3 The Clifford family and the $p=2$ phases

Let $F'=(\omega^{jl})_{j,l}$, so $F'^{-1}=d^{-1}F'^*$, $F'XF'^{-1}=Z$, $F'ZF'^{-1}=X^{-1}$, hence $F'^{-1}XF'=Z^{-1}$ and $F'^{-1}ZF'=X$ (valid for every $d$). For odd $d$ let $2^{-1}$ be the inverse of $2$ modulo $d$ and $Q_s=\operatorname{diag}(\omega^{s\cdot2^{-1}j^2})_j$ for $s\in\mathbb Z/d$; for $d=2^k$ let $\omega_{2d}=e^{2\pi i/2d}$ and $Q_s=\operatorname{diag}(\omega_{2d}^{sj^2})_j$ (well defined on $j\in\mathbb Z/d$ because $(j+d)^2\equiv j^2\pmod{2d}$). In both cases $Q_sZQ_s^{-1}=Z$ and $Q_sXQ_s^{-1}=\phi_sXZ^s$ with $\phi_s=\omega^{s\cdot2^{-1}}$ resp. $\omega_{2d}^{s}$, and using $(XZ^s)^a=\omega^{sa(a-1)/2}X^aZ^{sa}$ one gets $Q_sW_{(a,b)}Q_s^{-1}=\varepsilon\,X^aZ^{sa+b}$ with $\varepsilon=\omega^{s\cdot2^{-1}a^2}$ resp. $\omega_{2d}^{sa^2}$. Put $U_s=F'Q_s$ and $V_s=F'^{-1}Q_sF'$. Then, exactly in $M_d(\mathbb Q(\omega))$ resp. $M_d(\mathbb Q(\omega_{2d}))$,
$$U_sW_{(a,b)}U_s^{-1}=\varepsilon\,\omega^{-a(sa+b)}\,W_{(-(sa+b),\,a)},\qquad V_sW_{(a,b)}V_s^{-1}=\varepsilon'\,W_{(a-sb,\,b)},$$
with $\varepsilon'=\omega^{-s\cdot2^{-1}b^2}$ resp. $\omega_{2d}^{-sb^2}$ (compute $F'W_{(a,b)}F'^{-1}=\omega^{-ab}W_{(-b,a)}$, apply $Q_s$, and conjugate back). The first coordinates of the label maps are the linear forms $\lambda_s(a,b)=-(sa+b)$ for $U_s$ ($s\in\mathbb Z/d$), $\lambda_\infty(a,b)=a$ for $U_\infty=I$, and $\lambda'_s(a,b)=a-sb$ for $V_s$. The forms $\lambda_s,\lambda_\infty$ give the $p^k+1$ primitive directions $(s,1)$ and $(1,0)$; the forms $\lambda'_s$ with $s\in p\mathbb Z/d$, $s\ne0$, give the remaining $p^{k-1}-1$ directions $(1,t)$ with $p\mid t\ne0$. Hence the family $\{I\}\cup\{U_s:s\in\mathbb Z/d\}\cup\{V_s:s\in p\mathbb Z/d\setminus0\}$ realises each of the $p^{k-1}(p+1)$ primitive directions exactly once. (This closes the first gap of the review: the family $F'Q_s$ alone, as in [3W], realises only $p^k+1$ directions when $k\ge2$; the $V_s$ are needed, and they are what the verification script always used.) All phases lie in $\mu_d$, or in $\mu_{2d}$ when $p=2$.

*The phases for $p=2$.* Let $d=2^k$, $K\ni\omega_{2d}$ and $\mathcal O$ admissible. Then $2\in\mathfrak m$, and $2$ is a unit multiple of $(1-\omega_{2d})^{2^k}$ in $\mathbb Z[\omega_{2d}]$ (since $1-\omega_{2d}$ generates the totally ramified prime above $2$); as $\mathfrak m$ is prime, $1-\omega_{2d}\in\mathfrak m$. So every phase in $\mu_{2d}$ reduces to $1$, exactly as the $d$-th roots of unity do. If the coefficient field does not contain $\omega_{2d}$ one adjoins it and extends the valuation ring ([St, Tag 00IA]); ranks are unchanged. This closes the second gap of the review.

For each $U$ in the family put $C_U=UCU^{-1}=\sum_gc_g\varepsilon_gW_{M_Ug}$: it is an $\mathcal O$-combination of Weyl matrices, hence lies in $M_d(\mathcal O)$ although $U$ is not unimodular, and $\operatorname{rank}_KC_U=\operatorname{rank}_KC$.

### 4.4 Theorem B′

Let $\lambda$ be a primitive direction with $m_\lambda\ge1$ and $U$ the member of the family whose label map has first coordinate $\lambda$. Modulo $\mathfrak m$ every $Z^b$ and every phase becomes $I$, while $X$ becomes the cyclic shift $\bar X$ of order $p^k$ over $\Bbbk$. Therefore
$$\bar C_U=\sum_{t\in\mathbb Z/d}\gamma^\lambda_t\,\bar X^{\,t}=\bar g_\lambda(\bar X),\qquad \bar g_\lambda(x)=\sum_t\gamma^\lambda_tx^t\in\Bbbk[x],$$
with exponents taken in $\{0,\dots,d-1\}$ (legitimate since $\bar X^d=I$). The polynomial $\bar g_\lambda$ is nonzero, has $m_\lambda$ terms and degree $<p^k$; as in Section 4.2 the nullity of $\bar g_\lambda(\bar X)$ on $\Bbbk^d\cong\Bbbk[x]/(x-1)^{p^k}$ equals $\operatorname{ord}_1\bar g_\lambda$, which is at most $\Omega_k(m_\lambda)$ by Theorem A. Hence
$$\dim\ker_{\mathbb C}C=d-\operatorname{rank}_KC_U\le d-\operatorname{rank}_\Bbbk\bar C_U=\operatorname{ord}_1\bar g_\lambda\le\Omega_k(m_\lambda)\le\Omega_k(|S|),$$
the last step because $m_\lambda$ is at most the number of fibres meeting $S$ and $\Omega_k$ is nondecreasing. $\square$

### 4.5 Elementary proof of the classical affine-plane bound R

Suppose $f\ne0$ has zero sum on every affine line and $|\operatorname{supp}f|\le2p-1$. A line meeting the support meets it in at least two points (a single nonzero value cannot sum to zero). Call one direction vertical and let $E\subseteq\mathbb F_p$ be the set of $x$ whose column $\{x\}\times\mathbb F_p$ meets the support; $|E|=p$ would give at least $2p$ points, so $|E|\le p-1$, and $E\ne\emptyset$.

Let $R=\Bbbk[\mathbb Z/p]=\Bbbk[t]/(t^p-1)=\Bbbk[u]/(u^p)$ with $u=t-1$, acting on functions on $\mathbb F_p$ by translation, $(tg)(y)=g(y-1)$, and let $g_x=f(x,\cdot)\in R$. A zero column sum means $g_x\in uR$ (the augmentation ideal). For a slope $\mu\in\mathbb F_p$, the sums over the lines $y=\mu x+c$ are the values of $\sum_{x\in E}t^{-\mu x}g_x$ at $c$, so
$$\sum_{x\in E}t^{\mu x}g_x=0\quad\text{in }R\text{ for every }\mu\in\mathbb F_p.$$
Let $\nu=\min_{x\in E}\operatorname{ord}_u(g_x)$; then $1\le\nu\le p-1$ and $g_x=u^\nu g'_x$ with $g'_x(0)\ne0$ for at least one $x$. Now $t^{\mu x}=(1+u)^{\mu x}=\sum_{i<p}\binom{\mu x}{i}u^i$, and for $i<p$ the coefficient $\binom ni\bmod p$ depends only on $n\bmod p$ (Lucas) and equals $P_i(n)$ for the polynomial $P_i(n)=n(n-1)\cdots(n-i+1)/i!$ of degree $i$ over $\mathbb F_p$. The coefficient of $u^{\nu+j}$ ($0\le j\le p-\nu-1$) in the identity is $\sum_{x\in E}\sum_{i\le j}P_i(\mu x)\,g'_{x,j-i}$, a polynomial in $\mu$ of degree $\le j<p$ that vanishes on all of $\mathbb F_p$ and is therefore identically zero; its coefficient of $\mu^j$ comes only from $i=j$ and gives $\sum_{x\in E}x^jg'_x(0)=0$ for $0\le j\le p-\nu-1$. If $p-\nu\ge|E|$ this is an invertible Vandermonde system in the distinct $x\in E$, forcing all $g'_x(0)=0$, a contradiction; hence $\nu\ge p-|E|+1$.

By Frenkel's lemma (the $k=1$ case of Theorem A: a nonzero element of $u^\nu R$, viewed as a polynomial of degree $<p$ in $t$ with order $\ge\nu$ at $t=1$, has at least $\nu+1$ nonzero coefficients), every nonempty column carries at least $\nu+1\ge p-|E|+2$ support points. In particular $|E|=1$ is impossible, since a column has only $p$ points (equivalently, $\nu\ge p$ contradicts $\nu\le p-1$). Thus $2\le|E|\le p-1$ (so $p\ge3$; for $p=2$ we have already reached a contradiction) and
$$|\operatorname{supp}f|\ \ge\ |E|\,(p+2-|E|)\ \ge\ \min\big(2p,\ 3(p-1)\big)=2p,$$
the minimum of the concave function over $[2,p-1]$ being at an endpoint. This contradicts $|\operatorname{supp}f|\le2p-1$. (The review pointed out that minimising over $[1,p-1]$ would only give $p+1$; excluding $|E|=1$ first, as above, is the missing line.) Sharpness: for parallel lines $L_1,L_2$, a line of their class sums to $p\cdot(\pm1)=0$ or to $0$, and any other line meets each of $L_1,L_2$ once, giving $1-1=0$. $\square$

### 4.6 Lemma N

When $k=1$, primitive lines are the affine lines of $\mathbb F_p^2$, and the conclusion and sharpness follow directly from Lemma R. In the following incidence argument assume $k\ge2$.

Let $\bar c\ne0$ have zero sum on every primitive line and suppose $|\operatorname{supp}\bar c|\le2p-1$. Fix $g_0$ in the support. Each of the $p^{k-1}(p+1)$ primitive lines through $g_0$ contains another support point. A point $h\ne g_0$ with $h-g_0=p^vh'$, $h'$ primitive, $0\le v\le k-1$, lies on exactly $p^v$ primitive lines through $g_0$: the cyclic subgroups of order $p^k$ containing $p^vh'$ are the $\langle h''\rangle$ with $h''$ a lift to $\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Z/p^k)$ of the class of $h'$ in $\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Z/p^{k-v})$, and there are $p^{k-1}(p+1)/(p^{k-v-1}(p+1))=p^v$ of them. Let $n_1$ be the number of support points $h\ne g_0$ with $v=k-1$, i.e. $h\in g_0+T_1$, and $n_2$ the number with $v\le k-2$. Counting incidences,
$$n_1p^{k-1}+n_2p^{k-2}\ \ge\ p^{k-1}(p+1),\qquad n_1+n_2\le2p-2,$$
whence $n_1(p-1)\ge p^2-p+2$ and $n_1\ge p+1$ (for $p=2$, $n_1\ge4$). So every $T_1$-coset that meets the support contains at least $p+2$ support points; two such cosets would give $2p+4>2p-1$ points, so the support lies in a single coset $g_0+T_1$. A cyclic subgroup $H$ of order $p^k$ meets $T_1$ in its order-$p$ subgroup $p^{k-1}H$, every order-$p$ subgroup of $T_1\cong\mathbb F_p^2$ arises this way, and a coset of $H$ meets $g_0+T_1$ either trivially or in a coset of $p^{k-1}H$, i.e. in an affine line of the plane $g_0+T_1$; conversely every affine line of that plane is such an intersection. Since the support lies in the plane, the primitive-line sums are exactly the affine-line sums of the restriction of $\bar c$ to $g_0+T_1\cong\mathbb F_p^2$, and Lemma R gives $|\operatorname{supp}\bar c|\ge2p$, a contradiction. Sharpness: two parallel lines in $g_0+T_1$ have zero sum on every affine line of the plane, hence on every primitive line. $\square$

### 4.7 Theorem B

Let $C=\sum_{g\in S}c_gW_g$ with $|S|=\ell\le2p-1$ and all $c_g\ne0$. Let $K=\mathbb Q(\omega_{2d})(c_g:g\in S)$ (for odd $p$, $\mathbb Q(\omega_{2d})=\mathbb Q(\omega_d)$), a finitely generated field, and let $\mathcal O$ be a valuation ring with fraction field $K$ dominating the local subring $\mathbb Z[\omega_d]_{(1-\omega_d)}$ ([St, Tag 00IA]). This makes $1-\omega_d$ lie in its maximal ideal for every prime $p$. For $p=2$, Section 4.3 then puts $1-\omega_{2d}$ in that ideal as well; for odd $p$, only $d$-th root phases are used. Scale by a coefficient of minimal valuation. The residue vector $\bar c$ is nonzero with support in $S$, of size at most $2p-1$, so by Lemma N some primitive line carries a nonzero sum: there is a direction $\lambda$ with $m_\lambda\ge1$. Theorem B′ gives $\dim\ker C\le\Omega_k(m_\lambda)\le\Omega_k(\ell)$, and the closed forms of Section 2 give the explicit bounds. For the sharpness family, $C_\ell=f(Z)$ with $f(z)=\prod_{i=0}^{\ell-2}(z^{p^{k-1}}-\omega_p^i)$; by the $q$-binomial theorem ($\ell-1\le p-1$) $f$ has exactly $\ell$ terms, and it vanishes at the $(\ell-1)p^{k-1}$ elements of $\mu_d$ whose $p^{k-1}$-th power is one of $1,\omega_p,\dots,\omega_p^{\ell-2}$; so $s_W(C_\ell)=\ell$ and $\dim\ker C_\ell=(\ell-1)p^{k-1}=\Omega_k(\ell)$. $\square$

### 4.8 Why $2p-1$, and what the bound explains

At $\ell=2p$ the combination $C=\sum_{g\in L_1}W_g-\sum_{g\in L_2}W_g$, for two parallel lines in a $T_1$-coset, has integer coefficients whose residue vector lies in the obstruction code of Lemma N: every $m_\lambda$ vanishes and Theorem B′ is silent. The first-order method therefore stops exactly at $2p-1$; whether the *conclusion* survives is Conjecture C.

For $\ell\le3$ the corollary of Section 3 says that the only orders of vanishing available to the reduced circulant are $0,p^j,2p^j$ (resp. $0,2^j$): the digit weight explains the shape of the corank set $\mathcal N_p$ of [0WE] Theorem 3, though Theorem B′ by itself only bounds the nullity by such an order and does not force the nullity to be one. For $p<\ell\le2p-1$ and $k\ge2$, the operator rank bound $p^{k-1}$ can be strictly stronger than the integral field-free bound $\operatorname{rank}C\ge\lceil d/\ell\rceil$ ([3W] Section 5.2): strictness holds precisely when $\lceil p^k/\ell\rceil<p^{k-1}$. For example, $(d,\ell)=(9,4)$ and $(25,6)$ give equality after rounding. Whether $\Omega_k(\ell)$ is attained at *exact* sparsity $\ell$ in this range is open: the family $C_\ell$ stops at $\ell=p$, and collinear supports do not attain it (for $d=27$, $\ell=4$ the bound is $18$ while the collinear maximum at exact sparsity $4$ is $12$, Section 5.3). $\Omega_k$ is the monotone envelope, and Theorem Z shows that it is attained at exact sparsity $w_p(\Omega_k(\ell))$.

## 5. Composite dimension

### 5.1 Binomials

*Proof of Theorem T2.* Up to a nonzero factor and a phase, $C=c_1W_g+c_2W_h=c_1W_g(I+cW)$ with $W=W_{h-g}$, $c\ne0$. If $u=h-g$ has order $r\ge2$ in $G$, then $W^r$ is a scalar $\theta I$ and $\operatorname{Tr}W^j=0$ for $r\nmid j$. The eigenvalues of $W$ are the $r$ roots of $\theta$, with multiplicities $m_i$ satisfying $\sum_im_i\zeta_r^{ij}=0$ for $0<j<r$, so all $m_i=d/r$. The nullity of $I+cW$ is the multiplicity of $-1/c$, i.e. $0$ or $d/r$, and $r$ ranges over the orders of nonzero elements of $(\mathbb Z/d)^2$, all of which divide $d$; the maximum $d/p_{\min}$ is attained by $I-Z^{d/p_{\min}}$. $\square$ (In polynomial form, $1+cx^s$ vanishes on a fibre of $x\mapsto x^s$ on $\mu_d$, of size $\gcd(s,d)$; this matches the two-label criterion of [0WE].)

### 5.2 Trinomials

*Proof of Theorem T3, assuming [0WE] Lemma 1 and Theorem 2.* After right multiplication by a Weyl adjoint, $D=cI+aU+bV$ with $abc\ne0$ and $U=W_u$, $V=W_v$ of distinct nonzero labels. With $UV=\zeta VU$, $q=\operatorname{ord}\zeta$, $H=\langle u,v\rangle$, $h=|\langle qu,qv\rangle|$, [0WE] Lemma 1 gives $|H|=q^2h$ and a common sector multiplicity $m=d/(qh)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$, and [0WE] Theorem 2 (4b) gives $\dim\ker D\le m\min(2,h)$. If $h\ge2$: $\dim\ker D\le2d/(qh)$, where $qh$ divides $d$; if $q=1$ then $qh=|H|\ge3$, and if $q\ge2$ then $qh\ge4$; so $qh\ge r_3(d)$ and $\dim\ker D\le2d/r_3$. If $h=1$, then $|H|=q^2$ forces $q\ge2$ and $\dim\ker D\le d/q$. If $q=2$, $q\mid d$ makes $d$ even and gives $\dim\ker D\le d/2$. If $q\ge3$, the divisor $q$ satisfies $q\ge r_3(d)$, so $\dim\ker D\le d/q\le d/r_3(d)\le2d/r_3(d)$. Attainment: (i) let $u$ have order $r_3$ and choose a phase so that $T=\xi'W_u$ satisfies $T^{r_3}=I$; its eigenvalues are the $r_3$-th roots of unity, each of multiplicity $d/r_3$; with $\xi=e^{2\pi i/r_3}$, $(T-I)(T-\xi I)=T^2-(1+\xi)T+\xi I$ has three nonzero Weyl coefficients (labels $0,u,2u$ distinct since $r_3\ge3$, and $1+\xi\ne0$) and nullity $2d/r_3$ — this is the construction of [0WE] Theorem 3 with $p^j$ replaced by $d/r_3$. (ii) If $d\equiv2\pmod 4$, $U=Z^{d/2}$ and $V=X^{d/2}$ are Hermitian involutions with $UV=\omega^{d^2/4}VU=(-1)^{d/2}VU=-VU$, so $(U+V)^2=2I$ and $\operatorname{Tr}(U+V)=0$: $U+V$ has eigenvalues $\pm\sqrt2$ with multiplicity $d/2$ each, and $\sqrt2\,I+U+V$ has $s_W=3$ and nullity $d/2$ (here $q=2$, $h=1$, $m=d/2$). If $4\mid d$ then $r_3\le4$ and $2d/r_3\ge d/2$ already. The "$\le3$" statement adds Theorem T2 (note $\tfrac d2[d\text{ even}]\le d/p_{\min}$). $\square$

For $d=10$ and $14$ the anticommuting branch wins: $\nu_3=5$, resp. $7$, whereas the collinear maximum at exact sparsity three is $Z(10,3)=Z(14,3)=4$ (exact, Section 5.3). Collinear supports are therefore not extremal at fixed sparsity, but they are extremal in the monotone sense: $\max_{s_W\le3}=\max(d/p_{\min},2d/r_3)$ is attained on the $Z$-axis. The [0WE] examples in $d=12$ and $36$ illustrate the sector mechanism and are not maxima: Theorem T3 gives $\nu_3(12)=8$ and $\nu_3(36)=24$. Numerically (sector formula and SVD, $d\in\{6,8,9,10,12,14,15\}$) the exact-sparsity-three maxima are $4,4,6,5,8,7,10$, all equal to the formula, with the $d=10,14$ witnesses anticommuting.

### 5.3 What fails for composite $d$, what survives, and the exact tables

If $d$ has two distinct prime factors then $\Phi_d(1)=1$, $1-\omega_d$ is a unit and no prime of $\mathbb Z[\omega_d]$ has $\omega_d\equiv1$: the valuation argument has no starting point ([3W] Section 5.4). No additive law in the largest prime factor $p_{\max}$, or largest prime-power factor $P$, of $d$ can hold: at $\ell=2$ any bound of the form $(\ell-1)d/p_{\max}$ or $(\ell-1)d/P$ is $<d/p_{\min}$, the true maximum of Theorem T2, whenever $d$ is not a prime power ($d=10$: bound $2$, truth $5$; $d=12$: bound $4$ or $3$, truth $6$; at $\ell=3$, $d=12$: truth $8=2d/3$). The extremal parameters are the smallest divisors $p_{\min}$ and $r_3$, in line with Lam–Leung [LL00]: a vanishing sum of $m$ $d$-th roots of unity exists only if $m$ is a nonnegative combination of the prime factors of $d$. What survives for all $d$ is the field-free bound $\operatorname{rank}C\cdot s_W(C)\ge d$ and, for collinear supports, Meshulam's inequality [Me06] on $\mathbb Z/d$; the latter is not attained in general: on $\mathbb Z/10$ it allows $6$ zeros at $m=3$ and $7$ at $m=4$, while the exact values are $4$ and $6$.

*Exact tables.* $Z(d,m)$ was computed by enumerating, for each support $S$ with $|S|=m$ up to the affine group of $\mathbb Z/d$ (units and translations), the flats cut out by at most $m-1$ character rows over $\mathbb Q(\omega_d)$, with arithmetic in $\mathbb Q(\omega_d)$ realised through the regular representation and kernels over $\mathbb Q$; a flat carries a full-support vector if and only if no coordinate vanishes identically on it, and its closure is then a zero set. (Correctness: a full-support $f$ with zero set $T$ spans a subspace of dimension $\le m-1$ of row conditions, so some $R\subseteq T$ with $|R|\le m-1$ cuts the same flat, and every full-support vector of that flat vanishes on the closure.) Only the entries certified in this way are called exact; the remaining entries come from the earlier finite-field model ($\mathbb F_q$ with two independent primes $q\equiv1\bmod d$, which can only under-estimate ranks and is therefore not a certificate in either direction at fixed $m$, although two agreeing primes make an error very unlikely). The working report had labelled all of them exact; that label is withdrawn here.

### 6.2 Torsion cosets

Let $2j\le k$ and let the support lie in a coset of $T_j$. After a translation the support lies in $T_j=p^{k-j}G$, where $\langle g,h\rangle\in p^{2(k-j)}\mathbb Z=0$ in $\mathbb Z/d$, so $g\mapsto W_g$ is an honest representation of $T_j\cong(\mathbb Z/p^j)^2$; by trace orthogonality each character $\chi$ occurs with multiplicity $p^{k-2j}$, and $C=\sum_\chi\hat c(\chi)P_\chi$ with $\hat c(\chi)=\sum_gc_g\chi(g)$. Hence $\dim\ker C=p^{k-2j}\cdot\#\{\chi:\hat c(\chi)=0\}$, a commutative uncertainty problem on $(\mathbb Z/p^j)^2$.

*$p$-torsion ($j=1$, $k\ge2$).* Meshulam's inequality on $\mathbb F_p^2$ with the consecutive divisors $1,p,p^2$ gives, for $\ell\le p$, at most $p(\ell-1)$ zero characters, and for $p\le\ell\le p^2$ at most $p^2-p-1+\lfloor\ell/p\rfloor$; multiplied by $p^{k-2}$ these are exactly $(\ell-1)p^{k-1}$ and $(p-1)p^{k-1}+(\lfloor\ell/p\rfloor-1)p^{k-2}$, i.e. $\Omega_k(\ell)$ (with $\Omega_k(p^2)=p^k-p^{k-2}$ at $\ell=p^2$). So Conjecture C holds for every support in a coset of the $p$-torsion, for every $k\ge2$. (Re-derived by the reviewer; the comparison script reports no case with $j=1$ in which the Meshulam bound exceeds $\Omega_k(\ell)$, over all $p\le7$, $p^k\le2401$ and $\ell\le p^2$, i.e. $316$ cases.)

*$4$-torsion in $d=16$ and $32$ ($p=2$, $j=2$).* Here Meshulam's bound on $(\mathbb Z/4)^2$ is slack (it allows $10>8$ zeros at $\ell=3$ and $13>12$ at $\ell=6,7$), so exact values are needed. The exact maxima of zero characters on $(\mathbb Z/4)^2$ for full-support functions with exactly $m$ points are $0,8,8,12,8,12,10$ for $m=1,\dots,7$ (author, exhaustive over the orbits of the affine group of order $1536$; reviewer, independent method, $m\le6$). Fourier duality closes the rest: $|\operatorname{supp}f|\in\{2,3\}$ forces $|\operatorname{supp}\hat f|\ge8$, hence (applying this to $\hat f$) $|\operatorname{supp}\hat f|\in\{2,3\}$ forces $|\operatorname{supp}f|\ge8$, and $|\operatorname{supp}\hat f|=1$ forces $|\operatorname{supp}f|=16$. Therefore $|\operatorname{supp}f|\le7$ gives $|\operatorname{supp}\hat f|\ge4$, at most $12$ zeros, and $8\le|\operatorname{supp}f|\le15$ gives at most $14$ zeros. Since $\Omega_4(m)=12$ for $4\le m\le7$ and $14$ for $8\le m\le15$, Conjecture C holds for every support in a coset of $T_2$ in $d=16$ (multiplicity $1$), and, with multiplicity $2^{5-4}=2$, in $d=32$: the bounds $16,24,28,30$ for $m\in\{2,3\},[4,7],[8,15],\{16\}$ equal $\Omega_5$ there.

### 6.3 Numerical searches and a false alarm

Alternating projections between the span of the support Weyls and the variety of rank-$\le d-\kappa$ matrices were run in the open ranges: $d=16$, $\ell\in\{5,6,7\}$, target nullity $13$ ($300$ random and $100$ "blocked" supports containing a $T_1$-coset, $6$ restarts, $300$ iterations each); $d=27$, $\ell\in\{6,7,8\}$, target $22$; $d=25$, $\ell\in\{10,12,14\}$ (target $22$) and $16$ (target $23$). No full-support witness was found (best relative residuals $\ge0.46$, $0.35$, $0.18$). Control searches against the proved statements ($d=9$, $\ell=4,5$; $d=8$, $\ell=3$; $d=16$, $\ell=3$; $d=25$, $\ell=8,9$; $d=27$, $\ell=4,5$) likewise found nothing, as Theorem B requires. These are finite searches, not evidence of the same kind as Sections 6.1–6.2.

Three instances in $d=16$ had been flagged numerically as having nullity $1$ where Theorem B′ predicts $0$ (a single coefficient survives modulo $2$). Exact computation of the rank of the $128\times128$ integer matrix of the regular representation gives $128$ in all three cases, so $\det C\ne0$; the matrices are merely ill-conditioned ($\sigma_{\min}/\sigma_{\max}$ between $10^{-16}$ and $10^{-12}$, the sector determinant $c^{16}-(a^{16}s+b^{16}t)$ nearly cancelling). The reviewer confirmed the first instance by hand: its relative labels have commutator of order $16$, one sector, and determinant $1-8^{16}(s+t)$ with $s,t$ roots of unity, which cannot vanish. No violation of Theorem B′ exists.

## 8. Open questions

1. **Conjecture C at $d=16$, $\ell\in\{6,7\}$.** A proof would need either a second-order (Newton-polygon) refinement of Theorem B′ for residue vectors in the obstruction code, or an exact exhaustive computation over the $AGL(2,\mathbb Z/16)$-orbits of $6$- and $7$-point supports, which is within reach of the flats method.
2. **Exact sparsity.** Is $\Omega_k(\ell)$ attained at exact sparsity $\ell$ for $p<\ell\le2p-1$? The collinear answer is no ($d=27$, $\ell=4$: $12<18$); the non-collinear answer is unknown, and by [0WE] Theorem 3 the coranks at $\ell=3$ are constrained to $p^j,2p^j$.
3. **Composite $d$, $\ell\ge4$.** Conjecture U and a closed form for $Z(d,m)$; the product constructions of Section 5.3 and the values $3d/r_4$ suggest that the smallest divisors continue to govern, but $Z(10,5)=8=4d/5$ and $Z(15,5)=12=4d/5$ are already of the form $(m-1)d/r$ with $r=5$ the *largest* divisor below $m+1$.
4. **The obstruction code.** Determine the minimum-weight words and the weight distribution of $N(p,k)$ beyond the two-line words; a characterisation of low-weight words would tell exactly where Theorem B′ can and cannot be applied.
5. **Other groups.** The proof uses only that $1-\omega$ is a prime with residue characteristic $p$ and that the Clifford family covers all primitive directions; the same argument should apply to Weyl systems over $(\mathbb Z/p^k)^{2n}$ and, with Meshulam's inequality replacing Theorem A, to groups of order $pq$ in the spirit of [Lo25].
