# The inverse self-commutator cost at inertia $(m,n)$: ordered-layering bounds, interlacing chains, and the Horn tiles that certify them

**Lluis Eriksson** (Independent researcher)

## Abstract

For a traceless Hermitian $F\in M_d(\mathbb C)$ let $\kappa_d(F)=\tfrac12\min\{\|C\|_{HS}^2:CC^*-C^*C=2F\}$. On the inertia-$(m,n)$ stratum ($m$ positive and $n$ negative eigenvalues, $z$ zeros) we prove three results valid for all $m,n,z$: a dimension-free lower bound $\kappa_d\ge\operatorname{cost}(L)$ for every ordered full-capacity layering $L$, certified by Lidskii–Wielandt and Weyl inequalities alone; an exact additive formula on the aligned-spike cone, where an optimizer is a direct sum of weighted shifts; and the closed form $\kappa_d=\operatorname{cost}(L_k)$ on explicit full-dimensional regions $R_k$, $1\le k\le m-n+1$, obtained from one rank-one interlacing step. Open question 1 of the inertia-$(m,2)$ paper is refuted by an exact weak-duality certificate: at inertia $(5,4)$ a Horn tile with $n=4$ boxes is necessary, and one valid form cannot be certified using Pieri-shaped tiles alone. The $d=8$ rank-onset example is explained by a late negative. Conjectures for general $(m,n)$ are stated with their numerical evidence.

## 1. Introduction

The quantity $\kappa_d(F)$ is the cheapest realisation, in Hilbert–Schmidt norm, of a traceless Hermitian $F$ as a self-commutator $\tfrac12(CC^*-C^*C)$. Its theory has been developed in a line of records of the AI Research Record archive, cited by descriptive keys (identifiers in the reference list); everything used from them is restated here. [FourLevel] reduced $\kappa_d$ to a finite linear program over Horn inequalities (the *Horn program* (2.1)); [OneSpike] proved the one-spike formula $\kappa_d=\sum_jj\,a_j$ when one sign has multiplicity one; [RankOnset] proved that the minimal optimal rank $r_*$ equals the inertia bound $r_0=\max(n_+,n_-)$ for $d\le7$ and exhibited the first spectrum, $d=8$ of inertia $(4,4)$, with $r_*>r_0$. The published inertia-$(m,2)$ paper [Mtwo] gave a dimension-free closed formula for two negative eigenvalues (Lidskii–Wielandt, Weyl and one Pieri-type Horn triple below, $2\times2$-block weighted shifts above) and asked in its Open question 1, quoted verbatim in Section 6, what survives at inertia $(m,n)$. The unpublished inertia-$(m,3)$ manuscript [Mthree] answered for three negatives and $m\le9$ by computer-assisted "tiling" certificates and $3\times3$-block shifts, proved a dimension-free lower bound for its ordered family A, and found a padding phase.

**Contribution.** We treat general inertia $(m,n)$. Theorem 1 proves, for every $m,n\ge1$ and $z\ge0$, that the cost of every *ordered full-capacity layering* — positives in decreasing order, every non-final layer holding exactly as many positives as there are negatives before it, any order of the negatives — is a lower bound for $\kappa_d$, certified by Lidskii–Wielandt and Weyl inequalities alone. It contains Theorem 2 of [Mthree] and the $F_k$ bounds of [Mtwo] and extends them to schedules with inversions of the negatives; a rearrangement lemma shows that the best ordered bound is attained by a nondecreasing schedule, and an exact counterexample shows that full capacity cannot be relaxed. Theorem 2A characterises the layerings whose block-shift chain can be solved *diagonally*: exactly those carrying a balanced path system; the cost is then additive, $\kappa_d=\sum_i\sum_jj\,a^{(i)}_j$, and an optimizer is a direct sum of one-spike weighted shifts, while alignment is a genuine restriction ($23<24$ in an explicit example). Theorem 2B gives, for every $n\ge2$ and $1\le k\le m-n+1$, a full-dimensional region $R_k$ on which $\kappa_d$ equals the explicit form $\operatorname{cost}(L_k)$, by a chain that is diagonal except for one rank-one step whose feasibility is the converse of Cauchy interlacing (Golub [Go73], Chu [Chu98]); for $n=2$ the regions are the $F_k$ chambers of [Mtwo]. Theorem 3 refutes the first half of Open question 1 of [Mtwo]: at inertia $(5,4)$ a valid form is certified by no combination of Horn inequalities with at most $n-1=3$ boxes (an exact rational weak-duality certificate) but by a tiling with a $4$-box Pieri tile. The second half (blocks of size $n$ attain) is supported numerically on every stratum tested and stated as a conjecture. Section 7 explains the $d=8$ example of [RankOnset]: its two optimal forms are costs of feasible block shifts in which the largest negative enters a late layer, which forces rank $5$ without padding. Section 8 collects the numerical structure of discovered form candidates on $14$ strata (up to $d=12$) and states a conjecture; Section 9 lists open questions.

**Status, stated honestly.** Theorems 1, 2A, 2B and 3 are proved (Theorem 3 with an exact rational computation); Section 8 is numerical and Section 8.3 conjectural, and every numerical statement is labelled as such. The author-supplied history reports a separate Fable-agent adversarial review of the proofs and computational ingredients (Section 10); the corrections attributed to that development review — the range of $k$ in Theorem 2B, the Weyl tiles of the box-$4$ certificate, a counterexample to a conjectured "Pieri shapes suffice" statement — are incorporated. Literature searches (Section 10) found no prior closed formula on an inertia stratum and no prior use of the interlacing converse inside a block-shift chain; the nearest works [JOS13,AS15] bound different objectives over factorisations $A=[B,C]$. A null search does not certify priority.

## 2. Setting

Throughout $\|X\|^2=\operatorname{Tr}X^*X$ and $F\in M_d(\mathbb C)$ is Hermitian and traceless with ordered spectrum $\lambda_1\ge\dots\ge\lambda_d$. On the inertia-$(m,n)$ stratum
$$\lambda=(a_1\ge\dots\ge a_m>0,\ 0^{z},\ -b_n,\dots,-b_1),\qquad b_1\ge\dots\ge b_n>0,\qquad \sum_ja_j=\sum_ib_i=:P,\qquad d=m+n+z .$$
The spectrum index of $a_j$ is $j$, that of $-b_i$ is $d+1-i$, and the zeros occupy $m+1,\dots,m+z$. We write $A_u:=\sum_{j\ge u}a_j$ ($A_{m+1}=0$) and $B':=b_2+\dots+b_n$. By $F\mapsto-F$ (which replaces $C$ by $C^*$) every statement transfers to inertia $(n,m)$.

Given $C$ with $CC^*-C^*C=2F$, put $R=CC^*/2$, $S=C^*C/2$: they are positive semidefinite with the same spectrum $s_1\ge\dots\ge s_d\ge0$, $R-S=F$, $\tfrac12\|C\|^2=\sum_ts_t$, and conversely any such pair comes from $C=U\sqrt{2\Sigma}V^*$. Hence ([FourLevel] Theorem 2.2, [RankOnset] Proposition 2.2, [Mtwo] (2.1))
$$\kappa_d(F)=\min\Big\{\sum_{t<d}s_t:\ s_1\ge\dots\ge s_{d-1}\ge s_d=0,\ (s,-s^{\mathrm{rev}},\lambda)\ \text{Horn-feasible}\Big\},\tag{2.1}$$
where Horn-feasible means that $\alpha=(s_1,\dots,s_{d-1},0)$, $\beta=(0,-s_{d-1},\dots,-s_1)$ and $\gamma=\lambda$ satisfy every Horn inequality $\sum_{k\in K}\gamma_k\le\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_i+\sum_{j\in J}\beta_j$, $(I,J,K)\in T^d_r$ [Ho62,Kl98,KT99,Fu00]. The normalisation $s_d=0$ is legitimate because subtracting $s_d\mathbf 1$ from $R$ and $S$ preserves positivity, equal spectra and $R-S=F$, and lowers the cost ([Mtwo], remark after (2.1)). In $s$-notation a triple reads
$$\sum_{i\in I,\,i<d}s_i-\sum_{j\in J,\,j>1}s_{d+1-j}\ \ge\ \sum_{k\in K}\lambda_k.\tag{2.2}$$
With $\lambda(I)=(i_r-r,\dots,i_1-1)$ the partition attached to $I=(i_1<\dots<i_r)$, (2.2) holds for all Hermitian triples whenever the Littlewood–Richardson coefficient $c^{\lambda(K)}_{\lambda(I)\lambda(J)}$ is nonzero (Klyachko [Kl98]); this necessity direction is all that the lower bounds use. Sufficiency (Klyachko, Knutson–Tao) enters only in identifying the optimal common spectra with the optimal face of (2.1) (Section 7) and inside the numerical chain LP of Section 8. We use that $\lambda\mapsto\kappa_d(\lambda)$ is convex and piecewise linear (an LP value with $\lambda$ in the right-hand sides) and that $\kappa_{d+1}(F\oplus0)\le\kappa_d(F)$.

**Forms.** A *form* is $g(\lambda)=\sum_j\alpha_ja_j+\sum_{i<n}\beta_ib_i$ with integer coefficients, written $(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_m;\beta_1,\dots,\beta_{n-1})$; the coefficient of $b_n$ is normalised to $0$ using $\sum a=\sum b$. It is *valid* if $g\le\kappa_d$ on the stratum and *exposed* if it is the unique maximiser of the set of valid forms on a nonempty open set; its *chamber* is the closed set where it is a maximiser. $S(m,n,z)$ denotes the finite set of numerically discovered, numerically validated exposed-form candidates in Section 8. Its completeness is not asserted. The putative complete set in Conjecture 8.3 is denoted $\mathcal S(m,n,z)$.

**Layerings and costs.** A *layering* $L$ is an ordered partition of the multiset $\{a_j\}\cup\{-b_i\}$, possibly together with some zeros, into layers $\Lambda_0,\Lambda_1,\dots,\Lambda_T$. Write $t(x)$ for the index of the layer of $x$, $t(b_i):=t(-b_i)$, $\mathrm{tail}_u:=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_u\cup\dots\cup\Lambda_T}x$, and $\operatorname{cost}(L):=\sum_{u=1}^T\mathrm{tail}_u$.

**Lemma 2.1.** $\operatorname{cost}(L)=\sum_xt(x)\,x=\sum_j\big(t(a_j)-t(b_n)\big)a_j+\sum_{i<n}\big(t(b_n)-t(b_i)\big)b_i$.

*Proof.* $\sum_{u\ge1}\mathrm{tail}_u=\sum_xx\cdot\#\{u\ge1:u\le t(x)\}=\sum_xt(x)x$; adding $t(b_n)(\sum_ib_i-\sum_ja_j)=0$ gives the canonical coordinates. $\square$

Conversely a form with $\alpha_j=t(a_j)-t(b_n)$, $\beta_i=t(b_n)-t(b_i)$ for nonnegative integers $t$ determines its *level layering* $L(g)$ (level sets of $t$, ordered, common shift fixed by $\min t=0$). The *b-schedule* of a layering is $u=(u_1,\dots,u_n)$, $u_i:=t(b_i)$, and the *capacity* of layer $u\ge1$ is $c_u:=\#\{i:u_i<u\}$.

**Block shifts** ([Mtwo] Section 5.1, valid for any layer sizes). Let $D_t:=\operatorname{diag}\Lambda_t$ and $C=\sum_{t=1}^TM_t$ with $M_t:\Lambda_{t-1}\to\Lambda_t$. With $2R_t:=M_tM_t^*$ (on $\Lambda_t$) and $2S_t:=M_t^*M_t$ (on $\Lambda_{t-1}$), $CC^*-C^*C=2F$ on the layered entries iff
$$S_1=-D_0,\qquad R_t-S_{t+1}=D_t\ (1\le t<T),\qquad R_T=D_T,\qquad R_t,\ S_t\ \text{have the same nonzero spectrum};\tag{2.3}$$
conversely positive semidefinite $R_t,S_t$ with equal nonzero spectra are realised by $M_t=V\sqrt{2\Sigma}W^*$. Then $\tfrac12\|C\|^2=\sum_t\operatorname{Tr}S_t=\operatorname{cost}(L)$ and $\operatorname{rank}C=\sum_t\operatorname{rank}S_t\le\sum_t\min(|\Lambda_{t-1}|,|\Lambda_t|)$; zeros of $F$ left outside the layering lie in $\ker C\cap\ker C^*$. A layering is *feasible* at $\lambda$ if (2.3) is solvable; then $\kappa_d\le\operatorname{cost}(L)$.

## 3. Statements

**Definition 3.1 (ordered full-capacity layering $L(u)$).** Let $u\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n$ with $\min u=0$ and capacities $c_u=\#\{i:u_i<u\}$. Place $-b_i$ in layer $u_i$. Place $a_1,a_2,\dots$ in this order into layers $1,2,\dots$, layer $u$ receiving exactly $c_u$ positives while positives remain; let $T$ be the layer receiving the last positive (it receives $q$ positives, $1\le q\le c_T$). Require $u_i\le T$ for all $i$. Write $t_u$ for the index of the first positive in layers $\ge u$ ($t_1=1$, $t_{u+1}=t_u+c_u$ for $u<T$) and $B_u:=\{d+1-i:u_i<u\}$ for the spectrum indices of the negatives before layer $u$ ($|B_u|=c_u$).

Family A of [Mthree] is the case of nondecreasing $u$; the layerings $\mathcal L(F_k)$ of [Mtwo] are $n=2$, $u=(0,k)$; the class also contains every schedule with inversions of the negatives, e.g. $u=(0,2,1)$.

**Theorem 1 (all $m,n\ge1$, $z\ge0$, every $u$ as in 3.1).** $\kappa_d(F)\ge\operatorname{cost}(L(u))$ on the whole inertia-$(m,n)$ stratum, in every dimension $d\ge m+n$. The certificate consists of Lidskii–Wielandt and Weyl triples with Littlewood–Richardson coefficient $1$; each $s_t$ with $t\le m$ occurs exactly once with coefficient $+1$ and the only negative terms are $-s_\beta$ with $d-n+1\le\beta\le d-1$.

**Lemma 3.2 (rearrangement).** For a fixed multiset of schedule values, $\operatorname{cost}(L(u))$ is maximised by the nondecreasing assignment $u_1\le\dots\le u_n$. Hence $\max_u\operatorname{cost}(L(u))$ is attained by a family-A layering.

**Proposition 3.3 (full capacity is necessary).** For $m=n=2$, $a=b=(1,1)$, the layering $\{-b_1,-b_2\}\{a_1\}\{a_2\}$ (capacity $c_1=2$, one positive placed) has cost $3>\kappa_4=2$.

**Theorem 2A (aligned-spike cone; all $m,n,z$).** Let $L=L(u)$ be ordered and full-capacity. The chain (2.3) has a solution with all $R_t,S_t$ diagonal iff $L$ carries a balanced path system (Definition 5.1); then the spectrum splits into $n$ one-spike blocks $F_i=(a^{(i)}_1\ge a^{(i)}_2\ge\dots;\,-b_i)$ with $\sum_ja^{(i)}_j=b_i$, and
$$\kappa_d(F)=\operatorname{cost}(L)=\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_jj\,a^{(i)}_j=\sum_i\kappa(F_i).$$

**Theorem 2B (interlacing regions; all $m\ge n\ge2$, $z\ge0$, $1\le k\le m-n+1$).** With $L_k$, $\tau(k)$ and $R_k$ as in Definition 5.5, $\kappa_d(F)=\operatorname{cost}(L_k)$ for every $\lambda\in R_k$, where
$$\operatorname{cost}(L_k)=k\,b_1+\sum_{j\le k}(j-k)\,a_j+\sum_{j>k}\Big\lceil\frac{j-k}{n}\Big\rceil a_j .$$
$R_k$ has nonempty interior whenever $a_1>\dots>a_m$; for $k\ge m-n+2$ the set $R_k$ is empty. For $n=2$, $R_k$ is the $F_k$ chamber $E_{k+2}\le b_2\le E_{k+1}$ of [Mtwo] Proposition 5.4(1). An optimizer of rank $m$ is constructed explicitly.

**Theorem 3 (Open question 1 of [Mtwo], first half).** For a Horn triple put $\mathrm{box}(I,J,K):=\min(|\lambda(I)|,|\lambda(J)|)$; every triple "adding $n-1$ boxes to a rectangle" has box $\le n-1$. On the inertia-$(5,4)$ stratum ($z=0$) the valid form $g=(3,1,1,2,2;-2,-1,0)$ admits no certificate — no nonnegative combination of Horn inequalities of box $\le3$, ordering and nonnegativity constraints — while it is certified by a tiling containing one box-$4$ tile. Hence tiles with $n$ boxes are necessary at $n=4$.

## 4. Lower bound: proof of Theorem 1

Fix $u$, write $L=L(u)$ with $T$, $t_u$, $B_u$ as in Definition 3.1, and note $B_u\subseteq\{d-n+1,\dots,d\}=\{m+z+1,\dots,d\}$.

*Tile $u$ for $1\le u<T$.* Take the Lidskii–Wielandt triple $I=K=\{t_u,\dots,d\}\setminus B_u$, $J=\{1,\dots,r\}$, $r=|K|=d-t_u+1-c_u$. Then $\lambda(J)=\emptyset$ and $\lambda(I)=\lambda(K)$, so $c^{\lambda(K)}_{\lambda(I)\lambda(J)}=1$, and $\sum I+\sum J=\sum K+r(r+1)/2$ holds because $I=K$; (2.2) is the Lidskii–Wielandt inequality $\sum_{k\in K}\lambda_k(R-S)\le\sum_{k\in K}\lambda_k(R)+\sum_{j\le r}\lambda_j(-S)$ [Li50,Wi55], valid in every dimension. Its right side is
$$\sum_{k\in K}\lambda_k=\sum_{j\ge t_u}a_j-\sum_{i:\,u_i\ge u}b_i=\mathrm{tail}_u ,$$
because the positives in layers $\ge u$ are exactly $a_{t_u},\dots,a_m$ (placed in order, all placed), zeros contribute nothing, and the negatives in layers $\ge u$ are those with index outside $B_u$. Its left side is $\sum_{i\in[t_u,d-1]\setminus B_u}s_i-\sum_{i=d+1-r}^{d-1}s_i$ with $d+1-r=t_u+c_u$; since $t_u+c_u-1=t_{u+1}-1\le m-1$ while $\min B_u\ge m+z+1$, the block $[t_u,t_u+c_u-1]$ misses $B_u$ and $B_u\setminus\{d\}\subseteq[t_u+c_u,d-1]$, so the left side equals
$$\sum_{i=t_u}^{t_u+c_u-1}s_i\;-\sum_{\beta\in B_u,\ \beta<d}s_\beta .$$

*Tiles for the last layer $T$.* Use the Weyl triples $I=K=\{j\}$, $J=\{1\}$ ($\lambda(J)=\emptyset$, $\lambda(I)=\lambda(K)=(j-1)$, coefficient $1$, sum condition $j+1=j+1$), i.e. $s_j\ge a_j$, for $j=t_T,\dots,m$. Their sum is $\sum_{j\ge t_T}s_j\ge\sum_{j\ge t_T}a_j=\mathrm{tail}_T+\sum_{i:\,u_i=T}b_i\ge\mathrm{tail}_T$.

*Summation.* The positive blocks $[t_u,t_{u+1}-1]$ ($u<T$) and $[t_T,m]$ partition $\{1,\dots,m\}$, and the negative terms involve only $s_\beta$ with $d-n+1\le\beta\le d-1$. Adding all tiles,
$$\sum_{t\le m}s_t-\sum_{u<T}\ \sum_{\beta\in B_u\setminus\{d\}}s_\beta\ \ \ge\ \ \sum_{u=1}^T\mathrm{tail}_u=\operatorname{cost}(L),$$
and $\sum_{t<d}s_t$ exceeds the left side by $\sum_{m<t<d}s_t+\sum_{u<T}\sum_{\beta\in B_u\setminus\{d\}}s_\beta\ge0$. Hence every $s$ feasible for (2.1) has $\sum_{t<d}s_t\ge\operatorname{cost}(L)$, i.e. $\kappa_d\ge\operatorname{cost}(L)$. Only the index bookkeeping depends on $z$. $\square$

*Example* ($n=3$, $m=4$, $z=0$, $d=7$, the inverted schedule $u=(0,2,1)$): $L=\{-b_1\}\{-b_3,a_1\}\{-b_2,a_2,a_3\}\{a_4\}$, $c_1=1$, $c_2=2$. Tile $u=1$: $I=K=\{1,\dots,6\}$, $J=\{1,\dots,6\}$, reading $s_1\ge a_1+a_2+a_3+a_4-b_2-b_3=\mathrm{tail}_1$. Tile $u=2$: $B_2=\{5,7\}$, $I=K=\{2,3,4,6\}$, $J=\{1,2,3,4\}$, reading $s_2+s_3-s_5\ge a_2+a_3+a_4-b_2=\mathrm{tail}_2$. Last layer: $s_4\ge a_4$. Sum: $s_1+s_2+s_3+s_4-s_5\ge\operatorname{cost}(L)=a_2+a_3+2a_4+b_1-b_2$, the form $(0,1,1,2;1,-1)$.

**Remark 4.1 (what the proof uses).** Only (i) positives in decreasing order, (ii) every non-final layer holding *exactly* $c_u$ positives, (iii) $B_u\subseteq\{d-n+1,\dots,d\}$. The order of the negatives never enters ($B_u$ is used as a set), which is why arbitrary b-schedules are covered. Theorem 2 of [Mthree] is the case $n=3$, $u$ nondecreasing; its alternative last-layer tile is unnecessary. The certificate was verified exactly by the reviewer's own code (own Fulton recursion for $T^d_r$, own LR counter) for every schedule of the strata $(3,3,0)$, $(3,3,1)$, $(4,3,0)$, $(4,4,0)$, $(5,4,0)$, $(5,3,1)$ ($28,28,43,157,251,61$ schedules), including the coefficient pattern stated in Theorem 1. Numerically, $\max_u\operatorname{cost}(L(u))-\kappa_d\le2\cdot10^{-15}$ at every one of $400$ random points in each of $20$ strata up to $d=12$ (Section 8; one $2\cdot10^{-7}$ outlier at $(8,4,0)$ was a solver-tolerance artefact, re-solved to $-9\cdot10^{-16}$).

*Proof of Lemma 3.2.* The capacities $c_u$, hence the placement of the positives and $\sum_jt(a_j)a_j$, depend only on the multiset $\{u_i\}$. The negative part of the cost is $-\sum_iu_ib_i$ with $b_1\ge\dots\ge b_n$, maximised by the rearrangement inequality when $u_i$ is nondecreasing in $i$. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 3.3.* $\operatorname{cost}=a_1+2a_2=3$, while the four-level formula $\kappa_4=\max(\lambda_1-\lambda_3,\lambda_2-\lambda_4,\lambda_1-2\lambda_2-\lambda_3,\lambda_2+2\lambda_3-\lambda_4)$ ([FourLevel] Theorem 3.1) gives $\max(2,2,0,0)=2$ at $(1,1,-1,-1)$. $\square$

The failing step is visible in the proof of Theorem 1: if layer $u$ holds $q<c_u$ positives, the next block starts at $t_u+q<t_u+c_u$, the positive blocks overlap and some $s_t$ receives coefficient $2$ (here $s_2$: the tile $u=1$ reads $s_1+s_2\ge a_1+a_2$ and the Weyl tile $s_2\ge a_2$), so the summed left side is no longer bounded by $\sum_{t<d}s_t$. Full capacity is exactly the disjointness condition $t_{u+1}=t_u+c_u$. Numerically, the maximum over under-filled ordered layerings exceeds $\kappa_d$ at $50$–$78\%$ of the random points in every stratum tested. Under-filled layerings *can* be valid when the schedule has inversions (families B and C of [Mthree], e.g. $\{b_1\}\{b_3,a_1\}\{b_2,a_2\}\{a_3\}$), but their certificates need Pieri tiles.

**Remark 4.2 (mirror and late negatives).** Applying Theorem 1 to $-F$ gives the mirror class (negatives in decreasing order at full capacity with respect to the positives before them), the relevant class when $m<n$: in the sampled $(4,5,0)$ catalogue none of the $62$ candidates lies in the class of Definition 3.1. Sign reflection is an exact symmetry, but the sampled catalogue counts $62$ and $63$ for the reflected strata do not establish complete reflected sets (Section 8.1). If some $u_i>T$, the tails beyond $T$ are $\le0$ and dropping their tiles proves the bound for the larger truncated cost; such schedules are dominated by moving the negative to layer $T$.

## 5. Chains and exact regions: proofs of Theorems 2A and 2B

### 5.1 Monomial chains are path systems

**Definition 5.1.** For a layering $L$ (possibly with zeros), a *path system* is a partition of the layered entries into sequences $\pi=(x_0,x_1,\dots,x_k)$ with $x_j\in\Lambda_{u_0+j}$ (one entry per consecutive layer) such that $x_0+\dots+x_j\le0$ for $j<k$ and $x_0+\dots+x_k=0$ (a *balanced* path).

**Proposition 5.2.** (i) If $L$ admits a path system, $L$ is feasible: $C=\bigoplus_\pi W_\pi$, $W_\pi$ the weighted shift $e_{x_{j-1}}\mapsto w_je_{x_j}$ with $|w_j|^2=-2(x_0+\dots+x_{j-1})$, satisfies $CC^*-C^*C=2F$ on the layered entries and vanishes on the rest, and $\tfrac12\|C\|^2=\sum_\pi\sum_jj\,x_j=\operatorname{cost}(L)$. (ii) If (2.3) has a solution with every $R_t,S_t$ diagonal in the eigenbasis of $F$, then $L$ admits a path system.

*Proof.* (i) On a path, $(CC^*-C^*C)e_{x_j}=(|w_j|^2-|w_{j+1}|^2)e_{x_j}$ with $w_0=w_{k+1}=0$; the recursion $|w_{j+1}|^2=|w_j|^2-2x_j$ has the stated solution, nonnegative exactly under the partial-sum condition, and $w_{k+1}=0$ is the total-sum condition. Distinct paths use disjoint basis vectors. Cost: $\tfrac12\sum_j|w_j|^2=-\sum_{j\ge1}\sum_{i<j}x_i=-\sum_i(k-i)x_i=\sum_iix_i$ using $\sum_ix_i=0$; by Lemma 2.1 the layering cost restricted to the path is $\sum_i(u_0+i)x_i=\sum_iix_i$. (ii) If $R_t,S_t$ are diagonal, $M_t=V\sqrt{2\Sigma}W^*$ with $V,W$ permutation matrices times phases, so every $M_t$ and hence $C$ is monomial; its support graph has edges only from $\Lambda_{t-1}$ to $\Lambda_t$, in- and out-degree $\le1$ and no cycles, hence is a disjoint union of directed paths through consecutive layers (isolated vertices must be zeros), and on each path the diagonal identity is the recursion of (i), which forces the partial-sum conditions. $\square$

**Lemma 5.3.** Let $L=L(u)$ be ordered and full-capacity (no zeros used) with a path system. Then every path starts at a negative, contains no other negative, takes exactly one positive from every layer strictly after its start up to layer $T-1$, and possibly one from layer $T$. Consequently the spectrum splits into one-spike blocks $F_i=(a^{(i)}_1\ge a^{(i)}_2\ge\dots;-b_i)$ with $\sum_ja^{(i)}_j=b_i$, $a^{(i)}_j$ being the positive taken by path $i$ in layer $u_i+j$.

*Proof.* A path starts with $x_0\le0$, hence at a negative. In layer $u\le T-1$ the $c_u$ positives lie on $c_u$ distinct paths started at distinct negatives of layers $<u$, of which there are exactly $c_u$; so every earlier negative's path is alive at $u$ and takes a positive there. This excludes a second negative inside a path at a layer $\le T-1$, a path ending before layer $T-1$, and a negative at layer $T$ inside a path (its total would be $<0$). A path ends when its sum reaches $0$, i.e. when $b_i=\sum_ja^{(i)}_j$. Conversely, any assignment of the positives to alive paths with balanced spikes has partial sums $\le0$ automatically. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 2A.* The equivalence "diagonal solution iff path system" is Proposition 5.2; the block structure is Lemma 5.3. Lower bound: Theorem 1. Upper bound and cost: Proposition 5.2(i), and for path $i$, $\sum_j(u_i+j)a^{(i)}_j-u_ib_i=\sum_jj\,a^{(i)}_j$, the one-spike value of [OneSpike] Theorem 3.1. $\square$

*Remarks.* (a) The cone has codimension $n-1$ (the balance equalities); on it the cost is additive over spikes whose offsets $u_i$ make the merged layering sorted and full. ### 5.2 One rank-one interlacing step

**Lemma 5.4 (rank-one update, both directions).** Let $D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1\ge\dots\ge d_N)$ and $s\ge0$. The spectra of $D+s\,vv^*$ over unit vectors $v$ are exactly the vectors $\sigma_1\ge\dots\ge\sigma_N$ with
$$\sigma_1\ge d_1\ge\sigma_2\ge d_2\ge\dots\ge d_{N-1}\ge\sigma_N\ge d_N,\qquad \textstyle\sum\sigma=\sum d+s .$$

*Proof.* Necessity is Cauchy–Weyl interlacing for a positive semidefinite rank-one perturbation plus the trace. If $s=0$, interlacing and the equal trace force $\sigma=d$, and any unit vector works. For $s>0$, sufficiency for distinct $d_i$: with $v_i^2:=\prod_j(\sigma_j-d_i)\big/\big(s\prod_{j\ne i}(d_j-d_i)\big)$, interlacing gives numerator and denominator the sign $(-1)^{N-i}$, so $v_i^2\ge0$; the secular identity $\det(D+svv^*-x)=\prod_i(d_i-x)\big(1+s\sum_iv_i^2/(d_i-x)\big)$ then has exactly the roots $\sigma_j$, and $\sum_iv_i^2=1$ follows from the trace condition [Go73,Chu98,HJ]. Tied $d_i$: the attainable set (continuous image of the sphere) and the interlacing polytope are closed and vary continuously with $d$; take limits. $\square$

**Definition 5.5.** For $n\ge2$ and $1\le k\le m$ let
$$L_k:=\{-b_1\},\{a_1\},\dots,\{a_{k-1}\},\{a_k,-b_2,\dots,-b_n\},\{a_{k+1},\dots,a_{k+n}\},\{a_{k+n+1},\dots,a_{k+2n}\},\dots$$
(last layer partial), the ordered full-capacity layering with schedule $u=(0,k,\dots,k)$, and $\tau_i(k):=\sum_{r\ge0}a_{k+i+rn}$ ($i=1,\dots,n$; $a_j:=0$ for $j>m$), the column sums of the pure phase, $\tau_1\ge\dots\ge\tau_n\ge0$, $\sum_i\tau_i=A_{k+1}$. The tails are $A_u-B'$ ($u\le k$) and $A_{k+1+rn}$ ($r\ge0$), which gives the form of Theorem 2B. Let
$$R_k:=\{\lambda\ \text{in the stratum}:\ \tau_i(k)\le b_i\le\tau_{i-1}(k)\ \text{for}\ i=2,\dots,n\}.$$

*Proof of Theorem 2B.* *Range.* If $k\ge m-n+2$ then $k+n-1>m$, so $\tau_{n-1}(k)=0$ and $R_k$ would force $b_n\le0$: $R_k$ is empty. If $k\le m-n+1$ and $a_1>\dots>a_m$, then $\tau_{i-1}(k)-\tau_i(k)\ge a_{k+i-1}-a_{k+i}>0$ for $i=2,\dots,n$ (with $a_{m+1}=0$), so the box $\{\tau_i<b_i<\tau_{i-1}\}$ is open and nonempty; at its lower corner $b_i=\tau_i$ ($i\ge2$) one has $b_1=P-\sum_{i\ge2}\tau_i=a_1+\dots+a_k+\tau_1>\tau_1\ge b_2$, so the box meets the stratum in an open set.

*Lower bound.* $L_k$ is ordered and full-capacity ($c_u=1$ for $u\le k$, $c_u=n$ for $u>k$), so Theorem 1 applies.

*Singleton phase.* $S_1=b_1$ and, layer by layer, $R_u=S_u$ (scalars) and $S_{u+1}=S_u-a_u$, so $S_u=b_1-a_1-\dots-a_{u-1}=A_u-B'$ for $u\le k$. These are nonnegative on $R_k$: $b_i\le\tau_{i-1}$ gives $B'\le\tau_1+\dots+\tau_{n-1}\le A_{k+1}\le A_k$ (strictly positive inside the stratum).

*Mixed layer $k$.* $S_k=s:=A_k-B'$ is $1\times1$; take $R_k=s\,vv^*$ on $\Lambda_k$ with $v$ a unit vector of $\mathbb C^n$ (same nonzero spectrum). Then $S_{k+1}=R_k-D_k=s\,vv^*+\operatorname{diag}(-a_k,b_2,\dots,b_n)$. By Lemma 5.4 with $d=(b_2\ge\dots\ge b_n\ge-a_k)$, every $\sigma$ with $\sigma_1\ge b_2\ge\sigma_2\ge b_3\ge\dots\ge b_n\ge\sigma_n\ge-a_k$ and $\sum\sigma=B'-a_k+s=A_{k+1}$ is attainable. Choose $\sigma=\tau(k)$: the trace matches, $\tau_n\ge0\ge-a_k$, and the remaining conditions are $\tau_i\le b_i\le\tau_{i-1}$ ($i=2,\dots,n$), i.e. $\lambda\in R_k$. So $S_{k+1}$ is positive semidefinite with spectrum $\tau$.

*Pure phase (diagonal continuation).* For $r\ge0$ put $\tau^{(r)}_i:=\sum_{r'\ge r}a_{k+i+r'n}$ and $R_{k+1+r}:=\operatorname{diag}(\tau^{(r)}_1,\dots,\tau^{(r)}_n)$ on the slots $a_{k+1+rn},\dots,a_{k+n+rn}$ of $\Lambda_{k+1+r}$ (exhausted columns carry $0$; a partial layer uses only its existing slots, and the nonzero spectra still match). Then $\operatorname{spec}R_{k+1}=\tau=\operatorname{spec}S_{k+1}$; $S_{k+2+r}=R_{k+1+r}-D_{k+1+r}=\operatorname{diag}(\tau^{(r+1)})$ is positive semidefinite with the nonzero spectrum of $R_{k+2+r}$; and at the last layer $\tau^{(r)}_i=a_{k+i+rn}$, so $R_T=D_T$. Thus (2.3) holds and $\kappa_d\le\operatorname{cost}(L_k)$ on $R_k$.

*Rank.* $\operatorname{rank}C=\sum_t\operatorname{rank}S_t=k+\sum_{r\ge0}\#\{i:k+i+rn\le m\}=k+(m-k)=m$.

*$n=2$.* $\tau_1(k)=E_{k+1}$, $\tau_2(k)=E_{k+2}$ in the notation of [Mtwo], so $R_k=\{E_{k+2}\le b_2\le E_{k+1}\}$, the region of Proposition 5.4(1) there. $\square$

*Remarks.* (a) The chain is diagonal except for one rank-one interlacing step, and its rank equals the inertia bound $\max(n_+,n_-)$ when $m\ge n$. (b) Numerical: $1\,320$ random points of the regions $R_k$ in nine strata up to $(6,4,1)$ and $(5,5,0)$ give $\max|\operatorname{cost}(L_k)-\kappa_d|\le2.2\cdot10^{-15}$ (author); the reviewer's explicit $C$ (Golub's $v$, eigendecomposition of $S_{k+1}$, diagonal continuation) at $10$ points of every nonempty $R_k$ in six strata up to $(7,4,0)$ has residual $\|CC^*-C^*C-2F\|\le1.6\cdot10^{-15}$, $|\operatorname{cost}-\kappa_d|\le1.1\cdot10^{-15}$ and rank $m$ at every point, and reports $R_k$ empty exactly for $k>m-n+1$. The missing part of the chamber needs non-diagonal steps in the pure phase (for $n=2$, the $2\times2$ rotations of [Mtwo] Lemma 5.2). (d) The same mechanism gives sufficient regions for every family-A schedule with several mixed layers (at layer $u_i$ one alive path absorbs the new negatives by a rank-one step, the others continue diagonally), a finite union of polytopes; this general version was neither written out nor tested and is not claimed. ### 5.3 The pure phase: a necessary condition

Consider a state $\sigma=\operatorname{spec}S$ ($n$ values) followed by pure layers $\delta^{(1)},\dots,\delta^{(q)}$ of $n$ sorted positives each (the last possibly shorter), with column sums $\tau_i=\sum_t\delta^{(t)}_i$.

**Proposition 5.6.** If the pure chain is feasible from $\sigma$, then $\sigma\prec\tau$ (majorisation: the bottom-$j$ sums of $\sigma$ dominate those of $\tau$ for every $j$).

*Proof.* By Lidskii, $\operatorname{spec}(R_t-D_t)-\operatorname{spec}R_t$ lies in the permutohedron $\Pi(-\delta^{(t)})$. Summing along the chain, $\sigma^{(q)}-\sigma\in\sum_{1\le t<q}\Pi(-\delta^{(t)})=\Pi(-\sum_{1\le t<q}\delta^{(t)})$ (Minkowski sums of permutohedra of similarly sorted vectors are permutohedra of the sums, since the support functions $c\mapsto\langle c^\downarrow,x^\downarrow\rangle$ add), with $\sigma^{(q)}=\delta^{(q)}$ sorted. Hence $\sigma\in\delta^{(q)}+\Pi(\tau')\subseteq\Pi(\tau)$, $\tau'=\sum_{t<q}\delta^{(t)}$. $\square$

Numerically (chain LP of Section 8 in dimension $n$): for $n=2$ the exact region is $\tau_2\le\sigma_2\le\tau_1-\max_t(\delta^{(t)}_1-\delta^{(t)}_2)$ (no violation either way at $140$ feasible of $800$ sampled points; it is the $F_0$ region $E_2\le b_2\le E_1-g^*$ of [Mtwo]); for $n=3,4$ majorisation is never violated ($0$ of $67$ feasible points) but the analogous gap inequalities are ($13$–$14$ of $24$–$27$ at $n=3$), so the exact region for $n\ge3$ lies strictly between the two and its facets are not identified — the obstacle to a closed form for the $F_0$-type chambers.

## 7. The $d=8$ example

**8.3 Conjecture (inertia $(m,n)$).** For every $(m,n)$ and $z$, $\kappa_d$ is the maximum of a finite set $\mathcal S(m,n,z)$ of integer forms, each the cost of its level layering $L(g)$, and:

1. (*structure*) $\Lambda_0$ consists of negatives only, $\Lambda_T$ of positives only, no layer is empty; layer $u\ge1$ holds at most $c_u$ positives (capacity rule) and $|\Lambda_u|\le\min(n_+,n_-)$ for all $u$;
2. (*inversion balance*) for $z\ge1$, $\#\{j<j':t(a_j)>t(a_{j'})\}+\sum_u(c_u-q_u)\cdot\#\{\text{positives in layers}>u\}=\#\{i<i':t(b_i)>t(b_{i'})\}$, $q_u$ the number of positives in layer $u$ (the $n$-negative version of the rule of [Mthree]); the forms violating it are exactly the $z=0$-only forms;
3. (*ordered part*) the exposed family-A forms are those whose chamber meets the interior of the stratum, and the mirror family plays the same role for $n>m$;
4. (*upper bound*) for every $g\in \mathcal S(m,n,z)$ and every $\lambda$ in its chamber, $L(g)$, with at most one inserted zero when $z\ge1$, is chain-feasible; so $\kappa_d$ is attained by block weighted shifts with blocks of size $\le\min(n_+,n_-)$ in every dimension, while the minimal optimal rank can exceed $\max(n_+,n_-)$ already at $z=0$ (Section 7);
5. (*lower bound*) every $g\in \mathcal S(m,n,z)$ has a tiling certificate (multipliers $1$, each $s_t$ with $t\le m$ once) whose tiles have box $\le\min(n_+,n_-)$, and for some forms no certificate with fewer boxes exists once $m\ge n+1$ (proved for $(5,4)$: Theorem 3);
6. (*padding*) $\mathcal S(m,n,z)$ is independent of $z\ge1$; $\mathcal S(m,n,0)\setminus \mathcal S(m,n,1)$ consists of the forms violating 2, and their existence is what gives $\kappa_d>\kappa_{d+1}(\lambda\oplus0)$ on an open set (the $z=1$-only forms are dominated at $z=0$, e.g. $(2,1,2,3,4;-2,-1,-1)-(1,2,2,3,4;-2,-1,-1)=a_1-a_2\ge0$); a padding phase exists for $(m,n)=(5,4)$, $(4,5)$, $(6,4)$ but not for $(4,4)$ or $(5,5)$, so the set of $(m,n)$ with a padding phase is not a congruence condition in $m$ alone ($n=3$: $3\nmid m$).

Numerical status (tests on the discovered candidates, not verification of a complete $\mathcal S$): 1 holds for all $14$ sampled strata; 2 holds for all forms at $z\ge1$ ($(3,3,1)$, $(4,3,1)$, $(5,3,1)$, $(4,4,1)$, $(5,4,1)$) and fails at $z=0$ exactly for the $z=0$-only forms of $(4,3)$, $(5,3)$ and for $3$ forms of $(5,4,0)$, $3$ of $(4,5,0)$, $5$ of $(6,4,0)$; 4: $300/300$ in $13$ strata; 5: box histograms of Section 6. What is proved towards the conjecture: Theorem 1 (every ordered form is valid, all $d$), Lemma 3.2, Theorem 2A, Theorem 2B, Proposition 5.6, Theorem 3.

**8.4 Question (Pieri shapes).** At $z\ge1$ every discovered candidate in the seven tested strata has a numerically returned certificate built from Lidskii–Wielandt tiles and tiles with $\lambda(I)$ or $\lambda(J)$ a single row or column, whereas the form observed only at $z=0$, $g'=(1,1,2,3,4;-2,-1,0)\in S(5,4,0)$ has none (Section 6). Do all forms of $S(m,n,z)$ with $z\ge1$ — the forms present after padding, including any created by padding — admit tilings with Pieri-shaped tiles of at most $\min(n_+,n_-)$ boxes? The general-$z$ validity of such tilings would follow from the strip condition of the Pieri rule tile by tile, as sketched in [Mthree] Section 4.3.

## 9. Open questions

1. *The chambers of $L_k$ for $n\ge3$.* Describe the chain-feasibility region of $L_k$ beyond $R_k$ (non-diagonal pure phase) and the facets of the exact pure-phase region of Section 5.3; for $F_0$-type layerings ($u=0^n$) this is the whole problem.
2. *Several mixed layers.* Write out and test the multi-step interlacing regions of Remark (d) in Section 5.2, which would give closed forms for every family-A layering on a union of polytopes.
3. *Beyond the ordered class.* The b-inverted exposed forms need Pieri or two-row tiles; a general-$z$ proof of their validity and a characterisation of the exposed layerings are open. Question 8.4 is the shape part.
4. *Padding phases.* Prove the inversion-balance rule, determine the set of $(m,n)$ with a padding phase, and explain why padding can create exposed forms for $n\ge4$.
5. *Optimizers.* Classify the chambers by minimal optimal rank (Section 7: rank $m+1$ at $z=0$ for $(4,4)$) and by uniqueness of the optimal spectrum.
6. *Growth.* A formula, or the growth rate, for the cardinality of the complete exposed set $|\mathcal S(m,n,z)|$ (the currently discovered counts are $10,19,27,34$ at $n=3$; $32,63,92$ at $n=4$; $108$ at $(5,5)$).
