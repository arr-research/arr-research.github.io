# Exact Hive Certificates for the Balanced-Inertia Stability Constants (N = 3 to 12) in Every Dimension, the $(A_N,B)$ Cost Law, and the Twenty-Two-Form Six-Level Cost Formula

**Lluis Eriksson** (Independent researcher)

## Abstract

For a traceless Hermitian $F$ with $N$ positive, $N$ negative and $d-2N$ zero eigenvalues, the inverse self-commutator cost $\kappa_d(F)$ satisfies $\delta_d\ge\gamma_{N,d}D_*$ with an optimal rational constant $\gamma_{N,d}$; the values $\gamma_2,\dots,\gamma_5$ were known and $\gamma_6,\dots,\gamma_{10}$ only numerically. We give exact, independently machine-checked certificates proving $\gamma_{N,d}=\gamma_N$ for $N=3,\dots,12$ and every $d\ge2N$: $17/36$, $4/7$, $113/152$, $9/10$, $781/740$, $317/264$, $23/17$, $3/2$, $773/468$, $289/160$. The minimising pairs switch at $N=7$ from $(A_N,B)$ to $(u_3,z_N)$. The certificates are exact rational hive primals at every positive-distance vertex of the finite reduction and matching duals at the listed minimisers, and dimension-independent Horn combinations (Weyl, two size-two and Lidskii–Wielandt inequalities) at the minimisers, written out in full. We also prove the $(A_N,B)$ cost law for $N\le14$ and the exact six-level formula $\kappa_6=\max$ of twenty-two explicit linear forms, all exposed. The supplied package reports a separate adversarial replay; fresh checks using separately written code are documented in Section 7, with provenance limits stated explicitly.

## 1. Introduction

**Antecedents.** The quantity studied here is the least Hilbert–Schmidt cost of realising a prescribed traceless Hermitian $F$ as a self-commutator, $\kappa_d(F)=\tfrac12\min\{\|C\|_{HS}^2: CC^*-C^*C=2F\}$. Its theory has been developed in a sequence of records of the AI Research Record archive, cited by descriptive keys (identifiers and titles are in the reference list); everything used from them is restated in Section 2. [FourLevel] reduced $\kappa_d$ to a finite linear program over Horn's inequalities (its Theorem 2.2, the *Horn program*) and gave the exact four-level formula (its Theorem 3.1: the maximum of four spectral forms). [FiveLevel] gave the twelve-term five-level formula (its Theorem 3.1) together with the *per-chamber certificate template* — a sparse Horn dual for each form as a global lower bound and, on the chamber where that form is the largest, an explicit feasible construction attaining it — which Section 6 follows. [RankOnset] introduced the hive machinery into this line (exact rational hive primals and duals, its Section 6 and Lemma 6.1 on coarse graining) and showed (its Remark 3.3) that zero padding can lower $\kappa_d$, which is why "every dimension" is a genuine issue. [Ceiling] proved the sharp inertia ceiling $\kappa_d\le\frac{N+1}2P$ on the balanced stratum (its Theorem 1), the stability inequality $\gamma D_*\le\delta_d\le\frac{N-1}2D_*$ with the sharp reverse constant (its Theorem 3) and the optimal forward constants $\gamma_2=1/2$ and $\gamma_3=17/36$ (its Theorems 3 and 4). [Bal44] proved the finite reduction (its Theorem A: $\gamma_{N,d}$ is the minimum of a ratio over an explicit finite rational set $W_N$, independent of $d$) and $\gamma_4=4/7$ (its Theorem B); its Lemma 3 is the first dimension-independent Horn lower bound at an extremal pair. [Bal55] proved $\gamma_5=113/152$ (its Theorem 1) by enumerating all $191{,}353$ Horn triples of $d=10$. The published $(m,2)$ paper [Inertia_m2] listed as its Open question 3 the *numerical* values $\gamma_6=9/10$, $\gamma_7=781/740$, $\gamma_8=317/264$, $\gamma_9=23/17$, $\gamma_{10}=3/2$, obtained by floating-point hive LPs, "without exact certificates", and noted the switch of the extremal family at $N=7$. Externally: Horn's conjecture, proved by Klyachko [Kl98] and Knutson–Tao [KT99] (saturation), with the hive model expounded by Buch [Bu00] and surveyed by Fulton [Fu00], is the tool; Weyl, Lidskii and Wielandt [We12, Li50, Wi55] supply the classical inequalities that turn out to suffice at the minimisers.

**Contribution.** (i) Theorem 1: $\gamma_{N,d}=\gamma_N$ exactly for $N=3,\dots,12$ and *every* $d\ge2N$, with the two minimising pairs and their costs; $N=3,4,5$ reproduce the published constants, $N=6,\dots,10$ turn the numerical values of [Inertia_m2] into theorems, and $N=11,12$ are new. (ii) Theorem 2: the $(A_N,B)$ cost law $\kappa_{2N}(A_N,-B^{\mathrm{rev}})=\frac32+(\lfloor(N-2)^2/4\rfloor-1)\frac{N+2}{4N(N-1)}$ for $N\le14$, in every $d\ge2N$ for $N\le6$. (iii) Theorem 3: $\kappa_6$ is the maximum of twenty-two explicit rational forms on the ordered trace-zero cone, every form exposed, so that the non-wall facet sequence of the unit ball of $\kappa_d$ begins $2,4,12,22$ for $d=3,\dots,6$. (iv) A certificate architecture in which each claim is a finite list of exact rational objects checked by two independent verifiers: hive primals and duals, windowed Horn templates valid in every dimension (with a reflection trick that halves the search), and ray witnesses with global duals for the chambers of $\kappa_6$. (v) The dimension-independent certificates at the minimisers are so short — three to seven inequalities, multipliers $1$ or $2$ — that they are written out in full in Section 5 and can be checked by hand. The findings of the adversarial review (Section 7), including three reporting errors of the working report, are incorporated.

## 2. Setting

Throughout $d\ge2$, Hermitian matrices are $d\times d$, spectra are listed in decreasing order, and $x^{\mathrm{rev}}$ is the reversed list. For traceless Hermitian $F$ with spectrum $\lambda$, [FourLevel] Theorem 2.2 (also [RankOnset] Proposition 2.2, in the normalisation $s=p/2$ used here) gives the *Horn program*
$$\kappa_d(\lambda)=\min\Big\{\sum_{t=1}^d s_t:\ s_1\ge\dots\ge s_d\ge0,\ (s,-s^{\mathrm{rev}},\lambda)\ \text{Horn-feasible}\Big\}, \tag{2.1}$$
where a triple $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ of decreasing real $d$-vectors is *Horn-feasible* if Hermitian $A,B$ exist with spectra $\alpha,\beta$ and $\operatorname{spec}(A+B)=\gamma$. (Given $C$, put $R=CC^*/2$, $S=C^*C/2$: they have a common spectrum $s\ge0$, $R-S=F$ and $\tfrac12\|C\|^2=\sum s_t$; conversely $R,S\succeq0$ with common spectrum and $R-S=F$ come from some $C$.) Letting $s_d$ be free rather than $0$ is harmless: if $(s,-s^{\mathrm{rev}},\lambda)$ is feasible with $s_d=c$, then $(A-cI,B+cI)$ shows $(s-c\mathbf 1,\dots)$ is feasible at lower cost, so the minimum has $s_d=0$.

**Horn's inequalities.** For $1\le r<d$ let $T^d_r$ be Horn's recursively defined set of triples $(I,J,K)$ of $r$-subsets of $\{1,\dots,d\}$: $\sum_I i+\sum_J j=\sum_K k+r(r+1)/2$ and, for every $q<r$ and $(F,G,H)\in T^r_q$, $\sum_{f\in F}i_f+\sum_{g\in G}j_g\le\sum_{h\in H}k_h+q(q+1)/2$ (elements listed increasingly). The theorem of Klyachko and Knutson–Tao [Kl98, KT99, Fu00] states that $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ is Horn-feasible iff $\sum\gamma=\sum\alpha+\sum\beta$ and
$$\sum_{k\in K}\gamma_k\le\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_i+\sum_{j\in J}\beta_j\qquad\text{for all }(I,J,K)\in T^d_r,\ 1\le r<d, \tag{2.2}$$
and that $T^d_r$ is exactly the set of triples whose Littlewood–Richardson coefficient $c^{\lambda(K)}_{\lambda(I)\lambda(J)}$ is positive. The counts are $|T^d|=3,12,41,142,522,2062$ for $d=2,\dots,7$ and $8{,}752$ for $d=8$. In (2.1), $\alpha=s$, $\beta_j=-s_{d+1-j}$, so the trace condition is $\sum\lambda=0$ and (2.2) reads $\sum_{k\in K}\lambda_k\le\sum_{i\in I}s_i-\sum_{j\in J}s_{d+1-j}$.

**Hives.** An order-$d$ hive is a function $h$ on $\{(i,j)\in\mathbb Z^2: i,j\ge0,\ i+j\le d\}$ such that for every unit rhombus of the triangular lattice $h(o_1)+h(o_2)\ge h(a_1)+h(a_2)$ ($o$ the obtuse, $a$ the acute vertices; there are $3\binom d2$ rhombi). With $h(0,0)=0$ we say $h$ has boundary $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ if the increments along $(0,0)\to(d,0)$ are $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_d$, along $(d,0)\to(0,d)$ are $\beta_1,\dots,\beta_d$ and along $(0,0)\to(0,d)$ are $\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_d$ (the Knutson–Tao/Buch convention; the labelling was calibrated against the explicit Horn LP, Section 7). Knutson–Tao [KT99] prove that for integral data an integral hive exists iff $c^\gamma_{\alpha\beta}>0$, and by saturation iff the hive polytope is nonempty; since the projection of the hive cone to boundaries and the cone cut out by (2.2) are closed rational polyhedral cones that agree on rational points, they agree on real points:
$$(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\ \text{Horn-feasible}\iff\text{an order-}d\text{ hive with boundary }(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\text{ exists}. \tag{2.3}$$
Hence $\kappa_d(\lambda)$ is the value of the *hive LP*: minimise $\sum s_t$ over $s$ decreasing $\ge0$ and $h$ a hive with boundary $(s,-s^{\mathrm{rev}},\lambda)$, with $(d+1)(d+2)/2$ hive variables and $d$ common-spectrum variables (including variables fixed by the boundary constraints), and $3\binom d2$ rhombus rows instead of $|T^d|$ rows.

**The balanced stratum.** Fix $N\ge2$ and $d\ge2N$, normalise $P=1$, and write the spectrum of an exact-inertia-$(N,N,d-2N)$ target as $\lambda=(a,0^{d-2N},-b^{\mathrm{rev}})$ with $a,b$ in the ordered simplex $\mathcal S_N=\{a_1\ge\dots\ge a_N\ge0,\ \sum a_i=1\}$ (closure allowed). Following [Ceiling, Bal44]: $e=(1,0,\dots,0)$, $u=(1/N,\dots,1/N)$, $H_N=(N+1)/2$,
$$E(a)=\|a-e\|_1=2(1-a_1),\qquad U(a)=\|a-u\|_1,\qquad \delta_d(a,b)=H_N-\kappa_d(a,0,-b^{\mathrm{rev}}),$$
$$D_*(a,b)=\min\{E(a)+U(b),\,U(a)+E(b)\},$$
and $\gamma_{N,d}$ is the largest constant with $\gamma_{N,d}D_*\le\delta_d$ on the stratum. **Theorem A of [Bal44]** (finite reduction): for every fixed $d\ge2N$,
$$\gamma_{N,d}=\min_{(a,b)\in W_N,\ D_*(a,b)>0}\frac{H_N-\kappa_d(a,0,-b^{\mathrm{rev}})}{D_*(a,b)}, \tag{2.4}$$
where $W_N$ is an explicit finite rational set independent of $d$ (the cut vertices of the cell complex of $D_*$, constructed by `geometry.py` of the [Bal44] record; the supplied provenance describes this copy as byte-identical to the record's; the present fresh checks do not authenticate that historical byte comparison). Its sizes are $|W_N|=22,89,267,628,1354,2611,4615,7614,12120,18385$ for $N=3,\dots,12$; exactly two members, $(e,u)$ and $(u,e)$, have $D_*=0$. (The working report misprinted $4617$ and $7616$ for $N=9,10$; the certified counts $|W_N|-2$ were right.)

**The two families.** For $N\ge3$ put
$$A_N=\Big(\tfrac{3N-2}{4N},\ \big(\tfrac{N+2}{4N(N-1)}\big)^{N-1}\Big),\quad B=\big(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,0^{N-2}\big),\quad u_3=\big(\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13,0^{N-3}\big),\quad z_N=\Big(\tfrac{4N-3}{9N},\tfrac{4N-3}{9N},\ \big(\tfrac{N+6}{9N(N-2)}\big)^{N-2}\Big).$$
Direct computation gives $D_*(A_N,B)=\frac{5N-6}{2N}$ and $D_*(u_3,z_N)=\frac{4(7N-12)}{9N}$, both orientations being equal in each case ($E(u_3)=4/3$, $U(z_N)=\frac{16N-48}{9N}$, $U(u_3)=\frac{2(N-3)}N$, $E(z_N)=\frac{2(5N+3)}{9N}$). For $N\le12$ the pairs of Table 1 are members of $W_N$ (cut vertices in the sense of [Bal44] Lemma 2).

The imported theorems are exactly those of Section 2: the Horn program (2.1), Horn–Klyachko–Knutson–Tao (2.2), the hive equivalence (2.3), and Theorem A (2.4). Nothing else is imported; in particular no floating-point computation enters any proof.

**Theorem 2 ($(A_N,B)$ cost law).** For $N=3,\dots,14$,
$$\kappa_{2N}\big(A_N,-B^{\mathrm{rev}}\big)=\frac32+\Big(\Big\lfloor\frac{(N-2)^2}4\Big\rfloor-1\Big)\frac{N+2}{4N(N-1)}, \tag{3.1}$$

**Theorem 3 (six-level formula).** For every traceless Hermitian $6\times6$ $F$ with decreasing spectrum $\lambda$ and gaps $g_j=\lambda_j-\lambda_{j+1}\ge0$ ($j=1,\dots,5$),
$$\kappa_6(F)=\max_{r=1,\dots,22}\ q_r\cdot g, \tag{3.2}$$
with the twenty-two rational forms $q_r$ of Table 2 (Section 6), a list closed under reversal $g\mapsto g^{\mathrm{rev}}$. Every form is exposed: its dominance chamber $C_r=\{g\ge0:(q_r-q_j)\cdot g\ge0\ \forall j\}$ is full-dimensional, so $q_r$ is the unique maximiser on a non-empty open set. Consequently the unit ball $\{\kappa_d\le1\}$ of the ordered trace-zero cone has exactly $2,4,12,22$ non-wall facets for $d=3,4,5,6$ (the counts for $d=3,4,5$ follow from the closed formulas of [FourLevel] and [FiveLevel]).

## 4. Certificate architecture and proofs

### 4.1 Exact hive primals and duals

A *hive primal* for $\lambda$ in dimension $d$ is a rational decreasing $s\ge0$ together with a rational hive $h$ (stored as integers $h_{\mathrm{int}}$ and a common denominator) with boundary $(s,-s^{\mathrm{rev}},\lambda)$. Checking it means checking $3\binom d2$ rhombus inequalities and $3d$ boundary equalities in exact arithmetic; by (2.3) it proves $\kappa_d(\lambda)\le\sum s_t$. A *hive dual* is a list of nonnegative rational multipliers $y$ on the rhombi, $z$ on the ordering rows $s_k-s_{k+1}\ge0$, $s_d\ge0$, and signed multipliers $\mu$ on the boundary equalities, such that
$$\sum_t s_t-v=\sum_{\text{rhombi}}y\cdot(\text{rhombus slack})+\sum z\cdot(\text{ordering slack})+\sum\mu\cdot(\text{boundary residual})+\sum_t\nu_ts_t \tag{4.1}$$
holds as an identity of linear forms in $(h,s)$ with $\nu\ge0$; then $\sum s_t\ge v$ on every feasible point, and by (2.3) $\kappa_d(\lambda)\ge v$. When primal cost and dual value coincide, $\kappa_d(\lambda)$ is known exactly. The generators solve the hive LP in floating point (HiGHS dual simplex), rationalise $s$, $h$ and the multipliers by continued fractions, and re-verify exactly before writing anything; a fallback exact re-solve on the floating-point support exists but was never needed (all failure counters are $0$ in every run).

### 4.2 Dimension-independent lower bounds: windowed Horn templates

A *template* is $(I_0,I_1,J_0,J_1,K_0,K_1,r,\mathrm{refl})$ with $I_0,\dots,K_1\subseteq\{1,\dots,M\}$, $|I_0|+|I_1|=|J_0|+|J_1|=|K_0|+|K_1|=r$ and $|I_1|+|J_1|=|K_1|$. In dimension $d\ge2M$ it defines $I=I_0\cup\{d+1-t:t\in I_1\}$ and likewise $J$, $K$: "small" indices $\le M$ and "large" indices $\ge d+1-M$, which do not overlap. Write $s'_t:=s_{d+1-t}$. For $\lambda=(a,0^{d-2N},-b^{\mathrm{rev}})$ ($M\ge N$; $a_k:=0$ for $k>N$, $b_t:=0$ for $t>N$) the Horn inequality of the template reads
$$\sum_{k\in K_0}a_k-\sum_{t\in K_1}b_t\ \le\ \sum_{i\in I_0}s_i+\sum_{t\in I_1}s'_t-\sum_{j\in J_0}s'_j-\sum_{t\in J_1}s_t, \tag{4.2}$$
an inequality whose coefficients do not depend on $d$.

**Lemma 4.1 (stability).** Fix a template and $d_0\ge2M$. Membership $(I,J,K)\in T^d_r$ is a finite conjunction of conditions each of which is affine in $d$ (the sum condition has zero slope; each recursive condition $(F,G,H)\in T^r_q$ contributes an inequality whose $d$-coefficient is $\#\{f\in F: i_f\text{ large}\}+\#\{g\in G: j_g\text{ large}\}-\#\{h\in H: k_h\text{ large}\}$). If every condition holds at $d=d_0$ and has slope $\le0$, the template is a Horn triple in every $d\ge d_0$, and (4.2) holds for every feasible $s$ in every such $d$.

*Proof.* For $d\ge2M$ the sorted order of $I$ is $d$-independent, so the recursive conditions refer to fixed positions; an affine function of $d$ with slope $\le0$ that is satisfied at $d_0$ is satisfied for all $d\ge d_0$. The inequality then holds by the necessity half of (2.2). $\square$

**Lemma 4.2 (reflection).** $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ is Horn-feasible iff $(-\alpha^{\mathrm{rev}},-\beta^{\mathrm{rev}},-\gamma^{\mathrm{rev}})$ is (negate $A,B$) iff $(\beta,\alpha,\gamma)$ is (commute the sum). Applied to $(s,-s^{\mathrm{rev}},\lambda)$ this gives $(s,-s^{\mathrm{rev}},-\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}})$, and $-\lambda^{\mathrm{rev}}=(b,0^{d-2N},-a^{\mathrm{rev}})$. Hence the feasible sets of $s$ for the targets $(a,b)$ and $(b,a)$ coincide, $\kappa_d$ is the same for both orientations, and the Horn inequalities of the reflected target ($\mathrm{refl}=1$: exchange the roles of $a$ and $b$ in (4.2)) are valid constraints on the same $s$.

An *all-$d$ certificate* for $(a,b)$ with bound $v$ is a list of stable templates with nonnegative rational multipliers $y$ (and, optionally, multipliers on the ordering rows, unused in all the certificates here) such that the $y$-combination of the inequalities (4.2) is $\sum_ic_is_i+\sum_tc'_ts'_t\ge v$ with every $c_i,c'_t\le1$. Since $s\ge0$, $\sum_{\text{all}}s\ge\sum_ic_is_i+\sum_tc'_ts'_t\ge v$ in every $d\ge d_0$, i.e. $\kappa_d(a,0^{d-2N},-b^{\mathrm{rev}})\ge v$ for all $d\ge d_0$. The verifier re-derives stability of every template from Horn's recursion (its own implementation) and re-assembles the combination; a second checker instantiates each template at $d=d_0,\dots,d_0+8$ and tests membership in $T^d_r$ directly.

The certificates were found by linear programming over families of stable templates: a generic enumeration of all stable templates with $r\le5$ (`stable_templates.py`; $1.53$ million templates for $N=6$, $496$ s) and, much more cheaply, the *structured family* (`alld_family.py`) consisting of all stable templates with $r\le2$ together with all Lidskii–Wielandt templates $I=\{1,\dots,r\}$, $J=K$ and their mirrors, in both orientations. The structured family reproduces the $N=6,7$ certificates in $0.5$ s and $1.4$ s and certifies $N=3,4,5,8,\dots,12$ in at most $74$ s each. A generic search restricted to $r\le4$ cannot certify $N=7$ (best bound $466/315<479/315$): the tight certificate needs an $r=6$ Lidskii–Wielandt inequality.

### 4.3 Chambers of $\kappa_6$: ray witnesses and global duals

Work in $d=6$ with $s_6=0$ (Section 2) and gap coordinates $g\in\mathbb R^5_{\ge0}$, $\lambda(g)$ the trace-zero spectrum with those gaps; (2.1) becomes the explicit LP with the $522$ rows of $T^6$ and four ordering rows. For a form $q$ two objects are stored. (a) A *global dual*: multipliers $y\ge0$ on the $522$ triples and $z\ge0$ on the ordering rows with $c:=\sum_ry_r\alpha_r+D^{\top}z\le(1,1,1,1,1)$ componentwise, where $\alpha_r$ is the $s$-coefficient vector of row $r$ and $D$ the ordering matrix, and $\sum_ry_r\mathbf 1_{K_r}=q$ as a linear form on trace-zero $\lambda$ (i.e. in gap coordinates). Summing the rows gives $c\cdot s\ge q\cdot g$; since $s\ge0$ and $c\le\mathbf 1$ componentwise, $\sum s\ge c\cdot s\ge q\cdot g$ for every feasible $s$ and *every* $g$, since the multipliers do not depend on $\lambda$; hence $\kappa_6\ge\max_rq_r\cdot g$ everywhere. (b) *Ray witnesses*: the extreme rays $\rho$ of the dominance chamber $C_r$ (primitive integer vectors) and, at each, an exact feasible $s(\rho)$ with $\sum s(\rho)=q_r\cdot\rho$. The feasible set $\{(s,\lambda)\}$ of the Horn program is a polyhedral convex cone, $\lambda(g)$ is linear, and $C_r\subseteq\mathbb R^5_{\ge0}$ is pointed, hence generated by its extreme rays; for $g=\sum c_\rho\rho$ with $c\ge0$ the point $\sum c_\rho s(\rho)$ is feasible for $\lambda(g)$ with cost $q_r\cdot g$. So $\kappa_6\le q_r\cdot g$ on $C_r$, and with (a), $\kappa_6=q_r\cdot g=\max_jq_j\cdot g$ there. Coverage is automatic: for every $g$ some $r$ attains the maximum and then $g\in C_r$ by definition. Extreme rays were computed by brute force over $4$-subsets of the chamber inequalities (complete for a pointed cone in $\mathbb R^5$: an extreme ray has at least four linearly independent tight inequalities) and, in review, by `pycddlib` (exact GMP arithmetic); the two sets coincide.

### 4.4 Padding monotonicity and the proof of Theorem 1

**Lemma 4.3 (padding monotonicity).** If $C$ realises $F$ in dimension $d$ then $C\oplus0_{e-d}$ realises $F\oplus0_{e-d}$ with the same Hilbert–Schmidt norm; the sorted spectrum of $F\oplus0$ is $(a,0^{e-2N},-b^{\mathrm{rev}})$ (the new zeros land between $a_N\ge0$ and $-b_N\le0$). Hence $\kappa_e(a,0^{e-2N},-b^{\mathrm{rev}})\le\kappa_d(a,0^{d-2N},-b^{\mathrm{rev}})$ for $e\ge d\ge2N$; the ratios in (2.4) are non-decreasing in $d$ and $\gamma_{N,d}\ge\gamma_{N,2N}$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 1.* Fix $N$ and let $m$ be the value in Table 1. (i) By Theorem A, $\gamma_{N,d}$ is the minimum of (2.4) over the $|W_N|-2$ pairs with $D_*>0$, for every fixed $d\ge2N$. (ii) For every such pair $p$ the stored order-$2N$ hive primal gives $\kappa_{2N}(p)\le\sum s(p)$, and by Lemma 4.3 $\kappa_d(p)\le\kappa_{2N}(p)$; the verifier checks $(H_N-\sum s(p))/D_*(p)\ge m$ for every $p$. So every ratio in every $d\ge2N$ is $\ge m$. (iii) At the two minimising pairs $p^*$ the stored hive dual gives $\kappa_{2N}(p^*)=\sum s(p^*)$ exactly, and the all-$d$ certificate of Section 5 gives $\kappa_d(p^*)\ge\kappa_{2N}(p^*)$ for every $d\ge2N$; with Lemma 4.3, $\kappa_d(p^*)=\kappa_{2N}(p^*)$ and the ratio at $p^*$ equals $m$ in every $d$. By Lemma 4.2 one all-$d$ certificate covers both orientations. Hence $\gamma_{N,d}=m$ for all $d\ge2N$. $\square$

Which imported facts are used where: the upper bounds (ii) use the hive equivalence (2.3) in the direction "hive $\Rightarrow$ feasible" in dimension $2N$ only; the exact values at the minimisers use (2.3) in the direction "feasible $\Rightarrow$ hive" in dimension $2N$; the all-$d$ lower bounds use only the *necessity* of Horn's inequalities, in every $d$. ## 8. Observations and open questions

**Observation 8.1 (quasi-polynomial cost at $(u_3,z_N)$; conjecture only).** Exact primal-equals-dual hive values (not stored, `uz_kappa_scan.py`, $0.4$ s) give, for $N=6,\dots,16$, $\kappa_{2N}(u_3,-z_N^{\mathrm{rev}})=$ $13/9$, $479/315$, $169/108$, $43/27$, $49/30$, $1481/891$, $101/60$, $2206/1287$, $73/42$, $1028/585$, $899/504$. No closed form is claimed.

**Observation 8.2 (conjectured minimiser).** For all $N\ge7$ the minimiser of (2.4) is $(u_3,z_N)$ and $\gamma_N=9N(H_N-\kappa_{2N}(u_3,z_N))/(4(7N-12))$; exact for $N\le12$, and the ratios at $N=13,\dots,16$ would be $6803/3476$, $363/172$, $913/403$, $677/280$ if so. **Open questions.** (1) $\gamma_N$ for all $N$: a proof that the argmin over $W_N$ lies in the $(u_k,z_{k,N})$ family, and a closed form for $\kappa_{2N}(u_3,z_N)$; the uniform certificate skeleton of Section 5 suggests that the Lidskii–Wielandt index sets can be given as a function of $N$ (which $r$ occur, and where the large indices sit), but this was not attempted. (2) The general-$N$ cost law (3.1) and its all-$d$ version for $N\ge7$. (3) The non-wall facets of the unit ball of $\kappa_7$ (the sequence $2,4,12,22,\dots$): the per-chamber template scales, but the $2062$-row LP and the number of chambers grow.
