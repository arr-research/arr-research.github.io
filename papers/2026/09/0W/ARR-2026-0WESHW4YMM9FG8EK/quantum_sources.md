# Quantum final revision: primary-source comparison and research boundary

5 September 2026. This is a focused literature check for the prime-power rank question and the final universal central-multiplicity extension. It is not a repeat of the full corpus audit or an exhaustive priority search. The original cycle-3 source and review remain unchanged.

The preceding results were read before selecting a new problem: the complete cycle-2 rank-five manuscript, its separate internal review, the exact replay source and source notes, and the cycle-1 Weyl list and Bayes manuscripts. Thus rank 6 or 7 in dimension 8 was deliberately excluded as a research target: the general two-label theorem already settles both. Re-proving the dimension-eight thirty-state classification alone would also add no result to this programme.

The original question was whether the dimension-eight trinomial obstruction reflects a prime-power rank law. The final revision identifies the exact arithmetic obstruction outside prime powers: a central sector has a common cyclic multiplicity m=d/(qh), with q the commutator order and h the central label-group order. The rank formula holds universally, while the genuine-trinomial nullity bound becomes 2m. This is a concrete application of classical cyclic representation machinery, not a new representation classification. A dimension-36 example attains 2m=6, and a zero-constant example proves that the coefficient hypothesis is necessary. The prime-power rank classification and operational applications remain unchanged.

## Primary reading in this cycle

1. **Johnston–Lovitz–Russo–Sikora, arXiv:2510.20789v1.** [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.20789v1). Rechecked Section 2.1, including Lemma 9 and Corollary 11. These supply the established learning-width/factor-width framework. Our operational sparse-square reduction is the previously derived Weyl specialization and normalization; it is not a new general equivalence.

2. **Gheorghiu, arXiv:1101.1519.** [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1101.1519). Rechecked Section III, Theorem 1 and its group-average proof, PDF pages 3–4. The dimension/order identity and character projectors are credited as standard. The present arithmetic evaluates the particular subgroup generated by the two commutator-order powers in the fixed cyclic Weyl representation, now in arbitrary dimension.

3. **Hyun Kyu Kim, arXiv:1703.05513.** [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.05513), with [publisher version](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpaa.2018.08.011). Read Section 2.1 through Proposition 2.6 and its short irreducibility proof, with the adjacent phase conventions. It describes classical cyclic quantum-torus representations and attributes the classification to earlier work. Its working convention has odd root order. The present manuscript therefore proves its even-order determinant and multiplicity statements directly, rather than applying an odd-order theorem to powers of two. A direct HTML route returned 404; the PDF route succeeded.

4. **Fock–Goncharov, Annales ENS 42 (2009).** [Primary PDF](https://smf.emath.fr/download/pdf/43892). Read the title/bibliographic page and Section 3.1.1, PDF page 39, together with the root-order hypotheses surrounding Theorem 3.11. This is background for the standard root-of-unity center/quantum-binomial mechanism. The relevant cyclic determinant identity is proved in full in our draft. The source's additional cluster/Frobenius conclusions are not imported.

5. **Farenick, Ojo and Plosker, Universality of Weyl Unitaries, arXiv:2101.00129v1.** [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.00129). Read the abstract and Section 2, particularly Lemmas 2.1–2.2 and the cyclic normal-form proof. Equal-dimensional eigenspaces and repeated cyclic blocks are direct classical antecedents of Lemma 1. Their use here is not described as a new representation-theoretic result. The article proves the common multiplicity within the fixed cyclic Weyl representation by trace orthogonality and label arithmetic, then derives a scalar rank test and a two-circle bound.

The article's general rank theorem has no unreproduced external proof step: character multiplicity, the label-group arithmetic, the cyclic block determinant and the circle argument are proved there. The optional gcd-of-maximal-minors evaluation is a standard integer-lattice identity; neither the universal block theorem nor its independent valuation proof of the prime-power corollary depends on it. The source comparisons identify shared machinery; they do not establish external priority of the final theorem.
