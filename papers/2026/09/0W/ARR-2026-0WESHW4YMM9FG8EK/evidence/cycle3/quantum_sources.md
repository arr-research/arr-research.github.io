# Quantum cycle 3: primary-source comparison and research boundary

5 September 2026. This is a focused literature check for the new prime-power rank question, not a repeat of the full corpus audit and not an exhaustive priority search.

The preceding results were read before selecting a new problem: the complete cycle-2 rank-five manuscript, its separate internal review, the exact replay source and source notes, and the cycle-1 Weyl list and Bayes manuscripts. Thus rank 6 or 7 in dimension 8 was deliberately excluded as a research target: the general two-label theorem already settles both. Re-proving the dimension-eight thirty-state classification alone would also add no result to this programme.

The chosen question was whether the dimension-eight trinomial obstruction reflects a prime-power rank law. The decisive arithmetic experiment checked central-label orders for every noncommuting pair in eleven small prime-power dimensions. The analytic proof then handled arbitrary coefficients. Two tempting larger claims were tested and rejected: an unrestricted composite-dimension nullity bound, and a power-of-two corank law for positive sums. Their exact counterexamples are in the manuscript and verifier.

## Primary reading in this cycle

1. **Johnston–Lovitz–Russo–Sikora, arXiv:2510.20789v1.** [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.20789v1). Rechecked Section 2.1, including Lemma 9 and Corollary 11. These supply the established learning-width/factor-width framework. Our operational sparse-square reduction is the previously derived Weyl specialization and normalization; it is not a new general equivalence.

2. **Gheorghiu, arXiv:1101.1519.** [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1101.1519). Rechecked Section III, Theorem 1 and its group-average proof, PDF pages 3–4. The dimension/order identity and character projectors are credited as standard. The new arithmetic step is the evaluation of the specific subgroup generated by the two commutator-order powers in cyclic prime-power dimension.

3. **Hyun Kyu Kim, arXiv:1703.05513.** [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.05513), with [publisher version](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpaa.2018.08.011). Read Section 2.1 through Proposition 2.6 and its short irreducibility proof, with the adjacent phase conventions. It describes classical cyclic quantum-torus representations and attributes the classification to earlier work. Its working convention has odd root order. The present manuscript therefore proves its even-order determinant and multiplicity statements directly, rather than applying an odd-order theorem to powers of two. A direct HTML route returned 404; the PDF route succeeded.

4. **Fock–Goncharov, Annales ENS 42 (2009).** [Primary PDF](https://smf.emath.fr/download/pdf/43892). Read the title/bibliographic page and Section 3.1.1, PDF page 39, together with the root-order hypotheses surrounding Theorem 3.11. This is background for the standard root-of-unity center/quantum-binomial mechanism. The relevant cyclic determinant identity is proved in full in our draft. The source's additional cluster/Frobenius conclusions are not imported.

The draft's general rank theorem has no unreproduced external proof step: character multiplicity, the label-group arithmetic, the cyclic block determinant and the circle argument are proved there. The source comparisons identify shared machinery; they do not establish external priority of the final theorem.

The last broad searches returned many unrelated Weyl-curvature, Weyl-group and real-line HRT items. They were not proof dependencies. In particular, a real-line finite-cardinality Gabor statement is not a theorem about the rank of a sparse cyclic matrix, and a generic full-spark finite Gabor theorem has different coefficient and window quantifiers. No conclusion about the validity of those unrelated papers is made here.

The inspected primary sources did not state the exact prime-power corank list, the all-dyadic thirty-state low-rank stratum, or the exact list value at rank 13d/16. This is only a bounded negative search. Before an external submission, a wider search should examine equivalent statements in finite twisted-group-algebra rank, sparse finite Weyl transforms, and covariant factor-width cones.
