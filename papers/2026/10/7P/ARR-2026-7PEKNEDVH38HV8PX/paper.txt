The arithmetic of sampling static quantum disorder:
exact aliases, ﬁnite witnesses, and clock robustness
Lluis Eriksson
Independent researcher, Sweden
15 October 2026 — working paper
Abstract
Whether a dephasing experiment can exclude static Hamiltonian disorder depends on
the arithmetic of its measurement times. We prove that at any ﬁnite set of rationally in-
dependent positive times, every real dephasing semigroup with strictly positive oﬀ-diagonal
rates is reproduced exactly by one ﬁnite ensemble of static diagonal Hamiltonians. At most
1 + kd(d −1) Hamiltonians suﬃce for k times and d levels; no uniform energy bound is as-
serted. We also construct an explicit bounded-energy static ensemble reproducing the same
semigroup on an entire suﬃciently coarse uniform grid.
In the opposite direction, every
rate matrix outside the cut cone admits a separating test on ﬁnitely many odd harmonics
of one clock period. The proof combines a circle-distance cut representation with an ex-
plicit Fourier-tail bound, and applies to arbitrary static Hermitian ensembles through their
coherence-transfer entries. A qutrit example uses 20 times and 40,500 shots to bound each
error probability by 5%. Consequently, exact ﬁnite-time detectability can disappear under
arbitrarily small independent perturbations of the sampling times. A spectral-width assump-
tion restores robustness with an explicit timing error budget. Alternatively, fresh bounded
time randomization tolerates independent additive clock error without an energy prior; a
certiﬁed qutrit design needs 53,000 shots. All results concern fresh single-use channel exper-
iments, not interventions within one noise trajectory. The contribution is an identiﬁability
and experimental-design theory; no quantum environment or solution of an Erdős problem
is claimed.
1
Question and relation to existing work
An ensemble of time-independent Hamiltonians can produce irreversible- looking coherence de-
cay. A Cauchy-distributed ﬁxed frequency even gives exact exponential decay. Hamiltonian-
ensemble representations and their Fourier compatibility constraints are established tools [4, 5,
6, 7]. Nevertheless, an incompatibility of a whole time-dependent family need not be visible in
a speciﬁed ﬁnite collection of its channels. This paper asks which sampling designs preserve or
destroy that distinction.
The answer has three parts. Generic ﬁnite menus admit exact static aliases, carefully related
ﬁnite menus can exclude every static model, and coarse inﬁnite grids can admit aliases again.
These statements are compatible: they use diﬀerent arithmetic relations between times. Our
constructive separation also exposes a discontinuity relevant to clock calibration. Without an
energy bound, independent timing errors cannot be treated as a uniformly small perturbation
of the static null.
The mathematical ingredients have classical antecedents. The torus-density argument is the
continuous Kronecker theorem; the convex-hull and ﬁnite-support arguments are standard ﬁnite-
dimensional convex analysis [8, 9]. The phase-correlation body, ﬁnite convex representations,
and interiority of the identity are established in Dykema–Juschenko [1, Proposition 2.5, Remark
2.7, Section 4] and Kribs et al. [2, Theorem 27, Remark 28]. Wrapped Cauchy kernels and circular

Clock fragility and a quantitative repair
The set of time vectors satisfying (5) has full Lebesgue measure and is dense: its complement is
a countable union of integer hyperplanes.

Veriﬁcation, interpretation, and open questions
The companion code supplies an exact rational certiﬁcate for Corollary 6.2 and the randomized-
clock example, checks the Fourier moments and positivity of the explicit qutrit grid density,
and tests the phase estimator and spectral surrogate against direct unitary evolution in several
dimensions. A ﬁnite linear-programming example illustrates interpolation to ﬂoating-point pre-
cision at binary approximations of two intended rationally independent times. That numerical
interpolation is an illustration; Theorem 3.3 supplies its existence proof.
No proof-assistant
formalization or laboratory validation is claimed.
The results are stronger than simply observing ordinary frequency aliasing. They concern
one positive joint energy law matching many transition channels simultaneously, and identify
an arithmetic contrast between entire classes of experimental menus. At the same time, the
proof ingredients are elementary and the scope is speciﬁc. The null assumes a fresh single-use
channel on each shot.
Multi-time interventions, echo controls within a single trajectory, or
measurements of the hidden Hamiltonian may provide information unavailable in this model.
Likewise, failure of a static explanation can be caused by classical time-dependent noise; it does
not certify nonclassical environmental correlations.
Several quantitative problems remain. The coarse-grid positivity criterion is only suﬃcient.
Beyond the inverse-error lower bound, the smallest bandwidth of a ﬁnite-menu alias near a
harmonic menu is not determined. Neither the 20-time design nor its shot bound is claimed
optimal.
Quantitative Diophantine approximation may bound alias energies, while sharper
trigonometric majorants and statistical estimators may reduce certiﬁcate cost. These are open
extensions of the stated theorems, not results silently assumed in them.
