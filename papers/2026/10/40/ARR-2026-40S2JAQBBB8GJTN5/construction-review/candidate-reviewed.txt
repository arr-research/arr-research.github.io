Proposed result: noncomputability of numerical approximation of F_infty(A), even for real rational doubly stochastic tuples with scalar coefficient Gram matrix and known covariance-optimized capacity C(A)=1/s. Consequence: no total recursive uniform bound on approximate witness dimension. The source of hardness is inherited from Taller–Vidick, not a new proof of MIP*=RE or non-hyperlinear groups.

NOTATION
Normalized Hilbert–Schmidt norm ||X||_2^2=Tr(X*X)/m. Uniform list of N nonempty LCS contexts, each k_i in {1,2,3}, distinct variable names within a context, parity sigma_i. Remove unused variables. Let R=7N. For each row list k_i square words x_j x_j=I, one product word in a fixed order =sigma_i I, and one positive commutator x_j x_k x_j x_k=I for each pair. Pad its list to seven words using tautological EMPTY words =I. For unitary variables define xi as the mean squared residual over all R words. Repeated relations in different rows remain separate.

LEMMA 1: SYNCHRONOUS STRATEGY TO WORDS
A finite synchronous strategy winning 1-e has commuting involutions A_j^i per row, product sigma_i I, and shared variable involutions X_j. Define d_ij=||A_j^i-X_j||_2^2. Strategy failure e=(1/N) sum_i (1/(4 k_i)) sum_j d_ij, hence sum_ij d_ij <=12Ne.
Squares have zero error. A row's product error squared <=3 sum_j d_ij by unitary telescoping. Each pair's positive commutator error squared <=8(d_ij+d_ik), since four positions contain each twice and Cauchy–Schwarz. Sum of at most three pairs <=16 sum_j d_ij. Thus total residual squared <=19 sum_ij d_ij <=228Ne. Therefore xi <=(228/7)e <34e, with dimension unchanged. Empty words have zero error.

LEMMA 2: WORDS TO A SYNCHRONOUS STRATEGY
Given arbitrary unitary V_j, spectrally round to closest involution X_j (choose sign of real part, resolve ties +1). Then d_j^2:=||V_j-X_j||_2^2 <=||V_j^2-I||_2^2.
For each row the new product residual squared <=4(e_product^2 + sum_{j in row}d_j^2), including k=1,2. Each new positive commutator residual squared <=3 e_comm^2 +12 d_j^2+12 d_k^2. Thus new product plus all new pair-commutator residuals squared <=28 times the sum of the original seven squared residuals for that row.
For k=3, call the rounded variables X,Y,Z. Pinch T=(Y+XYX)/2, let Y'=sign(T), so Y' is an involution commuting with X. The off-diagonal error has square ||[X,Y]||_2^2/4. Since Y^2=I, sign-rounding square error <= the same off-diagonal square. Hence ||Y-Y'||_2^2 <=||[X,Y]||_2^2/2. Alice uses X,Y',sigma XY', commuting involutions of required parity. Total squared discrepancy to X,Y,Z <=2||XYZ-sigma I||_2^2 +3||Y-Y'||_2^2 <=2 times (new product and all new commutator squared errors). With maximally entangled state, transpose Alice's joint PVM; Bob uses X_j. Row failure <= new residual sum /6.
For k=2 Alice uses X,sigma X, giving row failure ||XY-sigma I||_2^2/8. For k=1 Alice outputs sigma, giving failure ||X-sigma I||_2^2/4. Every case is bounded by one quarter of the new residual sum (k=3 uses 1/6). Therefore total failure <=7 times the mean original row sum =49 xi <=50 xi. All measurements can be placed on the union of context and variable question sets and extended by the matching transposes, hence form a synchronous strategy in the source's convention. Check this identification explicitly.

AVERAGED DETERMINANT COMPILER
Use a=3/5,b=4/5,c=12/25 and H(U,V,W,Z)=[[aU,bW],[bZ,-aV]]. For unitary labels, H H*= [[I,cR],[cR*,I]], R=UZ*-WV*. Thus squared singular values lie in [1/25,49/25], op norm <=7/5, and -log|det H|^(1/m) <=(72/49)||R||_2^2. The frozen manuscript proves this local bound.
For EACH of R original words add a separate occurrence of t^2=I, using the SAME padding label t, then append tt to the original word. Use fresh prefix labels for lengths 2,...,k-1 of each padded word; k<=6. Every original word needs at most five gates; its assigned t^2 occurrence needs one more. Empty words padded tt need one gate. Include reference z; gate lower-left label z, final output sigma z, intermediate outputs fresh. No input is z or its own output, including empty original words. Include 25 copies of every gate and one singleton of every label. Let q_l be initial coefficient squared Frobenius weight (an integer), M=max q_l, s number of labels.
KEY CHANGE: append 2M-q_l singleton copies, rather than M-q_l. Then every label's total weight is 2M, D=2sM, and each label occurs in at least M+1 singleton blocks. Entries remain in {0,+/-1,+/-3/5,+/-4/5}. Both stochastic identities and Gram identity tau_D(A_l*A_h)=delta_lh/s hold. Set rho=R/D>0. This is explicitly computable rational and inverse-polynomial in the expanded construction length. There are G<=6R gates, O(R+n) labels, M=O(R+n), D polynomial. Each original word has its own padding-square gate; no heavily reused gate error can spoil averaged estimates.
The capacity proof from the frozen manuscript applies verbatim: C(A)=1/s and F_m<=1/s, where F_m=max_{sum tau(Y_l*Y_l)=1}|det sum A_l tensor Y_l|^{2/(Dm)}.

FORWARD AVERAGED BOUND
Given unitary original variables of mean error xi, set t=z=I and exact prefixes. Only the R original final gates may be inexact. With labels normalized by sqrt(s),
  -log(s*objective) <= (50/D)*(72/49)*sum_r e_r^2 = (3600/49)rho xi.
Thus s F_m >= exp[-(3600/49)rho xi]. This lower bound does not need perfect completeness.

REVERSE AVERAGED BOUND
If sF_m>=1-delta, choose a maximizing tuple and set X=sqrt(s)L(Y), normalized trace X*X=1. Its block polar P has Delta^2=||X-P||_2^2<=2delta. Polar-round individual labels Z_l=sqrt(s)Y_l to V_l. Singleton multiplicities >=M imply (1/s)sum_l||Z_l-V_l||_2^2 <=2Delta^2. Thus B=L(V) obeys ||B-X||_2<=sqrt(2)Delta and ||B-P||_2<=(1+sqrt(2))Delta. All labels unitary imply opnorm B<=7/5. Consequently
  ||BB*-I||_2^2 <= C0 Delta^2,
  C0=(144/25)(3+2sqrt(2)).
Twenty-five copies of each gate give (50c^2/D)sum_g e_g^2<=C0 Delta^2. Gauge labels by V_z*. A padded word telescopes over at most five gates; removing tt adds its OWN padding-square gate. Cauchy–Schwarz gives original residual squared <=6 times the sum of those assigned gate squared errors. Assignments of gates to original words are disjoint. Therefore
  xi <=(6/R)sum_g e_g^2 <=[12C0/(50c^2)]delta/rho
      =(18+12sqrt(2))delta/rho <35delta/rho.
Lemma 2 then yields a synchronous game strategy winning at least 1-1750 delta/rho.

CANDIDATE HARDNESS CONSEQUENCE
Take source synchronous soundness s0=71/72 and beta=(1-s0)/3500=1/252000. Fix rational epsilon=2^-40 (or any smaller source-permitted value); (3600/49)*34*epsilon <=beta/4. If a source YES value is >=1-epsilon but supremum not attained, take e approaching epsilon from above; supremum determinant still satisfies
  sF_infty >=exp[-(3600/49)rho*34epsilon]>=1-beta rho/4.
If source NO has synchronous value <=71/72, then sF_infty<=1-beta rho: a value strictly larger produces a finite witness with delta<beta rho, and recovered strategy wins >1-1750 beta=(1+s0)/2>s0, contradiction. Finite-level equality cases are irrelevant to this supremum argument.
So there is an input-computable additive gap of at least 3 beta rho/(4s) between YES and NO. Approximating F_infty with error beta rho/(8s), then comparing to an intermediate rational threshold, would decide Halt. C(A)=1/s is nevertheless explicit. This is NOT a fixed additive gap independent of the input, NOT perfect completeness, and NOT polynomial-time hardness in the succinct source input.
If a total recursive bound b(L,k) guaranteed an m<=b with F_m>=F_infty-2^-k on every input of length L, evaluate finitely many fixed-m maxima to sufficient rational accuracy and take their max. Fixed m is a compact semialgebraic optimization: real/imaginary entries, quadratic norm constraint, squared determinant polynomial; roots are monotone and algebraic. This would compute F_infty, contradiction. The same argument handles relative error since F_infty<=1/s<=1.
