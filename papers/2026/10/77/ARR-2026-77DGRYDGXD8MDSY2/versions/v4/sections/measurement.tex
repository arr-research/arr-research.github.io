\section{A binary measurement retaining the leading output information}\label{sec:binary}
An output measurement makes the previous separation a specified statistical experiment. Its denominator remains the \emph{quantum} input relative entropy. It does not define a new classical channel contraction coefficient or certify efficient implementation.

\begin{theorem}[Explicit joint two-outcome measurement]\label{thm:binary}
Use the fixed $t>0$, $\beta>1$ family of Theorem~\ref{thm:dimension-cost}, and write $x=t/d$, $a=d-1$, $q=a+x$, $\delta=d^{-\beta}$ and $\theta=1-\delta$. In its active plane let
\[
 |u\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt a}\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}|j,j\rangle,
 \qquad |k_d\rangle=
 \frac{\sqrt{ax}|u\rangle+|0,0\rangle}{\sqrt{1+ax}},
 \qquad \Pi_d^0=|k_d\rangle\langle k_d|.
\]
Extend this rank-one effect by zero outside the plane. The ambient projective measurement $\{\Pi_d^0,I-\Pi_d^0\}$ has rare-event probabilities
\begin{align}
 q_d&=\frac{\delta x}{q(1+ax)},&
 p_d&=q_d+\frac{4\theta ax}{(d^2-1)q(1+ax)}\label{eq:binary-probs}
\end{align}
under the output base $\N_{\lambda_d}(s_\theta)$ and output phase state $\N_{\lambda_d}(\rho_\pi)$, respectively. Set
\[
 D_d^{\rm bin}=p_d\log\frac{p_d}{q_d}
 +(1-p_d)\log\frac{1-p_d}{1-q_d}.
\]
Then
\[
 \frac{D_d^{\rm bin}}{D_{\mathrm U}(\N_{\lambda_d}(\rho_\pi)
                      \Vert\N_{\lambda_d}(s_\theta))}\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad \frac{dD_d^{\rm bin}}{I_d}\longrightarrow
       \frac{\beta-1}{(1+t)(\beta+3)}.
\]
The finite binary divergence is strictly smaller than the quantum output divergence. Its ratio to the quantum input divergence nevertheless eventually exceeds $1/(d^2-1)$, the common-Bell-class upper even when arbitrary output measurements are allowed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At $\theta=1$, the output block is proportional to
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&-\sqrt{ax}\\-\sqrt{ax}&ax\end{pmatrix},
\]
so $|k_d\rangle$ is its kernel vector. This limiting matrix is used only to choose a measurement: the actual states have $0<\theta<1$ and remain faithful. Taking the trace with the faithful block $W_{\lambda_d\theta}$ gives $q_d$ in~\eqref{eq:binary-probs}; flipping its coherence gives $p_d-q_d$. These are probabilities in the full ambient states, with the complementary outcomes $1-q_d,1-p_d$, including all inactive modes. Positivity and normalization therefore give $0<q_d<p_d<1$.

For the fixed parameters,
\[
 q_d\sim\frac{t}{1+t}d^{-(\beta+2)},\qquad
 p_d\sim\frac{4t}{1+t}d^{-3},\qquad
 \log(p_d/q_d)\sim(\beta-1)\log d.
\]
The complementary term is $q_d-p_d+O(p_d^2+q_d^2)=O(p_d)$ and is smaller than $p_d\log d$. Hence
\[
 D_d^{\rm bin}\sim\frac{4t(\beta-1)}{1+t}\frac{\log d}{d^3}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:dimension-cost} gives the same leading output constant and the input constant $4t(\beta+3)$, proving both limits.

Let $Q$ be the plane projector. Refining the measurement to $\{\Pi_d^0,Q-\Pi_d^0,I-Q\}$ yields distributions $(p_d,T-p_d,1-T)$ and $(q_d,T-q_d,1-T)$ with the same plane mass $T<1$. Merging the latter two outcomes loses divergence strictly by log-sum, since $p_d\ne q_d$ and $1-T>0$. Data processing bounds the refined divergence by the quantum divergence, proving strictness for the binary measurement. For any common-Bell pair, data processing and Proposition~\ref{prop:allbell-endpoint} bound \emph{every} measured output by its quantum input divided by $d^2-1$. The displayed positive limit, compared with this $d^{-2}$ benchmark, proves the eventual separation.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Spectral form, simplification and prior work.}
The original proposal used the exact smaller-eigenvalue projector of the output block. For its eigenvalues $h>l>0$, off-diagonal $b$, and plane projector $Q$, this is
\[
 \Pi_d^- =\frac{hQ-Q\N_{\lambda_d}(s_\theta)Q}{h-l},
 \quad q_d^-=l,\quad p_d^-=l+\frac{4b^2}{h-l}.
\]
It has the same leading limits. Audit1077 reconstructed that formula, then derived the simpler kernel-at-$\theta=1$ projector $\Pi_d^0$ and its exact probabilities as an additional result. The latter avoids eigenvalue diagonalization and depends on $d,x$, not on $\beta$. This provenance is distinct from the original1070/1073 inputs. No finite or global optimality of either measurement is claimed, and exact diagonalization is not necessary for the leading limit.

Measured relative entropy and data processing are prior. Berta--Fawzi--Tomamichel~\cite{BFT} establish the projective/POVM comparison and the strict gap from Umegaki for faithful noncommuting pairs. Our limit does not assert finite equality. Quantum Stein results and regularized measured-entropy achievability~\cite{OH} instead concern fixed states and a growing number of copies. Here the single-copy pair itself changes with dimension. Neither those general theories nor binary hypothesis testing are claimed as discoveries; the contribution is the explicit leading-constant realization for this family.

\paragraph{Falsifiers and costs.}
The support convention matters: replacing $Q$ by the full identity in $\Pi_d^-$ gives an effect exceeding one outside the plane. Renormalizing the subnormalized block removes the cost of postselection and changes the experiment. Measuring also the input changes the denominator: for $\Pi_d^-$ its classical input divergence is asymptotic to $8t/d^2$, rather than the quantum $4t(\beta+3)\log(d)/d^2$. The affine relation between these particular measured probabilities does not define a stochastic binary channel on all probabilities: its extrapolated transition can be negative. No classical SDPI is contradicted.

Fixed parameters and orientation remain essential. For the original spectral measurement, the inadmissible substitution $t_d=1/d$, $\beta=2$ gives measured/quantum output ratio tending to $1/2$, not one; reversing its Bernoulli orientation instead loses the logarithmic leading term. Replacing its eigenvector by the constant limiting vector $(\sqrt t,1)/\sqrt{1+t}$ gives limiting fraction $\min(\beta-1,2)/(\beta-1)$, losing information when $\beta>3$. This does not apply to the finite-$d$ kernel vector in the theorem.

Both measurements are joint on $A:R$, parameter dependent and generally entangled effects. Rare probabilities scale as $d^{-3}$ and $d^{-(\beta+2)}$; the information vanishes. For the exact spectral projector, a within-plane angular error $\epsilon$ changes $q_d^-$ by $(h-l)\sin^2\epsilon$; $\epsilon=o(d^{-(\beta-1)/2})$ is sufficient for a negligible relative error in that probability. This is not a universal apparatus tolerance, and leakage to inactive modes has another cost. No local/LOCC implementation, efficient preparation, precision fixed independently of dimension, optimal sample complexity, noncircular entanglement witness, fixed-noise advantage, capacity or MI conclusion is proved. Costs polynomial in $d$ may be exponential in a qubit encoding.

The full1077 report retains all adverse cases, controls, source scopes and failures; no priority or universal finite threshold is inferred.
