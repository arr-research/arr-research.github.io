+\section{A binary measurement retaining the leading output information}\label{sec:binary}
+An output measurement makes the previous separation a specified statistical experiment. Its denominator remains the \emph{quantum} input relative entropy. It does not define a new classical channel contraction coefficient or certify efficient implementation.
+
+\begin{theorem}[Explicit joint two-outcome measurement]\label{thm:binary}
+Use the fixed $t>0$, $\beta>1$ family of Theorem~\ref{thm:dimension-cost}, and write $x=t/d$, $a=d-1$, $q=a+x$, $\delta=d^{-\beta}$ and $\theta=1-\delta$. In its active plane let
+\[
+ |u\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt a}\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}|j,j\rangle,
+ \qquad |k_d\rangle=
+ \frac{\sqrt{ax}|u\rangle+|0,0\rangle}{\sqrt{1+ax}},
+ \qquad \Pi_d^0=|k_d\rangle\langle k_d|.
+\]
+Extend this rank-one effect by zero outside the plane. The ambient projective measurement $\{\Pi_d^0,I-\Pi_d^0\}$ has rare-event probabilities
+\begin{align}
+ q_d&=\frac{\delta x}{q(1+ax)},&
+ p_d&=q_d+\frac{4\theta ax}{(d^2-1)q(1+ax)}\label{eq:binary-probs}
+\end{align}
+under the output base $\N_{\lambda_d}(s_\theta)$ and output phase state $\N_{\lambda_d}(\rho_\pi)$, respectively. Set
+\[
+ D_d^{\rm bin}=p_d\log\frac{p_d}{q_d}
+ +(1-p_d)\log\frac{1-p_d}{1-q_d}.
+\]
+Then
+\[
+ \frac{D_d^{\rm bin}}{D_{\mathrm U}(\N_{\lambda_d}(\rho_\pi)
+                      \Vert\N_{\lambda_d}(s_\theta))}\longrightarrow1,
+ \qquad \frac{dD_d^{\rm bin}}{I_d}\longrightarrow
+       \frac{\beta-1}{(1+t)(\beta+3)}.
+\]
+The finite binary divergence is strictly smaller than the quantum output divergence. Its ratio to the quantum input divergence nevertheless eventually exceeds $1/(d^2-1)$, the common-Bell-class upper even when arbitrary output measurements are allowed.
+\end{theorem}
+\begin{proof}
+At $\theta=1$, the output block is proportional to
+\[
+ \begin{pmatrix}1&-\sqrt{ax}\\-\sqrt{ax}&ax\end{pmatrix},
+\]
+so $|k_d\rangle$ is its kernel vector. This limiting matrix is used only to choose a measurement: the actual states have $0<\theta<1$ and remain faithful. Taking the trace with the faithful block $W_{\lambda_d\theta}$ gives $q_d$ in~\eqref{eq:binary-probs}; flipping its coherence gives $p_d-q_d$. These are probabilities in the full ambient states, with the complementary outcomes $1-q_d,1-p_d$, including all inactive modes. Positivity and normalization therefore give $0<q_d<p_d<1$.
+
+For the fixed parameters,
+\[
+ q_d\sim\frac{t}{1+t}d^{-(\beta+2)},\qquad
+ p_d\sim\frac{4t}{1+t}d^{-3},\qquad
+ \log(p_d/q_d)\sim(\beta-1)\log d.
+\]
+The complementary term is $q_d-p_d+O(p_d^2+q_d^2)=O(p_d)$ and is smaller than $p_d\log d$. Hence
+\[
+ D_d^{\rm bin}\sim\frac{4t(\beta-1)}{1+t}\frac{\log d}{d^3}.
+\]
+Theorem~\ref{thm:dimension-cost} gives the same leading output constant and the input constant $4t(\beta+3)$, proving both limits.
+
+Let $Q$ be the plane projector. Refining the measurement to $\{\Pi_d^0,Q-\Pi_d^0,I-Q\}$ yields distributions $(p_d,T-p_d,1-T)$ and $(q_d,T-q_d,1-T)$ with the same plane mass $T<1$. Merging the latter two outcomes loses divergence strictly by log-sum, since $p_d\ne q_d$ and $1-T>0$. Data processing bounds the refined divergence by the quantum divergence, proving strictness for the binary measurement. For any common-Bell pair, data processing and Proposition~\ref{prop:allbell-endpoint} bound \emph{every} measured output by its quantum input divided by $d^2-1$. The displayed positive limit, compared with this $d^{-2}$ benchmark, proves the eventual separation.
+\end{proof}

+\section{A fixed local Hamiltonian and finite phase}\label{sec:phase}
+The nonunitary ladder tangent used above has a genuine obstruction to a Hamiltonian interpretation. The following different family removes that particular restriction while paying a larger order budget. Its base state and Schmidt-support decomposition have direct antecedents in Frey--Collins--Gerlach~\cite{FCG}; phase estimation with noisy probes and inactive ancillas, and the SLD calculation of a coherence block, are prior~\cite{CB}. We claim neither a new preparation family nor optimal absolute metrological information.
+
+Put $a=d-1$, $q=a+e^2$, $e>0$, and on $A_dR_d$ set
+\[
+ v=\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}|j,j\rangle+e|0,0\rangle,\quad
+ s=vv^*/q,\quad r=\Tr_A s=\operatorname{diag}(e^2,1,\ldots,1)/q,
+ \quad B=I_d/d\otimes r.
+\]
+Here $s$ is a pure preparation state, whereas the states used below are faithful mixtures. Define $s_p=(1-p)B+ps$ and the fixed local Hamiltonian
+\[
+ H=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,\ldots,1),\quad
+ U_\phi=e^{-i\phi H}\otimes I_R,\quad
+ \rho_\phi=U_\phi s_\theta U_\phi^*,\qquad 0<\theta<1.
+\]
+Both the norm and the spectral diameter of $H$ are one, independently of $e,\theta$ and the channel parameter.
+
+\begin{theorem}[Finite-phase realization]\label{thm:phase}
+For every $d\ge2$ and physical depolarizing parameter $-1/(d^2-1)\le\lambda\le1$, the pair $(\rho_\phi,s_\theta)$ is faithful with common reference marginal $r$, and each state's exact actual-marginal order is
+\[
+ K_\theta=1+(d^2-1)\theta.
+\]
+Let $W_p$ be the subnormalized two-dimensional block
+\[
+ W_p=\frac1{dq}\begin{pmatrix}
+ A_p&dp e\sqrt a\\dp e\sqrt a&F_pe^2
+ \end{pmatrix},\qquad A_p=1+(da-1)p,\quad F_p=1+ap,
+\]
+and let $\ell(W)=(\log w_+-\log w_-)/(w_+-w_-)$ for its positive eigenvalues, with its continuous extension when they coincide. For $\phi\notin2\pi\mathbb Z$, write $c=\theta e\sqrt a/q$. Then
+\begin{align}
+ D_{\mathrm U}(\rho_\phi\Vert s_\theta)
+   &=2c^2\ell(W_\theta)(1-\cos\phi),\label{eq:phase-entropy}\\
+ D_{\triangle}(\rho_\phi\Vert s_\theta)
+   &=\frac{4c^2(1-\cos\phi)}{\Tr W_\theta}.\label{eq:phase-triangle}
+\end{align}
+Consequently the finite Umegaki retention is exactly
+\[
+ \lambda^2\frac{\ell(W_{\lambda\theta})}{\ell(W_\theta)},
+\]
+equal to the BKM retention of the physical phase tangent. Triangular retention equals the SLD retention
+\[
+ \lambda^2\frac{\Tr W_\theta}{\Tr W_{\lambda\theta}}.
+\]
+For each fixed allowed $\lambda$, first letting $e\downarrow0$ at fixed $\theta$, and then $\theta\uparrow1$, makes these retentions approach $C_{P_d}(\lambda)$, $P_d=d(d-1)$. In particular, the phase may remain fixed at $\pi$ throughout. This establishes a realization of a lower bound, not a matching upper or an optimum over Hamiltonians.
+\end{theorem}
+\begin{proof}
+The sole nonzero eigenvalue of $B^{-1/2}sB^{-1/2}$ is $v^*B^{-1}v/q=d^2$. Thus $s_p$ is faithful for $-1/(d^2-1)<p<1$, and whitening gives the asserted exact order. Since $U_\phi$ commutes with $B$, it preserves the order and marginal; local depolarization takes $s_p$ to $s_{\lambda p}$ and commutes with the conjugation. All these states remain faithful at the negative channel endpoint because $\theta<1$.
+
+The uniform principal Schmidt vector and $|0,0\rangle$ span $W_p$. Its determinant is
+\[
+ \det W_p=\frac{e^2(1-p)[1+(d^2-1)p]}{d^2q^2}>0.
+\]
+The remaining $d-2$ Schmidt modes and $d^2-d$ off-diagonal-coordinate modes are phase independent. The physical tangent on the active block is $(\theta e/q)T$, with
+\[
+ T=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\sqrt a\\i\sqrt a&0\end{pmatrix}.
+\]
+For real symmetric $W$, $WT+TW=(\Tr W)T$. The Sylvester formula therefore gives phase SLD information $4c^2/\Tr W$. A real orthogonal diagonalization leaves $T$ unchanged up to sign and gives BKM information $2c^2\ell(W)$.
+
+Unitary invariance cancels the entropy term in $D_{\mathrm U}(U_\phi s_\theta U_\phi^*\Vert s_\theta)$. Functional calculus gives $(\log W)_{01}=\ell(W)W_{01}$, so the two changed coherences yield~\eqref{eq:phase-entropy}. The two-block spectral overlap formula for $f_\triangle$ gives $\Tr(\rho_\phi-s_\theta)^2/\Tr W_\theta$, which is~\eqref{eq:phase-triangle}. Other modes contribute zero. Blocks are never normalized separately. Substitution of $\lambda\theta$ proves the retentions.
+
+At fixed faithful $p$, $w_+=A_p/(da)+O(e^2)$ and $w_-=k_pe^2+O(e^4)$ with $k_p>0$. Hence $\ell(W_p)=(2da/A_p)\log(1/e)+O(1)$ and $\Tr W_p\to A_p/(da)$. Both retentions tend to $\lambda^2A_\theta/A_{\lambda\theta}=C_{1+(P_d-1)\theta}(\lambda)$. The final ordered limit gives the claim; $\lambda=0,1$ also follow directly.
+\end{proof}

+\paragraph{The resource tradeoff and nonuniform limits.}
+This realization uses reference dimension $d$ instead of $2d-1$, but order tending to $d^2$ rather than $M_d$. At a common budget $1<\kappa\le d^2$, its limiting lower is only $C_{1+(P_d-1)(\kappa-1)/(d^2-1)}$. It does not improve the earlier ladder's budget profile. At $\phi=\pi$, the trace distance is $2|c|=O(e)$, SLD information is $O(e^2)$ and Umegaki/BKM information is $O(e^2\log(1/e))$. A finite angle therefore does not keep the states a fixed distance apart. The order of limits is essential: for $0<\lambda<1$ and fixed $e>0$, sending $\theta\uparrow1$ first gives Umegaki retention zero, not $C_{P_d}$. This family does not settle unrestricted qubit BKM or actual-MI contraction.

+\begin{proposition}[The entire common-Bell class, throughout the noise range]\label{prop:allbell-endpoint}
+For every $-1/(d^2-1)\le\lambda\le1$, the global contraction coefficient of each normalized nonaffine operator-convex Petz divergence, restricted to pairs diagonal in one common orthonormal maximally entangled basis of $A_dR_d$, is exactly
+\[
+ b_{d^2}(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda^2}{\lambda+2(1-\lambda)/d^2}
+                =C_{d^2/2}(\lambda).
+\]
+The same value holds on the union of these classes over the common basis. For $d\ge3$ and each fixed physical $\lambda\ne0,1$, the phase lower $C_{P_d}(\lambda)$ is strictly larger; hence finite faithful phase pairs separating from this whole class exist. At the negative endpoint the ratio of the lower to this exact benchmark is $d-1$. This states a supremum and finite existence, not one finite parameter choice uniform in $\lambda$ or saturation by one finite pair for every generator.
+\end{proposition}
+\begin{proof}
+We give an elementary reconstruction of a classical benchmark, without claiming classical novelty. For the symmetric channel $Kr=\lambda r+c\mathbf1$ on $n\ge2$ symbols, put $c=(1-\lambda)/n$, $h=\lambda+2c>0$. Its stochastic range is $-1/(n-1)\le\lambda\le1$. For positive probability $r$ and real $z$ with $\sum z_i=0$, the Fisher upper is
+\[
+ \lambda^2\sum_i\frac{z_i^2}{\lambda r_i+c}
+ \le\frac{\lambda^2}{h}\sum_i\frac{z_i^2}{r_i}.
+\]
+For $\lambda=0,1$ this is direct. Otherwise rearrangement reduces it to nonnegativity of
+\[
+ \sum_i\frac{(1-2r_i)z_i^2}{r_i(\lambda r_i+c)}.
+\]
+If all $r_i\le1/2$, every term is nonnegative. Otherwise $r_1>1/2$ is unique. Set $S=1-r_1$ and $\varphi(x)=x(\lambda x+c)/(1-2x)$. Since $(\varphi(x)/x)'=h/(1-2x)^2>0$, $\sum_{i>1}\varphi(r_i)\le\varphi(S)$. Cauchy and $\sum z_i=0$ bound the positive terms below by $z_1^2/\varphi(S)$. This cancels the sole negative term because
+\[
+ r_1(\lambda r_1+c)-S(\lambda S+c)
+ =(2r_1-1)(\lambda+c)\ge0.
+\]
+For the resolvent generator $(x-1)^2/(x+u)$, put $z=\rho-\sigma$ and $r=(\rho+u\sigma)/(1+u)$. The divergence is $(1+u)^{-1}\sum z_i^2/r_i$; the Fisher upper applies after $K$ with the same factor. Include $u=0$, the separate quadratic endpoint and the positive representation~\eqref{eq:representation}; affine terms vanish. This gives the upper for every stated $f$. Common uniform regularization of both distributions extends the inequality to the standard boundary convention when the input divergence is finite and positive. The lower follows from two equal masses $a<1/2$, tangent $(1,-1,0,\ldots,0)$, first a faithful local perturbation, then $a\uparrow1/2$: the ratio is $\lambda^2a/(\lambda a+c)$ before the last limit. For $n=2$ use the uniform base directly. Each $f''(1)>0$ supplies a cancelling common Fisher factor. At the binary negative endpoint $K$ is a permutation and the formula is one; no endpoint denominator vanishes.
+
+For $n=d^2$, a common maximally entangled basis has shared marginal $I/d$, and local depolarization transforms its probabilities by this $K$. Petz divergences reduce to the classical expressions. Finally $\partial_K C_K=\lambda^2(1-\lambda)/[1+\lambda(K-1)]^2>0$ away from $\lambda=0,1$, and $P_d>d^2/2$ for $d\ge3$. Theorem~\ref{thm:phase} then gives finite existence for each fixed noise parameter. At the negative endpoint $C_{P_d}=1/(d+1)$ and $b_{d^2}=1/(d^2-1)$.
+\end{proof}

+The equivalence of global coefficients for nonlinear operator-convex generators is explicitly prior in Makur--Zheng~\cite[Proposition~6]{MZ}; Dobrushin theory~\cite{PW} already gives the negative-endpoint upper directly. Lee--Makur~\cite{LM} is a particularly close symmetric-channel antecedent: its author-provided conference abstract announces closed KL formulas for product symmetric channels. We have not obtained its formula, complete hypotheses or proof, and therefore do not assert precise coverage of the negative range or novelty of the formula. The reconstruction above supplies a benchmark for the quantum comparison, not a claimed classical discovery. A variable reference distribution in this global classical coefficient must not be replaced by a fixed uniform source.

+\section{Dimension-scaled costs of the finite-phase separation}\label{sec:dimension-cost}
+The exact phase identity permits an explicit succession of finite pairs, rather than unspecified successive perturbation limits at each fixed dimension. This is a quantitative refinement of the preceding family, not a new preparation scheme or an optimal resource frontier. The noise parameter in this section changes with dimension.
+
+\begin{theorem}[An achievable information--conditioning tradeoff]\label{thm:dimension-cost}
+Fix $t>0$ and $\beta>1$ independently of the integer $d\ge2$, and put $\nu=\beta+3>4$. In Theorem~\ref{thm:phase} choose
+\[
+ e^2=t/d,\quad \theta=1-d^{-\beta},\quad \phi=\pi,
+ \quad\lambda_d=-1/(d^2-1).
+\]
+The faithful input pair has reference dimension $d$, the stated norm-one local Hamiltonian, and exact actual-marginal order
+\[
+ K_d=d^2-d^{2-\beta}+d^{-\beta}.
+\]
+Write $I_d=D_{\mathrm U}(\rho_\pi\Vert s_\theta)$, let $R_d$ be its output/input relative-entropy retention, and let $\operatorname{cond}(s_\theta)$ be the ratio of largest to smallest eigenvalue. Then
+\begin{align}
+ dR_d&\longrightarrow\alpha=\frac{\nu-4}{(1+t)\nu},\label{eq:scaled-retention}\\
+ \frac{d^2I_d}{\log d}&\longrightarrow4t\nu,\qquad
+ \frac{\operatorname{cond}(s_\theta)}{d^\nu}\longrightarrow\frac1t,\label{eq:scaled-cost}\\
+ \frac d2\|\rho_\pi-s_\theta\|_1&\longrightarrow2\sqrt t.
+\end{align}
+In particular $R_d/b_{d^2}(\lambda_d)\sim\alpha d$, although both $R_d$ and the absolute input/output information tend to zero. The information of the output is asymptotic to $4t(\nu-4)\log(d)/[(1+t)d^3]$.
+\end{theorem}
+\begin{proof}
+Set $x=e^2$, $\delta=1-\theta$, $q=d-1+x$ and $\Xi=d(d-1)-1+(d-1)x$. Directly from the subnormalized $W_p$,
+\begin{align*}
+ T_{\rm in}&=1-\frac{\delta\Xi}{dq},&
+ \Delta_{\rm in}&=\frac{x\delta[d^2-(d^2-1)\delta]}{d^2q^2},\\
+ T_{\rm out}&=\frac{d[1+(d-1)x]+\delta\Xi}{(d^2-1)dq},&
+ \Delta_{\rm out}&=\frac{x\delta(d^2-\delta)}{(d^2-1)d^2q^2},
+\end{align*}
+where $T$ denotes trace and $\Delta$ determinant, not divergence. Substituting $x=t/d$, $\delta=d^{-\beta}$ gives
+\[
+ T_{\rm in}\to1,\quad d^\nu\Delta_{\rm in}\to t,
+ \quad d^3T_{\rm out}\to1+t,\quad d^{\nu+2}\Delta_{\rm out}\to t.
+\]
+These limits also hold for noninteger fixed $\beta>1$: use $u=1/d$ and $y=d\delta$, and evaluate the corresponding rational functions jointly at $(u,y)=(0,0)$. Their denominators are nonzero there for fixed $t>0$.
+
+For a positive two-dimensional matrix with trace $T$ and determinant $\Delta$, if $\Delta/T^2\to0$ then $w_+\sim T$ and $w_-\sim\Delta/T$. Therefore
+\[
+ \begin{array}{c|cc}
+ &w_+&w_-\\\hline
+ \mathrm{input}&1+o(1)&t d^{-\nu}(1+o(1))\\
+ \mathrm{output}&(1+t)d^{-3}(1+o(1))&
+       \dfrac{t}{1+t}d^{-(\nu-1)}(1+o(1)).
+ \end{array}
+\]
+It follows that $\ell(W_\theta)/\log d\to\nu$ and
+$\ell(W_{\lambda_d\theta})/(d^3\log d)\to(\nu-4)/(1+t)$. Since $d^4\lambda_d^2\to1$, the exact finite-phase identity proves~\eqref{eq:scaled-retention}. Its physical coherence obeys $d^2c^2\to t$, giving $I_d=4c^2\ell(W_\theta)$ and the information limit. The trace distance is exactly $2|c|$.
+
+The exact minimum eigenvalue of the full input state is
+\[
+ \lambda_{\min}(s_\theta)=\frac{\delta\min(1,x)}{dq}.
+\]
+Indeed $s_\theta\succeq\delta B$, and an off-Schmidt coordinate with a reference label of minimum weight attains equality. Eventually $x=t/d\le1$, so $d^\nu\lambda_{\min}\to t$. The largest eigenvalue tends to one, since it lies between $\theta$ and one. This proves the condition-number limit, including all ambient modes. The order and faithful positivity follow from Theorem~\ref{thm:phase} at each finite dimension, before taking limits.
+\end{proof}
+
+For any fixed $0<\gamma<1$, choose $t>0$ with $\gamma(1+t)<1$ and then
+\[
+ \nu>\frac4{1-\gamma(1+t)},\qquad\beta=\nu-3.
+\]
+This yields $\alpha>\gamma$ with parameters independent of $d$. The conditioning exponent and constants depend on $\gamma$; no fixed exponent works uniformly as $\gamma\uparrow1$, and $\alpha=1$ is not attained with finite parameters. This approximates the scale of an existing lower, not an unrestricted optimal coefficient.

+\paragraph{A different order cost and necessary quantifiers.}
+The alternative explicit choice $e=1/d$, $\theta=1-1/d$ gives $dR_d\to1/10$, $I_d\sim20\log(d)/d^3$, condition number $\sim d^5$ and trace distance $\sim2d^{-3/2}$. Its exact order is $d^2-d+1/d$. The choice $t=1,\beta=2$ above retains the same leading retention and condition exponent with input information larger by a factor asymptotic to $d$, but spends the larger order $d^2-1+d^{-2}$. It is not a fixed-budget improvement.

If $t$ is instead replaced by $t_d=1/d$ with $\beta=2$, the true limit is $dR_d\to1/3$, not the $1/5$ obtained by inserting $t_d$ into the fixed-$t$ formula and then taking $d\to\infty$. The input information is then $24\log(d)/d^3$, not the analogous constant20. Likewise $\beta=1$ is outside this theorem: it changes the output-root regime. No uniform substitution of variable parameters is justified.

+\paragraph{Evidence and operational boundary.}

For the $e=1/d$, $\theta=1-1/d$ sequence, it retains negative gaps versus Bell at $d=2,\ldots,7$; positive checked points beginning at8 do not prove a threshold for every integer $d\ge8$.
