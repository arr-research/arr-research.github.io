Uniform tropical bounds for amplified determinants and multiscale quantum noise

Purpose: test a broader successor to ARR-2026-6FWR12DH3F8QBA5C. No score,
priority claim, or claim of resolving a famous open problem is attached.

SETUP
Let A_1,...,A_s be complex d by d matrices. For z in (0,infinity)^s set
 F_m(z)=max_{sum_i Tr(Y_i*Y_i)/m=1}
             |det(sum_i z_i A_i tensor Y_i)|^{2/(dm)},
 F_infty(z)=sup_{m>=1}F_m(z).
Assume span(A_i) has full noncommutative rank; otherwise all F_m vanish.
Let P be its nc-Newton polytope: take all monomials in the generic block
determinants det(sum_i A_i tensor X_i), group their degrees by i, divide
by block size, and take their convex hull over all block sizes.
Write T_P(z)=max_{u in P} product_i z_i^{2u_i/d}.

EXISTING ALGEBRAIC INPUT, NOT CLAIMED AS NEW
Hirai, Iwamasa, Oki and Soma, arXiv:2310.15502, Proposition 2.6 and
Theorem 3.12: P is an integral polytope, and the support function of P
is attained at every block size m>=d-1. Their convention is maximum
degree in t. Equality for every integer weight implies equality of
the entire normalized Newton polytopes at every such block size.
For d=1 take m=1. Denote M=max(1,d-1).

PROPOSED THEOREM
There exist constants 0<c_A<=C_A, independent of z and m, such that
 c_A T_P(z) <= F_M(z) <= F_infty(z) <= C_A T_P(z)
for every positive z. One may take C_A=lambda_max(G)/d, with
G_ij=Tr(A_i* A_j). Moreover the lower bound is achieved within a single
fixed energy-normalized tuple Y, allowing only independent scalar
phases Y_i -> exp(i phi_i)Y_i to depend on z.
Consequently amplification beyond M improves the objective by at most
the finite factor C_A/c_A, uniformly over all positive reweightings of
this fixed matrix tuple. No coefficient-uniform or efficient numerical
approximation bound is asserted: c_A can be small and its construction
can require many coefficients/vertices.

PROOF OF UPPER BOUND
For any normalized Y, p_Y(z)=det(sum z_i A_i tensor Y_i) has Newton
polytope contained in mP. On the unit torus, changing phases of Y does
not change its energy. The Gram bound and scalar arithmetic-geometric
mean give
 |p_Y(omega)|^{2/(dm)} <= ||sum omega_i A_i tensor Y_i||_F^2/(dm)
 <= lambda_max(G)/d = C_A.
For an arbitrary polynomial p and positive z, the toric maximum-modulus
inequality is
 |p(z)| <= sup_{|omega_i|=1}|p(omega)| max_{alpha in Newt(p)} z^alpha.
Here is an elementary proof, so no new analytic convergence theorem is
being assumed. First let z_i=r^{k_i}, r>1 and k_i integers. The Laurent
polynomial f(w)=p(w^{k_1},...,w^{k_s}) has highest exponent at most
H=max_{alpha in Newt(p)} k.alpha. Then w^{-H} f(w) is analytic and bounded
on the exterior unit disk, including infinity. The maximum-modulus
principle on the Riemann sphere gives |f(r)|<=r^H sup_{|w|=1}|f(w)|.
Possible cancellation of the highest coefficient only improves this
bound. Approximate arbitrary log z by rational vectors, taking r=e^{1/N},
and pass to the limit. Apply the inequality to p_Y and take the power
2/(dm). The resulting bound is uniform in m; then take the supremum.

PROOF OF LOWER BOUND
At size M the normalized generic Newton polytope equals P. For every
vertex v of P, the coefficient of z^{Mv} in
det(sum z_i A_i tensor X_i) is a nonzero polynomial in the entries of X.
Finitely many such polynomials are simultaneously nonzero at a generic
tuple. Fix such a tuple and normalize its positive total energy to one.
Call the coefficients b_v; they remain nonzero. For every z>0, Parseval
on the s-dimensional phase torus gives
 integral |p_Y(z_1 exp(i phi_1),...,z_s exp(i phi_s))|^2 dphi
   = sum_alpha |b_alpha|^2 z^{2alpha}.
The supremum is at least the integral, and hence at least each vertex
term |b_v|^2 z^{2Mv}. Independent phases are allowed normalized tuples.
Thus c_A=min_v |b_v|^{2/(dM)}>0 gives the claimed lower bound.

COROLLARY: ALL RATES, NOT ONLY ONE NOISE RAY
For any real weight vector w, z_i=t^{w_i}, 0<t<1, gives
 F_infty(t^w)=Theta_A(t^{theta(w)}),
 theta(w)=(2/d) min_{u in P} w.u.
The two comparison constants are independent of w and t. The exponent
is continuous, concave, piecewise linear, and rational linear on each
cone (P is integral). The same exponent is already attained at size M.
For rational matrix data and rational weights the exponent can be found
using the existing weighted nc-determinant algorithms. This is a
complexity claim about the exponent, not about F_infty or its prefactor.

CHANNEL MIXTURES
Let E_g be finitely many CPTP maps on M_d, with fixed Kraus lists A_gj,
and full nc-rank for the union. Give each group the common amplitude
z_gj=sqrt(p_g), with p_g>0 and sum p_g=1. Let Q be the projection of P
that adds coordinates within each group. Then the regularized amplified
determinant of E_p=sum p_g E_g is within constants independent of p of
 max_{q in Q} product_g p_g^{q_g/d}.
In particular p_g(t)=t^{beta_g}/sum_h t^{beta_h} yields exponent
 theta_mix(beta)=(1/d)min_{q in Q} beta.q - min_g beta_g.
Every q in Q has sum q_g=d. This formula incorporates the normalization.
It applies to arbitrary finite noise families, rather than assuming a
depolarizing perturbation. It does not identify amplification with a free
ancilla; operational interpretation retains the access model in the
previous universal-testing manuscript.

GENERAL CPTP NORMALIZATION
If R(z)=sum z_i^2 A_i* A_i is positive definite, K_i(z)=z_i A_i R(z)^(-1/2)
is a Kraus representation of a CPTP map. Its regularized amplified
determinant is F_infty(z)/(det R(z))^{1/d}. The same identity holds at
every finite m. This subtraction matters for every proposed noise law.

EXPLICIT SIX-PARAMETER EXAMPLE
Take d=3, A_12=E12-E21, A_23=E23-E32, A_31=E31-E13, and D_j=Ejj.
Use positive amplitudes x,y,z on A_12,A_23,A_31 and a,b,c on D_1,D_2,D_3.
The nc-Newton polytope has exactly these five vertices, in the order
(x,y,z,a,b,c):
 (0,0,0,1,1,1), (2,0,0,0,0,1), (0,2,0,1,0,0),
 (0,0,2,0,1,0), (1,1,1,0,0,0).
Proof of containment: in every block-determinant monomial, row and
column counts imply 2u_a+u_x+u_z=2,
2u_b+u_x+u_y=2, 2u_c+u_y+u_z=2 and nonnegative coordinates. The resulting
polytope is the fractional perfect matching polytope of a triangle with
loops and has the displayed five vertices (enumerate basic feasible
solutions). The first four are already realized at m=1, since
 det(diag(a,b,c)+x A12+y A23+z A31)=abc+c x^2+a y^2+b z^2.
The last is realized at m=2 by choosing three Pauli matrices: the
6 by 6 block determinant is nonzero and has grouped degree (2,2,2).
Hence, up to constants uniform over all six amplitudes,
 F_infty ~ max{(abc)^{2/3}, (c x^2)^{2/3}, (a y^2)^{2/3},
                    (b z^2)^{2/3}, (xyz)^{2/3}}.
The scalar objective has only the first four branches. For CPTP use
R=diag(a^2+x^2+z^2,b^2+x^2+y^2,c^2+y^2+z^2) and divide by det(R)^{1/3}.
This gives a complete multirate phase diagram with a genuinely
noncommutative extra branch, extending the single W3 isotropic example.

NOVELTY AND LIMITS
The nc-Newton polytope, its integrality, stabilization, and algorithms
are prior art. Maximum modulus, Parseval, and generic nonvanishing are
standard. The candidate contribution is their combination into uniform
two-sided analytic bounds, a fixed-size phase-only witness family, and
all-direction normalized noise laws. The theorem does not solve the
open uniform capacity-factor approximation conjecture or finite-GUE
log-determinant convergence. Its significance relative to a score above
5 AIRR must be assessed by a reviewer; no such score is promised.
