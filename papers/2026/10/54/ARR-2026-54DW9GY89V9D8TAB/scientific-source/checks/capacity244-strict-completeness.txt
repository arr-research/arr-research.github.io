ROOT244: STRICT COMPLETENESS AND AN EXPLICIT ONE-SIDED STABILITY BOUND
New companion to240. No grade or priority claim; requires mathematical audit.

Let d=9n, U=X^(d/3), V=Z^(d/3), and K be the real traceless Hermitian
span of U,U*,V,V*. Let Q=I-P_K, and r be an integer with1<=r<2n.
Every nonzero H in K has at least2n strictly positive and2n strictly
negative eigenvalues. Consequently Q distinguishes every rank<=r density
matrix from ALL density matrices, without a failure set. This is the
classical strict-completeness kernel criterion applied to this geometry,
not a new general criterion.

Proof and quantitative version.
Write the joint eigenvalue grid h_ij=f_i+g_j, each repeatedn, with sorted
real zero-mean triples f,g. Put F=f1>=0,G=g1>=0,M=F+G>0.
The second-largest cell value is h2=max(f1+g2,f2+g1). Since zero mean and
ordering imply f2>=-F/2 and g2>=-G/2,
 h2>=max(F-G/2,G-F/2)>=(F+G)/4=M/4>0.
Thus the top two cells each have eigenvalue>=M/4 and dimensionn.
Apply the same argument to-H for at least two strictly negative cells.

Moreover sum f_i^2<=6F^2, since f2 in[-F/2,F] and f3=-F-f2;
the convex quadratic F^2+f2^2+(F+f2)^2 is maximized at an endpoint
and is at most6F^2. Likewise sum g_j^2<=6G^2. Therefore
 ||H||_F^2=3n(sum f_i^2+sum g_j^2)<=18n(F^2+G^2)<=18n M^2.
Both the top2n positive eigenvalues of H and the top2n negative
magnitudes are consequently >=||H||_F/sqrt(288n).

Let D be any traceless Hermitian matrix with n_+(D)<=r OR n_-(D)<=r.
Let H=P_K D. If H=0 the following bound is trivial. If n_+(D)<=r,
the squared Frobenius distance from H to D is at least the sum of
squares of H's positive eigenvalues after the r largest ones, hence
 ||D-H||_F^2 >= (2n-r)/(288n) ||H||_F^2.
This distance statement follows from the Hermitian eigenvalue
Hoffman--Wielandt inequality: after sorting the eigenvalues, D has
at most r positive entries, so every positive eigenvalue of H beyond
rank r contributes at least its square. Alternatively use the spectral
positive-part best-approximation inequality. If n_-(D)<=r, apply to-H,-D.
Pythagoras, with c=(2n-r)/(288n), yields
 ||D||_F <= B(n,r)||Q D||_F,
 B(n,r)=sqrt(1+288n/(2n-r)).
This is explicit and conservative, NOT a claim of the optimal one-sided
constant. In particular the sharp240 balanced-rank constant must NOT
be used in place of B for a reconstruction with unrestricted rank.

If rho is PSD trace1 rank<=r and sigma is any PSD trace1 matrix, then
D=rho-sigma has n_+(D)<=r. Indeed the quadratic form of D is nonpositive
on ker(rho), a subspace of dimension>=d-r; the min--max principle gives
the inertia bound. Thus the displayed inequality applies. Nonzero
kernel matrices with inertia(2n,2n), namely U+U^2-V-V^2, prove failure
of strict completeness for r>=2n exactly as in240. The threshold2n is
therefore exact for strict completeness as well as rank-r completeness.

Convex recovery consequence (ordinary deterministic stability corollary).
For data y=Q rho+e, ||e||_F<=epsilon, let sigma be ANY minimizer of
 ||Q sigma-y||_F over sigma PSD trace1.
The domain is nonempty compact convex, so a minimizer exists. Both rho
and a minimizer have residual at mostepsilon, so ||Q(sigma-rho)||<=2epsilon
and ||sigma-rho||_F<=2B(n,r)epsilon. This is a mathematical guarantee
for ideal coefficient data in this Frobenius normalization, not an
experimental sample-complexity claim, implementation or efficient
measurement-design result. No bound for approximate rank is asserted.

The cone and strict-completeness methodology is classical, e.g.
Carmeli et al. and Baldwin--Deutsch--Kalev1605.02109 sectionsII-III.
The geometric threshold, this elementary quantitative specialization,
and the separate sharp balanced-rank law240 are candidate contributions.
