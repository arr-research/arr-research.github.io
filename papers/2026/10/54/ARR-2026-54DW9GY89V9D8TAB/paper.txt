                                              Abstract
         Let d = 9n with arbitrary integer n ≥ 1, and omit the four cyclic Weyl coefficients
                        d/3               d/3
     associated with Xd , its adjoint, Zd and its adjoint. We determine the exact inverse
     Frobenius constant on differences of density matrices of rank at most r. Writing x = r/n,
     the squared fraction of√ energy that can be hidden in the missing coefficients is 2x/3 for
     0 < x ≤ 1, and (x + x2 − 3x + 3)/3 for 1 ≤ x < 2. The resulting     pinverse constant is
     attained by explicit orthogonally supported states and diverges as 2n/(2n − r) at the
     exact nonidentifiability threshold r = 2n. The same threshold holds when a rank-r state
     must be distinguished from every positive state of unrestricted rank. For this stronger
     task we give a separate, conservative stability bound and a convex least-squares recovery
     consequence. The proof reduces the missing-coefficient space to additive 3 by 3 spectral
     grids, then solves two four-dimensional symmetric matrix pencils. These are results for
     one fixed erasure pattern, not for arbitrary four erasures or a measurement scheme with
     subquadratically many coefficients.


1    Question, normalization and antecedents
A measurement may fail to determine arbitrary quantum states while still determining every
state below a rank threshold. In that situation a quantitative question remains: how much can
the omitted data amplify errors even before injectivity fails? We solve that question exactly
for a four-dimensional missing-coefficient space.

    The general kernel and inertia characterization of restricted quantum state determination
is due to Carmeli, Heinosaari, Schultz and Toigo [1]. Their Section 6.2, equations (12)–
(13) and Proposition 13, also establish the relation between symmetric Weyl zero sets and
realizable covariant POVM kernels. The elementary realization above is not claimed as a
new measurement-design principle. Baldwin, Deutsch and Kalev [2], especially Sections II–
III, distinguish rank-r completeness from rank-r strict-completeness and explain the latter’s
relation to convex reconstruction and noise robustness. We use this established distinction.
We supply direct proofs of the specialized inertia and stability claims rather than assert a new
general criterion.
    Recent work of Zhu and Wang [3] studies spectral floors and conditioning for minimal rank-
one Weyl–Heisenberg covariant measurements. Their full-space Gram-spectrum problem differs
from the restricted state-difference constant of the singular coefficient projection considered
here. The author’s earlier Weyl rank-uncertainty reductions [4] concern support versus arbitrary
complex matrix rank, rather than the exact positive-state conditioning law proved below.
Spectral rearrangement, orthogonal projection duality and symmetric matrix pencils are
classical tools; the contribution claimed here is their explicit solution for the kernel (1). This
comparison is not a certification of worldwide priority.

an improvement over compressed sensing protocols using many fewer measured coefficients.


7    Scope, checks and remaining questions
The argument depends only on the additive spectral kernel with nine equal-multiplicity cells.
It therefore applies after arbitrary unitary conjugation and to any commuting order-three pair
with all nine joint multiplicities equal to n. The cyclic Weyl construction realizes this kernel
in every dimension divisible by nine.
    Exact symbolic checks reproduce both pencil determinants and the attainer identities,
including a direct computation of projection energy. Finite exact tests additionally compare
sorted spectra with the formulas in 2,400 formula checks on 2,000 distinct instances, using
n = 1, . . . , 5 and 80 nonzero gap vectors. The 400 instances at r = n are checked in both
branches. These tests supplement the universal proofs; they do not establish unexamined
dimensions by extrapolation.
    The main open quantitative question here is the optimal constant in (4). Further questions
concern unequal joint multiplicities, other erasure patterns, and error bounds for approximately
low-rank states. The present result does not classify those problems. Only four coefficients are
removed out of d2 ; the theorem isolates an exact conditioning mechanism rather than a large
measurement reduction.
