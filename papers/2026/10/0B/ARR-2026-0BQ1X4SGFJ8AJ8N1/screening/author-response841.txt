R1. Close antecedents and the scope of novelty

Abu Salem, Gao and Lauder, Factoring polynomials via polytopes, author version2004-01-07:
https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/lauder/papers/AbuSalemGaoLauderISSAC2004Jan7.pdf
Selected abstract/intro, Newton-product statement and edge-factorization passages were inspected, not every algorithmic proof. Newton polytopes of products and edge polynomials are classical factorization tools. The present squarefreeness argument applies these tools to the complete coefficient-one lattice-point enumerator. No claim is made to originate the Newton-edge technique or to establish that the resulting squarefreeness lemma has worldwide priority. The full review explicitly requests a closer bibliographic comparison; this recommendation remains visible.

Averkov and Langfeld, On the reconstruction of planar lattice-convex sets from the covariogram, arXiv1011.5530v3:
https://arxiv.org/html/1011.5530v3
The abstract and selected group-ring/factorization passages, including Problem2.3, were read. Lattice-set generating polynomials and Laurent unique factorization are established tools. Autocorrelation ambiguities in the covariogram problem do not by themselves state the manuscript's product-power rigidity conclusion. This limited distinction is not an originality certification, nor a claim that homometric reconstruction is solved here. Complete geometric proofs in that paper were not audited.

Leake and Mohammadi Yekta, arXiv2609.39917v1, section3.1, is the essential external coefficientwise inequality. Tardos1986 supplies the LP complexity theorem. Neither input is claimed as a new contribution of this manuscript. The component and full reviews support only the exact hypotheses stated and retain these dependencies.

R2. Integral right-hand sides and rational bounds

In the midpoint theorem, all three b_s belong to Z^r and satisfy b_-+b_+=2b_0. The box bounds in the real-polytope translation theorem are integral. For a rational-bound counting interface, ceil/floor rounding first replaces the real fibers; it preserves integer points but does not establish translation of the original rational real fibers. The retained counterexample M=[1,1], box[0,1/2]x[0,2], RHS0,1,2 has enumerators1,y,y^2 and real dimensions0,1,1. It refutes the unrounded rational real-fiber extension.

The phrase "nonunit coefficient" after Theorem2.2 intends "coefficient different from1". The number2 is a unit in C. This is a wording clarification, not a change of coefficient field. A "nonunit factor" elsewhere has its usual Laurent-ring meaning; monomial units are allowed and are not counterexamples to squarefreeness.
