MATHEMATICAL ASSESSMENT

Pages 1--5, Theorem 1 and Lemmas 2--5. The family and the excluded wider
phase-covariant region are correctly delimited. The controlled-purification proof
has the correct direction of conditional-entropy data processing. At fixed row
masses and coherence, the three-vertex triangle, its weights and both triangular
Gram factors are correct; all vertices have the same scalar entropy. The boundary
cases and X=Y equality construction are valid. Lemma 5 applies to arbitrary finite
partners without requiring orthogonal conditional vectors. The lower convex
envelope is attained, and opposite-phase pairs implement the one-use optimum with
at most four signals. The ensemble proof gives unrestricted Holevo additivity;
the partner's operational capacity correctly retains its own regularization.

Pages 6--7, Theorem 6, Corollary 7 and Proposition 8. The fixed-population upper
bound F_Phi(sigma)<=e(p), rather than the generally false equality F=f, is used in
the right place. Pure-state one-step bounds and convexity then establish the full
arbitrary-input roof inequality. Positivity justifies support lifting for the
state superadditivity statement. Tensor powers and the standard state-cost
theorem yield exactly the claimed state costs. The moment formula preserves the
coherence constraint, has compact attainment, and allows a finite ensemble. The
known axial roof geometry is appropriately attributed. The former ambiguous
phrase about equality for product marginals is now the precise statement
"at product input states".

Pages 7--8, Theorem 9. The four-dimensional environmental realization, diagonal
output spectrum and phase covariance are correct, including the degenerate
overlap convention. Purity transfers the one-step entropy inequality to the
complement with the complementary partner; it does not presume an unsupported
operational equivalence. The resulting scalar capacity and arbitrary-partner
identities follow. The manuscript specifies the entire Stinespring environment,
which can include purification of an initially mixed thermal reservoir. It does
not silently identify this with the experimentally accessible bath alone.

Pages 9--10, Theorem 10. This is a sound integration of the new simulation
argument. The Kraus/HJW formula makes G_M concave in the input by entropy
perspectives. Continuity is separately justified by finite-dimensional E_F and
the canonical purification; it is not incorrectly inferred from an infimum of
continuous functions. Covariance therefore supplies a diagonal maximizer. The
standard subadditivity proof using the product Kraus instrument has the correct
conditioning inequalities and requires no unproved max/min interchange.

The normalized W_p columns are orthonormal; their traced channel has exactly the
parameters in Eq. (19). In particular k_p=Lk and lambda_p^2=L lambda^2, so it
remains in the Davies region. The reference-output state is W_p diag(1-t,t)W_p*,
in a DIFFERENT Stinespring range from the original state-cost application. Applying
Corollary 7 to that reversed channel establishes the strong state additivity
needed at the maximizing input. Product optimizing inputs then give the channel
lower bound, matching general channel-formation subadditivity.

Berta et al.'s theorem is the correct operational result for parallel tensor-power
diamond-norm simulation with free classical communication. Iterating the strong
channel identity keeps E_F^ch(M^{tensor n}) for an arbitrary partner M, so the
partner's regularization is not dropped. Endpoint inputs, vanishing t or 1-t,
pure replacement channels and zero formation are treated adequately. Ordinary
complement duality alone would not prove this cost statement; the manuscript
explicitly identifies why the reverse-range argument is necessary.

Pages 10--11, Corollary 11 and Eq. (20). The identities k_p=Lk, a_p b_p=Lab and

rate follows. The low-coherence endpoint-chord argument is also correct: the

the determinant gives the endpoint roof. Applying it at a maximizer of the global
upper bound J proves the sufficient plateau. No full phase diagram is asserted.

ATTRIBUTION, NOVELTY AND SCOPE

The manuscript now integrates the needed distinctions rather than appending an
unqualified new cost claim. It credits Tang's external triangular theorem and
OpenAI's GAD boundary, Amosov's controlled vertex, MSW's state/channel framework,
Holevo and King et al. on complements, HHT for state cost, HU for axial roofs,
Berta et al. for parallel channel simulation and Barnum--Knill for normalized
reversal. It cites Wilde's epolarizing result and acknowledges that the general
unital operational case follows from unital additivity and covariant channel/state
machinery. Those are substantive overlaps, and their exclusion from the novelty
claim is appropriate. The GAD-boundary simulation deduction is also identified as
using the earlier one-step premise.

The optional extension to complements of pure-output qubit channels is correctly
omitted as a separate manuscript contribution. A historical trace of that note in
the supplement is disclosed and labeled an antecedent corollary. This does not
alter the current theorem statements or add an unreviewed theorem to the paper.

The contribution is useful and specific: a sharp matrix extension supplies a
single chain of communication, state-formation and complementary-simulation
results across the stated nonunital Davies region, with an exact example showing
why the optimizer cannot generally be assumed to be the Choi input. It does not
settle general qubit additivity, adaptive cost for this nonunital family, the
original Phi's simulation cost, quantum/private capacity, or interacting
higher-dimensional Davies models. Dependence on the recent triangular theorem
and the lack of worldwide priority certification remain visible.
