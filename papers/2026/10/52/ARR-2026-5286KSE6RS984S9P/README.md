# Interior-zero complexity of polynomial differentiation residuals

We classify the numbers of interior zeros that permit vanishing and superalgebraic differentiation residuals for polynomials whose zeros lie in the closed unit disk. If a degree-$n$ polynomial has exactly $M$ strictly interior zeros, then, for every complex slope, its normalized $H^2$ residual is at least $n/[2\sqrt216^M(n+2M+4)^{2M+2}]$. Consequently a prescribed budget $b_n$ permits a superalgebraically small residual sequence if and only if $b_n\to\infty$. Sufficiency holds with real coefficients, normalization at the origin, the slope fixed by that normalization, and exactly $b_n$ interior zeros. In contrast, one interior zero already permits residuals $O(n^{-1/6})$, with family-specific asymptotic constant $\sqrt{15}/4^{4/3}$, whereas the circle-only infimum tends to $1/\sqrt8$. For one interior zero we also prove a universal $c n^{-13/6}$ lower bound; the optimal rate remains open. The finite-budget obstruction combines a reciprocal identity with classical Mahler, truncated-shift and Poisson-kernel estimates, and extends to a stable factor times a self-inversive cofactor. For unrestricted interior counts we prove $\exp(-C\sqrt n)$ lower bounds for every $C>\sqrt{(8/3)\log17}$. The upper construction corrects both the exact slope and degree rounding at every large degree. The resulting refinement of exterior power-sum bounds uses a known residual method; the negative answer to Erdos Problem 973 was already known. No matching fixed-budget rates or stretched-exponential upper bounds are claimed.
