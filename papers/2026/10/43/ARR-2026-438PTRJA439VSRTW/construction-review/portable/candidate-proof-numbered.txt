   2: Status: proposed proof, not externally reviewed; no score or publication claim.
   3: Working title: Undecidable determinant saturation at a known operator capacity
   4: 
   5: 1. Proposed statement and scope
   6: 
   7: There is an effective polynomial construction from binary linear-system games to real rational matrix tuples A=(A_1,...,A_s) in M_D(Q), with entries in {0,+/-1,+/-3/5,+/-4/5}, satisfying
   8:   sum_i A_i A_i^*=sum_i A_i^* A_i=I_D,
   9:   Tr(A_i^* A_j)/D=delta_ij/s.
  10: Consequently their covariance-optimized operator capacity C(A) is exactly 1/s. Nevertheless both problems are undecidable on this family:
  11:   (a) is F_m(A)=1/s for some positive integer m?
  12:   (b) is F_infty(A)=1/s?
  13: Here F_m is the maximum of |det(sum A_i tensor Y_i)|^(2/(Dm)) subject to sum tau_m(Y_i Y_i^*)=1; F_infty=sup_m F_m.
  14: There are tuples in this family with F_infty=1/s but F_m<1/s for every finite m. There is no total recursive upper bound, as a function of input size, for the least EXACTLY saturating m on the yes-instances of (a).
  15: These conclusions do NOT establish a constant promise gap, uncomputability of approximating the numerical value, or absence of a recursive bound for epsilon-optimal witnesses. Those stronger claims are not made.
  16: The essential undecidability and non-closure inputs are established results of Slofstra and collaborators, not new results here. The proposed contribution is a rational, balanced, quantitative reduction into determinant saturation.
  17: 
  18: 2. Signed words representing the source constraints
  19: 
  20: For a binary system Bx=b introduce unitary variables x_j and impose x_j^2=I, each row word product x_j=(-1)^b_i I, and x_j x_k x_j x_k=I for pairs in a row. Together with the square relations the last constraints are exactly commutation. This is the solution-group presentation with its central involution fixed to -I. Centrality and J^2=I are automatic after fixing J=-I.
  21: Add a fresh unitary variable t and the word t t=I. Append t t to every original signed word. Exact solutions are unchanged. Approximate solutions with arbitrarily small normalized-Hilbert-Schmidt error are also unchanged: extend using t=I in one direction; in the other direction the added square constraint removes the appended pair with at most one additional relation error. All word lengths are at least two, including originally empty row words.
  22: No scalar variables are identified with the reference label below. The original symbols, t, and every proper prefix label remain distinct from that label.
  23: 
  24: 3. Rational multiplication block
  25: 
  26: Let a=3/5, b=4/5, c=ab=12/25. For four unitary m by m matrices U,V,W,Z put
  27:   H(U,V,W,Z) = [[a U, b W], [b Z, -a V]].
  28: Then H is unitary if and only if U Z^*=W V^*, equivalently
  29:   (U Z^*)(V Z^*)=W Z^*.
  30: This follows by multiplying H H^*: its diagonal blocks are I and its off-diagonal block is c R, R=U Z^*-W V^*.
  31: Every such block, even when the equation fails, is uniformly invertible:
  32:   (1/25) I <= H H^* <= (49/25) I,
  33: because ||R||_op<=2. In particular ||H||_op<=7/5 and sigma_min(H)>=1/5.
  34: Moreover tau_(2m)(H H^*)=1 and
  35:   -log |det H|^(1/m) = -(1/2) tau_m log(I-c^2 R R^*)
  36:     <= (72/49) ||R||_(2,m)^2.
  37: Indeed c^2=144/625, 1-4c^2=49/625, and -log(1-x)<=x/(1-4c^2) on [0,4c^2]. The exponent 1/m is 2/(2m), as required for squared geometric mean.
  38: The unequal coefficients are essential to this proof: the symmetric 1/sqrt(2) block would lose the uniform lower singular-value bound.
  39: 
  40: 4. Homogeneous linear pencil and balancing
  41: 
  42: Use a reference variable Z and a variable for each original generator, t, and each proper word prefix. For a word x_1 ... x_k = sigma I, k>=2, make gates for the prefix products. The first uses U=x_1, V=x_2, with output a fresh prefix variable if k>2. Subsequent gates use the preceding prefix and the next original symbol. The last output is W=sigma Z. Include the reference Z as the lower-left entry of every gate. Thus a global gauge U_label -> U_label Z^* turns all gates into exactly the signed word constraints.
  43: Form a block diagonal pencil L(Y) containing one singleton block Y_l for EVERY label l, including the reference, followed by 25 copies of every multiplication block. Each matrix entry of the pencil contains exactly one label with a rational coefficient. In a gate U,V are not reference labels, and W is either a fresh label distinct from U,V or +/-Z. U=V is allowed. These restrictions imply that, for the coefficient matrices of each 2 by 2 scalar block, sum A_l A_l^* = sum A_l^* A_l = I_2: possible mixed row/column terms vanish. This must be checked rather than inferred just from the norms.
  44: The coefficient matrices have pairwise disjoint entry supports. Let q_l be the sum of squared entries of the coefficient of label l. Initially q_l is a positive INTEGER: singleton blocks contribute 1, and the 25 gate copies contribute 9 for each occurrence in a diagonal slot and 16 for each off-diagonal slot. Let M=max_l q_l. Append M-q_l further singleton copies of each label l. Now all q_l=M, and D=sM because the total sum of the q_l equals the scalar matrix dimension. Balancing adds only redundant unitarity constraints. The total number of labels, gates, copies and rows is polynomial in the signed-word input length.
  45: The final pencil is L(Y)=sum_l A_l tensor Y_l. Its coefficients are real rational, satisfy both stochastic identities, and have Gram matrix I_s/s. For arbitrary Y,
  46:   tau_(Dm)(L(Y)^*L(Y)) = (1/s) sum_l tau_m(Y_l^*Y_l).
  47: 
  48: 5. Known capacity and exact saturation
  49: 
  50: For Q=I_s/s, Phi_Q(X)=(1/s)sum A_l X A_l^*. The map sum A_l X A_l^* is doubly stochastic, hence has operator capacity one. For example operator concavity of log and trace preservation give Tr log Phi(X)>=Tr log X for positive X, and equality holds at X=I. Therefore cap(Phi_Q)^(1/D)=1/s.
  51: The identity scaling and the Gram matrix give cap(Phi_R)^(1/D)<=Tr(R I_s/s)=1/s for any density R, by determinant arithmetic-geometric mean at X=I. Thus C(A)=1/s without using any previous manuscript in the programme.
  52: For feasible Y, tau(L(Y)^*L(Y))=1/s, so determinant AM-GM gives F_m(A)<=1/s. Equality holds iff sqrt(s)L(Y) is unitary. All singleton blocks then force sqrt(s)Y_l to be unitaries, and the gates yield the exact signed-word solution after gauging by the reference. Conversely any m-dimensional unitary solution assigns unitary prefix labels and Z=I; Y_l=U_l/sqrt(s) is feasible and attains equality. Thus equality in dimension m is equivalent to an m-dimensional operator solution of the source system. A fixed-m maximum exists by compactness of the coefficient sphere.
  53: 
  54: 6. Approximate saturation: forward construction
  55: 
  56: Given unitary generators satisfying all signed words with normalized 2-error at most eta, set Z=I and assign each prefix its exact word product. All internal gates then hold exactly; each final gate has ||R||_2<=eta. The singleton blocks are unitary and every gate has uniformly bounded singular values as in section 3. The balanced pencil L(U) has normalized squared Hilbert-Schmidt norm one. Averaging log determinants over all blocks gives
  57:   -log |det L(U)|^(2/(Dm)) <= (72/49) eta^2.
  58: With Y_l=U_l/sqrt(s), this yields
  59:   s F_m(A) >= exp(-(72/49) eta^2).
  60: In particular arbitrarily accurate approximate solutions imply F_infty(A)=1/s. The explicit spectral lower bound avoids any illegitimate inference from 2-norm convergence to log-determinant convergence.
  61: 
  62: 7. Approximate saturation: extraction
  63: 
  64: Suppose a feasible tuple has s times its determinant objective >=1-epsilon, 0<epsilon<1. Put X=sqrt(s)L(Y), so tau(X^*X)=1. Its polar unitary P satisfies
  65:   delta^2:=||X-P||_2^2 = 2(1-tau|X|)
  66:        <= 2(1-sqrt(1-epsilon)) <=2 epsilon.
  67: X is invertible in this case, but a unitary polar extension also suffices generally. As X is block diagonal, take P block diagonal.
  68: For each label l, one original singleton block is Z_l=sqrt(s)Y_l. Let V_l be a polar unitary of Z_l. Its normalized squared error d_l^2<=D delta^2, because this singleton contributes d_l^2/D to the global polar distance. Set B=L(V). By the balanced Gram identity,
  69:   ||B-X||_2^2=(1/s)sum_l d_l^2<=D delta^2.
  70: All labels of B are unitary, so ||B||_op<=7/5. Therefore
  71:   ||B B^*-I||_2 <= (||B||_op+1)||B-P||_2
  72:                     <= (12/5)(sqrt(D)+1)delta.
  73: Each individual 2 by 2 gate has normalized squared defect c^2||R||_2^2, and contributes 2/D times that to the global squared defect. Hence
  74:   ||R||_2 <= 5 sqrt(D/2)(sqrt(D)+1)delta.
  75: Gauge V_l by V_Z^*. A word of length k has at most k-1 gates, so telescoping along unitary products bounds its signed relation error by (k-1) times the displayed gate bound. Removing the padding t t introduces at most one additional relation error. Thus a sequence of determinant objectives tending to 1/s yields arbitrarily accurate matrix approximate representations of the original signed presentation, in dimensions unchanged. No operator-norm control of the initial Y_l was assumed; it is the rounded pencil B that is uniformly bounded.

  77: 8. External theorems and consequences to verify
  78: 
  79: Slofstra, arXiv:1703.08618v2: Corollary 1.4 says finite-dimensional perfect strategies of binary linear-system games are undecidable; Theorem 1.2 says perfect strategies in the closure C_qa are undecidable; Theorem 1.1 gives non-closure. Theorem 3.2 gives the exact solution-group correspondence. Central J nontrivial can be restricted to its -1 eigenspace.
  80: Slofstra--Vidick, arXiv:1711.10676: verify the precise statements in section 4 and the converse linking quantum value 1 to approximate matrix representations with J=-I. This is a required dependency for asserting the general approximate equivalence, not merely an abstract-level citation.
  81: Once this equivalence is checked, sections 5--7 transfer both undecidability statements and nonattainment into the balanced tuple family. For no recursive exact dimension bound, assume such a bound b(input length) exists and use quantifier elimination over real closed fields to decide the polynomial equations L(U)^*L(U)=I in every dimension m<=b(length). Rational coefficients make every fixed-m question decidable. This would decide the source undecidable language, contradiction.

  83: 9. Context and limits
  84: 
  85: This is a proposed consequence of established group/game undecidability, not a new proof of those theorems. It gives an obstruction for exact finite saturation despite a known positive capacity. It does not contradict polynomial algorithms for capacity, noncommutative invertibility witnesses, or the earlier constant-factor determinant comparison. No global novelty, numerical score, publication status, formal verification or claim about a fixed approximation gap is currently established.
