Dimension bounds for common components after measurable
quantum preparation
Lluis Eriksson
29 October 2026
Abstract
For a channel on a d-dimensional system with ordinary trace-distance contraction at
most r, we give a common-component certificate after an explicitly specified measurable
preparation instrument. Its mass is at least max{0, 1 − (d− 1)r}, independently of
the outcome cardinality. The same certificate yields a completely positive replacement
component after the associated measure-and-prepare channel. We determine the worst-
case envelope of this positive component over the stated class using classical exclusion
channels. We separate these assertions from optimal complete contraction and from mixing
of arbitrary observed instruments. Two elementary examples show why the recorded
instrument and the topology on posterior laws must be retained. The measurement
coefficient, replacement framework, and downstream contraction principles are established
prior results; worldwide novelty of the dimension comparison is not asserted.

5 Recorded dynamics, calibration and limitations
A uniform prefix estimate ηtr(Ψ) ≤q L−1, 0 < q <1, yields a positive sufficient block certificate
whenever (d− 1)qL−1 < 1. Comparing this with a finite-preparation sufficient condition
NqL−1 < 1 illustrates the benefit when N is large. For d = 2, N = 100, q = 1/2, the respective
sufficient lengths are 2 and 8. These are certificate thresholds, not optimal mixing times.
When N < d−1, the count estimate may be better. Random-environment applications retain
joint measurability, the reference measure kernel, common full-measure sets and time-shift
conventions; no limit theorem is proved here.
A continuous qubit example uses ν = dθ/(2π), nθ = (sinθ, 0,cosθ ), Fθ = I + nθ ·σ and
τθ = (I+nθ·σ)/2. Circle moments give the summed channel with Bloch matrixdiag(1/2, 0, 1/2).
Taking this as the prefix gives Gθ = I + (nθ ·σ )/2, so b = 1/2 and (4) is attained. Four
equally weighted coordinate-axis points give the same channel and a rational calibration. These
examples validate identities, not universal originality.
Two obstructions explain the retained hypotheses. A qubit Pauli instrument with branches
UaρU∗
a /4 has completely replacing summed channel, but preserves the spectrum along each
recorded trajectory. Both the point mass at I/2 and the uniform law on the two computational
pure states are invariant posterior laws. They are distinct with the same barycenter; channel
contraction zero therefore does not imply posterior uniqueness. Second, a single visible branch
D(ρ) = ρ/2 + I/4 induces a deterministic posterior kernel. Starting at the two computational
pure states, its finite-time laws are distinct point masses and have TV distance one, although
their state trace distance and trace-based Wasserstein distance are 2 −L. Neither branch is of
the input-independent preparation form (2).
The mass b also depends on the chosen representation. A replacing channel described by
orthogonal rank-one measurement effects and the same prepared state for all labels has b = 0
before grouping labels, while its posterior kernel is constant and α+ = 1. Thus our feasible
certificate need not be optimal. Optimizing over arbitrary Hermitian common operators is a
different relaxation.
