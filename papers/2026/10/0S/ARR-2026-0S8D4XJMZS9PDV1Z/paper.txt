One calibration query activates a ﬁnite adaptive advantage
in frozen-versus-renewed dephasing tests
Lluis Eriksson
17 October 2026
Working-paper manuscript; not independently reviewed
Abstract
We quantify a ﬁnite calibration resource in one-sided discrimination between a frozen
unknown qubit phase and independently renewed dephasing. With ﬁve signal queries, de-
phasing parameter η= 7/10, and one separate noiseless calibration query, an explicit causal
strategy has failure probability 0.4657926731886833125. Every parallel ﬁve-signal strategy,
even with perfect knowledge of the carrier, has failure probability at least 0.46603. The gap
is certiﬁed by rational matrices and a solver-free veriﬁer. Without calibration, no causal
strategy improves the parallel minimax value, so one calibration query is suﬃcient and min-
imal at this signal budget. The resource distinction is essential: six parallel signal queries
already achieve 0.38442745, and the displayed strategy therefore gives no advantage when
calibration and signal queries can be freely exchanged at equal cost. A trajectory bound
also rules out an improved asymptotic error exponent in this model. The contribution is a
small explicit ﬁnite-resource construction, not a claim of a new phase-estimation framework
or large scientiﬁc impact.
1 Experiment and resource accounting
Let Uθ = diag(1,eiθ) and let
Eη(ρ) =pρ+ qZρZ, p = (1 +η)/2, q = (1− η)/2, 0 ≤ η≤ 1.
The signal black box has two hypotheses. Under H0, every call is the same Uθ, with arbitrary
unknown θ. Under H1, each call is independently Uϕ ◦ Eη, with arbitrary unknown common
carrier ϕ. A separate calibration oracle implements Uϕ under both hypotheses. Under H0, θ
and ϕ range independently. In particular, the calibration oracle contains no direct label of the
hypothesis.
A test must have rejection probability exactly zero under H0 for every pair (θ,ϕ). Its failure
probability β is the probability of accepting H0 under H1, maximized over ϕ. The resources are
an ordered pair (n,L): n signal queries and L calibration queries. Signal queries are ordinary
forward calls, with arbitrary ﬁnite quantum memory and ideal controls. There is no environment
access, coherent bypass of the signal, or indeﬁnite causal order. Parallel strategies apply their
signal calls in one layer; causal strategies may interleave controls and signal calls.
The calibration resource counts information about the unknown carrier. It does not charge
for the laboratory phase convention, preparation and readout apparatus, classical precision,
energy, or control synthesis. A complete physical implementation would need additional resource
accounting. No statement about consciousness, an ether, or the ontology of time is involved.

Proposition 2. For ﬁxed n≥ 1 signal calls, any one-sided causal protocol in this model, even
with arbitrary noiseless carrier-calibration assistance, has β ≥ pn + qn. Calibration cannot
improve the optimal asymptotic error exponent per signal query beyond − log p. The optimal
exponent under a freely reallocatable total-query budget is also − log p.
Proof. In the renewed mixture, the all- I history has probability pn. Its joint signal and cali-
bration experiment is exactly the allowed null experiment with θ = ϕ. The all- Z history, of
probability qn, is the null experiment with θ= ϕ+πand the same calibration oracle. Exact one-
sidedness forces acceptance on both histories, including when calibration and signal operations
are coherent and interleaved. Their probabilities give the lower bound.
Conversely, for any ﬁxed-weight parallel input the sum over that weight is bounded by the

The path argument extends an obstruction already present in Ref. [ 1]; it is not presented as
a new general no-go principle. Finite-sample gains may still matter when signal access is scarce,
calibration is reusable, or the two kinds of calls have diﬀerent costs. Those possibilities require
explicit new models and proofs.

4 Prior art, self-review, and open work
Finite quantum phase references and covariant phase estimation are established subjects [ 2,
3]. For a longer token ∑ L
j=0 cjeijϕ|j⟩, its Fourier error moments are autocorrelations of the
coeﬃcients cj. Optimizing an average phase-dependent payoﬀ is a Toeplitz eigenvalue problem,
a structure explicitly treated in recent phase-estimation work [ 4, 5]. We therefore make no
novelty claim for that reduction. The operational value of asymmetry for discrimination is
also known in broader settings [ 6]. Our narrow candidate contribution is the exact one-query
activation at the stated one-sided signal-testing task and its explicit resource comparison.
In the ﬁrst exploration, the earlier ﬁxed ﬁve-query tester needed a longer reference to show
an advantage. Reoptimizing the signal tester produced the present one-query certiﬁcate. This
refutes any inference that the reference threshold of one ﬁxed tester is a universal threshold.
The current certiﬁcate was recalculated by a separate exact veriﬁcation script, but all reasoning
and code were produced in the same assistant session: this is self-review, not review by an
independent researcher or model.
The strongest outstanding questions are quantitative and ﬁnite: the exact causal optimum
with one calibration query, the minimum signal budget allowing activation, explicit low-memory
circuits, comparisons with coherent rather than measured reference access, and robustness when
both test classes are jointly optimized at a positive false-positive allowance. No signiﬁcant impact
rating or priority guarantee follows from the current evidence. Before admission as an accepted
article, the scope and novelty need external scrutiny, and the physical cost of controls must be
separated from the ﬁnite carrier-information budget. A failure of a more ambitious conjecture
should be retained as a constraint on subsequent searches rather than hidden by changing the
benchmark.
