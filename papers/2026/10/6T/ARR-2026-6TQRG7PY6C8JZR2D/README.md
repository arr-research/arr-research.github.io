# A sharp circle-root residual barrier and its collapse with one interior zero

For degree-$n$ polynomials with all zeros on the unit circle, the least normalized residual $\|P'-\lambda P\|_{H^2}/\|P\|_{H^2}$ tends to $1/\sqrt8$, both for arbitrary complex $\lambda$ and for the restricted slope $\lambda=P'(0)$, $P(0)=1$. We prove the sharp lower limit through the classical Mathieu ground state and give an explicit circle-root family attaining it in every large degree. In contrast, exactly one zero strictly inside the disk, with all remaining zeros on the circle, permits restricted residual $O(n^{-1/6})$, giving the exact zero-count threshold for collapse. More generally, for each fixed $A>0$, a fixed number $M_A$ of interior zeros permits $O(n^{-A})$. Their distance and radial gap from 1 have order $n^{-1/3}$; explicit algebraic parameters in the one-root case, and an implicit-function construction in general, correct both degree rounding and the exact slope. For all disk-root polynomials and arbitrary slope, we also prove a uniform lower bound $\exp(-C\sqrt n)$ for every $C>\sqrt{(8/3)\log17}$, explicitly $\exp(-3\sqrt n)$ for $n\ge10^4$. This refines the known residual method for exterior power sums; the negative answer to Erdos Problem 973 was already known. The optimal disk and one-root decay rates, and small exterior power-sum constructions, remain unresolved.
