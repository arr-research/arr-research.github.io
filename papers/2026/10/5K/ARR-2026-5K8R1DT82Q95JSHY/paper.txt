Error-space geometry and positivity orders
                          of encoded reduction maps
                                             Lluis Eriksson

                                           25 October 2026
                                      Research manuscript, draft 2


                                                Abstract
          We study tensor products of reduction maps after restriction to perfect-tensor encodings.
      An error-support expansion of the unnormalized Choi matrix accommodates multiple logical
      reference parties and arbitrary complex absolutely maximally entangled states. For one logical
      party, odd physical length n and 0 < t ≤ 1, averaging orthogonal error families over fixed-size
      site subsets gives a completely positive output complement under the sufficient condition
      d ≥ 2n/t. The encoded map then has an exact Schmidt-rank sign criterion and optimal
      dimension-uniform positivity order ⌊t−n ⌋. At t = 1/2, a separate estimate covers d ≥ 2n. For
      two logical parties and four physical outputs, the half-parameter map is completely positive
      exactly in the admissible dimensions 2 ≤ d ≤ 12. In dimensions d ≥ 13 it is not completely
      positive, but is k-positive at least through ⌊2d(d − 2)/(d − 6)⌋. Explicit polynomial-evaluation
      codes give failure at rank 9d for every prime d ≥ 43, establishing linear growth up to unmatched
      constants for those examples. Code existence, the Schmidt-overlap criterion and the reduction-
      map family are prior ingredients. The results retain substantive code-support restrictions and
      belong to the completely copositive class.

The local factors need not be completely positive. We ask how restricting the entire input support
to a perfect-tensor code changes their positivity orders. A map is k-positive if its tensor product
with the identity map on Mk (C) is positive; k is always a positive integer. Complete positivity
(CP) is equivalent to positivity of its unnormalized Choi matrix.
    Our first result treats one logical input of dimension d, encoded into odd length n. The whole
physical output code gives a logical isotropic Choi block. Its complement is controlled by an
elementary averaging inequality for local-error projections. This gives an exact sign criterion in
stated sufficient dimension domains. The claimed dimension bounds are not optimal.
    The second result changes the logical input to dimension d2 and uses four physical outputs. Its
code block need not be isotropic. Instead, each fixed single-site error space Ei is invariant under
the full Choi matrix, which acts there as a scalar. Every vector of that fixed space is maximally
entangled across the logical–physical cut; this is not asserted for arbitrary superpositions across
the sum ran Q1 . This proves a CP classification and a sufficient positivity order growing linearly
with d. Concrete witnesses give an upper bound of the same growth order for an established MDS
family. The constants and exact first failing rank remain undetermined.

Here is the counting and normalization in detail. With the two reference values retained, the
selected code has hd2 words. The inner-product Gram sums of a slice of Ψd and a slice of z have
common squared-amplitude denominator hd5 (d − 1). After the Choi factor d2 , their denominator
is hd3 (d − 1).
    For u = 0, each reference pair has uniformly varying error evaluation, so centering makes the
Gram sum zero. For u = 1, tracing the error site leaves squared integer Gram sum h2 d3 (d − 1);
tracing another site gives zero by three-coordinate uniformity. For u = 2 containing the error site,
the corresponding sum is h2 d2 (d − 1). Otherwise, fix the two traced values. The error evaluation
is a nonconstant affine function of the remaining allowed reference evaluation. Its sum over B is
d times the indicator that its unique zero lies in B, minus h. Each possible zero occurs d times
among the d2 traced pairs. The squared sum is

                               d{h(d − h)2 + (d − h)h2 } = d2 h(d − h).

For u = 3, the squared sum is hd2 (d − 1), since three physical values identify the codeword. For

distinguish them. Dividing by the common denominator gives the table. Its multiplicities are 1
for each single site, three pairs of each kind, four triples and one full set. Summing gives (16).

6    Original-slot form, copositivity and limitations
For a physical coefficient matrix C, define

                                   qn,t (C) =         (−t)|S| ∥ TrS C∥2F .

Its row-vectorized Gram is the product of local matrices I − t|Ωd ⟩⟨Ωd |, after regrouping tensor
factors. If C = V B, compressing its first index gives JV,t , not generally JV,t . Indeed its coefficients
use Vaα Vbβ , whereas the Choi coefficients use Vaα Vbβ ; all matrix-unit coefficients of Φt are real.
Conjugating the coefficient vector restores the usual Choi form and preserves norm and Schmidt
rank. Also rank(V B) = rank B and ∥V B∥F = ∥B∥F . Adjoint symmetry q(C ∗ ) = q(C) treats
the opposite whole-support convention. These facts do not extend either theorem to a physical
support containing just one specified code vector.
    For 0 ≤ t ≤ 1, the partial transpose of the local Choi matrix is I − tFswap ≥ 0. Thus every local
factor is completely copositive, and so is its tensor product followed by an isometric input encoding.
These maps belong to the known trivial tensor-stable class [10]. We have not constructed nontrivial

tensor-stable positive maps, settled unrestricted Werner distillability, or justified tensoring merely
k-positive maps outside CP.
    The general one-input dimension domains are sufficient; omitted dimensions are not decided
here. The two-input CP classification is conditional on code existence and concerns t = 1/2.
Its lower positivity order and explicit upper witness rank do not match. No efficient physical
implementation, new AME construction or general advantage over standard positive maps is
established. The relevance is the structure of these encoded reduction products and the bounds
proved for them.


7     Reproducibility and responsibility
The accompanying author scripts use rational arithmetic and finite-field evaluation counts:

    • linear449-controls.py checks 360 scalar and combinatorial cases for the linear-dimension
      argument; these are not full Choi-matrix tests.

    • two-input451-controls.py checks coefficients, signs and sufficient orders for 299 integer
      dimensions.

    • two-input452-sparse.py constructs sparse MDS states at d = 7, 13, checks every three-
      party marginal, and evaluates all 16 physical trace subsets by exact prime cyclotomic sums.
      It reproduces the Choi value −3/104 at d = 13.

    • two-input452-rank-search.py checks 169 nested reference projections at d = 13; the first
      negative vector within that family has rank 164. This search does not establish optimality.

    • two-input453-strip.py evaluates five concrete reference-strip cases by weighted integer
      Gram contractions, checking every entry of the table in Lemma 7; its d = 43, h = 9 result is
      exactly −1/2408.

The proofs, rather than these finite controls, support the universal statements. The scripts are
author checks, including two distinct computational formulations, not an independent referee
audit or a formal proof.
