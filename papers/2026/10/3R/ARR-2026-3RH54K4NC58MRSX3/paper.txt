An exact relaxation optimum in a one-parameter family of four
                 quantum measurement bases
                                         Lluis Eriksson
                                 Independent researcher, Sweden

                                       11 October 2026
                                  Working manuscript, version 1



                                            Abstract
         Four orthonormal bases define a quantum measurement channel by averaging their
     nonselective rank-one projective measurements. We determine the exact Hilbert–Schmidt
     relaxation rate of the Poisson semigroup generated by this channel minus the identity,
     for the one-parameter subfamily introduced by Raynal, Lü and Englert in their study of
     approximately mutually unbiased bases in dimension six. A block decomposition gives
     the complete spectrum as explicit quadratic polynomials in the parameter. The largest
     relaxation rate within this subfamily, with equal measurement probabilities and unit
     total event rate, is exactly 73/100, attained uniquely at u = 7/10. This parameter does
     not maximize the mean squared chordal distance between the bases. An exact rational
     comparison exhibits a larger mean distance with a smaller relaxation rate. We also prove
     the 2/3 ceiling for arbitrary mixtures of product-vector measurement bases on C2 ⊗ C3 ,
     which the construction exceeds. The optimality statement is restricted to the specified
     subfamily; no global four-basis optimality or new mutually unbiased basis construction is
     claimed.


1    Introduction and scope
Mutually unbiased bases are useful in quantum state determination and related measurement
problems [1, 3]. In dimension six, Raynal, Lü and Englert (RLE) constructed a two-parameter
family of four bases and identified a one-parameter subfamily that contains their maximizer of
the mean squared chordal distance [2]. Their family provides an explicit setting in which to
compare geometric and dynamical objectives.
    We use those existing bases, with their attribution retained throughout. The quantity
optimized here is the slowest exponential decay rate of a continuous-time, randomized measure-
ment process. The resulting exact spectrum yields a different optimum from the mean-distance
objective. The general possibility that distinct objectives have distinct optimizers is not the
contribution; the result is the explicit spectral formula, its sharp optimization on the RLE
subfamily, and an exact comparison of the two objectives there.
    Measurement-induced mixing is related in motivation to noncommuting block-measurement
diffusion [5], but the processes differ. The present generator is a fixed average of complete
rank-one measurements minus the identity. It is not a small-angle product limit and contains
no additional Hamiltonian. Measurement-channel spectra also occur in quantum verification
[3] and classical shadows [4]. We use the standard operator and Gram-matrix framework
rather than claim that framework as new.
    Every optimality assertion below specifies the admissible family and fixes both the measure-
ment probabilities and the total event rate. In particular, the theorem does not optimize over
the full two-parameter RLE family, over all four bases in C6 , or over physical implementation

5    Product measurements and unrestricted four-basis bounds
Proposition 5.1 (Product-vector ceiling). Let T be any probability-weighted mixture of
complete orthonormal bases consisting of product vectors on C2 ⊗ C3 . For the rate-one
generator T − id, γ ≤ 2/3. This includes orthonormal product bases that are not tensor
products of two fixed local bases.
Proof. Consider the three-dimensional operator subspace V = {A ⊗ I3 : Tr A = 0}. The
orthogonal projection of a rank-one product projector Π = ρA ⊗ ρB onto V is
                                                                       1
                        QV Π = (ρA − I2 /2) ⊗ I3 /3,      ∥QV Π∥22 = .
                                                                       6
Every basis has six projectors, and its dephasing superoperator is k |Πk )(Πk |. Its compressed
                                                                    P

superoperator trace on V is therefore one, and the same holds for every probability mixture.
The largest eigenvalue of T |H0 is at least this compressed trace divided by dim V , namely 1/3.
Equation (2) proves the claim.
    The ceiling is sharp. For example, average independently over the three Pauli measurement
bases on the qubit and four mutually unbiased measurement bases on the qutrit. The local
traceless eigenvalues are 1/3 and 1/4, and the correlation-sector eigenvalue is 1/12, giving
γ = 2/3. These finite local constructions are standard [1]. The rate 73/100 is therefore 9.5%
larger than the product-vector ceiling, with the total event rate held fixed. This is a rate
comparison, not a statement about gate counts, laboratory time per measurement or energetic
cost.
    Let γ4⋆ (6) denote the supremum over all four bases with uniform probabilities in dimension
six. The construction and the elementary projector bound give

To see the upper bound, restrict each Ej to H0 , where it is a nonzero orthogonal projection.
On a unit vector in the range of any one of them, the quadratic form of their sum is at least
one. Thus λmax (4T |H0 ) ≥ 1. Equality in this bound requires the four traceless projection
ranges to be mutually orthogonal, equivalently four mutually unbiased bases; this standard
connection is also present in the verification formulation of [3]. We neither establish equality
nor sharpen the unrestricted upper bound. In particular, 73/100 is not asserted to be the
global optimum.


6    Reproducibility, attribution and limitations
The accompanying verify.py constructs the four matrices at the algebraic optimum, checks
unitarity exactly, forms their Gram matrix directly and checks its characteristic polynomial
over the rationals. Independently, it builds the polynomial block matrix (11), verifies the
full characteristic polynomial over Q[u], and checks the exact distance comparison and the
polynomial inequalities in the optimization proof. It uses Python and SymPy; no floating-
point tolerance is needed for these checks. The supplementary verification.json records
the actual run. The supplement contains source and environment requirements, but the
mathematical argument is self-contained in this manuscript.
    The RLE bases, their parameter relation and their original distance objective are prior
work. The projector-frame interpretation is also standard. A focused literature search did not
identify the particular sharp subfamily relaxation optimum proved here, but that search is
not an exhaustive priority certificate. The paper makes a narrowly stated contribution and
leaves its novelty and significance open to independent assessment. No formal proof-assistant
verification or independent human review is claimed.
