Six-spin Lorentzian obstructions for clique blowups
of independence-stable graphs
Lluis Eriksson
Independent researcher, Sweden
17 October 2026
Working-paper manuscript
Abstract
Let F be a connected simple graph with at least one edge, and suppose that deleting
any edge leaves its independence number unchanged. For every integer s ≥ 2, we construct
a strictly positive rational antiferromagnetic matrix of rank two on six spins for which the
partition polynomial of the clique blowup F[Ks] is not Lorentzian. The number of spins is
independent of the order and degrees of F and of the blowup size. The matrix itself may
depend on F and s. The proof combines the classical equality case of Turan’s theorem with
a conditioned Hessian identity. Edge-deletion independence stability forces all minimum-
edge occupations onto maximum independent sets. The ﬁrst four active proﬁles then form
a nonsingular block of inertia (2, 2, 0); a two-stage limiting argument realizes this block
using six spins. The theorem includes clique blowups of all connected biregular bipartite
graphs with at least one edge other than K2, and of connected graphs with at least one
edge and a unique maximum independent set. Exact checks cover 720 blowups of 360
bases of orders three through seven, and four explicit positive six-spin witnesses. The
connected-source classiﬁcation conjecture remains open.

We use the spectral convention that K is antiferromagnetic when it has at most one positive
eigenvalue. Lee–Oh–Seo prove that clique partition polynomials are Lorentzian for every such
target and propose a converse [ 2]. We use the connected-source formulation discussed by
Lee–Seo [ 3]: must a connected graph whose partition polynomial is Lorentzian for every anti-
ferromagnetic target be a clique? Connectedness is necessary because partition polynomials
factor over components and Lorentzian polynomials are closed under products [ 1].
For a homogeneous polynomial of degree n ≥ 2 with strictly positive coeﬃcients on the
full degree- n simplex, Lorentzianity is equivalent to every partial derivative of total order
n − 2 having a Hessian with at most one positive eigenvalue [ 1, Theorem 2.25]. The partition
polynomials for positive targets have this full support. Inertia triples list positive, negative
and zero eigenvalue counts, in that order.
Write α(F) for the independence number. A graph is α−-stable if α(F − e) = α(F) for
every edge e. This terminology and its characterization by maximum independent sets are
classical [ 5, Theorem 2.1]. The graph F[Ks] replaces each vertex of F by a clique of order s,
and each edge by all edges between the corresponding cliques.

The six-spin bound is uniform. The rational parameters are not asserted to be uniform,
and the theorem does not identify the smallest possible number of spins.
The preceding work [ 4] establishes a positive rank-two obstruction for connected noncliques
with no true twins among their maximum-degree vertices, together with several other source
classes. Every vertex of F[Ks] has true twins when s ≥ 2, so that maximum-degree theorem
does not apply to these blowups. Some special families overlap earlier results, including
particular joins of cliques. The present statement supplies one criterion for all α−-stable bases
and a spin bound independent of graph size. It is not a claim to solve the full conjecture.
For a concrete distinction, C6[K2] is covered here. It is 5-regular with true twins, and
adjacent pairs have either two or four common neighbours, so it is not edge-regular. The
earlier neighbourhood-gap quantities satisfy b = c = 2, so that strict-gap criterion also does
not apply. In contrast, P3[Ks] overlaps the earlier join-of-cliques family. The abstract block
form is already familiar from [ 4]; the contribution here is its uniform occurrence at the ﬁrst
active proﬁles, together with suppression of every later proﬁle by six-spin compression.

3 Minimum-edge occupations
We ﬁrst record the classical stability characterization and the part used in the proof.
Lemma 3 (Classical; see [ 5]). The following are equivalent:
1. α(F − e) =α(F) for every edge e;
2. every maximum independent set I is 2-dominating: |NF (u) ∩ I| ≥ 2 for every u /∈ I.
Under these conditions, two maximum independent sets cannot diﬀer by replacing a single
vertex.
Proof. Maximality excludes zero neighbours in I. If u has exactly one neighbour v ∈ I,
deleting uv makes I ∪ {u} independent. Conversely, an independent (α(F) + 1)-set created
by deleting uv must contain both endpoints; removing u gives a maximum independent set in
which u has just the neighbour v. For the last assertion, a replacement vertex is nonadjacent
to all the retained vertices, and can have at most one neighbour in the original maximum
independent set.

Lemma 4. The minimum number of edges induced by an h-vertex subset of G is e0. Every
minimizing subset is obtained from a maximum independent set I of F by selecting all vertices

Proof. The independence number of every induced subgraph of G is at most α. Turan’s
theorem and its equality case [ 7], applied to the complement of an h-vertex induced subgraph,
imply the bound ( 5). Equality requires a disjoint union of α cliques: one of order s − 1 and
the others of order s. The construction in the statement attains it.
It remains to exclude other equality occupations. Choose one occupied base class from
each of these α clique components. Their base vertices form a maximum independent set in
F. If a component occupied two or more base classes, replacing its chosen representative by
another would give two maximum independent sets diﬀering in one vertex. This contradicts
Lemma 3. Therefore each component occupies one class, and its size determines the asserted
construction. The omitted vertex has s choices.
This argument uses the equality case of a classical extremal theorem. It does not assert a
new extremal edge bound.

6 Examples and a ﬁnite construction procedure
Corollary 7. Theorem 1 applies to every connected biregular bipartite base with at least one
edge other than K2.
Proof. Let the bipartition sizes be a ≤ b, with respective degrees D ≥ d > 0, so aD = bd. For
an independent set J,
D|J ∩ A| + d|J ∩ B| ≤ | E(F)| = db.
It follows that α(F) = b. If D > d , equality in cardinality forces J = B, so the maximum
independent set is unique. If D = d, equality means every edge has exactly one endpoint in J,
so the complement is also independent. Connectedness gives just the two bipartition classes.
Except for K2, those two classes cannot diﬀer by a single-vertex replacement. In either case a
maximum independent set cannot have an outside vertex with just one neighbour in it; apply
Lemma 3.
Corollary 8.Every connected base with at least one edge and a unique maximum independent
set satisﬁes Theorem 1.
Proof. An outside vertex with one neighbour would give a diﬀerent maximum independent
set by replacement. The zero-neighbour case contradicts maximality.
This graph-class inclusion is classical [ 6, Lemma 2.3]; the conclusion about six-spin ob-
structions is its application here.

7 Exact checks and reproducibility
All 994 connected graphs of orders three through seven in the NetworkX graph atlas were
screened by two equivalent deﬁnitions: edge deletion and the maximum-independent-set neigh-
bour condition. Exactly 360 satisfy the hypothesis. For each such base, both s = 2and s = 3
were checked, giving 720 blowups. For every case, a separate enumeration of bounded occupa-
tion vectors found all minimum-edge patterns. They agreed exactly with Lemma 4, including
multiplicities, and the ﬁrst four-proﬁle block agreed with Proposition 5. These checks support
the argument; the general theorem follows from its proof, not an extrapolation of this census.

8 Scope and remaining questions
The result handles uniform clique blowups of α−-stable bases. It does not assert that every
noncomplete base is stable: odd cycles, for example, have critical edges and fall outside the hy-
pothesis. It does not classify all graphs whose partition polynomials are universally Lorentzian.
The six-spin construction also leaves open whether fewer spins suﬃce, whether target param-
eters can be chosen uniformly for useful subclasses, and whether eﬀective parameter bounds
can avoid full enumeration.
The proof uses a general transfer mechanism: a nonsingular initial proﬁle block can be
realized by a small number of evaluation nodes, with a separate base spin and pin spin.

Applying that mechanism requires an actual initial block with the needed inertia; an arbitrary
principal minor at later proﬁles does not automatically give the same spin bound.
