CLAIM-BY-CLAIM MATHEMATICAL AUDIT

1. Channel definition and Lemma 2 (PDF pp. 1–3).

The map T_U uses a maximally mixed ancilla and is unital, completely positive and trace preserving. With the specified output-first vectorization, the ancillary blocks give rho_U=(Dm)^(-1) sum_ab |vec(U_ab)><vec(U_ab)|. Hence its pairing with the orthogonal complementary projection is exactly the normalized squared Hilbert–Schmidt distance to S tensor M_m. The m^2 identity in the reshuffled vectorized quadratic form is correct; an m identity would have been wrong. The input marginal carries a transpose, which the manuscript correctly acknowledges later.

Minimizing the joint distance over X and U in either order proves the polar-distance formulation. Although S tensor M_m is unbounded, existence follows from projecting a minimizing unitary, so no compactness of the subspace itself is assumed. A singular square X admits a unitary extension of its polar partial isometry. The duality formula uses a compact unit sphere in the nonzero finite-dimensional subspace and trace-norm/unitary duality; both maxima may be interchanged. Its normalization is consistent with P_S=D^(-1) sum_l |vec(B_l)><vec(B_l)|.

The channel certificate uses the intermediate A tuple, not the normalized B tuple. This distinction is necessary: the stochastic sums belong to A. Its positive weights and independent coefficient vectors imply support(rho_0)=vec(S), not just inclusion. Normalized trace of rho_0 is one in the ordinary density-matrix convention. Rationality follows directly from rational A and the rational orthonormal B construction; no rational square root of a general Gram matrix is required.

The actual Alice question contains the information determining its parity bit; the checked answer position can be retained separately when questions coincide. Finite rational probabilities can be expanded computably; no polynomial bound in the machine description is needed.

With c=12/25, GG*=I plus off-diagonal cE, and ||E||_op<=2. The squared singular-value interval [1/25,49/25], hence singular values [1/5,7/5], is correct. Dividing by (s+1)^2 yields (s-1)^2 <=(25/36)(s^2-1)^2, so the per-gate distance bound is (4/25)||E||_2^2. Twenty-five copies have total scalar size 50. For reverse rounding, a nearest polar unitary of the block-diagonal W can be chosen block diagonal even if W is singular. Singleton multiplicities control the weighted squared label-to-unitary distance by 2 Delta^2. Orthogonal coefficient supports give ||K-W||_2<=sqrt(2) Delta. The operator-norm bound 7/5 then gives C_0=(144/25)(3+2sqrt(2)) for the global squared unitarity defect. Its gate contributions are exactly 50c^2 e_g^2/D. 4. Theorem 1, fixed gap and exact SDP certificate (pp. 2 and 5).

Completeness gives h_infinity <=17/(50*2^40)=3.092281986027956e-13, below gamma/4, where gamma=1/36288000000=2.7557319223985892e-11. It uses approaching strategies, so no attainment of the source supremum is needed.

In soundness, h_m<gamma<1 ensures that the projected X is nonzero. Its normalization W satisfies distance squared <=2-2sqrt(1-h_m)<=2h_m. Consequently the rounded game error is strictly below 1750*8000*gamma=1/2592, which is strictly below 1/1296. This is a real contradiction with NO soundness, not an unjustified assertion of perfect satisfaction. The limit over m preserves the lower bound. Error gamma/8 separates the YES upper bound and NO lower bound at 5gamma/8. The promise certificate holds in both alternatives and does not itself encode a test for halting.

5. Convex closure, Corollary 4, amplification (pp. 3 and 6).

Finite-dimensional tracial ancillary algebras permit arbitrary convex weights over full matrix blocks. This gives exactly conv(F_matr), and linear optimization has the same infimum after convexification and closure. The minimum over the closure exists because the ambient set of finite-dimensional channels is compact. A finite ancillary optimizer need not exist. This agrees with Musat Proposition 3.8, which additionally identifies the two closures. The manuscript carefully avoids identifying that closure with all finite-von-Neumann-algebra factorizations. [Musat, Proposition 3.8: https://arxiv.org/html/2512.00432v1]

At fixed m, real quantifier elimination applies to the compact polynomial unitary constraints and rational quadratic objective. A hypothetical recursive bound b(L) would allow enumerating every m<=b(L), rather than incorrectly evaluating only b(L). The gamma/16 numerical margins are 7gamma/16 and 15gamma/16, separated by 3gamma/4. This proves the corollary. Replication gives monotonicity along divisibility, not along every consecutive m; the factorial subsequence is a valid cofinal decreasing sequence. The stabilized tensor identity follows by permutation of tensor factors and replication into multiples of k. No finite stabilization dimension follows from that identity.

6. Proposition 5, completely positive projection (p. 6).

Complete positivity is a property of the map Pi_S, not positivity of its matrix on vectorized Hilbert space. The block-positivity argument yields Psi(U)=A^(1/2) C A^(1/2), with C contractive, including singular A after restriction to its support. Normalized Schatten Hölder bounds its 2-norm by ||A||_2; U=I attains the bound. Orthogonal projection duality then gives h_m=1-||Pi_S(I)||_2^2 for every m. The formula is rational and computable when Pi_S is rational. DJKR use unnormalized Schatten norms and explicit CP hypotheses in their multiplicativity statements; their result does not contradict the general non-CP subspace case. [DJKR, Theorem 13 and the block argument in Theorem 12: https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0506196]

LITERATURE POSITION AND SIGNIFICANCE

Yu–Duan–Xu Lemma 2 defines exactly the unnormalized-in-X minimization called C_Phi here, with the same division by system dimension. Their Theorem 2 tensors with a Schur channel and exploits its diagonal Kraus span. It does not establish stabilization under a full M_m Kraus span. The present credit and distinction are accurate. The distance identity itself is established background. [Yu–Duan–Xu, Lemma 2 and Theorem 2: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.1172]

Lee–Watrous Definition 1 and Section 3 concern a finite-dimensional unitary quadratic objective and an input-specified inverse-polynomial promise gap. The present theorem has a different unbounded ancillary quantifier; it is not a strengthening from NP-hardness to undecidability for one identical fixed-dimensional problem. The manuscript makes this distinction. [Lee–Watrous: https://arxiv.org/html/1902.03164]

Goldbring–Hart already prove noncomputability of the hyperfinite factor's universal theory and exclude computable general matrix-moment dimension bounds, including low-degree consequences. Thus “noncomputable tracial optimization” and “no recursive dimension bound” are not independently new phenomena. [Goldbring–Hart, Corollary 3.9, Theorem 4.1 and Remark 4.2: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.05629]

Cleve–Culf–Taller Section 6.4 gives constant-gap hardness for tracial tilted XOR games and a constrained noncommutative Max-Cut objective. This is a closer precedent than general logical undecidability, but it is not the exact single-projection cost with an explicit rational zero-cost bistochastic state treated here. [Cleve–Culf–Taller, Theorem 6.14 and Corollary 6.15: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06876v1]

I also checked the primary records for Haagerup–Musat (https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.6476) and Musat–Rordam (https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.10182v2); detailed closure verification above uses Musat's stated proposition. Targeted searches for factorizable-channel undecidability, Kraus-space distance, and tracial unitary-subspace hardness did not establish an earlier exact simultaneous theorem. These searches were selective and return noisy results; absence of a found match does not establish priority.

The strongest novelty candidate is the simultaneous combination of (i) one real rational orthogonal projection test, (ii) a fixed positive approximation gap over unrestricted finite tracial ancillas, and (iii) an explicitly given rational bistochastic optimizer of the zero-valued SDP relaxation whose support equals the kernel. The direct quadratic transfer and careful rounding establish this without a determinant witness. Most technical machinery, however, is reused from the supplied compiler, and the two formulations are closely related. This supports a substantive specialist result, not a field-shaping or historic score. The gap is extremely small, D varies with the machine, and the work supplies no circuit, capacity, laboratory or fixed-D claim.
