Undecidable projection costs for quantum channels
with ﬁnite tracial ancillas
Lluis Eriksson
17 October 2026
Research draft
Abstract
We transfer a quantitative unitary-relation construction to a quadratic optimization
problem for quantum channels. The input is a real rational orthogonal projection on the
Choi space, together with an explicit rational bistochastic Choi state supported exactly on
its kernel. The minimum cost over all bistochastic channels is therefore zero. Nevertheless,
minimizing the same cost over channels implemented by a unitary interaction with an unre-
stricted ﬁnite tracial ancilla is undecidable at one ﬁxed additive precision. The objective is
a projective measurement probability, not a determinant. Its equivalent matrix formulation
is the squared distance between a unitary and an ampliﬁed linear subspace. This distance
at ﬁxed dimension is the Kraus-space quantity studied by Yu, Duan and Xu; we examine its
stabilization under a completely depolarizing ancillary channel. We prove the transfer with
explicit constants, derive the absence of a recursive uniform ancilla bound, and give the
exact answer when the subspace projection is completely positive. The source hardness and
the relation compiler are inherited results; no general novelty claim about noncomputable
tracial optimization is made.

5 Ancilla bounds, ampliﬁcation and a computable boundary
Corollary 4. No total recursive function of the encoded input length uniformly bounds an
ancilla size m for which vm(H) ≤ v∞ (H) + γ/8 on the inputs of Theorem 1.
Proof. At ﬁxed m, unitarity is a ﬁnite system of real polynomial equations and the objective
is quadratic with rational coeﬃcients. The feasible set is compact. Real quantiﬁer elimination
therefore computes vm to arbitrary rational precision, uniformly in the ﬁnite input.
If a proposed bound is b(L), compute every vm, 1 ≤ m ≤ b(L), to error γ/16 and take
their minimum. In YES instances this is at most 7γ/16; in NO instances it is at least 15γ/16.
Comparison with 3γ/4 decides halting.
Since U ↦→ U ⊗ Ik preserves normalized cost, hmk(S) ≤ hm(S). The sequence hn!(S)
decreases to h∞ (S). Indeed every ﬁxed m divides all suﬃciently large factorials. Finite-
dimensional quantiﬁer elimination and rational error bounds consequently provide upper semi-
computability of h∞ ; Theorem 1 rules out a uniform computable convergence modulus at the
displayed precision. This does not assert that every individual input has a noncomputable
value.
For any ﬁxed k, associativity and a permutation of tensor factors give
hm(S ⊗ Mk) = hkm(S), h ∞ (S ⊗ Mk) = h∞ (S).
For the second identity, the inﬁmum over multiples is no smaller than the unrestricted one, while
replication of any witness into a multiple of k gives the reverse inequality. This invariance does
not provide a ﬁnite stabilization dimension.

6 Veriﬁcation, dependencies and remaining questions
The transfer is analytic and uniform in m. Finite complex-matrix diagnostics check projec-
tion, polar-distance, Choi, marginal and ampliﬁcation identities; rational arithmetic checks the
projection certiﬁcate and the gap margins. Such tests do not prove source-game undecidabil-
ity, replace the cited relation construction, or constitute a proof-assistant veriﬁcation. The
accompanying research record identiﬁes exact ﬁles and rerun results.
The dependency on [ 1] is substantial: its rational compiler, singleton proportions, question
normalization and game-rounding constants are reused. The forward estimate here is proved
directly from the gate singular values, and the reverse estimate applies to a projected arbitrary
unitary. The channel formulation and the zero-relaxation certiﬁcate make the consequence
externally interpretable, but do not remove that dependence. The interpretations of ( 10) and
(12) are established background, explicitly credited above. Targeted literature checking has not
certiﬁed worldwide priority.
The theorem gives no ﬁxed value of D, no bounded circuit architecture, no laboratory
resource estimate, and no communication-capacity result. It also does not show undecidability
of mixed-unitary membership at a ﬁxed ﬁnite dimension. Determining the least number of
Kraus directions needed for this phenomenon, or useful restrictions beyond complete positivity
of ΠS with eﬀective ancilla bounds, remain separate questions.
