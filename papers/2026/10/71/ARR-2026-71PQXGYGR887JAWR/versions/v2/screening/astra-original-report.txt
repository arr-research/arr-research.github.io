2. Supported scientific statement and its quantifiers

For fixed 0<kappa,lambda<1 and alpha=1 or a fixed finite alpha>1, the complete
uniform-in-finite-budget assisted minimax loss law holds along every prescribed
finite nonnegative path n(E) exactly when n(E)/E tends to zero. The benchmark
is lambda times the actual thermal attenuator assisted capacity, with the true
two-mode complementary entropy. The analogous compound law for fixed but
unknown kappa in [kappa0,1] and n in [0,N(E)], relative to the worst signal
(kappa0,N(E)), holds exactly when N(E)/E tends to zero, for fixed 0<kappa0<1.

The channel is fixed throughout a code. The coding limit is taken at each
fixed E and finite noise/envelope before E tends to infinity. Error is
average-message error, uniform over the compound index; assistance is
unlimited. The physical input constraint is expected block-average photon
number, and the type construction actually confines encoder outputs to
subspaces of the permitted block energy. Reset states may have arbitrary
orientation, infinite mean energy and, for alpha>1, infinite Shannon entropy.
No peak constraint on each transmitted mode is asserted.

The theorem is not uniform over every unspecified o(E) path at a given E,
over kappa0 tending to zero, lambda tending to an endpoint, or alpha tending
to one or infinity. It does not concern within-code variation. Necessity
excludes the FULL budget curve on a growing budget; it does not say that
every fixed small budget has the same threshold. These restrictions are
essential and are stated in the manuscript.

3. Gaussian benchmark, entropy estimate and bounded Choi form

I checked the covariance/entropy formula against Holevo–Werner's selected
primary passages and the operational use against Holevo–Shirokov Theorem 1.
The two complementary occupations, including the purification of the thermal
environment, are necessary. Replacing them by the weak one-mode complement
would change the benchmark and invalidate subsequent arguments; the paper
does not make that substitution. Gaussian optimality is used for the signal
channel, not asserted for the signal-plus-reset mixture.

For the Gibbs pullback, the displayed Bogoliubov transformation gives the
coefficient a(E;n) of the input number operator, including the contribution
from the purifier. The positive-form interpretation is appropriate for an
unbounded logarithm. Finite-energy input expectations and finite Fock
approximations justify this calculation; it is not an unjustified bounded
adjoint identity. Relative-entropy data processing gives the entropy-difference
bound with E|beta_E-a|. I checked the radical inequalities and the stated Xi
bound. Xi vanishes for the whole positive n=o(E) regime, including very small
n, without differentiating the path n(E). The derivative in Eq. (4) holds the
channel noise fixed. Its upper bound follows from a>=0 and M>=kappa E;
monotonicity follows from enlarging the feasible input set.

The flat Choi inequality is correctly attributed to Holevo 1004.0196v3,
Proposition 2 and its one-mode example: amplitude sqrt(kappa), noise
coefficient (1-kappa)(n+1/2), and norm 1/[(1-kappa)(n+1)]. I read that primary
statement and proof. The thermal regularization in the manuscript correctly
turns the form estimate into an operator estimate on finite input support,
then sandwiches with sqrt(rho^T). It does not use an unnormalizable Bell
vector as a density operator or claim this Gaussian Choi theorem as new.

The largest-eigenvalue estimate is valid: isolating a pure eigencomponent
costs at most the established 2h(w) mixture term, while the remaining input
has mean at most E/(1-w). Integrating Eq. (4) yields the displayed bound on w.
The lower bound C>=g(M)-g(n) forces C to diverge throughout n=o(E).
Holevo 1003.5765v1, Proposition 2 supplies Phi(I)=I/kappa, with the correct
photon/amplitude conversion. This gives the uniform small vacuum probability
for every near-capacity input. None of these steps assumes the optimizing
input of the mixed channel is thermal.

4. All-input converse and infinite-reference branch discrimination

The initial input split is indispensable and present. Inputs whose MIXED
mutual information is already below lambda C-h(lambda)-1 need no detector.
Every remaining input has signal information at least C-Delta with one
constant Delta, independent of the reset-mixture weight. Thus the subsequent
spectral and pinching estimates cover all relevant inputs. Combined with the
first case, they cover the entire physical energy ball.

D0 is consistently computed relative to the original rho_R tensor I_B.
That reference is unnormalized and has infinite trace, so normalized-state
relative-entropy theorems cannot be applied to it directly. The manuscript
does not do so. In particular, after conditioning on the good output window,
D0 includes D(rho_R^g || rho_R). It is not silently replaced by the negative
conditional entropy relative to the conditioned marginal. Domination of
conditioned reference marginals by rho_R/p keeps the relevant cross entropies
finite. The weighted pinching identity and the bad-block order bound give
Eq. (9) without a continuity loss proportional to log E.

The normalized comparator sigma_g is a different object. I checked both its
operator-order constant L and the +log_2(v) normalization term in Eq. (10).
On the chosen window, log L differs from log S only by O(sqrt(ln S)), whereas
the normalized relative entropy is log S minus lower-order terms. The signs
and factors are consistent.

Audenaert 1102.3040v2 Theorem 4 implies Eq. (11) by writing
s=L^{-1}r+(1-L^{-1})z. The factor 1/(1-1/L) is needed and is retained.
I reread the theorem and its selected proof. The source's printed auxiliary
difference has a sign inconsistency; reversing that auxiliary orientation
repairs the identity used in the proof, without changing the theorem or its
application. This is accurately disclosed. For an infinite reference, common
finite compressions with an overflow flag preserve normalization and order.
After embedding, trace-norm convergence, lower semicontinuity and data
processing give convergence of the relative entropies. Hence the extension
is justified, rather than inferred from a finite numerical test.

The resulting Helstrom projection lies in the nonvacuum receiver window.
Its original-state signal error is controlled by the discarded signal mass
plus normalized testing error; its reset acceptance is a*v times the other
normalized error. The vacuum component contributes zero. The test may depend
on the input and is only an analytical converse device, not a proposed
universal decoder. Binary Fano then controls the missing flag entropy
uniformly in the witness weight and in all retained inputs.

I checked the explicit R_minus envelope as well. Its positivity and
z_star<=1/2 restrictions are eventual restrictions, correctly stated. The
lower bounds p_star,v_star, the estimate T beta_M<=(2t+1)/ln2, and the
separation error epsilon_star give the announced branch error without a
missing factor of two. All terms vanish along each prescribed positive
sublinear path. They do not supply one rate valid over all unspecified paths.

Finally, the three-component entropy grouping in Eq. (12) is valid for the
finite-energy witness. The signal-plus-thermal group has mean at most M;
the vacuum/thermal ambiguity accounts for q h(a/(M+1)). Together with the
all-input argument and the constrained assisted capacity formula this gives
the required capacity converse. Evaluation at the thermal input supplies the
other inequality, and is not substituted for the all-input maximization.

5. Achievability, physical cost, budgets and unknown signal parameters

The full common-code lower bound has a fixed finite input and a fixed
physical receiver cutoff. It never bounds the reset's energy. The new
derivation of U_E is sound: the tail of gamma_(E-1) is a shifted thermal
state with the same entropy g(E-1); mutual-information mixing and I<=2S
bound the tail loss. The change from E-1 to E is taken at the same n using
Eq. (4), not by substituting n(E-1). The larger stated U_E dominates that
loss and tends to zero.

For n<=E the signal output has mean at most E, so the E^4 cutoff has a small
signal tail. Winter's conditional-entropy continuity bound uses the finite
reference dimension, not the dimension or entropy of the reset. The effective
reset may have arbitrary orientation before the cutoff. Its compressed
top-m positive part has rank at most m+1 and retains the needed spectral
mass. The hypothetical spectral branch test again only bounds information;
it does not identify the actual decoder.

The physical cost step is now explicit and correctly separated from Berta
et al.'s coding theorem. In the exact type subspace all vectors, including
superpositions, have the same total Fock energy. The total-correlation
identity gives I(pi_l,M^tensor l)>=l I(rho_l,M)-2d_l, with d_l at most
J log_2(l+1). Data processing bounds the output correlation; the environment
term is nonnegative. For a fixed finite input/output cut all relevant
entropies and environments can be finite. Approximating probabilities inside
the available energy slack preserves the cost. Berta Theorem 1/Lemma 9 then
applies to the entire arbitrary-index finite-dimensional compound, with
uniform average-message error and one encoder/decoder. Superdense coding
converts the quantum assisted rate to twice that classical rate while keeping
the physical channel input in the type subspace. Ordinary vacuum padding
handles unused positions if a desired length is not a multiple of the chosen
type block. No useful uniform blocklength is claimed.

This corrects the earlier pinpoint attribution issue known in the retained
context: the type information estimate is proved here and attributed to the
appropriate previous type lemma; Berta supplies the compound coding step.
The previously requested tail derivation is also present. Those earlier
comments are not unresolved objections to this PDF.

The spectral entropy bounds and budget matching include B=0, budgets below
one, clipped Shannon witnesses, and unbounded finite budgets. The Shannon
comparison uses an ABSOLUTE clipped-ratio bound, so it does not assume that
the witness parameter is always below the target. The modulus
|f(x)-f(y)|<=f(|x-y|) is suitable at zero, where a bounded derivative argument
would fail. For fixed alpha>1 the chosen vacuum weight is exactly admissible
and the top-m alpha-power estimate has the correct positive-part clipping.
This establishes uniformity in B without an energy constraint on general
resets.

For the unknown signal family, the degradation comparison is applied only
to the SIGNAL: additional attenuation to kappa0 followed by nonnegative
Gaussian noise reaches (kappa0,N). The requisite added noise is nonnegative
for every kappa>=kappa0,n<=N. It is not claimed that applying this operation
leaves the reset unchanged or orders the mixed channels. The same truncated
input therefore has at least the worst signal information throughout the
family. The effective finite compound is indexed by (kappa,n,tau), not by
tau alone. The converse selects the worst admissible signal. Endpoint
kappa=1 and n=0 in the achievability family are harmless; the positive-noise
Choi estimate is not applied at its singular identity endpoint.

6. The new dimension-independent strict-gap lemma

I independently reconstructed the lemma's chain of inequalities before
reading the included supportive audit627. It works for normalized states on
separable Hilbert spaces with finite c=I(R:B)_omega, without finite separate
marginal or reset entropies.

Because the two branches have the same R marginal,
I(R:BX)=lambda c. Data processing gives 0<=L=lambda c-I(R:B)_z.
The finite flag chain rules yield
h(lambda)-L=chi_B+H(X|RB), with each quantity finite. This avoids an
infinity-minus-infinity entropy calculation.

The convention F=||sqrt(r)sqrt(s)||_1 is ROOT fidelity throughout. Thus
D>=-2 log_2 F>=d_B^2/ln2. Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz and the Bures triangle
inequality imply chi_B>=lambda*q*d^2/ln2. The purification/Gram antecedent
has off-diagonal sqrt(lambda*q)*f0, not sqrt(lambda*q) times f0 squared.
Pinsker applied to this 2-by-2 Gram matrix gives
H(X|RB)>=2lambda*q*f0^2/ln2, with the correct constant.

Compatible approximate purifications give f0>=(a-d)_+, where
a=F(omega,omega_R tensor omega_B). The relative-entropy/fidelity inequality
gives a^2>=2^{-c}. The remaining scalar minimization is exact:
d^2+2(a-d)_+^2 >= 2a^2/3; on d<=a the remainder is
3(d-2a/3)^2, and d>=a is immediate. This proves the stated positive
epsilon_lambda(c)=2lambda*q*2^{-c}/(3 ln2).

The infinite-dimensional completion is sufficient. Finite-dimensional
spectral Jensen proves the relative-entropy bound via the affinity
Tr sqrt(r)sqrt(s), which is no greater than root fidelity. Common finite
compressions with an overflow flag and fidelity continuity extend the bound;
data processing provides the needed direction. The Gram matrix always has
dimension two, so no large-dimensional entropy continuity bound is hidden
there. Approximate purifications remove the need to assume attainment in a
particular fixed ancillary representation. Finite c and the finite flag
justify the chain rules.

I tested the possible escape routes analytically: identical reset/output
marginals make the marginal term vanish but leave positive joint fidelity;
widely separated reset/output marginals make chi_B positive; product omega
has c=L=0; singular supports are covered by limits. None contradicts the
bound. Finite diagnostics include product and pure entangled examples, but
the separable-space conclusion rests on this proof, not the diagnostics.

The Gram bound, the fidelity divergence inequality, Bures geometry and
Pinsker are prior ingredients. The manuscript credits them. A directed
primary search also located Jain–Nayak's 2007 author manuscript, whose
Lemma 3.7/Corollary 3.8 explicitly use the same pure-state eigenvalue and
purification route to a binary Holevo bound. That is an additional older
antecedent, not an exact theorem for the present operational minimax law.
I do not certify the combined strict-gap inequality as independently new.

7. Operational strict gap for arbitrary finite noise and sharp necessity

A pointwise state inequality would not by itself prove a common capacity
bound for every reset. Proposition 6 supplies the missing operational step.
I checked that it does not reuse the n<=E restriction of the earlier explicit
asymptotic cutoff construction.

For each fixed E>0 and finite N, first take a strict-energy-slack thermal
input and a finite input cutoff J. Lower semicontinuity of mutual information
and the signal capacity upper bound give convergence to C_*(E-s); continuity
of the explicit capacity as s decreases to zero supplies the desired rate
slack. Signal degradation gives the same lower bound throughout the envelope.
All signal outputs have mean at most E+N, even if N is much greater than E.
An output cutoff T can therefore be chosen after J to make the finite-reference
continuity error uniformly small. T may be extremely large: nothing here
asserts a blocklength or complexity bound uniform in E,N.

Applying the state lemma after the cutoff, then using the strictly increasing
G_lambda(x)=lambda*x+epsilon_lambda(x), gives one information lower bound
for all effective mixed channels. Its derivative is
lambda-(2lambda*q/3)2^{-x}>0 on x>=0. Choosing eta<C_* keeps the argument in
this domain. The exact type/compound-code construction then transfers the
bound to physical capacity for the FULL reset family. Entropy-budget
subfamilies and known resets inherit it. Neither reset entropy nor reset
energy enters this argument. A bound that happens to be negative at very
small C_* is merely vacuous, not inconsistent with nonnegative capacity.

For n=rE, r>0, the two complementary occupation coefficients are strictly
positive because d_r^2-delta^2(1-r)^2=4delta*r. Their product is delta*r.
The four g expansions consequently give
C_(kappa,rE)(E) -> log_2(1+kappa/(delta*r)). This is a direct asymptotic
consequence of the established Gaussian capacity formula, not a new channel
capacity formula.

If n/E does not vanish, there is an unbounded subsequence with n>=rE for
some fixed r>0. Added Gaussian noise orders the signal capacities, so they
are bounded by K_r+1 eventually on this subsequence. Proposition 6 then
keeps both deficits at most h(lambda)-epsilon_lambda(K_r+1), uniformly in
every budget. The choice B(E)=log_2(E+1) reaches the Shannon target exactly
and approaches h(lambda) for every fixed finite alpha>1. Thus the complete
uniform curve cannot converge. This also covers bounded capacities that
are too large for a converse based solely on capacity nonnegativity.

The envelope proof is the same argument at the worst signal, using the
compound operational bound just established for arbitrary finite N. No
monotonicity or smoothness of n(E) or N(E) is needed. A path can alternate
between zero and positive noise. Sufficiency follows on positive subsequences
from the displayed sublinear estimates and on zero subsequences from the
prior pure-loss theorem; necessity only needs its positive linear-or-larger
subsequence. I reread the prior pure-loss statement and its relevant proof,
including the kappa=1 detector endpoint and common-code construction. The
new theorem is conditional on that published working-paper result as an
explicit dependency; its prior assessment is not used as a proof.

No counterexample to the stated iff was found. In particular, N much greater
than E, oscillatory paths, n=0 subsequences, arbitrary reset orientation and
infinite reset energy do not expose a missing hypothesis. The proof does
not determine the critical-regime loss curve or prove necessity for each
restricted small-budget question, and the manuscript correctly says so.
