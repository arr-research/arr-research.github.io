2. Supported statement and exact quantifiers

For fixed kappa and lambda strictly between zero and one, fixed alpha=1 or finite alpha>1, and each prescribed positive occupation path n(E)=o(E), the known-reset and compound EA classical capacity deficits converge in absolute error, uniformly over every finite entropy budget B>=0, to b(lambda,(1-lambda)t_alpha(E,B)). The benchmark is lambda times the thermal signal channel's own constrained EA capacity. A single encoder/decoder at each fixed E,n,B serves the entire unknown fixed reset family, with average-message error uniform in that family and physical expected block energy at most E per use.

Proposition 3 is also supported: given a known positive envelope N(E)=o(E) and fixed 0<kappa_0<1, one common code handles all fixed kappa in [kappa_0,1], n in [0,N(E)] and admissible resets, relative to the worst signal benchmark at (kappa_0,N(E)). The claim allows arbitrary reset orientation and infinite reset energy. Super-Shannon reset classes may have infinite von Neumann entropy. The proof does not subtract those infinite entropies.

These statements do not mean that the parameters may change during a codeword, that one code works without knowing any envelope, or that the convergence rate is uniform over all unspecified sublinear paths at a given E. The coding limit comes first at each fixed parameter family. Neither a uniform coding blocklength nor an efficient encoder/decoder is established. The sublinear condition is sufficient; the paper does not characterize every critical or superlinear regime.

3. Gaussian benchmark and entropy-difference estimate

The capacity formula uses the true two-mode complement, including a purifier of the thermal environment. This distinction is essential. Holevo–Werner's Gaussian extremality and one-mode formulas, together with the constrained coding theorem of Holevo–Shirokov, support the signal benchmark. Gaussian optimality is invoked for the Gaussian signal, not for the non-Gaussian mixture with a reset.

The pulled-back complementary Gibbs logarithm has the stated coefficient a(E;n). The displayed Bogoliubov normal modes give the input coefficient delta[beta_ul+(beta_ul+beta_us)r]. The manuscript correctly treats this as a quadratic-form expectation identity on finite-mean states; an unbounded logarithm is not incorrectly passed through a bounded adjoint. Differentiation holds the channel occupation n fixed.

For an input of mean mu<=E, relative-entropy data processing gives
 S(rho)-S(Phi^c(rho)) <= g(E)-g(ul)-g(us)+(mu-E)(beta_E-a).
The last term is bounded by E|beta_E-a|. The sign and the absence of an assumption about reverse coherent-information optimization are correct.

I checked the radical bounds analytically. In particular D^2-[delta(E+n)+1]^2=4 kappa delta E n. For 0<n<=E this yields the stated ranges of ul and us. Combining those ranges with the logarithm inequality gives Xi(E,n)=O(1/E+n/E), with constants depending on fixed kappa. It tends to zero for every prescribed n=o(E), including extremely small positive n and paths with E/n diverging arbitrarily slowly. No derivative of n(E) enters the argument. The finite scalar checks are consistent with these identities but are not the reason for accepting them.

4. Choi order and reduction to near-capacity inputs

The constant c=1/[delta(n+1)] is correctly attributed to Holevo's established bounded Gaussian Choi operator. In his conventions the amplitude is sqrt(kappa), the noise parameter is delta(n+1/2), and the one-mode norm is [m+|kappa-1|/2]^{-1}. This is not a new Choi norm theorem.

The additional regularization argument is valid. For the auxiliary thermal input x, the product (ul+1)(us+1) bounds the largest eigenvalue of its reference/output state and is at least delta(n+1)(x+1). The weighted Choi form is therefore uniformly bounded. Testing on vectors with finite input-number support and sending x to infinity gives a bounded positive operator. Sandwiching its finite truncations and taking the trace-norm limit yields omega <= c rho_R tensor I. It does not require an unnormalizable Bell vector to be a state.

The fixed-channel derivative bound 0<=C'(E)<=2/(E ln 2) follows from capacity monotonicity, a>=0 and M>=kappa E. Isolating a largest pure eigencomponent of weight w gives I(rho,Phi)<=2h(w)+(1-w)C(E/(1-w)). Thus an input within a fixed Delta of the signal capacity has w<=constant/C. The selected Shirokov passage states the requisite almost-affinity bound. In the present finite-entropy setting it also follows directly by applying upper entropy mixing to the input and output and lower entropy mixing to the complement; a pure input has channel mutual information zero.

C>=g(M)-g(n)>=log_2 S+log_2 delta-log_2 e, where S=c(M+1), and S diverges for n=o(E). Hence w tends to zero even when capacity diverges very slowly. Holevo's regular-channel identity Phi(I)=I/kappa then gives signal vacuum probability at most w/kappa. These estimates apply to arbitrary coherent, non-Gaussian and infinite-rank finite-energy inputs.

5. Fixed-reference pinching, normalization, TRE and all-input converse

This is the principal substantive argument. It survives the checks that would invalidate a shortcut based only on thermal input or on an o(1) trace error multiplied by log E.

First, flagging gives I(rho,N)<=lambda I(rho,Phi). Inputs below lambda C-h(lambda)-1 already satisfy the desired upper bound since f(a)<=h(lambda). Every other input is within the same fixed Delta=(h(lambda)+1)/lambda of the signal capacity. The split covers all inputs without requiring existence of an optimizer.

Second, D0 uses the unnormalized infinite-trace reference rho_R tensor I. Its bounds are derived from finite entropy/cross-entropy identities and operator order, not from an inapplicable normalized-state inequality. After projecting the receiver onto levels 1 through T, the original rho_R remains the reference. The conditional good marginal generally differs from rho_R. The term D(rho_R^g||rho_R) is retained in D0(omega_g), and the pinching identity has the correct -h(p) term. The good and bad marginal bounds by rho_R/p and rho_R/(1-p) ensure finite cross entropies. The resulting lower bound (9) has the correct sign.

Third, sigma_g=rho_R tensor (P gamma_M P/v) is normalized. Its cross-entropy formula has +log_2 v. The operator-order coefficient L=(c/p)v(M+1)r_M^{-T} is correct. The comparison gives D(omega_g||sigma_g)>=log_2 S+log_2 v-O(1), whereas log_2 L=log_2 S+O(sqrt(ln S)). This relative scale, rather than a continuity estimate with the output dimension, is what handles all n=o(E).

Fourth, Audenaert's Theorem 4 gives the claimed order/trace-distance inequality after writing s=L^{-1}r+(1-L^{-1})z. I read its finite-dimensional proof and its supporting logarithmic derivative identities. The printed v2 proof does indeed define the auxiliary difference with the opposite sign to an identity subsequently used. Replacing that difference by (sigma-rho)/T(rho,sigma) repairs the identity and leaves the positive-part bound and proof intact. This is a local sign correction checked here, not an assertion that a separate official erratum was located.

For infinite R, a common finite compression with an orthogonal failure flag is a channel, preserves order and normalization, and converges after embedding. Data processing supplies an upper bound on the compressed relative entropies; lower semicontinuity supplies the reverse limiting inequality. The same finite L therefore passes to the limit. There is no unjustified appeal to general continuity of relative entropy.

The resulting trace distance tends to one. A Helstrom test within the non-vacuum window has small normalized errors; its original reset acceptance is av times the comparator acceptance, while its signal acceptance includes p. This controls H(F|RB) uniformly over a in [0,1] and over the entire near-capacity input class. The input-dependent test is only an analytical upper-bound device. It is not presented as the common operational decoder.

The entropy grouping with total weight lambda+qa has mean at most M and gives the stated all-input bound for the finite-entropy witness tau_a. Thermal-input evaluation supplies the opposite capacity inequality only after the all-input upper bound has been proved. No thermal optimizer for the mixture is assumed.

I also checked the explicit R_- envelope. The lower bounds p_* and v_* are valid. T beta_M is bounded by (2t+1)/ln 2 for M>=1. Combining the normalized relative-entropy lower bound and log L upper bound gives the stated epsilon_*. The actual weighted branch error is at most lambda(1-p_*)+epsilon_*; there is no missing factor of two from trace distance. Under the explicitly stated eventual conditions, Fano gives h(z_*). Xi, h((1+kappa E)^{-1}) and h(z_*) all vanish for the prescribed path. The formula is not represented as valid before its denominators and entropy range are admissible.

6. Achievability, physical cost and the unknown family

The truncated input has strict cost slack. Equation (14) is a valid loose bound. One direct verification writes gamma_{E-1}=(1-epsilon)sigma+epsilon theta, where theta is a shifted thermal tail and has entropy g(E-1). The almost-affinity upper bound for channel mutual information and I(theta,Phi)<=2S(theta) give
 C(E-1)-I(sigma,Phi) <= [2h(epsilon)+2epsilon g(E-1)]/(1-epsilon).
Since epsilon<1/16 for E>1, this is bounded by the larger error displayed in (14); the fixed-n derivative bound supplies 2log_2(E/(E-1)). This calculation confirms the asserted estimate without silently replacing n(E) by n(E-1). Adding this short derivation would improve checkability, but its omission is not a missing material premise.

The receiver cutoff uses only the signal mean, at most E once n<=E. The failure mass is at most E^{-3}. The gentle-projection plus failure-symbol estimate gives the stated e_E. Winter's finite-reference conditional-entropy bound then gives W_E because the reference dimension is L_in; output energy is not needed for continuity. All asymptotic uses occur for e_E<=1. For larger e_E the displayed expression is already above the trivial finite-reference entropy range.

Positivity bounds the truncated signal operator norm by the thermal output norm divided by 1-epsilon, and the failure flag contributes at most E^{-3}. Compressing a positive top-m reset part leaves a positive retained operator of rank at most m+1 and the same retained trace. A subspace test thus yields the stated branch-information bound using v_E. This works for noncommuting reset states and arbitrary reset energy. For alpha>1 the relevant mutual information is evaluated after finite output compression, avoiding infinite-entropy subtraction.

The physical cost is not inferred merely from a finite-dimensional capacity theorem. Rational input probabilities with strict cost slack define an exact type space, every vector of which has total number k sum_j j p_j<kE. Any encoder into tensor powers of that space respects this bound, including coherent superpositions and each encoded message. The type information estimate is valid: if d_k=D(pi_T||sigma_p^{tensor k}), data processing bounds the output total correlation by d_k; complementary entropy subadditivity and S(pi_T)=kH(p)-d_k then give I(pi_T,N^{tensor k})>=kI(sigma_p,N)-2d_k, uniformly in N. The constant behind d_k=O(log k) is for the fixed finite alphabet, which is all that the order of limits requires.

Berta–Gharibyan–Walter's arbitrary-index, uninformed-user compound theorem then applies to the fixed finite block channel. The entanglement-assisted quantum/classical conversion preserves a common encoder/decoder and the subspace restriction. First the type and the cuts are fixed, then outer coding length tends to infinity. Only afterwards are approximation slacks and the energy limit taken. No error exponent uniform in E or reset index is needed. The theorem is suitable; its pinpoint use for the earlier type inequality is the attribution error R1.

For the unknown signal family, the added Gaussian noise raises n to N at fixed kappa, and a thermal attenuator with transmissivity kappa_0/kappa and bath occupation N produces exactly Phi_{kappa_0,N}. This order is used only to lower-bound the signal mutual information of the same input. Applying it to the full reset mixture could change the reset entropy budget and would be invalid; the paper expressly avoids that step. The finite cuts and norm bounds are uniform in kappa>=kappa_0 and n<=N(E). The converse chooses the actual worst pair. The endpoints kappa=1 and n=0 are needed only in this achievability family, where singular Choi/Gibbs expressions are not used. The converse uses kappa_0<1 and N>0. The identically zero envelope is separately referred to the earlier pure-loss result.

7. Budgets and boundary tests

For Shannon budgets the ordered spectral tail estimate and m=floor(E/ln(E+1)) give the upper clipping curve. The witness weight clip((B-1)/g(M)) is feasible, including B<1 and the upper clipped endpoint. Since g(M)-log_2(E+1) remains bounded for fixed kappa and n<=E, the clipping error is uniform even when B grows arbitrarily fast.

For fixed alpha>1, Tr tau_a^alpha>=(1-a)^alpha supplies exact witness feasibility. The ordered-spectrum tail estimate gives the upper discrepancy at most (m+1)^{(1-alpha)/alpha}; m=floor(sqrt E) suffices. The concave modulus |f(x)-f(y)|<=f(|x-y|) controls the endpoints without assuming a bounded derivative at zero. Thus B=0, saturated budgets, changing budgets and unbounded sequences of finite budgets are covered. Neither alpha approaching one with E nor alpha=infinity is claimed.

The linear-noise example is correct: at kappa=lambda=1/2 and n=E, C(E) tends to one bit, so the flagged benchmark tends to one half bit, while the saturated proposed loss would be one bit. Nonnegative capacity rules out that extension. This is an obstruction at one critical point, not a proof that n=o(E) is necessary for every possible parameter regime. I found no counterexample within the stated sublinear hypotheses.

8. Novelty and significance, with actual source scope

The closest inspected antecedent is the author's earlier entropy/optical paper, ARR-2026-7SWAJZ0X278GJ9NK v1, specifically Lemma 3 and Theorems 7–8 with their proofs. It already contains the entropy-budget curve, witness family, spectral matching, physical type blocking and common-code pure-loss extension. Those components should not be counted again as new results of this manuscript.

The new contribution supported by this review is the growing thermal-environment extension, particularly its all-input branch-discrimination argument on the scale S of order E/(n+1), together with the matching uniform errors and the unknown fixed signal-envelope statement. It permits the whole sublinear regime rather than a restriction imposed by a coarser rank count. It remains an asymptotic specialized EA minimax theorem, not a general capacity solution for thermal mixtures or fading channels.

The Gaussian benchmark, bounded Choi order, regular-channel identity, mixture inequality, TRE inequality, entropy continuity and compound coding are established tools. Catalano et al., arXiv:2604.26874v2, Section IV and Appendix F.2, also provide a close channel-model antecedent: identity/vacuum and pure-loss/vacuum mixtures, with non-Gaussian optimization issues. I read those model and analytic-reduction passages, not that paper's entire appendix proof or numerical suite. They do not establish the present entropy-constrained arbitrary-reset growing-thermal minimax law in the passages inspected.

The literature search was directed and incomplete. No exact antecedent theorem was located in the sources actually inspected. That supports a qualified novelty assessment, not worldwide priority. A broad field-impact or historic-impact claim would require evidence not present here. The 4.0 research-importance judgment reflects a strong technical extension within a narrow established line.
