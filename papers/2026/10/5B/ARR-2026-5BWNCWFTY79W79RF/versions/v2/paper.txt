Temporal adjoints and injectivity lengths
in stationary quantum processes
Lluis Eriksson
29 October 2026
Version 2: revised proof and source conventions
Abstract
Taking local matrix adjoints along a forward random process does not reverse its
temporal order. We give finite, ergodic qubit examples in which first and eventual
Kraus-span injectivity lengths change under this operation. The failure persists for
bistochastic, two-sided faithful local channels; a three-phase example also changes the onset
distribution. These examples contradict the pointwise adjoint-length identity in Lemma
A.1 of arXiv:2607.01446v1, without providing a counterexample to that manuscript’s main
parent-Hamiltonian or spectral-gap theorems. We state a corrected inverse-base identity. It
preserves fixed-block distributions, and, under local and dual faithfulness, preserves first and
eventual onset laws. We also replace an invalid common-kernel step in the source appendix
by a Choi-rank convergence argument, recovering its eventual-strict-positivity/injectivity
implication under the cited ergodic convergence theorem. The required normalization and
endpoint shift are explicit. Fixed-block, first-occurrence, eventual, and pathwise assertions
remain distinct.

7 Scope, reproducibility and review context
The counterexamples invalidate the specific local-adjoint identity in [ 1, Lemma A.1]. Corollary
7 repairs a separate appendix proof using the cited convergence theorem with its actual
assumptions. Neither correction refutes or certifies the main parent-Hamiltonian construction,
spectral-gap criteria, or a classification of gapped ergodic matrix product states. Inspection
of selected primary statements and proof passages is not a whole-paper audit of either cited
source.
The all-length conclusions rest on algebraic proofs, not extrapolation from finite ranks.
