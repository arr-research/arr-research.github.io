Temporal adjoints and injectivity lengths
in stationary quantum processes
Lluis Eriksson
29 October 2026
Abstract
Taking local matrix adjoints along a forward random process does not reverse its
temporal order. We give finite, ergodic qubit examples in which first and eventual
Kraus-span injectivity lengths change under this operation. The failure persists for
bistochastic, two-sided faithful local channels; a three-phase example also changes the onset
distribution. These examples contradict the pointwise adjoint-length identity in Lemma
A.1 of arXiv:2607.01446v1, without providing a counterexample to that manuscript’s main
parent-Hamiltonian or spectral-gap theorems. We state a corrected inverse-base identity.
It preserves fixed-block distributions, and, under local and dual faithfulness, preserves first
and eventual onset laws. The distinction between fixed-block, first-occurrence, eventual,
and pathwise assertions is essential.

5 Scope, reproducibility and review context
The counterexamples concern the specific adjoint identity of Lemma A.1 in [ 1], v1. They do
not demonstrate failure of its main parent-Hamiltonian construction, its spectral-gap criteria,
or its eventual-strict-positivity equivalence under the additional hypotheses of that proposition.
An invalid helper identity is not itself a counterexample to a main theorem. The results here
do not classify gapped ergodic matrix product states.
