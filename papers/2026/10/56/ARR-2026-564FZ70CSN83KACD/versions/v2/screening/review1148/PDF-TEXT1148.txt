Support and quantum-reference activation of classical channels
Lluis Eriksson
28 October 2026
Abstract
For an arbitrary finite classical channelK, we characterize when quantum side information
prevents strict relative-entropy contraction. The complete equal-reference Umegaki coefficient
equals one if and only if the Doeblin common-component coefficientd(K) = ∑
y mini K(y|i )
vanishes. Positive d(K)gives the known upper1 −d(K). For the converse, one channel-
independent family of faithful separable classical–quantum hypotheses, with reference dimension
equal to the input alphabet size, admits channel-dependent binary readouts whose input, output
and measured relative entropies tend tolog 2. Explicit scalar bounds make this convergence
uniform over all zero-Doeblin channels with a fixed input alphabet, even as supports and output
cardinalities vary. For symmetric channels we additionally determine exact SLD, minimal chi-
square, triangular, BKM and Umegaki coefficients:(n−1)λ2/[1 + (n−2)λ]. Every two-symbol
restriction retains its ordinary classical coefficient, exposing the failure of classical binary-support
reduction. Conditioning, rare events and parameter-dependent effects remain essential costs.
Certified asymmetric examples, failed numerical selectors and regularizer counterexamples are
retained. No capacity advantage, robust implementation, minimal reference dimension or
worldwide priority is claimed.
1 Problem and relation to prior work
A classical noisy channel acts on probability distributions, but its input may be correlated with a
quantum memory that remains available after transmission. The relevant comparison is then between
two joint states with thesamememory marginal. This constraint rules out the trivial strategy
of putting all distinguishing information in an untouched memory. It still permits noncommuting
conditional memory states, even when the input and memory are separable.
For ordinary classical strong data processing, optimizing over a large input alphabet can be
reduced to a pair of input symbols, by Ordentlich and Polyanskiy [3]. We first quantify the failure
of that reduction with a quantum reference for a full symmetric family. We then characterize strict
complete Umegaki contraction for every finite classical channel: Theorem 6.1 is a necessary and
sufficient common-support criterion. Its converse uses a channel-independent input family and
explicit binary measurements. The input alphabet and reference dimension are fixed; the common
reference marginal and measurement may vary with the preparation parameter.
The definitions of complete and conditional contraction coefficients are prior; we use the
equal-reference complete convention described by Hirche, George, Nuradha and Wilde [5]. Their
characterization using a binary hypothesis label is distinct from restricting theinput alphabetto
two symbols. We do not claim a new correspondence between divergence and reference-assisted
information measures. Hiai and Ruskai [1] provide the operator-convex divergence and metric
framework. Classical equivalence of global contraction coefficients is treated by Makur and Zheng [2].
The classical symmetric-channel formula below is a benchmark, not a novelty claim; Lee and

another contraction question. Selected primary passages, rather than entire source corpora, were
inspected. The general converse here extends the same simplex mechanism used for the symmetric
examples; it is not a new data-processing principle or an independently invented construction.
