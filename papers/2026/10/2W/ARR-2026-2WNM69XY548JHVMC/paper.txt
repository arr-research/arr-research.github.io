Classical limits and Gaussian-average gaps
for compound bosonic fading channels
Lluis Eriksson
26 October 2026

Abstract
We study unassisted classical communication through memoryless mixtures of noisy,
phase-insensitive bosonic Gaussian channels. Both thermal noise and photon budget scale
with an energy parameter. An explicit measure–prepare representation converts every
quantum block code, including entangled codewords and collective receivers, into a classical code with identical error and an exactly quantified cost increment. The same simulation works for an unknown fixed mixing law. A Gaussian-smoothing estimate and
a uniform finite-alphabet reduction give an explicit O(E −1 ) comparison with a classical
compound variational value. This value optimizes one input distribution before taking the
worst mixing law. We also prove a strict separation between classical optimal modulation
and the time-sharing hull of Gaussian coherent modulations for a matched-reset example. Matching bounds determine the high-energy limit of a regularized Holevo functional
for ensembles with an exactly Gaussian average quantum state, permitting squeezed and
entangled averages. Its gap from the unknown-fixed-phase operational capacity remains
strictly positive in that limit. The classical coding tools and Gaussian measure–prepare
representations are established ingredients. The Gaussian-average restriction is an ensemble condition, not a converse for every finite codebook described as Gaussian.

Prior work, limits and verification context

Gaussian measure–prepare structure, the single Gaussian classical limit, data processing, Fano,
heat-flow score methods, and type coding are prior tools [1, 2, 3, 4]. Recent general Gaussianencoding optimality results for single-mode Gaussian channels are given by Adesso, Lami and
Giovannetti [11]. Their Gaussian-channel hypothesis excludes the generic mixtures studied
here; a mixture of Gaussian components is not in general a Gaussian channel. Their fixederror energy-rescaling formula is not imported into our compound average-error problem.
Uniform quantization using compactness and Dini-type arguments is also explicit in earlier
compound-channel work [5, 6]. The Mitran–Devroye–Tarokh operational model assumes that
the channel parameter is known to the receiver; its theorem is therefore not a substitute for our
decoder without that side information. Our use of its quantization mechanism is separately
justified above. Classical on/off fading already motivates non-Gaussian input optimization [7].
Non-Gaussian advantages in entanglement-assisted quantum fading are also prior research [8].
Near-capacity coherent modulation for non-Gaussian attenuator environments and additive
classical noise is also established [10]. Those bounds depend on the environment and are
independent of input energy at fixed channel. Our joint scaling and unknown-law comparison
must be distinguished from that earlier setting; coherent near-optimality itself is not new. Our
example therefore does not discover the broad non-Gaussian phenomenon. The contribution
here is the uniform simulation and the persistent Gaussian-average Holevo gap in the declared
joint-scaling model, with its ensemble condition and its unknown-fixed-law coding quantifiers.
The results do not cover within-block changes of the unknown law, channel memory,
unbounded gains, a vanishing normalized noise floor, arbitrary nonclassical reset states, or
entanglement assistance. Expected photon cost is not a deterministic photon-number bound.
A directed primary-source search is not an exhaustive literature audit. The proof does not
identify the general classical fading optimizer.
