Classical limits and learning gaps
for compound bosonic fading channels
Lluis Eriksson
26 October 2026
Abstract
We study classical communication over noisy mixtures of phase-insensitive bosonic
Gaussian channels. Both thermal noise and photon budget scale with an energy parameter.
An explicit measure–prepare representation converts every quantum block code, including
entangled codewords and collective receivers, into a classical code with identical error and
an exactly quantified cost increment. The same simulation works for an unknown fixed
mixing law. A Gaussian-smoothing estimate and a uniform finite-alphabet reduction give
an explicit O(E−1) comparison with a classical compound variational value. This value
optimizes one input distribution before taking the worst mixing law. We also prove a strict
separation between classical optimal modulation and the time-sharing hull of Gaussian
coherent modulations for a matched-reset example. Matching bounds determine the high-
energy limit of a regularized Holevo functional with Gaussian average marginals. A block-
uniform, energy-controlled continuity modulus removes both exact marginal Gaussianity
and Gaussianity of the full average state. We prove that almost every product coherent
Gaussian random codebook from a fixed finite deterministic schedule has the required
approximate marginals, controlling empirical moments separately.

A matched-reset/AWGN pair has a strict learning gap: one feedback bit after a zero-
photon pilot eventually outperforms arbitrary prior entanglement without feedback. The
Gaussian, coding and continuity tools used in the argument have established antecedents.

Two necessary cautions
Trace closeness alone does not control the moment-matched Gaussian, even with uniformly
bounded energy. Indeed ρL = (1−1/L)|0⟩⟨0|+(1/L)|L⟩⟨L| has energy one and trace distance
1/L to vacuum, while its Gaussian with the same moments is always γ1. Thus Dtr(ρL, γ1) →
1/2, not zero. This is why the empirical moments above were controlled separately. Also,
for a saturated circular design CN(0, 1), the actual cost is the average of mMm independent
unit-mean exponentials, so Pr( bPm ≤1) →1/2 by the central limit theorem. Design cost in
expectation does not imply typical feasibility without slack or correction.
No theorem here covers every deterministic realization named Gaussian, arbitrary post-
selection, arbitrary message weights, message-bearing covariance schedules, or design lists
growing with block length. No trace approximation of the whole average to a product Gaus-
sian is claimed or needed. Equation (21) remains a functional statement; (22) is a separate
converse for a defined operational code class, not a proof of its exact capacity or random-code
achievability.

Prior work, limits and verification context
Gaussian measure–prepare structure, the single Gaussian classical limit, data processing, Fano,
heat-flow score methods, and type coding are prior tools [1, 2, 3, 4]. Recent general Gaussian-
encoding optimality results for single-mode Gaussian channels are given by Adesso, Lami and
Giovannetti [11]. Their Gaussian-channel hypothesis excludes the generic mixtures studied
here; a mixture of Gaussian components is not in general a Gaussian channel. Their fixed-
error energy-rescaling formula is not imported into our compound average-error problem.
Uniform quantization using compactness and Dini-type arguments is also explicit in earlier
compound-channel work [5, 6]. The Mitran–Devroye–Tarokh operational model assumes that
the channel parameter is known to the receiver; its theorem is therefore not a substitute for our
decoder without that side information. Our use of its quantization mechanism is separately
justified above. Classical on/off fading already motivates non-Gaussian input optimization [7].
Non-Gaussian advantages in entanglement-assisted quantum fading are also prior research [8].
Near-capacity coherent modulation for non-Gaussian attenuator environments and additive
classical noise is also established [10]. Those bounds depend on the environment and are
independent of input energy at fixed channel. Our joint scaling and unknown-law comparison
15


must be distinguished from that earlier setting; coherent near-optimality itself is not new. Our
example therefore does not discover the broad non-Gaussian phenomenon. Common-mixture
continuity methods and exceptional-energy penalties have antecedents in Winter [13] and
Shirokov [14]. Winter’s energy-constrained bounds use Gibbs hypotheses; Shirokov’s ensemble
distances do not simply become distances between average marginals. Neither attribution
replaces our direct proofs of (19)–(22). The contribution here is the quantitative common
simulation, Gaussian-hull separation, and robust marginal converse for the stated joint scaling,
including the defined typical books and their physical cost correction. These tools and that
consequence are distinguished from a general Gaussian-protocol impossibility theorem or an
exact Gaussian-code capacity.
The results do not cover within-block changes of the unknown law, channel memory,
unbounded gains, a vanishing normalized noise floor, or arbitrary nonclassical reset states.
The feedback direct theorem requires a finite family; the assistance convention is arbitrary
finite-dimensional prior entanglement per block, with unbounded growth allowed. No sep-
arate theorem for a differently defined infinite-resource assistance model is claimed.
The
compound minimax change and training mechanisms are prior results [15, 16, 17].
Con-
strained entanglement-assisted capacity is longstanding theory [18]; its general formula is not
substituted for the direct energy-controlled argument. Single Gaussian-channel entanglement-
assisted formulas already give related simultaneous high-energy limits [19]. They do not by
themselves prove a uniform compound-mixture comparison. Finite-energy entanglement ad-
vantages, including noisy optical experiments [20], are consistent with the stated limit. The
additional resource result is quantitative and specific; standalone significance and worldwide
priority are not established.
Expected photon cost is not a deterministic photon-number
bound. A directed primary-source search is not an exhaustive literature audit. The proof
does not identify the general classical fading optimizer.
