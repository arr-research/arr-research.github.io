Sharp entropy budgets for communication
with unknown resets and optical loss
Lluis Eriksson
26 October 2026
Abstract
A memoryless reset channel transmits the input with fixed probability0< λ <1
and otherwise prepares a stateτ. We investigate recovery of its flagged-erasure capacity
benchmarks when only an entropy budget on the reset is available, with no bound on its
energy or orientation. For oscillator input energyE, and each fixed finite Rényi order
α > 0, the sharp conditions for uniform absolute entanglement-assisted recovery are
2B(E) /E→ 0for α< 1, B(E)/log 2(E+ 1) →0for α= 1, andB(E) →0for α> 1. The
sufficient conditions recover four benchmarks even with one encoder and decoder for the
entire compound channel class. For Shannon and fixed super-Shannon order we further
determine the full bounded entanglement-assisted minimax loss, uniformly over every
budget: b(λ,qmin{1,B/log 2(E+ 1)})and b(λ,q[1 −2−((α−1)/α)B]), respectively, up to
absolute o(1), whereq= 1−λand b(u,v) = (u+v)h(u/(u+v)). Known-noise and unknown-
fixed-noise minimax losses coincide asymptotically. The same loss curves hold with a
quantum-limited attenuator in the transmitted branch, uniformly over transmissivities
bounded away from zero, relative to its attenuated flagged benchmark. One common
assisted code handles an unknown fixed reset and an unknown fixed transmissivity. A fixed
finite output compression, spectral branch-information bounds and exact type-subspace
blocking preserve the physical input constraint. A vacuum–thermal mixture supplies the
converse through a finite-flag continuity argument. Finite-codimension energy avoidance
also gives uniform pointwise recovery of two-way benchmarks. Spectral approximation
estimates and compound coding theorems are established ingredients; the claimed addition
concerns their operational entropy-budget quantifiers and the matching uniform loss curves.
The noise is fixed throughout a code block. No error-exponent, arbitrarily varying-channel
or universal two-way coding result is asserted.

9 Limits, evidence and responsibility
The zero-loss classification and the unassisted and two-way conclusions concern the iden-
tity/replacement model. Section 8 extends the assisted minimax curves to a quantum-limited
12

attenuator/replacement model. Its environment is vacuum and its transmissivity is bounded
away from zero. No thermal-loss or arbitrary memory channel is covered. The vacuum–thermal
resets are asymptotically least favorable for the entanglement-assisted curves of Section 7; no
exact finite-energy optimizer or uniqueness is proved. No nonzero-loss curve is supplied for
α< 1. Rényi order and transmission probability remain fixed. Efficient encoders, finite-block
resource estimates, universal two-way codes and peak-cost/local-energy constraints are outside
the conclusions.
The six supplementary programs, listed with their outputs in the supplement’s README,
execute deterministic finite diagnostics in a writable copy of their folder, using Python,
NumPy, SciPy and mpmath. They check interlacing, noncommuting finite-code information
bounds, spectral compression, type-subspace information comparisons. The Rényi geomet-
ric/asymptotic rows are evaluations of the displayed closed form, not independent geometric-
series equality assertions. The additional minimax controls check noncommuting finite-support
inputs with exactly summed thermal tails; the scalar bound controls use 220-digit arithmetic
to resolve their largest energy scale. They do not optimize an operational capacity or construct
a universal code. The optical diagnostics add finite-dimensional dilation, entropy-difference,
branch-test and thermal-truncation checks; their signal-array entries are bound slacks, not
capacity estimates. They are corroboration, not proofs of an infinite-dimensional coding limit.
The analytical proof uses the explicitly cited coding and distillation dependencies. Literature
comparisons were directed and selective, with no certification of worldwide priority.
