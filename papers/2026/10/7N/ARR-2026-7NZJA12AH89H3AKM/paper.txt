Three sufficient criteria, short-time cancellation, and limits of sector averaging
Lluis Eriksson | 18 October 2026 | Candidate working paper, revision 1
Abstract
A finite quantum probe repeatedly interacts with a finite, inaccessible memory through a
fixed Lindblad generator. The unknown scalar multiplies a nontrivial Hamiltonian acting
only on the probe. We prove sufficient conditions under which a fixed prepare-and-measure
protocol has classical Fisher information at least proportional to the number of rounds,
without resetting the memory. The protocol records its randomized, informationally
complete preparation labels. Our first criterion is primitivity of the infinitesimal averaged
memory generator. A cancellation identity makes the parameter derivative of the averaged
memory channel third order in the interaction duration; the derivative of its stationary state
is consequently second order. A one-round signal therefore survives for every sufficiently
short fixed duration. Two complementary criteria use full ambient linear minimality, or a
common stationary product state with an explicit duration bound. We give physical two-qubit
examples and counterexamples to discarding preparation labels and averaging sector-wise
Fisher lower bounds. The conclusions are local lower bounds, not universal metrological
optimality, and do not resolve the general dissipative hidden-memory problem.
1. Question and contributions
A probe can be prepared and measured repeatedly while an unobserved environment retains
a conditional state between rounds. Noise and memory can then obscure a local Hamiltonian
parameter. The relevant question is whether the classical record actually retained by this
restricted experiment has a nonzero information rate. Information in an inaccessible bath or
in a joint quantum output is insufficient by itself.
This paper proves three alternative sufficient criteria. Theorem 2 uses a mixing condition on
a reduced generator computable before choosing the duration. Theorem 3 uses primitivity
and full reachability and observability of the induced memory instrument, with duration
bound tM ≤ 1/3. Theorem 4 replaces the rank condition by a common stationary product
state and gives another explicit duration bound. None of these assumptions is claimed
necessary. In every case t is fixed before the number N of rounds tends to infinity.
The contribution is the stated combination of a local-signal obstruction or short-time
cancellation with an accessible classical-record argument. Similarity methods,
finite-dimensional realization arguments, and inference from mixing output statistics have
substantial antecedents [2-6]. Priority for the combined criteria has not been established by
an exhaustive literature review. The proofs and exact algebra checks below support a
conditional mathematical result, not a claim of exceptional scientific impact.
Mann et al. [1], Section IV, motivates this question through special analytical cases and
numerical evidence for dissipative hidden memory. Our recorded randomized preparations
and mixing assumptions do not establish universal attainability for a single fixed pure
preparation.

2. Model, protocol and conventions
Let P and E have finite Hilbert dimensions d and e, respectively, with m = e². Write L(P) for
the complex operator space and Herm(P) for its real Hermitian part. In orthonormal
Hilbert-Schmidt bases, the joint generator and channel are
Lω = L0 + ωDf, Df = D ⊗ Im, D(X) = -i[G,X], Φt,ω = exp(tLω). (1)
Here G is Hermitian and not scalar. The background generator, including every jump
operator, is independent of ω. The parameter lies in an open interval about ω₀. Norms ||·||₂
and ||·||F of superoperator matrices mean spectral and Frobenius norms; ||X||₁ is the trace
norm of an operator. All differentiations below are with respect to ω unless specified
otherwise. The joint dynamics uses the convention L(X) = -i[H,X] + Σj(VjXVj† - {Vj†Vj,X}/2).
Choose finitely many probe states σr spanning Herm(P), probabilities πr > 0, and a fixed
informationally complete POVM {Fs} whose effects are strictly positive. Independently each
round, draw and record r, prepare σr, apply Φt,ω, measure and record s, then discard only
P. The conditional memory is retained. The alphabet a = (r,s) induces the completely positive
instrument
Ars(X) = πr trP[(Fs ⊗ I) Φt,ω(σr ⊗ X)], Et,ω = Σr,sArs. (2)
A word w = a₁...aℓ acts as Aw = Aaℓ...Aa₁, and pρ(w) = tr(Awρ). The initial memory ρ₀ is any
fixed density matrix independent of ω. Its preparation is not an available control. Classical
Fisher information IN(ω₀) refers to the complete retained record of N pairs, at the specified
point. Strict positivity of Fs and πr makes every finite word probability positive, even for an
initially rank-deficient memory.
We call a channel primitive when it has a unique faithful stationary state ρ* and its powers
converge exponentially to P*(X) = ρ* tr X. For a generator A, primitive means that zero is
simple, its stationary state is faithful, and all remaining eigenvalues have negative real part.
These finite-dimensional spectral definitions state the mixing used in the proofs.
For Theorems 2 and 4 we also require Σr πrσr = I/d. Such full-rank informationally complete
preparations and effects exist in every finite dimension: choose sufficiently small positive
and negative perturbations of the maximally mixed state along a traceless Hermitian basis.
Their average is I/d; the corresponding sufficiently small perturbations of scalar effects form
a POVM. Theorem 3 needs spanning but not this average condition.
Lemma 1. An informative word gives a linear information lower bound
Suppose Eω is primitive at ω₀ and differentiable in a neighborhood. If a stationary word w of
length ℓ has c = ∂ω tr(Awρ*) ≠ 0, then constants B < ∞ and V < ∞ exist such that, with n =
floor(N/ℓ),
IN(ω₀) ≥ (n|c| - B)²/(nV), whenever n|c| ≥ B. (3)
B and V can be chosen uniformly over all fixed, parameter-independent initial memory
states. In particular IN(ω₀) ≥ c₀N for all sufficiently large N, for some c₀ > 0. This is a lower
bound only; it does not assert IN = Θ(N).

3. Proof of the information lemma
Divide the first nℓ rounds into n disjoint blocks. Let Zk indicate occurrence of w in block k,
and let Sn = ΣkZk. At ω₀ choose C ≥ 1 and 0 < r < 1 so that ||EjX||₁ ≤ Crj||X||₁ for Hermitian
trace-zero X and every j ≥ 0. Such constants exist by primitivity, including in the presence of
Jordan blocks, by taking r above the spectral radius of the trace-zero restriction.
Let ρk be the unconditional memory before block k and pk = tr(Awρk). For h ≥ 1,
subtracting the product of the marginals in the two-block probability gives
Cov(Zk,Zk+h) = tr AwE(h-1)ℓ(Awρk - pkEℓρk). (4)
The operator in parentheses is Hermitian, trace zero, and has trace norm at most 2pk ≤ 2.
The output functional tr Aw is an effect bounded by the identity. Therefore |Cov| ≤ 2Cr(h-1)ℓ,
with no assumption that the blocks are independent. Since Var(Zk) ≤ 1, summing both
covariance triangles yields
Var(Sn) ≤ nV, V = 1 + 4C/(1-rℓ). (5)
It remains to control the derivative of the transient contribution, not merely its magnitude. A
simple isolated stationary eigenvalue implies that ρ*(ω) and P*(ω) are differentiable locally.
Put Qω = Eω - P*ω. For j ≥ 1, Ej = P* + Qj, because EP* = P*E = P*. The j = 0 term is kept
separately. A local exponential bound ||Qj|| ≤ C₁r₁j is available in any fixed finite-dimensional
norm, with r₁ < 1 and enlarged C₁; continuity and a resolvent contour inside the unit disk
give this bound uniformly nearby.
∂ωQj = Σi=0j-1Qi(∂ωQ)Qj-1-i. (6)
Its norm is at most C₂jr₁j-1. Differentiating tr AwE(k-1)ℓρ₀ and summing over k now shows ∂ω
E[Sn] = nc + δn with |δn| ≤ B. Both geometric series and their differentiated series
converge. Compactness of the state space makes B uniform over fixed ρ₀. This argument
includes ∂ωρ*: setting that derivative to zero without a reason would be incorrect.
For a finite positive-probability record let U be its score. Then E[U] = 0 and ∂ω E[Sn] =
E[(Sn-E[Sn])U]. Cauchy-Schwarz gives (∂ω E[Sn])² ≤ Var(Sn)IN. Equations (5)-(6) give (3).
Discarding any leftover rounds cannot increase information, so the same lower bound holds
for the full N-round record. A statistic of zero variance cannot have nonzero derivative in
this positive finite model. For n ≥ 2B/|c| the numerator is at least n²c²/4, proving the claimed
linear rate. □
The deterministic interaction time is T = Nt. Ideal preparation, measurement and classical
recording costs are excluded. The lemma proves local sensitivity and a lower information
rate. It provides no general efficient estimator, minimax risk, global parameter
identifiability, or quantum advantage. Constants may deteriorate near loss of primitivity or
of the informative derivative.

4. A criterion from the infinitesimal memory generator
Define J(X) = (I/d) ⊗ X and K = trP, so KJ = I on L(E). With maximally mixed average
preparation, Et,ω = K exp(tLω)J. Let
A = KL0J. (7)
A is a legitimate finite-dimensional Lindblad generator: Eh,ω is CPTP, E0,ω = I and its
derivative at h = 0 is A. Equivalently, exp(sA) is the limit of the CPTP products (Es/n,ω)ⁿ. This
interpretation is for proving the generator property, not an additional physical reset
protocol.
Theorem 2. Primitive infinitesimal averaging is sufficient
If A is primitive, then for each fixed ω₀ there is t₀ > 0 such that every fixed duration 0 < t <
t₀, with any spanning recorded preparations of average I/d and any strictly positive IC
POVM, has an informative stationary one-letter probability. For every fixed initial memory
independent of ω it consequently satisfies IN(ω₀) = Ω(N). No common joint stationary
product state and no full ambient linear minimality are required.
Proof. The two elementary identities KDf = 0 and DfJ = 0 follow respectively from the
vanishing partial trace of a commutator on P and from D(I) = 0. Thus the coefficients of t and
t² in ∂ωEt,ω vanish. Differentiating the exponential series gives the more precise identity
∂ωEt,ω = (t³/6) KL0DfL0J + O(t⁴). (8)
Indeed, in the cubic derivative only LωDfLω survives between K and J; replacing its exterior
Lω factors by L₀ does not change it. All remainders here and below are in a fixed
superoperator norm and uniform for ω in a sufficiently small compact neighborhood. Finite
dimensionality and analyticity of the exponential justify termwise differentiation and these
bounds.
Extend Ft,ω = (Et,ω-I)/t analytically to t = 0 by F0,ω = A. Trace preservation makes its range
trace zero. On the trace-zero subspace, A is invertible; hence so is Ft,ω for small |t|. The
normalized solution ρt,ω of Ft,ωρt,ω = 0 is analytic, equals the faithful stationary state ρA at t
= 0, and remains positive definite for small positive t. This can be obtained explicitly by
solving on the trace-zero subspace after fixing any trace-one reference vector.
The other eigenvalues of Ft,ω remain in a left half-plane Re z ≤ -γ < 0 and bounded in
modulus by some H. The associated eigenvalues of Et,ω are 1+tz; their squared moduli are at
most 1-2γt+H²t² < 1 for small positive t. Therefore Et,ω is primitive. No diagonalizability is
needed for this conclusion.
By (8), ∂ωFt,ω = O(t²). Differentiating its stationary equation and using tr(∂ωρt,ω) = 0 gives
∂ωρt,ω = -(Ft,ω|tr=0)-1(∂ωFt,ω)ρt,ω = O(t²). (9)

4.1. The accessible signal survives stationary feedback
Continue the proof of Theorem 2. Define the stationary reduced probe channel Rt,ω(X) = trE
Φt,ω(X ⊗ ρt,ω). Its derivative has a direct term and a stationary-memory term. The first is
tD(X) + O(t²)||X||, since ∂ωΦ = tDf + O(t²) and tr ρt,ω = 1. In the second term the identity
part of Φ contributes X tr(∂ωρt,ω) = 0. Its remainder is O(t)O(t²)||X|| by (9). Consequently
∂ωRt,ω = tD + O(t²), (10)
as a map on the entire probe operator space. Since D ≠ 0, this derivative is nonzero at ω₀ for
all sufficiently small positive t. If every stationary prs = πr tr[FsRt,ω(σr)] had zero derivative,
spanning of the inputs and effects would imply ∂ωR = 0. At least one letter is therefore
informative. Lemma 1 with ℓ = 1 proves the conclusion, including arbitrary fixed initial
memory states. □
The theorem is an existence result for a duration interval depending on the model and
parameter neighborhood. Equations (8)-(10) do not give a universal numerical t₀, and the
limits t → 0 and N → ∞ are not exchanged: choose any valid t once, then increase N.
Primitivity of A is sufficient, not implied by arbitrary joint Lindblad dynamics or by
primitivity at some isolated finite duration.
4.2. Exact two-qubit example without a stationary product
Put the probe first, use Pauli matrices X,Y,Z, and choose
H0 = (XX+YY+ZZ)/4 + IX/5, G = ZI/2,
V1 = I ⊗ |0⟩⟨1|, V2 = ZI/2. (11)
For a general memory operator Y, the infinitesimal averaged generator is A(Y) = -i[X/5,Y] +
VYV† - {V†V,Y}/2, where X is the Pauli matrix and V = |0⟩⟨1|. Its Bloch equations are
ẋ = -x/2, ẏ = -y/2 - 2z/5, ż = 2y/5 - z + 1. (12)
The stationary Bloch vector is (0,-20/33,25/33). Its density determinant is 16/1089 > 0. The
trace-zero eigenvalues are -1/2 and -3/4 ± i√39/20, so A is primitive. Theorem 2 applies for
sufficiently small fixed t.
This example cannot have a stationary product (I/2) ⊗ ρ for L₀. If such a product existed,
partial tracing its stationarity would force Aρ = 0 and thus ρ = ρA. But the exchange
interaction has a nonzero commutator with (I/2) ⊗ ρA. Direct Pauli-basis calculation gives
||L₀((I/2) ⊗ ρA)||²HS = 1025/8712 > 0. Thus Theorem 2 covers a case outside the
product-state criterion below.

exact finite algebra check, not a formalization of the analytic perturbation proof or an
experimental test.

5. Local signal cannot be a memory-only similarity
This section supplies a complementary route when the averaged generator A is not known to
be primitive. Let ||Lω||₂ ≤ M on an interval J containing ω₀. Fix t > 0 with tM ≤ 1/3. Write τE
for matrix partial trace over the m-dimensional operator space L(E); this is not the physical
state partial trace trE. Define
Wt(ω) = Re⟨D, τE(Φt,ω)⟩F / (m||D||²F). (13)
For any invertible linear map S on L(E), τE((I⊗S)B(I⊗S⁻¹)) = τE(B), by cyclicity within each
block. No positivity or unitarity of S is assumed. Differentiating the exponential series, each
term of degree k contains k insertions of Df among k-1 copies of Lω. Therefore
∂ωΦ = tDf + R, ||R||F ≤ t√m ||D||F(etM-1). (14)
The inequality ||τE B||F ≤ √m||B||F and Cauchy-Schwarz give |W′t-t| ≤ t(etM-1). Since eˣ ≤
1/(1-x) for 0 ≤ x < 1, we obtain
W′t(ω) ≥ t(2-etM) ≥ t/2 > 0. (15)
It follows that no two distinct parameters in J have channels related by a similarity acting
only on the memory operator space. Infinitesimally, no matrix K can satisfy ∂ωΦ = [I⊗K,Φ],
since τE annihilates that commutator. The witness is algebraic: access to τEΦ is not being
assumed experimentally.
Theorem 3. Full ambient minimality criterion
Use the protocol (2) at tM ≤ 1/3. Suppose Et,ω₀ is primitive with stationary state ρ*, and
suppose the following real spans have dimension m = e²:
span{Awρ*: w a finite word} = Herm(E),
span{tr Aw: w a finite word} = Herm(E)*. (16)
Then some stationary word of length at most 2m-1 has nonzero parameter derivative, and
IN(ω₀) = Ω(N) for every fixed initial memory independent of ω. Condition (16) is full ambient
linear reachability and observability. It is stronger than minimality of the Hilbert dimension,
and cannot be silently replaced by that weaker property.
The proof below explains why the operational rank condition is essential to this argument. A
changing joint channel need not be visible in a restricted record. Nor can one first reduce to
an arbitrary minimal realization and assume that its coordinates preserve the tensor
factorization used in (13).

5.1. Proof of the minimality criterion
Suppose, to obtain a contradiction, that all stationary probabilities p(w) = tr Awρ* have zero
derivative at ω₀. Put xw = Awρ*. Define Kxw = ∂ωxw. To see that this gives a well-defined
linear map, let Σw cw xw = 0, with finitely many constant real coefficients. For every suffix
v,
tr Av Σwcw∂ωxw = Σwcw∂ωp(wv) - (tr ∂ωAv)Σwcwxw = 0. (17)
Observability in (16) forces Σw cw∂ωxw = 0. Reachability then defines K on all Herm(E),
extended complex-linearly. Differentiate xwa = Aaxw and use the definition of K to obtain
∂ωAa = KAa-AaK, ∂ωρ* = Kρ*, tr ∘ K = 0. (18)
The last identity follows by differentiating tr xw = p(w); it is composition with the trace
functional, not the matrix trace of K. The spanning input states and output effects determine
every probe input-output block of the joint superoperator. Thus (18), holding for every (r,s),
implies ∂ωΦ = [I⊗K,Φ], contradicting (15). Some stationary word is informative.
For the length bound, the series ∂ωp(w) has a linear representation of dimension 2m, with
initial vector (ρ*,∂ωρ*), output functional (0,tr), and letter matrices
Ba = [ Aa 0 ; ∂ωAa Aa ]. (19)
The reachable spans generated by words of length at most k either grow strictly at each step
or, once equal at consecutive steps, are invariant under every Ba and never grow again.
Starting with one vector in dimension 2m, they stabilize by k = 2m-1. If the output functional
vanished on every word up to that length, it would vanish on all words, which has been
excluded. Lemma 1 completes the proof. □
5.2. What the rank criterion does and does not certify
Theorem 3 does not claim that every joint generator admits a protocol satisfying (16), or that
every primitive averaged memory channel has that rank. Positivity of a finite set of
numerical singular values is not an exact rank certificate. A rationally bounded determinant
or an algebraic argument is needed if a computed instance is used as proof.
A supporting local calculation for (11), with four full-rank tetrahedral preparations and
effects at t = 1/32, uses degree-20 Taylor bounds to certify rank four and strict Choi
positivity of the averaged memory channel. This calculation is included in the accompanying
reproducibility package, but no theorem here depends on its decimal output. Theorem 2 and
the explicit reduced generator (12) already establish a nonempty physical class without that
calculation.
The statistical conversion of an informative word into a rate is the same Lemma 1 in all three
criteria. This reuse prevents a false inference that full quantum-output information
automatically remains available after a measurement or after an inaccessible bath is
discarded.

6. A common stationary product state
Theorem 4. Product-stationary criterion with explicit duration
Suppose there is a faithful density matrix ρ on E, independent of ω, such that L₀((I/d)⊗ρ) = 0.
Suppose the protocol has average preparation I/d and its averaged channel is primitive at
ω₀. On an interval where ||Lω||₂ ≤ M, put q = d√e||ρ||HS. If
tM ≤ 1/(1+2q), (20)
then a stationary one-letter probability has nonzero derivative, and IN(ω₀) = Ω(N) uniformly
over fixed initial memory states in the sense of Lemma 1. There is no full ambient rank
assumption. For unital joint dynamics, one may take ρ = I/e and q = d, provided the
averaged channel is primitive.
Proof. The product (I/d)⊗ρ is stationary for every ω because D(I) = 0. Thus Et,ωρ = ρ, and
primitivity locally makes this the stationary state. Let Rω(X) = trE Φt,ω(X⊗ρ). As
Hilbert-Schmidt maps, Jρ:X ↦ X⊗ρ and Kₚ: Y ↦ trE Y have spectral norms ||ρ||HS and √e. The
differentiated exponential satisfies
∂ωΦ = tDf + Q, ||Q||₂ ≤ t||D||₂(etM-1). (21)
Since KₚDfJρ = D, and a matrix on the d²-dimensional probe operator space satisfies ||B||F ≤
d||B||₂, we have ||∂ωR-tD||F ≤ qt||D||₂(etM-1). Hence the reduced witness Uω =
Re⟨D,Rω⟩F/||D||²F obeys
|U′ω-t| ≤ qt(etM-1) ≤ qt·tM/(1-tM) ≤ t/2. (22)
The middle inequality uses tM < 1. Thus ∂ωR ≠ 0. Informational completeness makes at
least one stationary prs = πr tr FsRω(σr) informative; Lemma 1 applies. The stationary state
need not be physically prepared. □
This theorem replaces one substantial hypothesis by another. Unitality alone does not imply
mixing. The fixed-state assumption cannot be weakened in this proof to an unspecified
parameter-dependent ρ: its derivative would add a term. Theorem 2 controls precisely that
type of term under a different, infinitesimal primitivity assumption.
6.1. A rank-deficient but informative memory
For two qubits take H₀ = ZPZE/4, G = ZP/2, and jumps IPXE/2, IPYE/2, IPZE/2. This
generator is unital. With average preparation I/2, the averaged memory channel has Bloch
matrix diag(e-tcos(t/2), e-tcos(t/2), e-t). It contracts trace distance by at most e-t < 1 and has
stationary state I/2.
Nevertheless, memory operators diagonal in Z form an invariant subspace under each
conditional instrument, while the off-diagonal subspace is also invariant and never
contributes to a future trace. Starting at I/2, both reachable and observable dimensions are
at most two, rather than m = 4. Theorem 3's ambient rank condition fails exactly, whereas
Theorem 4 applies.

6.2. Exact signal and the role of preparation labels
In the last example, the joint generator on the span of XPIE and YPZE has matrix B = [0,-1/2;
1/2,-1]. Since (B+I/2)² = 0, the stationary reduced probe coherence is
χ(t) = e-t/2(1+t/2) > 0. (23)
The reduced probe channel applies diag(χ,χ,1) on Bloch vectors followed by rotation about Z
through angle ωt. Therefore U′(0) = tχ(t). Take four sign vectors s₀ = (1,1,1), s₁ = (1,-1,-1), s₂
= (-1,1,-1), s₃ = (-1,-1,1), states σr = (I+sr·σ/4)/2, πr = 1/4, and effects Fs = (I+ss·σ/4)/4.
They are full rank and informationally complete, and the average state is I/2. Directly,
∂ωp0,2(0) = tχ(t)/128 > 0. (24)
For |ω| ≤ 1, the exact squared Frobenius norms ||L₀||²F = 14 and ||Df||²F = 8 permit M = 8.
At t = 1/64, condition (20) is satisfied. The formula (23), the invariant subspaces and a
rational enclosure of (24) are checked in the supporting verifier. These are algebraic and
computational checks, not measured laboratory data.
Proposition 5. Erasing preparation labels can erase all information
For unital joint dynamics, start E at I/e and use randomized preparations of average I/d. If
the preparation labels are discarded, the entire remaining outcome record is independent of
ω and its Fisher information is zero.
Proof. The average joint input is I/(de), which remains fixed under any unital channel.
Conditioning on effect Fs leaves the normalized environment I/e and outcome probability tr
Fs/d. Induction over rounds gives an iid record with these parameter-independent
probabilities. The unrecorded preparation average must be taken before conditioning, which
is exactly what discarding the labels does. □
This proposition concerns the reduced record in the stated stationary initial condition; it
does not contradict any theorem above. In particular, the informative probability in (24) is a
joint probability for a recorded preparation and outcome. Describing the procedure merely
as repeatedly preparing the average maximally mixed state would change the experiment
and invalidate the conclusion.
7. A physical obstruction to averaging hidden sectors
Consider a different two-qubit model with fixed duration t = 1, H₀ = (π/2)ZP⊗|1⟩⟨1|E, signal
ωZP/2 and jump √(log 2/2) ZP⊗IE. Initialize E at I/2, repeatedly prepare +X on P and
measure Y. The environment sector is conserved. In sector zero the outcome probability is
p₀(ω) = (1+sin(ω)/2)/2; in sector one it is p₁(ω) = (1-sin(ω)/2)/2. The jump gives visibility 1/2,
while the sector-one background adds a phase π.

7.1. Local Fisher information cancels in the mixture
At ω = 0, p₀ = p₁ = 1/2 and p′₀ = -p′₁ = 1/4. If the sector is observed, N iid outcomes have
Fisher information N/4 in either sector. With the sector hidden and weights 1/2, the
probability of any word is the average of two products exchanged by ω ↦ -ω. Each such
probability is positive and even, so its derivative at zero vanishes. Consequently
IN(0 | known sector) = N/4, IN(0 | hidden equal mixture) = 0 for every N. (25)
There is still quadratic sensitivity: the probability of two positive outcomes is 1/4+sin²(ω)/16,
whose second derivative at zero equals 1/8. Zero local Fisher information here is not
parameter independence away from zero. Nor is it proof that no other duration, preparation
or measurement can work.
This counterexample refutes only the inference that positive rates in every unobserved
sector automatically give a positive rate for their mixture. Averaging sector Fisher
information supplies an upper bound through data processing, not a lower bound. The
example lacks primitivity, does not meet our short-time conditions and does not use
informationally complete readout. It therefore contradicts none of Theorems 2-4. The all-N
claim follows from evenness; checking 510 words of lengths one through eight is only a finite
cross-check.
8. Scope, related work and open cases
Our three sufficient routes differ. Theorem 2 requires a faithful mixing infinitesimal reduced
generator, and yields an unspecified but positive duration interval. Theorem 3 instead
assumes mixing and full ambient ranks at the selected duration. Theorem 4 uses a common
stationary product and mixing at that duration, with an explicit norm bound. A degenerate A
can still lead to a primitive finite-duration channel, but Theorem 2 does not cover that
situation automatically.
Burgarth and Yuasa [2] establish similarity-based identification under rank conditions.
Guţă's output-statistics analysis [3] and Guţă and Kiukas's primitive-chain equivalence and
local asymptotic theory [4] are direct antecedents for the realization and statistical
ingredients. We do not claim those methods are new. Our proofs track the particular
preparation labels and classical outcomes retained after discarding an inaccessible bath.
The full quantum output in a dilation can contain information absent from that retained
record: data processing gives Iclassical ≤ Fquantum,full. A lower bound on the right-hand
side alone therefore cannot prove our conclusion. This comparison is about available
subsystems and measurements, not a defect in the cited quantum-output theorems. The
newer theory for irreducible periodic chains [5] makes output mixtures and their
identifiability especially relevant; removing primitivity requires more than averaging sector
rates.
Finite-dimensional word and realization methods have further explicit antecedents in [6].
Learning an arbitrary Lindbladian with error correction [7] uses control and ancillary
resources that do not amount to automatically controlling the inaccessible E in this paper.
None of these comparisons establishes priority for our sufficient criteria; the targeted
source check is not a comprehensive proof of novelty.

9. Reproducibility and review status
All general arguments in this paper are analytic, not formally verified. A standard-library
Python package accompanies the local release: it includes a rational Pauli-generator
reconstruction for Theorem 2's example, the earlier rational duration/rank certificate, the
unital example and the sector counterexample. The verifiers require no optimizer,
floating-point tolerance or external numerical library. A clean replay is compared

Open direction
When the infinitesimal averaged generator has multiple stationary sectors, the inverse used
in (9) is singular. One must analyze the first nonzero inter-sector dynamics and the
stationary-record derivative together. A positive information rate in each labeled sector is
insufficient, as (25) shows. Establishing a usable criterion in that degenerate regime, or a
genuine protocol-independent obstruction, is the next structural problem. Optimizing a
constant within the already covered examples does not answer it.
