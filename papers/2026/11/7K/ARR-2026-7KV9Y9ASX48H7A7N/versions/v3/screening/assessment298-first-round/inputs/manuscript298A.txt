                                                  Abstract
         Fix a positive Ising coupling. We construct strictly positive weights on Boolean cubes whose
     labelled marginals on every set of at most a fixed positive fraction of the coordinates equal the
     uniform product marginals, while exponentially many normalized eigenvalues of the weighted
     transfer kernel approach one exponentially fast in the number of coordinates. The stationary law
     of the Perron Doob transform has exactly the same marginals as well. The unweighted product
     kernel has a dimension-independent gap. The construction combines binary codes with positive
     primal and dual distances, a positive product admixture, and a finite block-norm estimate.
     We prove the coding input by a direct entropy union bound and give explicit finite spectral
     constants. As a negative control, we calculate the full transfer spectrum of a single positive
     parity tilt: unlike its single-site heat-bath gap, its transfer gap stays uniformly positive at fixed
     coupling. Conversely, for the Boolean noise parameter 0 < ρ < 1, density moments of order
     s > (1 + ρ)/(1 − ρ) give an explicit dimension-independent transfer gap, without pointwise upper
     or positive lower bounds and even allowing zero weights. For every 1 < s < (1 + ρ)/(1 − ρ)
     and every B > 1, we construct normalized densities with ∥rL ∥s ≤ B and rL ≥ 1/B whose gaps
     collapse exponentially. Two antipodal product tilts and a Gram-matrix comparison supply this
     converse. The critical exponent has budget-dependent behavior: budgets sufficiently close to one
     give a uniform positive gap, whereas every B > 2 admits a strictly positive normalized family
     with rL ≥ 1/B and stretched-exponentially closing gaps. The latter construction balances one
     product tilt against the uniform channel. The optimal critical budget remains unresolved. The
     ingredients are classical and external priority of these joint conclusions is not established. Global
     many-body weights are allowed; no fixed-range Gibbs or local-dynamics claim follows.


1    The question and the transfer convention
Local marginal agreement and rapid relaxation are different kinds of information. The distinction
is familiar in limited independence and Markov-chain theory, but a comparison must specify the
dynamics: a slow single-site heat-bath chain does not automatically give a slow transfer operator.
We make this distinction within a single fixed-coupling transfer family and require exact, rather
than approximate, agreement of marginal input.

4    Stationary marginals survive the Perron transform
The weighting law in a transfer congruence need not equal the stationary law of its associated
Markov chain. Matching the former alone would leave a significant interpretive gap. Code symmetry
pays the latter exactly.
Lemma 4. For the mixture (9), the Perron stationary law π(x) = vx2 satisfies Mr (π) = Mr (uL ).
Proof. For every c ∈ C, translation x 7→ x + c preserves µ, Hamming distance, and hence Aµ . The
corresponding permutation commutes with Aµ and takes a positive unit Perron vector to another
such vector. Uniqueness forces vx+c = vx , so π is invariant under translations by C. If |S| ≤ r,
every translation on FS2 lifts to some c ∈ C by the assumed surjectivity. The S-marginal of π is
therefore invariant under all translations and must be uniform.

Completion of Theorem 1. The Perron eigenvector equation gives row sums one for (6). Symmetry
gives detailed balance:

                              π(x)PL (x, y) = vx Aµ (x, y)vy /λ1 = π(y)PL (y, x).

Conjugation by diag(v) shows that PL is similar to Aµ /λ1 . It has strictly positive entries and the
claimed spectrum. Apply Lemma 4 to obtain its stationary marginal assertion. For the uniform
weight, the Perron vector and stationary law are uniform, and (3) supplies the comparison chain.

6    What marginal certificates cannot establish
Corollary 6. Fix the constants of Theorem 1. Suppose F is a valid lower bound on the transfer
gap for every strictly positive weight, and factors through the tuple

                                      (L, β, MrL (µ), MrL (π)).

At the product input and for all sufficiently large L, its value is at most 7e−γL . The same conclusion
holds if only one of the two marginal tuples is supplied.

Proof. At the same L and β, the constructed weight and its stationary law supply exactly the same
input as the uniform weight and uniform stationary law. A function of that input takes the same
value on both. Validity on the constructed example and (5) for j = 2 force the asserted bound.

    The corollary does not assert that every such functional is zero or that the product chain is slow.
Its actual gap is 1 − tanh β. Nor does it concern a certificate using full conditionals, uniform density
bounds, the operator entries, or code membership: those are additional information. In particular,
the weights here can have exponentially small probabilities and global many-body interactions. The
theorem says nothing against a result restricted to uniformly controlled, fixed-range Gibbs weights.
The Perron Doob chain updates a full configuration and is not a local single-site dynamics. We make
no claim about continuum Yang–Mills, a physical mass gap, entropy tensorization, or a specified
spatial mixing rate. The many-mode conclusion is spectral; no separate theorem about metastable
sets is implicit.

10     Comparison with hyperbounded and perturbation results
The value sc is the classical Boolean hypercontractive boundary, not a new integrability scale. Indeed,
for as = 2s/(s + 1), the norm-one estimate ∥P√ρ f ∥2 ≤ ∥f ∥as holds when s ≥ sc by the standard
inequality recalled in [5]. Its local sharpness is seen from f = 1 + ux on one bit: ∥P√ρ f ∥22 = 1 + ρu2 ,
whereas ∥f ∥2as = 1 + (as − 1)u2 + o(u2 ). Tensorization amplifies a violated one-bit norm bound.
Such top-norm growth alone does not imply a closing normalized spectral gap; Theorem 10 adds
two antipodal channels and their Gram comparison to obtain a near-degenerate second eigenvalue.
    The inequalities and matrix arguments used above are classical. Related literature already
supplies both qualitative gap criteria and, under different hypotheses, dimension-independent
quantitative perturbation bounds. Miclo’s Theorem 1 in [8] gives a gap for ergodic self-adjoint
hyperbounded Markov operators. Its Proposition 11 explains why a general quantitative gap does
not follow from hyperboundedness alone. Wang’s Theorem 1.1 in [9] gives a tail-norm criterion for
the gap of an invariant Markov operator’s symmetrization. These fixed-space conclusions must not
be confused with a bound uniform over a family of increasing dimensions.
    Here Tr is generally not Markov in uL . Its Perron Doob transform is Markov in its own
stationary law, which generally differs from ruL . The ground-state multiplication is an L2 isometry
between these spaces, but does not automatically identify their higher norms uniformly in L.
Thus an implication from a qualitative hyperboundedness theorem would still need a quantitative
change-of-measure argument.
    Gross’s Theorem 2.2 in [6] does give dimension-independent gaps for additive Schrödinger
perturbations on Riemannian probability manifolds with a base logarithmic Sobolev inequality
and suitable moments of both eV and e−V . We have not identified that additive operator with
the logarithm of this discrete transfer congruence. Our moment hypothesis in Theorem 9 supplies
no inverse-density moment and permits zeros. In contrast, the counterexample in Theorems 10
and 12 have r ≥ 1/B and therefore bounded inverse moments. For these families, missing inverse
control is not a distinction from Gross; the operator/form and exponent correspondences are the

                                                      13
unestablished steps. Glück [7] gives hyperboundedness criteria and uniform essential spectral-radius
estimates; those estimates alone do not control the second eigenvalue of a finite matrix. These
differences do not prove originality or exclude a stronger implication. The retained comparison also
records incomplete access to Aida’s original gap theorem. No external priority, improvement over
this literature, or exhaustive novelty clearance is asserted.

Proposition 5 shows precisely why it does not supply the transfer conclusion. The present synthesis
obtains linear-order marginal matching for both the weight and the Perron stationary law, together
