                                                 Abstract
          We compare exact marginal agreement and density-moment control for finite Boolean Ising
      transfer operators. Positive code-based weights can preserve uniform labelled marginals on a
      fixed positive fraction of coordinates while exponentially many normalized transfer eigenvalues
      approach one; their Perron stationary law has the same marginals. An exact parity spectrum
      distinguishes this transfer behavior from single-site heat-bath relaxation.
          For noise parameter 0 < ρ < 1, the critical density exponent is s = (1+ρ)/(1−ρ). Supercritical
      moment bounds force a uniform normalized gap, while subcritical bounds allow collapse at every
      norm budget above one. At the critical exponent the exact worst-case norm boundary is 21/s ,
      and the escaped critical-moment boundary is one. We also determine the universal strict-tail
      threshold for (s − 1)/s < K < 1 using reverse hypercontractivity, sign compression and a sharp
      scalar moment envelope. The critical-norm endpoint construction can retain any prescribed
      density floor below one.
          Our joint marginal–moment result gives positive densities at the exact critical norm cap with
      uniform weighting-law marginals through ⌊Lα ⌋, for every fixed 0 < α < 1/2, whose first two
      transfer eigenvalues tend to one. Conversely, any bounded critical norm and exact matching
                               2
      through order kL with kL   /(L log(L+1)) → ∞ force operator-norm convergence to the unweighted
      noise operator, and normalized gap 1 − ρ. The quantitative bound follows from an explicit
      quadratic expansion of the published Kirshner–Samorodnitsky homogeneous moment formula.
      Critical-power uniform integrability gives rigidity at any diverging matching order. A sharp
      square-root transition and necessity of the logarithmic factor remain open. External priority is
      unestablished. Global weights are allowed; no fixed-range Gibbs, local-dynamics or physical-gap
      conclusion follows.

    Section 18 connects exact weighting-law marginal matching and the critical moment budget in a
single family, and proves quantitative rigidity above square-root logarithmic marginal order.
    The constructions serve different conclusions. The code family in Theorem 1 preserves labelled
marginals and produces many slow transfer modes. The product tilts in Theorems 10 and 13
establish the moment-budget boundary, without the code family’s marginal matching. The critical-
tail section gives a sufficient tail condition, stable counterexamples to its necessity, and collapse at
the finite-cutoff endpoint in Theorem 17. Section 12 then optimizes finite parity backgrounds to
extend the sub-two tail-budget collapse region below cutoff one. Section 13 attains the constructive
budget and allows the floor to equal the cutoff. Theorem 22 gives the exact scalar total-moment
envelope. Theorem 23 then proves necessity of the constructed tail budget for every collapsing family.
Readers interested in this sharp tail classification can start at Sections 14–15, using Theorem 21 for
the matching endpoint. These are separate families; their properties are not asserted jointly.

4    Stationary marginals survive the Perron transform
The weighting law in a transfer congruence need not equal the stationary law of its associated
Markov chain. Matching the former alone would leave a significant interpretive gap. Code symmetry
pays the latter exactly.
Lemma 4. For the mixture (9), the Perron stationary law π(x) = vx2 satisfies Mr (π) = Mr (uL ).
Proof. For every c ∈ C, translation x 7→ x + c preserves µ, Hamming distance, and hence Aµ . The
corresponding permutation commutes with Aµ and takes a positive unit Perron vector to another
such vector. Uniqueness forces vx+c = vx , so π is invariant under translations by C. If |S| ≤ r,
every translation on FS2 lifts to some c ∈ C by the assumed surjectivity. The S-marginal of π is
therefore invariant under all translations and must be uniform.

Completion of Theorem 1. The Perron eigenvector equation gives row sums one for (6). Symmetry
gives detailed balance:

                              π(x)PL (x, y) = vx Aµ (x, y)vy /λ1 = π(y)PL (y, x).

Conjugation by diag(v) shows that PL is similar to Aµ /λ1 . It has strictly positive entries and the
claimed spectrum. Apply Lemma 4 to obtain its stationary marginal assertion. For the uniform
weight, the Perron vector and stationary law are uniform, and (3) supplies the comparison chain.

6    What marginal certificates cannot establish
Corollary 6. Fix the constants of Theorem 1. Suppose F is a valid lower bound on the transfer
gap for every strictly positive weight, and factors through the tuple

                                      (L, β, MrL (µ), MrL (π)).

At the product input and for all sufficiently large L, its value is at most 7e−γL . The same conclusion
holds if only one of the two marginal tuples is supplied.
Proof. At the same L and β, the constructed weight and its stationary law supply exactly the same
input as the uniform weight and uniform stationary law. A function of that input takes the same
value on both. Validity on the constructed example and (5) for j = 2 force the asserted bound.

    The corollary does not assert that every such functional is zero or that the product chain is slow.
Its actual gap is 1 − tanh β. Nor does it concern a certificate using full conditionals, uniform density
bounds, the operator entries, or code membership: those are additional information. In particular,
the weights here can have exponentially small probabilities and global many-body interactions. The
theorem says nothing against a result restricted to uniformly controlled, fixed-range Gibbs weights.
The Perron Doob chain updates a full configuration and is not a local single-site dynamics. We make
no claim about continuum Yang–Mills, a physical mass gap, entropy tensorization, or a specified
spatial mixing rate. The many-mode conclusion is spectral; no separate theorem about metastable
sets is implicit.

16     Comparison with hyperbounded and perturbation results
The value sc is the classical Boolean hypercontractive boundary, not a new integrability scale. Indeed,
for as = 2s/(s + 1), the norm-one estimate ∥P√ρ f ∥2 ≤ ∥f ∥as holds when s ≥ sc by the standard
inequality recalled in [10]. Its local sharpness is seen from f = 1 + ux on one bit: ∥P√ρ f ∥22 = 1 + ρu2 ,
whereas ∥f ∥2as = 1 + (as − 1)u2 + o(u2 ). Tensorization amplifies a violated one-bit norm bound.
Such top-norm growth alone does not imply a closing normalized spectral gap; Theorem 10 adds
two antipodal channels and their Gram comparison to obtain a near-degenerate second eigenvalue.
    The inequalities and matrix arguments used above are classical. Related literature already
supplies both qualitative gap criteria and, under different hypotheses, dimension-independent
quantitative perturbation bounds. Miclo’s Theorem 1 in [13] gives a gap for ergodic self-adjoint
hyperbounded Markov operators. Section 6 and Proposition 11 also discuss a norm-budget transition
at two for the fourth power of an L2 -to-L4 Markov norm; this is an important precedent for budget
thresholds, not the raw density norm used here. Hyperboundedness alone does not give a general
quantitative gap. Wang’s Theorem 1.1 in [14] gives a tail-norm criterion for the gap of an invariant


                                                    29
Markov operator’s symmetrization. These fixed-space conclusions must not be confused with a
bound uniform over a family of increasing dimensions.
    Here Tr is generally not Markov in uL . Its Perron Doob transform is Markov in its own
stationary law, which generally differs from ruL . The ground-state multiplication is an L2 isometry
between these spaces, but does not automatically identify their higher norms uniformly in L.
Thus an implication from a qualitative hyperboundedness theorem would still need a quantitative
change-of-measure argument.
    Gross’s Theorem 2.2 in [11] does give dimension-independent gaps for additive Schrödinger
perturbations on Riemannian probability manifolds with a base logarithmic Sobolev inequality and
suitable moments of both eV and e−V . We have not identified that additive operator with the
logarithm of this discrete transfer congruence. Our moment hypothesis in Theorem 9 supplies no
inverse-density moment and permits zeros. In contrast, the counterexamples in Theorems 10 and 12
have r ≥ 1/B and therefore bounded inverse moments. For these families, missing inverse control is
not a distinction from Gross; the operator/form and exponent correspondences are the unestablished
steps. Glück [12], especially Theorem 5.1, gives hyperboundedness criteria and uniform essential
spectral-radius estimates; those estimates alone do not control the second eigenvalue of a finite
matrix. These differences do not prove originality or exclude a stronger implication. Aida’s directly
accessible author preprint [2] uses an additive Schrödinger form with a derivation and chain-rule
hypotheses, together with ground-state and weak-Poincaré inputs. Its setup and Theorem 10 were
read in a targeted comparison; no identification of those hypotheses with this multiplicative Boolean
transfer congruence has been established. No external priority, improvement over this literature, or
exhaustive novelty clearance is asserted.

Scope of the separation. Theorems 24 and 27 operate at the same critical cap B∗ . Collapse is
possible for every fixed sub-square-root power, whereas kL2 /(L log(L + 1)) → ∞ suffices for rigidity.

is necessary, or decide the regime kL comparable to L. Qualitative concavity alone supplies

statements concern finite Boolean transfer operators with global weights. They imply no fixed-range

criterion and coefficient erasure are prior methods; the homogeneous moment improvement is
explicitly attributed. Historical priority for their joint spectral application remains unestablished.
