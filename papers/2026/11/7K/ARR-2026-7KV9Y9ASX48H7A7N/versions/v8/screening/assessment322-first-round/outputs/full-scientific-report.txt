SUMMARY JUDGMENT
I found no counterexample or unresolved proof gap invalidating any of the formally stated Theorems 1–27 (including the propositions, lemmas and corollaries in that numbering). The important sharp claims are supported by complementary constructions and necessity arguments, rather than by trial vectors alone. The joint marginal/moment statements in Section 18 are technically coherent: the construction removes small low-degree Fourier coefficients while preserving positivity, and the converse exploits a strictly improved homogeneous moment bound at the critical hypercontractive parameter. The explicit quadratic expansion has the stated coefficient and supports the advertised sufficient order above sqrt(L log L).

There is a mathematically consequential ambiguity in the abstract: operator-norm convergence is true for S_r = P_sqrt(rho) M_r P_sqrt(rho), as the formal theorems say; it is generally false for T_r = M_sqrt(r) P_rho M_sqrt(r), the original transfer congruence. I give an analytic counterexample to the latter interpretation in objection O1. This does not refute the spectral or normalized-gap conclusions. There is also stale introductory language in Section 18 that treats the entire intervening sublinear regime as open, despite Theorem 27 resolving part of it.

Originality and importance must be separated from correctness. The exact critical norm cap with an arbitrary floor below one, the universal strict-tail threshold on the stated interval, and the joint growing-marginal separation are plausible publishable novelty candidates. The methods themselves—codes with primal/dual distance, Walsh correction, forward/reverse hypercontractivity, sign regions, Gram comparison and scalar moment optimization—are established. This review did not locate a prior exact duplicate, but neither the search nor the manuscript establishes historical priority. The contribution is to finite Boolean positive transfer operators with unrestricted global weights. It establishes no local-dynamics, fixed-range Gibbs, continuum, or physical mass-gap result.

DETAILED MATHEMATICAL ASSESSMENT

1. Conventions and the code construction: pages 2–5, Theorem 1, Lemma 2, Proposition 3 and Lemma 4.
The conversion M_beta = (1+exp(-2 beta))^L P_rho, with rho = tanh(beta), gives the stated normalized eigenvalue convention. Positive definiteness follows from the two positive tensor eigenvalues at beta>0. Strict Perron separation is correctly distinguished from uniform separation. Lemma 2 uses two union bounds on a random generator matrix. A primal low-weight failure includes rank failure, so its complement really gives dimension ceil(RL); the dual calculation is valid for each fixed nonzero column vector. The inclusive binomial bound covers the strict primal event without an integrality assumption. The dual-distance condition implies surjective labelled projections, not merely exchangeability.

For Proposition 3, the code block is a M_CC, with all eigenvalues in a[1-s,1+s]. Interlacing gives the required m-th lower bound even if 1-s is negative. The outside block is positive semidefinite and has trace at most eta; the cross block has Frobenius norm at most sqrt(eta). Subtracting the lower eigenvalue bound from the upper Perron bound and dividing by lambda_1>=a gives 2s+2m(sqrt(eta)+eta). The choices eta=exp(-2 beta L)/m^2 and R log 2<=beta/4 produce the stated 7 exp(-gamma L), with m=2^ceil(RL) and gamma>=beta/4. Eigenvalue indices exist because ceil(RL)<L eventually.

The stationary-law assertion is stronger than weighting-law marginal agreement and is actually proved: code translation commutes with the matrix; uniqueness of its normalized positive Perron vector makes v and v^2 code-translation invariant. Surjectivity then makes each stated marginal uniform. The Doob normalization and detailed balance are correct. No equality between mu and pi is assumed. I attempted the obvious attacks involving rank defects, zero support, floors, and confusing stationary with input marginals; none invalidated this argument.

2. Parity control and the information consequence: pages 5–6, Proposition 5 and Corollary 6.
Multiplication by full parity swaps complement Walsh characters. The 2-by-2 blocks have trace rho^s+rho^(L-s) and determinant (1-t^2)rho^L. Their upper roots are ordered by trace; the empty/full lower root cannot outrank the singleton upper root. The ratio comparison using upper-root/trace monotonicity is valid. Its maximum over L>=2 is 2rho/(1+rho^2), approached at L=2, |t|->1. Thus the heat-bath parity obstruction really does not give the transfer obstruction. The supplied parity predecessor calculates a different generator and its gap does tend to zero at fixed size; the contrast is justified. The congruence predecessor supplies antipodal concentration but not pairwise marginal agreement. The new code conclusion is not a mere restatement of either supplied predecessor.

Corollary 6 is a literal indistinguishability statement about a specified input tuple. It neither makes the product chain slow nor excludes certificates using extra conditional or pointwise information. These restrictions are stated clearly.

3. Upper caps and supercritical moments: pages 7–9, Theorems 7 and 9, Corollary 8.
The key argument uses a second eigenvector's positive and negative supports. Normalizing each sign separately avoids losing control when one sign has very small mass. For a sign vector h and f=sqrt(r)h, positivity yields lambda_2 f<=r P_rho f without division by r, so zeros are allowed. The forward inequality gives an improved exponent, ordinary Holder interpolates with a positive exponent below one, and the reverse bilinear inequality supplies a positive cross term. No triangle inequality in a quasi-norm is used. The compressed matrix has its other eigenvalue lambda_2+D/(cd), with cd<=1/2. This is an actual upper Rayleigh bound on lambda_1, not an assertion that the sign span is invariant.

Theorem 7's exponent (1+rho^2)/(1-rho)^2 follows from its interpolation identity. For Theorem 9, Q= s(1+rho)/(s rho+1+rho)>1+rho is exactly the supercritical requirement. The bound lambda_1<=B uses the density moment and forward hypercontractivity. In the branch lambda_2>=lambda_1/2, the displayed exponent e and the lower bound (2B)^(-2e) follow. The one-point active support and the other eigenvalue branch are treated. Corollary 8 is a valid necessary maximum-density-growth consequence; it is not a sufficient condition for collapse.

4. Subcritical counterexamples and the balanced critical family: pages 9–13, Theorems 10–12.
The product tilts have unit L^s norm and small mean. Normalization of their sum gives the advertised norm cap and positive floor. The one-site eigenvalue expansion has quadratic coefficient 2rho/(1-rho)-(s-1), positive exactly below the critical exponent. The strict cross-norm inequality omega<kappa is justified: equality in both norm inequalities would require a common top eigenvector of H_+ and H_-, impossible because their difference is invertible. The rectangular Gram matrix contributes extra zeros only; the positive leading eigenvalues used in the estimate are genuine eigenvalues of the square target. The noise summand has norm one, producing the stated bound after division. This establishes a second slow mode, not just unbounded top norm.

For Theorem 11, the scalar convexity remainder bounds |r-1|^s directly for s>=2 and by the weighted quadratic remainder for 1<s<2. Holder gives the stated power of B+1. The perturbation bound is for S_r, isospectral with T_r. In Theorem 12 the quadratic variation of log kappa cancels at criticality, while log tau has negative coefficient -rho/[2(1-rho^2)]. With delta=d L^(-1/3), L delta^4 tends to zero and L delta^2 diverges. Balancing the two diagonal Gram top eigenvalues exactly is essential. Both leading eigenvalues, not just the second, are controlled. No uniform rate near the exponent endpoint is claimed.

5. Exact critical budget: pages 13–15, Theorem 13.
The restricted-support estimate <h,T_r h> <= (E[r^s 1_C])^(1/s)||h||_2^2 follows at p=1+rho from p/(2-p)=s. Applying it separately to the signs of a second eigenvector gives 2lambda_2^s<=E r^s, including zeros and repeated lambda_2. Together with lambda_1>=1 this gives the universal subthreshold gap. The balanced tilt has E R^s=1 and all fixed interior positive moments tending to zero. The elementary mixed-power bound proves the full moment tends to two; convergence in probability alone is not used to infer this.

The final dilution toward 1 is small in critical L^s norm and therefore small after sandwiching by Q. It enforces the cap in each dimension while preserving both eigenvalue limits. Its floor tends to one, so finite-prefix replacement gives any fixed b<1. The exception b=1 is correct and necessary. This supplies actual endpoint attainability for the worst-case problem. The sign-splitting technique itself has prior analogues; the exact multiplicative Boolean endpoint construction is a substantive candidate for new content, subject to priority limits.

6. Escaped moment and cutoff one: pages 15–20, Theorem 14, Propositions 15–16, Theorem 17 and Corollary 18.
Theorem 14 splits a normalized sign function at a fixed density threshold K. A tail moment below one cannot carry all the Rayleigh energy. The low-density part therefore has a positive forward norm and bounded L^2 norm; interpolation and reverse hypercontractivity produce a positive cross term. The parameter identity theta=s^(-2) is correct and gives the displayed power K^(1-s^2). The proof needs one fixed cutoff for the family and obtains it from Theta<1; it does not interchange supremum and limit without justification.

The stable-spike example in Proposition 15 is valid because its sandwiched rank-one norm is ((1+rho)/2)^L. Multiplication by the spike size 2^(L/s) still gives a contracting base (1+rho)/2^(2rho/(1+rho))<1. Thus escaped critical mass can be arbitrarily large while S_r remains close to P_rho. Proposition 16 correctly handles the finite prefix in identifying the escaped moment. The fixed-cutoff construction in Theorem 17 uses the stronger relative-mean tail estimate P(R_L>E R_L)->0. The choice a_L=1-P(R_L>m_L)^(1/s) gives the finite strict-tail cap by Minkowski. The subsequent dilution preserves the event above one and decreases the values there. This handles the otherwise delicate cutoff discontinuity. Corollary 18 is a valid sufficient estimate for K<1 and does not pretend to be optimal.

7. Finite backgrounds and attained strict-tail budget: pages 20–26, Proposition 19 and Theorems 20–21.
The scalar optimization follows from a supporting affine function. For K>k_s, y^s/(y-K) has its unique minimum at sK/(s-1), giving the two-point optimizer and the strict/non-strict distinction. For K<=k_s, the tangent at one gives cost at least one. This is scalar optimization, not by itself a spectral classification.

For Theorem 20 the long-parity substitution suppresses each nonconstant background coefficient after Q-sandwiching by rho^(|U|/2). The fixed background law is implemented with dyadic probability and the strict budget slack absorbs that approximation. The tensor comparison controls the top two eigenvalues. The tail calculation in the low sector retains the escaped unit of moment, while the high sector contributes its constant moment plus that unit; mixed terms vanish by interior-moment estimates. The limiting full moment can exceed two, so there is no hidden contradiction with the exact total-cap theorem.

Theorem 21 removes slack more carefully. The growing number of Walsh coefficients is bounded by 2^k, while M/k tends to infinity, so (2^k-1)rho^(M/2)->0. Clipping at c_n makes the strict event in the low sector sparse; compensating the mean only on the high sector preserves the floor K. The reserve e_L includes both dyadic excess and mixed-tail errors. Its identity in (28) yields the finite budget rather than only an asymptotic bound. The four-term norm comparison to P_b tensor F_n controls both actual leading eigenvalues. Constant-density prefix replacement is admissible. Replacing r>K by r>=K would invalidate the result, and the manuscript explicitly excludes that replacement.

8. Scalar envelope and universal necessity: pages 26–29, Theorems 22–23.
Conditional Jensen gives the high-sector cost h(p) and forces p>=p_tau on its decreasing branch. The complementary contribution is at most K^s(1-p), giving the exact envelope U(tau). The two-point law attains it on arbitrary probability spaces; the dyadic approximation approaches it from the feasible side on uniform cubes. Uniqueness at tau=g and the integrality condition are correct. The example s=2,K=3/4 gives tau_c=25/17. This bound is properly distinguished from the exact spectral threshold 7/4 in that example.

Theorem 23 supplies a separate, genuinely needed necessity argument. Sign compression bounds the normalized cross term by B^(1/s)gamma/2, even for unequal sign masses. Reverse hypercontractivity forces one weighted sign to have small p quasi-norm. Truncation below a fixed M then has small forward energy, whereas the total sign Rayleigh quotient is at least 1-gamma. The triangle inequality is applied after Q in Hilbert space. Consequently the high-density moment is bounded below by (32), and tends to at least one along a collapsing family. Applying the scalar affine minorant to r 1_{r<=M} on the original probability space yields the separate g-AB/M^(s-1) contribution. The pieces are disjoint. This establishes tau>=1+g without presupposing a background/tilt decomposition. The explicit contradiction constant uses positive D and M; its inequalities have the correct directions. Theorem 21 matches the same strict event and parameters, so the boundary claim is supported throughout the specified k_s<K<1 interval.

9. Joint marginal construction: pages 31–33, Theorem 24.
For a=3/8-alpha/4, L delta^4=d^4 L^(alpha-1/2)->0 and L delta^2=d^2 L^(1/4+alpha/2)->infinity. The balanced family therefore retains its critical spectral and moment limits. The low Fourier correction has mean zero and exactly removes all nonconstant coefficients through k=floor(L^alpha). Its uniform bound is at most m_L k L^k. The negative logarithm of m_L has order L^(1/4+alpha/2), which dominates L^alpha log L precisely for alpha<1/2. The correction thus tends to zero uniformly. This fact, together with the constant baseline, is what protects positivity; arbitrary Fourier erasure would not do so. Small final dilution enforces the exact cap and preserves the vanished coefficients. It follows that the constructed weighting laws simultaneously satisfy the moment cap, prescribed floor, growing exact marginals and collapse. Stationary-law marginals are not claimed here and are not implied by the proof.

10. Rigidity and the quantitative expansion: pages 33–36, Theorems 25–27.
Exact marginal matching kills the low-low Walsh block of Q M_(r-1) Q because |A symmetric-difference C|<=2floor(k/2)<=k. For Theorem 25, the bounded part can be factored through a noisier sandwich, while the critical tail is small by uniform integrability and endpoint Holder. The block decomposition incurs the stated factor two. First taking L to infinity at fixed M, then M to infinity is legitimate. It proves norm convergence of S_r and consequently eigenvalue and gap convergence for T_r.

In Theorem 26, the credited homogeneous inequality gives a strictly positive exponential deficit away from degree zero. Above degree L/2, full parity maps to complementary degree while preserving L^p norms; retaining the original noise factor is the correct operation. Summing at most L+1 levels costs sqrt(L+1). The low-low block cancellation means each remaining operator block has a high-degree factor to which the improvement applies. No false L^p contraction for the Walsh projection is assumed.

I checked Theorem 27's expansion analytically from the manuscript's explicit formula, without software algebra. With A(z)=((1+z)^p+(1-z)^p)/2, the fourth logarithmic coefficient is

Writing v=z^2 and a=p-1 gives x=a v+(4b4/p)v^2+O(v^3), hence v=x/a-(4b4/p)x^2/a^3+O(x^3). Inserting this and h(x)=-x log x+x-x^2/2+O(x^3) cancels x log x and yields the stated deficit p(p-2)x^2/[12(p-1)]+O(x^3). Analytic inversion is legitimate for fixed real p>2 because a>0. The positivity and continuity of a_p(x)/x^2 on the compact interval then give c_p>0; its minimum is not incorrectly identified with its value at zero. The high-degree bound exp(-c_p j^2/L), the level sum, and the two block estimates imply (36). The k=L case is separately trivial since r=1. NUMBERED OBJECTIONS AND RESERVATIONS

O1 — MEDIUM; mathematically consequential wording, repair required.
Exact location: page 1, abstract, third paragraph, sentence asserting operator-norm convergence; compare pages 33–36, Theorems 25–27 and equation (36).
The object converging in norm must be named S_r=Q M_r Q. Isospectrality does not transfer ||S_r-P_rho||->0 to ||T_r-P_rho||->0 in the fixed Walsh coordinates. A counterexample to the latter reading is r_L=1+t chi_[L], with fixed 0<t<1. It is bounded above and below, has all proper uniform marginals and critical-power uniform integrability, and satisfies k_L=L-1. Write sqrt(r_L)=a+b chi_[L], where a=(sqrt(1+t)+sqrt(1-t))/2, b=(sqrt(1+t)-sqrt(1-t))/2 and 2ab=t. On the Walsh span of 1 and chi_[L], T_r has off-diagonal entry ab(1+rho^L), while P_rho has zero there. Thus ||T_r-P_rho||>=t(1+rho^L)/2, which does not vanish. In contrast ||S_r-P_rho||=t rho^(L/2)->0. Small fixed t even meets any chosen critical norm cap strictly above one. The formal theorems correctly state S_r convergence and T_r spectral convergence, so they survive this example. Requested repair: specify the sandwiched, isospectral operator in the abstract and retain the distinction wherever norm convergence is summarized. This is not a request to weaken the valid spectral conclusion.

O2 — LOW; internal summary inconsistency.
Exact location: page 31, Section 18, final introductory paragraph, ending “The intervening sublinear regime is open here.”
Read literally, this includes sublinear orders such as L^(3/4), which Theorem 27 resolves. Replace it with the precise unresolved window between the construction and k_L^2/(L log(L+1))->infinity, or direct the reader to the accurate scope paragraph on page 36. The abstract and page-36 limitation are more accurate than this leftover paragraph. No proof is affected.

A subject expert's literature comparison would materially strengthen a publication case. This is a limitation on novelty confidence and on a high-ceiling score, not an unsupported allegation of duplication.

The material is inspectable, but a new reader should see a compact statement of the final exponent/norm/tail classifications and joint marginal theorem near the start. Preserve complete historical reports externally, while moving much of the narrative history and genuinely auxiliary estimates to appendices or the evidence package. This is an editorial recommendation, not a reason to delete adverse evidence or suppress provenance.

The joint critical-budget construction asserts exact marginals of the weighting law only. It does not carry over Theorem 1's stationary-law assertion. The rigidity/construction separation does not settle k comparable to sqrt(L), the necessity of the logarithmic loss, or a sharp marginal-order threshold. Global weights and nonlocal transfer updates remain essential to the stated scope. These are already acknowledged restrictions, not missing hypotheses in the formal results. They limit the significance rating and prohibit extrapolation to local Gibbs dynamics or physical mass gaps.
