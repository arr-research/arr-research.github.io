SCIENTIFIC ASSESSMENT

A. What is being proved, and why the distinction of operators matters (pages 1-7).

The underlying noise parameter is rho=tanh(beta), with 0<rho<1. The operator whose normalized spectral gap is studied is T_r=M_sqrt(r) P_rho M_sqrt(r), up to the explicitly stated common scalar relating it to A_mu. The useful linear sandwich S_r=P_sqrt(rho) M_r P_sqrt(rho) has the same full finite spectrum by the square-product identity. It is not generally the same operator and need not have the same distance to P_rho. The manuscript keeps this distinction in the formal norm theorems and supplies a convincing parity counterexample on page 40: the off-diagonal entry of T_r-P_rho stays at least t/2, while the S_r difference decays. This prevents a materially false inference from isospectrality.

Theorem 1 is a valid combination of a standard primal/dual code argument with elementary matrix estimates. The random-generator union bound includes rank failure, the dual-distance condition gives labelled uniform coordinate projections, and the specified rate makes both entropy inequalities strict. In Proposition 3 the code block is close to a scalar identity, while the complement and cross block have norms bounded by eta and sqrt(eta). Interlacing therefore controls all the claimed exponentially many eigenvalues, not just two trial vectors. The choice eta=exp(-2 beta L)/m^2 pays the factor m; the constant 7 follows from the displayed separate errors. Translation by codewords preserves the positive Perron vector and hence gives the claimed stationary marginals. I found no assumption that pi=mu.

Proposition 5 is an appropriate negative control: the complement-pair Walsh blocks have the displayed trace and determinant, the top and second eigenvalues are correctly selected, and the ratio bound is uniform at fixed beta. Its heat-bath comparison is compatible with the supplied parity predecessor. The corollary about certificates follows from identical labelled inputs and a valid worst-case bound. None of these steps establishes local Glauber mixing or a physical mass gap, and the text explicitly excludes those readings.

B. Moment regimes and the exact critical cap (pages 7-15).

Theorems 7 and 9 use forward hypercontractivity to control the energy of normalized sign parts and reverse hypercontractivity to bound their interaction. The two-dimensional compression is handled without assuming balanced sign norms: its other eigenvalue is lambda_2+D/(cd), and cd<=1/2 supplies the usable lower separation. The interpolation involving exponents below one is power-Holder, not an illicit quasi-norm triangle inequality. The mean-one test supplies lambda_1>=1. The constants in the supercritical theorem diverge as the exponent approaches the boundary, as they should; no uniform endpoint claim is concealed there.

Theorem 10 does more than demonstrate growth of the top norm. For the two antipodal tilted tensor channels, the cross Gram norm is strictly smaller than the one-site top eigenvalue. Two top eigenvalues of the block diagonal survive the perturbation. This gives normalized gap collapse, with the stated floor and norm budget in every dimension. The critical construction in Theorem 12 instead uses one tilted channel and the uniform channel, balanced at top eigenvalue one. The conditions L delta^2 -> infinity and L delta^4 -> 0 genuinely separate overlap loss from top-eigenvalue mismatch.

For Theorem 13, endpoint Holder on each sign support gives lambda_2^s <= E[r^s 1_sign]. Summing the disjoint supports proves 2 lambda_2^s <= E r^s. This correctly yields the lower gap bound below B*=2^(1/s). The exact-cap construction uses smaller moments of the normalized tilt to show the total s-moment tends to two; convergence in probability alone would not suffice. The final dilution is controlled in the S_r sandwich through the endpoint L^s-to-operator estimate. It preserves the limiting first two eigenvalues and the prescribed floor after a finite prefix replacement. Consequently both equality and the strict subcritical budget regime are covered. These arguments withstand the potential zero-density and extra-Gram-zero issues.

C. Escaped moments, strict cutoffs and exact tail threshold (pages 16-29).

The cutoff estimate in Theorem 14 is a valid sign/truncation argument: a tail below one unit cannot account for the energy of both nearly leading sign parts. Proposition 15 supplies a different, stable spike family, where the smoothed rank-one perturbation decays despite a nonzero escaped critical moment. Proposition 16 correctly includes the finite-prefix argument needed to pass from each fixed-cutoff limit to the supremum defining Theta. Thus escaped moment at least one is necessary for collapse, not sufficient.

Theorem 17 deserves credit for respecting the strict event at cutoff one. The relative-mean estimate P(R_L>E R_L)->0, rather than mere R_L->0, justifies the amplitude reserve. The subsequent dilution leaves the event above one unchanged and decreases its integrand, so the finite tail cap is actually maintained. Corollary 18 follows from the total moment estimate when the cutoff is below one.

Proposition 19 and Theorem 22 are scalar extremal-law results. The affine minorant and conditional Jensen calculations are sound, including nonattainment for the non-strict event and the dyadic restriction on a finite uniform cube. I checked the scalar s=2, K=3/4 specialization: the envelope threshold 25/17 is distinct from the eventual spectral threshold 7/4. The text correctly does not identify a scalar extremizer with a spectral extremizer.

Theorem 20's finite parity background has an operator error that vanishes because every substituted nonconstant Walsh support is long. Theorem 21 pays the growing number of coefficients explicitly: 2^k rho^(M/2)->0 with k of order L^(1/3) and M of order L^(2/3). Clipping makes the low sector sit exactly at K except on a small event; mean compensation acts on the high sector, preserving the floor. The amplitude reserve pays both the dyadic excess and mixed-power errors. Comparison to actual tensor eigenvalues controls lambda_1 as well as lambda_2.

The necessity proof in Theorem 23 is substantive. Small normalized gap forces a small normalized cross term; reverse hypercontractivity then selects a sign part with small low quasi-norm. At a fixed large amplitude cutoff its bounded part has vanishing energy, forcing at least one unit of high s-moment. The scalar affine inequality applied to r 1_{r<=M} reserves an additional g(K) in the middle tail, up to a first-moment error of order M^(1-s). The order 'dimension limit at fixed M, then M to infinity' is legitimate. The displayed explicit contradiction bound also pays both errors. The claimed exact threshold 1+g(K) on (s-1)/s<K<1 follows from a necessity theorem and an attained family; neither alone would have sufficed.

D. Exact marginals and rigidity before the sharp scale (pages 31-36).

Theorem 24's elementary sup-norm erasure is small at the stated fixed-power orders. Its uniform baseline preserves positivity, and the last mixture imposes the exact cap while keeping all Fourier zeros. Theorem 25's vanishing low-low block, bounded/tail split, and choice of an auxiliary noise parameter give its stated rigidity under critical-power uniform integrability. There is no assumption that a sharp Fourier projection contracts higher L^p norms. Theorems 26 and 27 use the cited homogeneous moment bound correctly, with full parity converting levels above L/2 and preserving L^p norms without commuting parity through Q. The quantitative quadratic expansion of the deficit is consistent with the cited parameterization. The sqrt(L log L) sufficient scale is explicitly superseded later; it is not presented as sharp.

E. Endpoint high-degree operator norm (pages 37-40).

The external distribution-sensitive inequality is used with epsilon=(1-sqrt(rho))/2, so that its input exponent is q0=1+rho and its noise operator is Q. This parameter match is essential and is correct in the PDF and TeX. The primary source confirms the theorem, domain, defining functions and strict negativity used here.

I analytically checked Lemma 29's finite two-sign entropy calculation. The quartic coefficient of K(lambda) is q0/3-q0^2/2. The quadratic pieces in I(t,t)/2-I1(t)/q0 cancel; the remaining coefficient is (q0-1)(2-q0)/(12 q0^3). With u=(q0-1)I1(t)/q0 this gives the stated positive quotient (2-q0)/(3 q0(q0-1)). The global modulus is obtained by compactness and is not silently replaced by this local coefficient.

Theorem 28's bounded/tail decomposition uses only the L^2 spectral projection bound on the bounded piece. The other piece either has a small q0 norm or a large log norm-ratio, to which the refined inequality applies. This proves the displayed three-term estimate without summing a dimension-dependent number of homogeneous levels. The converse product has binomial Fourier mass, and its endpoint norm has quartic loss d_rho=(1-rho^2)/(12 rho). Choosing t_L^2=A/sqrt(L), first with fixed A>C, makes the removed Fourier mass vanish and gives a positive lower bound. Taking A down to C afterwards is valid. The witnesses after projection can be signed, so the text correctly refuses to infer a density counterexample from them. Corollary 30 then follows from the zero low-low block and two Holder estimates.

F. Positivity-preserving erasure and finite-ratio construction (pages 40-44).

Lemma 31 addresses the actual obstruction rather than assuming a small L^s correction is small pointwise. The L^s bound follows from homogeneous hypercontractivity and increasing factorial-denominator terms. The one-sided pointwise bound is separate: Cauchy's coefficient estimate controls the central region; the derivative recurrence makes the alternating terms monotone on the negative side, where odd truncation is crucial; and positivity of the (m+1)st derivative gives the Taylor remainder sign on the positive side. Differentiating (1-t^2)g'_z=(z-Lt)g_z gives exactly equation (45). The numerical assumptions u>=4m and the region z>=2Ldelta pay the induction inequalities. No root-location theorem or unjustified projection contractivity is needed.

For Theorem 32, u=sqrt(m sqrt(L)) gives m/u->0, u->infinity and u^2/L->0. Both erasure errors then vanish, while balanced channels still converge to eigenvalues one. The final dilution has vanishing S_r norm cost and preserves exact marginals. This covers any admissible sub-square-root sequence, not just fixed powers.

For Theorem 33, u=Km instead leaves Ldelta^4->K^2 C^2. Retaining the quartic term gives log kappa=-s(s^2-1)delta^4/24+O(delta^6), consistent with both displayed versions of gamma_rho. Using no balancing coefficient is necessary for the claimed profile. The two blocks then have first eigenvalues one and t_C<1, so the actual second limit is max(rho,t_C), including their coincidence. The same erasure and exact-cap repair have vanishing effect. This is an attained profile and an upper bound on an infimum; it is not the universal optimal profile.

G. The finite-ratio lower bound and small-C order (pages 44-48).

Theorem 34's strongest point is that the full low/high coupling is controlled. Lemma 35 reserves one unit of critical moment for the mean-one bounded part via Jensen, giving a limiting tail L^s norm at most one. Lemma 36 splits the low subspace once more at a fixed degree n. On the intermediate part exact compression and positive-semidefinite Cauchy-Schwarz control the coupling by sqrt(B* rho^(n+1)). On the fixed-degree part a subcritical tail norm controls the tail coupling; bounded amplitudes are paid by M rho^(m/2). The order n, then M, then dimension is valid and uniform over the densities.

The high-block bound uses the square of the endpoint L^2-to-p0 norm. Since m/sqrt(L)->C/2, its limiting bound after sending M to infinity is exp(-alpha_rho C^2/2). The low block begins with 1,rho, the coupling tends to zero, and min-max therefore gives lambda_1->1 and the stated lambda_2 upper bound. Restricting to the constant and a singleton gives the lower bound lambda_2>=rho. The finite uniform inequality (55) follows from the same density-independent errors, not merely a pointwise sequential argument. The example in Remark 37 accurately explains why bounding the high block alone would have failed.

Corollary 38 is a legitimate comparison between a universal lower bound and one admissible attained family. It proves quadratic order at fixed rho,b as C decreases after the dimension limit. It does not prove an optimal coefficient, an exact finite-C infimum, or a joint dimension/small-C asymptotic.

ORIGINALITY AND LITERATURE COMPARISON

The following are primary sources actually consulted. The comparison is targeted, not an exhaustive review of all cited papers or later literature. Source statements were checked, not reproved in full.

P1. Kirshner and Samorodnitsky, arXiv:1909.11929v1, Theorem 1.3, Propositions 2.6-2.7, Theorem 1.8, Sections 2.1.6-2.1.7 and Lemma 2.14: https://arxiv.org/html/1909.11929v1 . These supply the homogeneous and distribution-sensitive inequalities. The manuscript's parameter substitutions agree with these statements. Its claimed application and elementary expansions must be distinguished from inventing the underlying improved hypercontractivity.

P2. Mossel, O'Donnell, Regev, Steif and Sudakov, Corollary 3.3: https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~odonnell/papers/nicd.pdf . This confirms the reverse bilinear inequality for nonnegative functions at rho<=sqrt((1-p)(1-q)), including the choice p=q=1-rho used here.

P3. Biskup and Koenig, Lemma 4.5 and equation (4.29): https://www.wias-berlin.de/people/koenig/www/PPP4PAM-CMP-final.pdf . This is a genuine sign-region/budget-splitting precedent for a second-eigenvalue bound in an additive lattice setting. It supports the manuscript's attribution of the mechanism; it is not the same multiplicative Boolean classification.

P4. Alon, Andoni, Kaufman, Matulef, Rubinfeld and Xie, Fact 2.3 and Section 3.1: https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~andoni/papers/kwise.pdf . Fourier characterization, erasure and positivity repair are explicit prior methods. The specialized one-sided odd truncation and critical moment/spectrum conclusions here are not stated in the passages checked. The manuscript's MIT URL timed out during this review; the author-hosted Columbia copy was accessible.

P5. Miclo, Theorem 1 and Section 6, especially Proposition 11: https://perso.math.univ-toulouse.fr/miclo/files/2012/04/hyperboundedness.pdf . This gives hyperbounded reversible Markov gap results and a sharp obstruction at two for a fourth-power operator norm. It is an important conceptual predecessor, but that norm is not E r^s, and T_r is not Markov under the base uniform law.

P6. Wang, Theorem 1.1: https://arxiv.org/html/1305.4460v6 . Its invariant-Markov operator tail norms and spectral criterion are different from the scalar density-tail budget in this manuscript. No equivalence between those quantities was established in this assessment.

P7. Gross, Theorem 2.2 and the spectral-gap discussion: https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20282v2 . The result concerns additive Schroedinger perturbations under a logarithmic Sobolev hypothesis and moments of both signs of the potential. It supplies dimension-independent estimates. Missing inverse-density control is not a valid distinction for the manuscript's examples with a fixed positive floor; the unproved operator/form correspondence is the relevant distinction.

P8. Heilman, Mossel and Oleszkiewicz, introductory tail-space results, Theorems 1.2-1.4: https://www.stevenheilman.org/~heilman/papers/hmo.pdf . These concern same-exponent heat smoothing, Poincare estimates and special-valued tail functions. They are relevant context, but the checked statements do not give the precise endpoint L^2-to-(1+1/rho) projection norm threshold of Theorem 28.

P9. Volberg, introduction and Section 2: https://arxiv.org/html/2208.00472v2 . This develops tail-space estimates in Banach-valued L^p spaces and discusses the earlier heat-smoothing literature. A square root or power appearing there is not by itself an identification of the current dimension-versus-degree endpoint threshold. A systematic comparison should nevertheless be cited rather than referring only to unnamed tail-space literature.

P10. Brehm and Resch, publisher abstract and bibliographic record: https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.28 . This confirms the stronger algorithmic primal/dual-code background cited in the manuscript. I did not audit its full construction; it is not needed for the paper's self-contained random-code lemma.

The most plausible originality candidates are: (i) the exact critical cap including its positive-floor endpoint, (ii) the universal strict-tail threshold with equality attained at the cutoff floor, (iii) the exact endpoint high-degree norm scale, and (iv) the complete sub-square-root construction together with the finite-ratio lower bound and quadratic-order infimum. The code/Perron marginal combination is also a concrete useful synthesis. None is certified here as historically first. The mere absence of an exact phrase in search results is not a novelty argument.

RETAINED OBJECTIONS AND LIMITATIONS

O1. External priority and tail-space positioning remain insufficiently established (pages 30, 36-40, 48, 51). The manuscript responsibly admits this, but the central endpoint and joint-density results deserve a more explicit comparison to named tail-space sources such as P8-P9, with input/output exponents, degree scale and operator spelled out. The literature consulted does not duplicate the exact statements; it also does not clear all predecessors or implications. This limits the novelty rating, not the mathematical validity found here. Requested revision: add a short precise related-results comparison and bibliographic citations; retain the priority caveat.

O2. The main research narrative is burdened by superseded intermediate results and review chronology (particularly pages 11-13, 30-36, 48-50). The full 51 pages are readable, but an abstract nearly a page long, repeated balanced-product arguments, and extensive source-assessment histories make the strongest results hard to locate and risk confusing historical process with evidence. Requested revision: provide a compact theorem/dependency map, foreground the final classifications, and move detailed chronology and redundant preliminary material to a clearly marked appendix or archival supplement without deleting the original evidence. This is a presentation request, not a request to rescore the PDF.

O3. The abstract's finite-ratio sentence omits the fixed positive floor imposed in the formal Theorem 34 and Corollary 38 (page 1 versus pages 45 and 48). The displayed proof appears to work with nonnegative densities too: Lemmas 35-36 and the eigenvalue comparison do not require a numerical positive floor, and the active-support Perron interpretation can handle zeros. Nevertheless, the front matter should match the theorem actually stated. Requested revision: either mention the floor in the abstract or explicitly state and justify the nonnegative extension. This is a minor scope mismatch, not an exhibited counterexample or an invalidating defect in Theorem 34 as written.

O4. Practical significance and effective bounds are limited (Theorems 28, 33-34; Corollary 38). The global deficit modulus, large-dimension cutoffs and choice of K are existential/conservative, while exact finite-C optimum and optimal small-C coefficient remain open. The rigidity proof uses exact Fourier zeros; no stability theorem for noisy or approximate marginal data is supplied. These limits are largely disclosed and do not need to be solved for a credible working paper. They do mean the results cannot yet be sold as a calibrated finite-size certificate or an applied mixing guarantee. Requested clarification: keep these limitations prominent and separate proved consequences from possible applications.
