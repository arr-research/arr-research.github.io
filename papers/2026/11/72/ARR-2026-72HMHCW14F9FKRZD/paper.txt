1 Introduction
Let G = (V, E) be a simple, undirected, connected graph on n ≥ 3 vertices with adjacency
matrix AG, diagonal degree matrix DG = diag(dG(v))v∈V (where dG(v) ≥ δ(G) ≥ 1), and
combinatorial Laplacian LG = DG − AG. The simple random walk on G has transition ma-
trix PG = D−1 G AG, which is reversible with respect to the stationary distribution πG(v) =
dG(v)/ volG(V ), where volG(S) := ∑ v∈S dG(v). Its spectrum is governed by the symmetric
normalized Laplacian [4]
LG := I − D−1/2 G AGD−1/2 G = D−1/2 G LGD−1/2 G , (1)
whose eigenvalues satisfy 0 = λ1(G) < λ2(G) ≤ · · · ≤ λn(G) ≤ 2. The spectral gap λ2(G)
controls the relaxation time trel(G) = 1/λ2(G) of the lazy random walk, while the absolute
spectral gap
γ∗(G) := min
(
λ2(G), 2 − λn(G)
)
(2)
controls the mixing rate of the non-lazy random walk [7], with 2 − λn(G) measuring distance
from bipartiteness.
Whereas Weyl’s monotonicity theorem guarantees that adding an edge e /∈ E(G) strictly or
weakly increases every eigenvalue of the combinatorial Laplacian LG, the normalized Laplacian
exhibits a striking non-monotonicity: adding an edge can decrease λ2(G), a spectral analogue of
Braess’s paradox [1]. Classic interlacing results by Chen et al. [3] and Butler [2] give λj−1(G) ≤
λj (G + e) ≤ λj+1(G), which for j = 2 reduces to the vacuous bound λ2(G + e) ≥ 0. Eldan, Rácz,
and Schramm [5] proved that in Erdős–Rényi graphs G(n, p), a constant fraction of non-edges
decrease λ2, and Kirkland–Zeng [6] analyzed analogous paradoxes for Kemeny’s constant. Yet
the fundamental quantitative question has remained open:
Given a connected graph G and an edge-disjoint added subgraph H, what is the exact
worst-case degradation λ2(G + H)/λ2(G) as a function of the degrees of G, the edge
budget |E(H)|, or the structural sparsity (degeneracy) of H?

5 Regular-Graph Localization Obstruction and Kr,r + e
