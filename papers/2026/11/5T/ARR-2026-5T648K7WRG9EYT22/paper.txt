1 Introduction
Hirsch’s 1957 conjecture asked whether everyd-dimensional polytope withnfacets has vertex–edge
graph diameter at mostn−d [6]. Santos [5] disproved the linear bound via a43-dimensional
bounded polytope with86facets and diameter at least44, while Kalai and Kleitman [1] gave a quasi-
polynomial upper bound. In November 2026, OpenAI [4] settled the polynomial Hirsch conjecture
negatively: for every subpolynomials(n) = no(1), bounded simple rational polytopes withnfacets
can haves(n)-move diameternω(1) (where one step may cross any face of dimension≤s (n)).
Combined with OpenAI’s companion strongly polynomial linear programming algorithm [3],
Corollary 14.3 of [4] separates general LP algorithms from all edge-following simplex pivot rules on

5 Scope Boundaries and Outlook
This work certifies explicit rational polytope witnesses, projective packing isomorphisms, fold-
crossing lower bounds, and simplex-pivot separations rather than mechanizing the full asymptotic
clock-scheduler construction of [4]. In particular, Mode 1’sd-regular vertex-completeness check
scales with|V(P)|(MAX_VERTICES = 256), making it directly suited to small-to-medium explicit
rational polytopes and prismatoids (such as Santos-type spindles [5]), whereas Mode 3 certifies
the fold-crossing superadditivity backbone independently of vertex count. Extending Mode 1 to
sparse dual-graph certificates of5-dimensional high-width prismatoids and formalizing Balinski’s
connectivity theorem in Lean 4 are natural next steps.
