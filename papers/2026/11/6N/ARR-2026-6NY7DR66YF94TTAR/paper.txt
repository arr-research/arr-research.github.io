1 Introduction
Detecting and quantifying multipartite thermal entangle-
ment in quantum spin chains from experimentally accessi-
ble two-point correlations (structure factors) is a central
problem in quantum many-body physics [2, 1, 6, 7]. In a
recent work, Mathé, Usui, Gühne, and Vitagliano [5] solved
the exact optimal covariance matrix criterion (CMC) for
symmetric spin-1/2XY chains [4], reducing the full many-
body separability test to a closed-form piecewise-linear
witness ωCMC(ϱ)in three normalized structure-factor vari-
ances( sx,sy,sz)and linking ωCMC to both the best sepa-
rable approximation (BSA) [3] and the geometric measure
of entanglementEG(ϱ)[8].
Two analytical and certification gaps remain open in [5]:
1. Product-state maximization forEG(ϱ):Evalu-
ating the Legendre-transform lower boundEG(ϱ) ≥
1 + λ⟨W′⟩ϱ −sup |ϕ⟩prod λmax(λW′+ |ϕ⟩⟨ϕ|)requires
optimizing over allN-qubit product states|ϕ⟩. Sec-
tion H of [5] observes numerically that the Néel state
appears to maximize the largest eigenvalue and resorts
to a PPT-relaxation semidefinite program (SDP) to
obtain safe bounds.
2. Floating-point sensitivity of narrow thermal
PPT-bound entanglement:For the N = 4peri-
odic XX chain, thermal PPT-bound entanglement (all
seven nontrivial partial transposes positive semidef-
inite while ωCMC < 1) exists only inside a narrow
temperature windowT∈ (1.7280,1.7402)where the
minimum partial-transpose eigenvalue is of order10−4
to10 −3, making floating-point eigensolver claims vul-
nerable to rounding artifacts.
We close both gaps rigorously and package the
entire theoretical hierarchy into a machine-verified
framework (XY-CMC-NEEL-CERT-1.0) combining 20 con-
structive Lean 4.35.0 theorems (zero sorry, zero
Classical.choice, zero Mathlib dependencies) with an
AST-audited zero-float exact rational Python verifier over
Q.
