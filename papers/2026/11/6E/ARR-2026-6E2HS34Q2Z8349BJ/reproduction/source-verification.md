# Scope and source verification

The manuscript groups three related results on one-step local retention: a uniform killed-norm bound for subsets of small positive-radius-fraction Hamming balls, a failure of exact radial domination for the same geometry, and a projected-edge comparison for shallow coordinate cuts. The November 18 Boolean Doob working paper supplies related background. Its public score applies only to the predecessor PDF. No prior score, publication receipt or version grant is reused.

At half noise the positive-moment chain uses (9, 9/4, 21, 21/8, 15/2). The final two norm changes use respectively Holder with the third moment and forward hypercontractivity at correlation 1/2. Stationary normalization is uniform probability and the cubic counting-measure sum is bounded by 2^(L+1). The entropy per coordinate in the product transition row is (3/4) log(3) - log(2). The Schur test weights the symmetric killed matrix before applying Holder; reversing these operations or replacing the subset by a full ball before testing can weaken the estimate. The center of the ball can be translated independently of the potential.

For gamma, the coefficient of log(2) is -29/15, that of log(3) is 6/5. The paper gives elementary rational logarithm bounds. This is a paper-and-pencil check; no mathematical oracle or proof assistant was executed. In dimensions below the onset of the exponential estimate the contraction bound is needed. When a set is empty conditional retention is undefined and the theorem excludes it. If epsilon is at least one, the ratio comparison in the corollary is not claimed. The exact radial counterexample uses the largest odd radius at most L/97 at half noise; this lies in the range L/96. The denominator in the corollary is conductance, not retention. Absolute control of two small retentions does not bound their ratio. Full spectral gaps and larger-radius comparisons are open.

The explicit radius, exponent and nonlinear stationary-weight specialization are the claims under review. Primary references for context were Bonami (1970), Polyanskiy and Samorodnitsky (2019), and Boyd, Diaconis, Parrilo and Xiao (2005). The comparison is bounded and does not establish priority. No third-party figures, simulations or experimental data were imported.
