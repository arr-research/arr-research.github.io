1 Introduction
Fair division of a divisible, heterogeneous cake [0,1]
intoncontiguousintervals—using onlyn−1 cuts—so
that every agent prefers her own share to every other
share is a classical benchmark in computational so-
cial choice [4, 6]. Because Stromquist [5] proved
that no finite Robertson–Webb protocol can find
an exact contiguous envy-free allocation forn≥3
agents, algorithmic research focuses onε-envy-free
(ε-EF) divisions satisfyingv i(Ai) +ε≥v i(Aj) for
alli, j∈[n] using poly(log(1/ε)) queries. Following
the four-agent breakthrough of Hollender and Rubin-
stein [3], Filos-Ratsikas, Hollender, Latifian, and Ty-
rovolas [2] solved the five-agent case inO(log 3(1/ε))
value queries, simultaneously yielding a new exis-
tence proof requiring no fixed-point theorem be-
yond the one-dimensional intermediate value theo-
rem (IVT).
Executing nested bisection and softmax weight
balancing numerically over floating-point intervals
introduces subtle verification hazards: a tiny round-
ing drift in an anchor cutmor piece lengthℓ j
can silently open an unallocated gap, break a Hall
matching inG δ(m, ℓ) for one of the five depart-
ing agents, leaveG δ disconnected, or violateε-EF
on zero-density regions. By decoupling untrusted
witness discovery from exact-rational verification, a
compact certificate overQcan be independently re-
played in sub-millisecond time with zero floating-
point error.
