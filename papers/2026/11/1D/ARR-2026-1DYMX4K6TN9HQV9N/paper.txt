1 Introduction
In the minimum-cost 2-edge-connected spanning sub-
graph (2ECSS) problem, we are given an undi-
rected multigraphG= (V, E) (n=|V|) with
nonnegative edge costsc:E→Q ≥0 and seek
a minimum-cost edge subsetF⊆Esuch that
(V, F) remains connected after removing any sin-
gle edge. While 2-approximations are known via
rooted 2-arborescences [5] and iterative rounding,
every primal-dual algorithm since Frederickson–
J´ aJ´ a [4], Williamson–Goemans–Mihail–Vazirani [6],
and Beyer–Chimani–Spoerhase (BCS20) [1] stalled
at approximation ratio 3.
In a breakthrough work, Danciu and Ibrahim-
pur [2] surpassed the primal-dual barrier of 3, at-
taining ratio
√
3 + 1<2.74 in a single primal-
dual phase relative to the natural cut-LP relaxation
(P
e∈δ(S) xe ≥2, 0≤x e ≤1). Their core innova-
tion isnon-uniform dual growth: while maintaining
a contracted forestH= (V H, EH), dual variables
of leaves (L) and isolated nodes (I) grow at rate 1,
whereas nontrivial connected components (C) grow
at rateβ∈[0,1/2] wheneverHis disconnected. Be-
cause the optimal rateβ ∗ = (
√
3−1)/2 is irrational,
practical exact-arithmetic implementations naturally
instantiate rational continued-fraction convergents
βk ∈(0,1/2)∩Q(such as 1/3,4/11,15/41,56/153),
eliminating floating-point drift in tight-edge selec-
tion, cycle contraction, and dual constraint verifica-
tion while preserving strict better-than-3 guarantees.
