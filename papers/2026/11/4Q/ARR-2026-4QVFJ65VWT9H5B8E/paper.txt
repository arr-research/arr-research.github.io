1 Introduction and Geometric Setup
Information distances turn probabilistic and algorithmic dependence into metric geometry. Given
two random variablesXi,Xj with finite Shannon entropy, Rajski’s variation of information [1]
dH(Xi,Xj) :=H(X i|Xj) +H(Xj|Xi) = 2H(XiXj)−H(X i)−H(X j)(1)
defines a canonical pseudometric whose Kolmogorov complexity analogue underpins the universal
similarity distance of Bennett, Gács, Li, Vitányi, and Zurek [2], as well as modern metric formulations
of algorithmic information distance by Hutter [9] and Hou [10]. Because Shannon entropy is monotone
(H(XY) ≥H (Y)) and submodular (H(XY)+H(YZ) ≥H (XYZ )+H(Y)), everyn-tuple of random
variables—indeed, every polymatroidh∈ Γn—automatically induces a semimetricdh ∈MET n.
Classical information theory therefore sits comfortablyinsidethe metric cone.
When random variables are replaced by subsystemsA0,...,A n−1 of ann-party quantum state
ρA0···An−1 with von Neumann entropyS(X) = −Tr(ρX log2 ρX), the structural geometry changes
fundamentally. Defining thequantum variation of informationby the same formula,
dQ(Ai,Aj) :=S(A i|Aj) +S(Aj|Ai) = 2S(AiAj)−S(A i)−S(A j),(2)
gives dQ a direct operational meaning: by the quantum state-merging protocol of Horodecki,
Oppenheim, and Winter [6], S(Ai|Aj)is the asymptotic entanglement rate required to mergeAi
into Aj, so dQ(Ai,Aj)is thebidirectional quantum state-merging costbetween parties i and j.
Because entanglement makesS(Ai|Aj)negative on a Bell pair|Φ+⟩ij (where S(i) = S(j) = 1and
S(ij) = 0, givingdQ(i,j) = −2), dQ need not even be nonnegative on general states. Remarkably,
even if we enforce pairwise nonnegativityS(Ai|Aj) ≥0everywhere—eliminating obvious negative
distances—entanglement still breaks the triangle inequality.
