Conceptual article and proposed experimental protocol. No original participant data have been 
collected, and the proposed trial has not been conducted. 
Abstract 
Access to technology can improve performance during practice without producing competence that 
persists when assistance is removed. This article examines a more specific hypothesis than the general 
superiority of technology: instructional designs that preserve independent attempts while supporting 
feedback, retrieval and subsequent application may increase durable independent learning within a 
fixed study period. A critical narrative synthesis of thirteen research publications, supplemented by 
two contextual sources on El Salvador, brings together research on learning, computer tutoring, 
generative artificial intelligence and chess. Findings include benefits, null effects and harms. They do 
not establish a universal technological advantage or isolate technology as the cause of historical 
improvements in chess. The proposed framework distinguishes the organization of practice from the 
cognitive operations learners are intended to acquire. It operationalizes an observable attempt, 
feedback and reapplication cycle without treating cycle frequency as evidence of learning by itself. A 
proposed four-arm randomized trial compares active print study, fixed digital practice, constrained 
adaptive digital practice and direct access to solutions, with common core blocks protecting scheduled 
syllabus coverage. The primary outcome is unaided performance on novel chess positions four weeks 
after training. Improved assisted performance without a delayed advantage would not establish 
durable learning. The contribution is a falsifiable research framework and a proposed protocol, rather 
than a new empirical finding or a registered study. 
Keywords: educational technology; deliberate practice; independent learning; retention; near transfer; 
chess; experimental design. 
1 Introduction 
A study hour can contain very different activities. A learner may search for a suitable exercise, attempt 
a solution, locate feedback, interpret an error or try a related problem. The motivating intuition is that 
a well-designed tool can reorganize this hour so that more of it contributes to developing competence. 
The scientific task is to translate that intuition into claims that can be compared and potentially 
refuted. 
The expression effort plus technology does not identify a testable intervention. It leaves the tool, 
instructional design, comparator and outcome unspecified. It also fails to distinguish obtaining a

correct answer from learning how to produce one. A useful investigation must state which operations 
the intervention facilitates, which operations remain the learner's responsibility and what competence 
remains after assistance is removed. 
Chess offers a bounded setting in which problems have assessable answers, attempts can be recorded 
and assisted training can be separated from independent assessment. It also exposes a common 
inferential error. The existence of engines stronger than humans and highly prepared contemporary 
players does not show that every use of an engine improves learning, or that studying a book 
necessarily produces less effective practice. 
The principal question is whether constrained adaptive instructional design improves durable 
independent learning relative to active digital practice with a fixed sequence and the same protected 
core syllabus. A second question compares fixed digital practice with equally active print study. A third 
examines direct access to solutions. These comparisons separate components that are often bundled 
when an interactive tool is compared with an inadequately specified alternative. 
2 Method of the Narrative Synthesis 
Directed literature searches were conducted on 31 October 2026. An initial background text supplied 
by the study's initiator served as a starting point for locating publications; it was not treated as 
independent evidence. English search combinations included intelligent tutoring systems, learning 
outcomes, learning versus performance, cognitive offloading, productive failure, generative AI learning 
randomized trial, and chess practice learning efficiency. Searches were extended to conceptual 
references and relevant positive and negative findings available in 2026. A second supplied text 
prompted the El Salvador case discussion. 
Original studies, meta-analyses, publisher pages and institutional or author repositories were 
prioritized. Selection was purposive, based on relevance to unaided learning, explicit comparators and 
practice mechanisms. Thirteen research publications form the core synthesis. Two additional sources 
provide contextual evidence on El Salvador. Three of the core 2026 publications are discussion papers 
or preprints hosted by IZA or arXiv; peer review is not assumed for those documents. 
Relevant sections of accessible full texts were consulted for Ma, Sinha and Kapur, Kestin, Bastani, 
Strittmatter, Gallegos, Liu, and Contractor and Reyes. Publisher or institutional records and available 
texts were consulted for the conceptual foundations. The Southwick finding is taken from the 
publisher's abstract because the full article was not available for inspection. The El Salvador analysis 
was available as an accepted-article abstract, with the final formatted text pending at the consultation 
date. 
This was not an exhaustive database search, duplicate screening exercise or formal risk-of-bias 
assessment. No new meta-analysis was performed. Numerical effects belong to the original 
publications and should not be compared as if they measured the same outcome under the same 
conditions. The selection supports the construction and criticism of an argument; it cannot estimate 
the proportion of favorable findings across the entire literature. Source access and major verification 
decisions are documented in Appendix A.

3 What the Evidence Supports 
3.1 Learning and Performance During Acquisition 
Soderstrom and Bjork [1] distinguish changes observed during acquisition from learning inferred 
through retention or transfer. Momentary fluency is therefore an insufficient indicator of lasting 
competence. Assisted performance is a legitimate outcome, but it is not interchangeable with 
independent learning. 
Dunlosky and colleagues [2] identify practice testing and distributed practice as techniques with high 
utility across a range of conditions. These findings help identify operations a tool could support. They 
do not imply that those operations require a screen: a book containing questions and an appropriate 
review schedule can implement them. A meaningful comparator should therefore preserve a credible 
instructional design. 
Sweller [3] examines how some problem-solving demands can interfere with acquiring the underlying 
structure of a task. This perspective cautions against both unnecessary overload and the assumption 
that all difficulty is beneficial. Complementing this concern, Sinha and Kapur [4] synthesize 53 studies 
with 166 comparisons. Problem solving before instruction favored conceptual knowledge and transfer, 
with g = 0.36, while the effect on procedural knowledge was not significant. The value of an initial 
attempt depends on the learning objective and the instruction that follows it. 
3.2 Computer Tutoring and Interaction Design 
Ma and colleagues [5] analyzed 107 effect sizes involving 14,321 participants. Their abstract reports 
an advantage of intelligent tutoring systems over textbooks or workbooks of g = 0.35 and no 
statistically significant difference from individualized human tutoring. The effect is a standardized 
difference, not a percentage increase in learning. The synthesis supports comparative benefits under 
particular conditions; it does not isolate the effect of every system feature or demonstrate long-term 
retention for every included intervention. 
In university physics, Kestin and colleagues [6] studied 194 eligible students and reported better 
immediate outcomes with an AI tutor than with active-learning classes. The intervention covered two 
lessons, and the conditions also differed in pacing and setting. This is favorable evidence for a 
particular instructional package, not an automatic basis for extrapolation to entire courses or 
competence maintained over months. 
Bastani and colleagues [7] studied almost one thousand mathematics students. A general AI interface 
improved practice performance but produced a relative 17% reduction against the control in a 
subsequent unassisted examination. A tutor designed to provide instructional safeguards mitigated 
that harm without producing a significant positive examination effect. Preventing harm and 
demonstrating superiority are different findings. Pedagogical intentions cannot be treated as evidence 
that an intervention works. 
3.3 Recent Evidence and Competing Interpretations 
Gallegos [8], in an IZA discussion paper, distinguishes encouraging adoption of an econometrics 
assistant from guiding students toward learning-oriented use. The adoption intervention did not 
improve the midterm examination. The later guidance intervention improved final-examination 
performance, with the abstract reporting an effect of 0.21 standard deviations. The comparisons

concern different interventions and assessment occasions. Their value is in separating more use from a 
different form of use. 
Liu and colleagues [9] report a randomized trial of access to course-integrated tutoring involving 
2,379 students and 30 instructors. The reported grade reduction of 0.37 standard deviations concerns 
an exact-match subsample of sections within the same course, containing 1,353 students. It is not a 
significant academic-loss estimate for the full sample. The full text reports no significant academic 
harm in the overall analysis and reduced participation in existing learning activities. Reading only the 
abstract could encourage an overly broad interpretation. This source remains a discussion paper. 
Contractor and Reyes [10], in a preprint revised in October 2026, compare AI access during a study 
and writing assignment, followed by unaided assessments immediately and one week later. They 
report improved test performance and a smaller subsequent gain. Differences associated with using AI 
for explanation versus delegating writing are suggestive, but students' chosen use styles were not 
randomly assigned. Those subgroup patterns do not establish what would happen if the study imposed 
one style rather than another. 
These results are not pooled into a new average. They differ in subject matter, duration, allocation, 
actual uptake and assessment. Together they justify examining conditions and intervention 
components. They also identify displacement of existing activities, limited adoption and learner 
differences as alternatives to a simple account based on tool quality. 
3.4 Chess as Evidence and as a Boundary 
Southwick and colleagues [11] analyzed a longitudinal cohort of 44,213 Chess.com players. Their 
publisher abstract reports a 3.61-fold association in learning efficiency for activities aligned with 
deliberate practice relative to gameplay. The design is observational and is assessed here only through 
the abstract. It does not establish a causal comparison with books, a universal prescription or a specific 
conclusion about elite players. 
Strittmatter, Sunde and Zegners [12] examined historical professional games using engine-based move 
evaluation and found performance improvements across cohorts. The study supports discussion of 
historical performance changes. It does not experimentally isolate the effect of training with 
technology. Player selection, access to accumulated knowledge and other developments can 
accompany those changes. Attributing stronger contemporary play exclusively to technology exceeds 
what this design identifies. 
Chess is useful as a laboratory for specific practice. Better chess-problem performance would not 
demonstrate improved general intelligence, mathematics achievement or school performance. 
Transfer to another domain requires an appropriate study in that domain. 
3.5 El Salvador and the Limits of Attribution 
The El Salvador case illustrates the same problem at the system level. The World Bank [14] reported 
that 1,198 volunteers from 171 schools scored 417 in mathematics, 492 in reading and 462 in science 
in the July 2026 PISA for Schools assessment. The source explicitly states that the results do not 
represent the country or the entire program. A comparison with the national average does not 
measure the progress of these same students. The intervention also combined curriculum, materials, 
teacher support, management, assessment and technology.

An analysis by Restrepo-Morales and colleagues [15], accepted on 29 October 2026 and available as an 
abstract, examines how much selection could be compatible with the reported scores without 
assuming learning gains. It concludes that the available information cannot distinguish strong 
selection from exceptional improvement, or attribute any improvement specifically to the tutor within 
the broader reform. Its calculations are not reproduced without access to the final text. This case 
neither confirms nor refutes the effectiveness of a particular AI brand. It shows why baseline 
measurement and an appropriate comparator matter. Differences between groups are not converted 
here into school years recovered. 
4 Conceptual Framework and Contribution 
4.1 Redistributing Demands While Preserving Learning Operations 
Risko and Gilbert [13] describe cognitive offloading as altering processing requirements through 
actions and external supports. This raises the question of what is externalized. The present design 
proposal would automate aspects of selecting exercises and organizing reviews while retaining the 
learner's responsibility for deciding, explaining and retrieving. This is a testable instructional 
specification, not a natural boundary already demonstrated by research. 
An operation is not peripheral to every learning objective. If the objective is planning one's own study, 
selecting exercises is part of the intended competence. If the objective is tactical calculation, selection 
can be an additional organizational demand. The classification must be fixed relative to the objective 
before the experiment; otherwise favorable results could be explained retrospectively as removal of 
supposedly unnecessary demands. 
Mental energy is used only as a motivating metaphor. No single energetic reserve is proposed, and 
metabolic consumption is not inferred from clicks, fatigue or time. Duration, perceived effort, reported 
fatigue, persistence and response quality are separate variables. An intervention could reduce fatigue 
while impairing learning, or increase perceived difficulty while improving retention. 
4.2 Observable Practice Cycles 
A complete cycle is defined as a recorded sequence containing an independent attempt before solution 
disclosure, consultation of relevant feedback, and a subsequent unassisted application on a related 
problem. The cycle is a process unit. An incorrect response still counts as an attempt, and the 
subsequent application may also fail. This avoids defining productive practice as whatever happens to 
lead to the desired outcome. 
Cycle density is completed cycles per 450 assigned minutes. It is a descriptive interface-behavior 
measure, not an estimate of learning efficiency. Attempts, initial accuracy, hint use, duration and 
difficulty are also reported. Error-driven scheduling and difficulty progression make cycle counts 
endogenous to treatment and performance: more errors can generate more opportunities without 
more learning. Appendix B specifies initiated episodes, delivered opportunities, completion fractions, 
one-to-one linkage and end-of-study censoring. Feedback-open duration is an observable exposure 
proxy, not proof of cognitive consultation. 
For measurement, a feedback episode will be linked to the first prespecified related reapplication 
opportunity. A reapplication will not close multiple earlier cycles. Cycles may span sessions, and their 
completion date will be recorded. Linking rules and the mapping of related problems will be frozen

after the pilot, before the confirmatory trial. Failure to inspect feedback and absence of a later 
application will be recorded separately rather than treated as completed cycles. 
The proposed mechanism is that instructional design changes time allocation, task selection and the 
probability of applying a correction again. These changes could support retention, but novelty, 
dependence, frustration or withdrawal could also occur. Motivation and prior competence may 
influence both process measures and outcomes. An association between completed cycles and 
improvement would not establish causal mediation. 
The contribution is operational: combining an active comparator, observable process measures and 
delayed independent assessment in a falsifiable protocol. This article does not claim to have 
discovered deliberate practice, adaptive tutoring or the distinction between learning and performance. 
Establishing the empirical originality of the combination would require a more exhaustive literature 
review and original data. 
5 Hypotheses and Falsification Criteria 
H1, primary. Following training, adaptive digital practice will outperform fixed digital practice on 
novel, unaided problems four weeks later, adjusting for baseline performance and allocation strata. 
This comparison evaluates the adaptation package against an active digital comparator. 
H2, secondary. Fixed digital practice will outperform active print study on the same delayed outcome. 
This comparison estimates a difference between specified practice environments, not the universal 
effect of technology versus its absence. 
H3, secondary. Fixed practice requiring a prior attempt will outperform direct solution access on the 
delayed assessment, even if direct access improves within-session correctness. Effects could differ by 
experience; observing a solution is not assumed to be inherently harmful. 
H4, exploratory and descriptive. Adaptation may change cycle density and opportunity delivery, and 
these measures may be associated with delayed performance. Because the scheduling policy and 
learner errors mechanically affect those measures, neither arm differences nor outcome associations 
identify efficiency or causal mediation. Fatigue is another measured correlate, not an established 
mechanism. 
An advantage confined to practice would not confirm H1. An imprecise null estimate would leave the 
question open. A narrow interval incompatible with an educationally meaningful advantage would 
weaken the practical hypothesis. A delayed disadvantage would require revising the design. 
Satisfaction, exercise counts or a favorable subgroup will not replace the primary outcome after 
results are known. 
6 Proposed Experimental Protocol 
6.1 Participants and Preparation 
A multicenter trial is proposed for adults who know the rules of chess and perform sufficiently well on 
a common baseline test to attempt the problem bank. Complete beginners and experts with near-
ceiling performance would be excluded. Eligibility thresholds will be calibrated in a separate pilot of 
40 participants, ten per condition. The pilot will assess comprehension, duration, accessibility and 
measurement quality, not confirmatory efficacy.

The feasibility pilot is distinct from an independent assessment-calibration stage targeting 180 
complete three-visit records, with up to 225 recruits. Neither sample enters the main trial. Appendix C 
supplies the rationale, form blueprint, example positions and scoring decisions, reliability and 
comparability gates, and mandatory pause/recalibrate rules. Before main-trial recruitment, eligibility 
rules, materials, the algorithm, rubrics and analysis plan will be frozen in a dated public registration. 
This manuscript is not that registration. No recruitment, ethical approval, committed funding, 
implemented intervention or validated bank is claimed to exist. 
6.2 Conditions and Assigned Study Time 
Training will last six weeks, with three supervised 25-minute sessions per week: 450 assigned minutes 
per participant. Conditions will share learning objectives, a common source bank and expert-reviewed 
explanations. Training material will remain separate from assessment items. No condition will receive 
additional individualized tutoring during the sessions. 
Condition A, active print study. A workbook and physical board will present a fixed sequence, decision 
and explanation prompts, solutions in a separate section, and subsequent application problems. 
Learners will be instructed to record an attempt before consulting the solution. This is an active 
instructional comparator that recognizes the possibilities of a well-designed book. 
Condition B, fixed digital practice. The protected core and fixed supplementary sequence and schedule 
used in A will be delivered through a digital board. A recorded attempt will be required before 
feedback opens. Hints and explanations will be equivalent to the workbook. The system will log events 
and facilitate navigation and representation of variations. The comparison with A includes differences 
in medium, manipulation and compliance; it does not isolate a single property of screens. 
Condition C, constrained adaptive digital practice. B's interface, explanations and attempt requirement 
are retained. Every session has common core blocks in minutes 0-10 and 18-25 and a supplementary 
block in minutes 10-18; all arms share the core. C adapts only the supplementary block, using errors to 
queue next-session and one-week reviews and correct streaks to adjust difficulty. The queue is capped 
at 24 active entries, expires reviews after two scheduled sessions beyond their due date and cannot 
displace core blocks. Appendix B defines eight motifs, item records, seeded selection, priorities, 
eviction, exhaustion and boundary rules. All values are prospective choices requiring implementation 
and feasibility testing. This deliberate pre-data revision preserves scheduled syllabus exposure while 
estimating a bundled adaptation package; it does not isolate spacing from difficulty progression. 
Condition D, direct solution access. The interface and fixed sequence from B will allow the solution and 
explanation to be revealed before an attempt. A generative model is not required: reviewed answers 
will prevent answer accuracy from becoming a second experimental difference. Voluntary attempts 
will be logged. D estimates the effect of permitting early solution access; it does not guarantee that 
every participant delegates reasoning. 
The exact exposure in C will differ from the fixed sequence because selection is part of its treatment. 
Exposure to problems, motifs and difficulty will be reported by group. Assigned time is equal; actual 
mental effort cannot be equated with a timer. Outside practice will be recorded weekly and will not 
justify exclusion from the primary analysis.

6.3 Allocation and Assessment 
Participants will be individually randomized in a 1:1:1:1 ratio using variable block sizes, stratified by 
center and baseline ability. Someone uninvolved in assessment will prepare the allocation sequence 
and conceal it until enrollment is complete. Participants will know their environment; outcome raters 
and the initial analysis will use masked group labels. 
Assessments will occur before training, within 72 hours after the final scheduled training session and 
at a target of 28 days after that scheduled session, irrespective of attendance. The delayed window is 
days 21-35 inclusive; Appendix C specifies repeat-selection and out-of-window rules. The primary test 
will use the same paper and board format for every group, without engines, internet, hints or study 
material. A brief common familiarization with that format will precede baseline assessment. 
The primary outcome will be the delayed score on 30 novel positions: 20 involving trained motifs in 
unseen configurations, and ten involving near application through different combinations of those 
motifs. Each position will yield one point for the correct decision and one for a separately scored 
correct tactical explanation, using the closed rubric in Appendix C. An incorrect selected move does not 
automatically eliminate explanation credit, but credit requires a correct explanation of an accepted 
move and its critical continuation, without contradictory claims. The two components remain 
separately reportable. The total will range from zero to 60; item categories will be reported as 
secondary outcomes. This measures near transfer within chess, not general cognitive ability. 
Three disjoint forms will be prepared, with eight motifs represented by two or three single-motif 
positions each and ten prespecified combined-motif positions per form. Six form orders will be 
balanced within treatment and center. The separate calibration stage in Appendix C will evaluate 
difficulty, form/visit effects, reliability, interrater agreement and floor/ceiling behavior under explicit 
gates; failing a gate pauses the main trial and requires revision and independent recalibration. 
Equivalent alternative solutions will be validated by an expert and an engine, documenting engine 
version, configuration and search depth. Engine checks will support legality and evaluation, while the 
human rubric will assess explanations. Rater agreement will be measured in a random 20% of 
responses, with discrepancies resolved through masked review. 
6.4 Process Measures and Fatigue 
Secondary measures will include immediate performance, within-session performance, solution 
consultation before an attempt, cycle density, persistence and attendance. Fatigue will be reported 
from zero to ten before and after each session; perceived effort will be reported from one to nine 
afterward. These are separate self-report indicators, not metabolic measurements or clinical 
instruments. 
Condition A will use timed attempt sheets and sampled observation; B, C and D will use event logs. 
Lower-resolution print records could bias process comparisons. Accordingly, the principal cycle-
density comparison will be C versus B, which shares instrumentation. A sample of sessions in every 
group will be audited using an equivalent observation protocol and appropriate consent. Inactivity will 
not automatically be labeled inattention, since it may contain mental calculation. 
6.5 Sample Size and Analysis 
For initial planning, detecting a standardized difference of 0.30 with 80% power and a two-sided 0.05 
test requires approximately 175 complete outcomes per group in a simple independent comparison.

Allowing 20% attrition, 220 allocations per condition are proposed, or 880 participants overall. This 
normal approximation makes no allowance for a potential gain in precision from baseline adjustment. 
It is not a protocol simulation or a guarantee of power for interactions, mediation or equivalence. 
Cluster randomization, if adopted, would require recalculation before recruitment. 
The primary analysis will follow intention to treat, comparing C with B in a regression of delayed score 
on treatment, baseline score, allocation strata and assessment form. Stable supervision cohorts will be 
established before allocation; cohort-clustered CR2 standard errors with Satterthwaite degrees of 
freedom will address shared session conditions. Main-trial power must be revised for the planned 
cohort structure. Appendix C gives the treatment-aware imputation and sensitivity specifications. 
Results will include the difference in score points, a 95% confidence interval and a standardized 
difference with an explicitly identified denominator. The primary test will be two-sided. Secondary 
contrasts B versus A and B versus D will be adjusted together using Holm's procedure. Remaining 
analyses will be labeled exploratory. 
A difference of 0.20 outcome standard deviations is provisionally proposed as the smallest effect of 
interest, not a validated threshold, and will be translated into points using calibration data. Power is 
planned for 0.30, not equivalence at 0.20. At 175 complete cases per arm, an unadjusted 95% interval 
near a zero effect has approximate half-width 1.96 times sqrt(2/175) = 0.210 SD. Thus even an exact 
zero estimate cannot exclude a 0.20-SD benefit under that approximation. No baseline precision gain is 
assumed. Cohort dependence requires recalculating power before recruitment. Only an actual 
sufficiently narrow interval could support an exclusion statement; nonsignificance alone cannot falsify 
a 0.20-SD benefit. 
Attrition and reasons will be reported by group. Under a missing-at-random assumption, 100 
treatment-aware chained-equation imputations will include assigned arm, baseline score, arm-by-
baseline terms, center, ability stratum, cohort, form, attendance and available assessments, using 
predictive mean matching for bounded scores. Complete-case, out-of-window and arm-specific delta 
sensitivity analyses will be reported; Appendix C fixes a 25-cell delta grid for C versus B and 
corresponding secondary contrasts. Missing participants will not be scored zero, and last observation 
carried forward will not be the primary method. Specific imputation and sensitivity procedures will be 
frozen before unmasking outcomes by condition. 
Process measures occur after allocation and will not be added to the primary model to remove their 
effects. Such adjustment could block part of the treatment effect or introduce bias. Relationships 
among cycles, fatigue and retention will be exploratory and will state their assumptions. Identifying a 
mechanism would require further randomized components or additional defensible assumptions. 
6.6 Ethics and Reproducibility 
Implementation requires appropriate ethical review, informed consent, separation from course 
grading and voluntary withdrawal without penalty. Only relevant data will be collected, using 
pseudonymous identifiers. Any recordings used for auditing require separate consent and a deletion 
schedule. The initial proposal concerns adults; extension to minors requires a dedicated protocol. 
Registration will include prior exclusion rules, material licenses, software versions, the adaptive 
algorithm, rubrics, assessment windows and analyses. Authorized materials, a data dictionary and 
analysis code will be made available. Individual data will be shared only with adequate consent and 
protection. No commitment is made to disclose information that could identify participants.

7 Discussion 
The hypothesis does not require learning to feel easier. A tool could reduce time spent locating 
feedback while requiring more demanding attempts. Alternatively, it could feel comfortable because it 
removes the need to reason. Both may appear efficient during practice. Delayed unaided assessment 
separates their consequences. 
An active workbook comparator prevents instructional structure from being attributed automatically 
to technology. Similar outcomes in A and B, with improvement in C, would favor the adaptation 
package. An advantage of B over A without an additional advantage of C could point toward 
representation, navigation or compliance. If D matched or exceeded B, the presumption that 
mandatory attempts add value in these conditions would require revision. None of these patterns 
should be relabeled after the fact as automatic confirmation of the framework. 
Practical usefulness also depends on producing accurate explanations, reviewing errors, maintaining 
software and sustaining participation. A small benefit may matter if it expands access at low cost; a 
laboratory benefit may lose value if it requires intensive supervision. The study will record 
implementation resources without inferring economic cost-effectiveness from a score difference alone. 
The broader claim concerning effort requires another study. A subsequent factorial design could 
allocate fixed versus adaptive practice and two session durations, such as 15 versus 30 minutes. The 
interaction would ask whether the effect of greater assigned study time depends on design. Assigned 
duration would still not equal mental effort. That experiment needs its own power calculation; the 
proposed sample of 880 does not cover it. 
Substantial limitations remain. The synthesis is selective, several recent findings are preprints, and the 
chess efficiency finding is assessed here through an abstract. Cycle measurement needs validation and 
could overlook learning through observation. Appendices B-C now provide provisional algorithm and 
assessment specifications, but the actual item bank and implementation remain to be built and 
independently checked. The proposed assessment is not calibrated and effectiveness remains 
unknown. The search does not establish priority for a new general principle. 
The defensible contribution is an explicit set of research conditions: compare credible active 
alternatives, preserve the reasoning operation being learned, observe process without equating it with 
learning, and allow findings that contradict the initial expectation. Extending any result to languages, 
mathematics or professional training requires domain-specific replications and relevant outcome 
measures. 
8 Conclusion 
The literature examined supports investigating how technological design changes practice. It does not 
establish that adding technology to effort always improves outcomes. The proposed hypothesis 
predicts a delayed advantage for an adaptive intervention that preserves independent attempts over 
active fixed digital practice. The trial specifies how that prediction could be examined and how its 
failure would be recognized. Until data are collected, this work provides an operational framework and 
proposed protocol rather than empirical proof of superiority.

Appendix B Provisional Intervention And Recorded-Cycle Specification 
Status and Scope 
This deliberate pre-data protocol revision protects syllabus coverage against review crowd-out. Every 
parameter below is proposed, not validated. This specification describes a candidate implementation 
for the adult chess trial; no tagged problem bank, software implementation or validated difficulty scale 
currently exists. Expert review, an independent feasibility pilot and a versioned final specification are 
prerequisites to recruitment. The assessment bank remains separate and inaccessible to training 
software. 
B1 Finite taxonomy and source-bank records 
The provisional syllabus contains eight tactical motifs: (1) fork or double attack; (2) pin; (3) skewer; 
(4) discovered attack or discovered check; (5) removal or deflection of a defender; (6) attraction or 
decoy; (7) interference or clearance; and (8) mating net or back-rank weakness. Each training position 
has one primary motif and optionally one secondary motif from this list. Two qualified chess reviewers 
independently assign tags and reconcile disagreement before the bank is frozen. Untagged positions 
are excluded. 
Each record contains immutable item_id, family_id, position in FEN, side to move, primary and 
secondary motifs, difficulty level 1-3, source and license, accepted first moves and continuations, 
decision and explanation rubrics, reviewed explanation, permitted hints, and expert-review status. 
Records also contain engine name/version/configuration/search limit and analysis output, reviewer 
decisions, estimated completion time, and a content checksum. Document alternative solutions.

Family_id groups closely related configurations; exact item reuse and related-family reuse are 
distinguished in reporting. 
A separate frozen mapping assigns each item eligible reapplication items. A related item must share 
the primary motif and require a fresh decision in a different position; mere replay of the disclosed 
solution is ineligible. Mapping edges receive expert approval and identify whether the target is same-
level or harder. The bank manifest states available counts by motif, level and family. The feasibility

reuse; failing this gate requires expanding the bank and repeating affected checks before registration. 
B2 Common delivery and fixed sequence 
There are 18 supervised 25-minute sessions; training ends at session 18. Each session reserves 
minutes 0-10 for a common core, minutes 10-18 for supplementary practice, and minutes 18-25 for a 
second common core. All groups receive the same session-specific core items, prompts and reviewed 
content. The first core targets motif 1+((session-1) modulo 8); the second targets motif 1+(session 
modulo 8). Each core contains an index item and a mapped application. Index difficulty is level 1 in 
sessions 1-6, level 2 in 7-12 and level 3 in 13-18. Every motif therefore has scheduled exposure 
independent of errors. Report actual completion. 
Generate the core manifest before recruitment using a published seeded permutation within each 
motif-level cell and approved application mapping. Core and supplementary inventories are disjoint. 
Reserve every future core item against adaptive selection. At a block boundary save the response, close 
the item and open the next block; no response grace extends a block. Record unfinished items and 
feedback not reached. Never delay a core block to finish reviews or add training after session 18. 
Missed core blocks are not reassigned. 
A and B require a decision and explanation before disclosure; A records attempts on timed sheets and 
B enforces field completion. An incorrect response counts; blank or explicitly declined responses do 
not. D permits immediate solution disclosure and records voluntary attempts. In A, B and D, the 
supplementary block follows a common frozen index/application sequence, cycling motifs 1-8 and 
levels 1-3. Participants resume their supplementary cursor across sessions. C changes supplementary 
selection only. C versus B therefore estimates this constrained adaptation package, not unrestricted 
personalization or a single mechanism. 
B3 Adaptive state and deterministic selection 
C shares B's interface and attempt requirement. For each motif maintain level L=1, correct streak S=0, 
unused inventory and review entries. A first move is correct if it matches the reviewed accepted-move 
set; explanation scoring does not drive adaptation. Every C response, including core responses, 
updates state: a correct move increments S; two consecutive correct moves raise L by one, capped at 3, 
and reset S. An error resets S without lowering L and queues reviews for the next scheduled session 
and the first scheduled session at least seven calendar days later. Both use source difficulty; coincident 
due dates merge. Errors on reviews may generate new entries. 
Only the eight-minute supplementary block can serve the queue. Missing sessions create overdue 
entries, not replacement minutes. A queued review expires after two scheduled sessions beyond its 
due session, whether attended or not. Retain expired entries in the audit log. Cap the active queue at 
24 entries. On overflow evict the entry with the latest due session, then latest source-feedback

B4 Recorded cycles, opportunities and censoring 
A source episode requires an independently recorded attempt before disclosure and a feedback-open 
event. In digital groups, feedback-open means the reviewed explanation panel was visibly displayed 
for at least five cumulative seconds while the session was active. This is an interface exposure proxy, 
not proof of attention, comprehension or cognitive consultation. Panel timestamps, dwell time and 
premature closure are retained. Print records use a corresponding declared consultation event with 
sampled observer checks; their lower resolution prevents treating print and digital cycle counts as 
interchangeable. 
Each eligible source episode receives at most one designated first reapplication opportunity. Its 
mapping and intended session are recorded when feedback qualifies. In C this is the first scheduled 
review following an error; following a correct source response it is the first later approved related 
item actually selected. B, A and D use the next mapped application in the fixed sequence. The 
additional one-week review is reported as maintenance practice rather than a second closure of the 
original cycle. A response may close one earlier source cycle and start a new source episode of its own; 
it cannot close multiple earlier cycles. If multiple sources qualify, link the oldest source-feedback 
timestamp, breaking ties by item_id; record both identifiers. 
A cycle closes when a valid unassisted attempt is submitted at the designated opportunity before its 
feedback opens, regardless of correctness. Disclosure before the application attempt prevents closure. 
No qualifying later application means an incomplete cycle. Report incomplete episodes separately as 
feedback not opened, no mapped item available, opportunity offered but not attempted, session 
missed, queue expiration or eviction, or follow-up outside the assigned training window. Session-18 
errors and reviews due after session 18 receive no extra training. They remain administratively right-

B5 Pilot whitelist, freeze and audit 
The independent 40-person pilot may change only: wording and accessibility; five-second feedback-
proxy threshold within 3-15 seconds; bank additions/removals and corrected expert tags; difficulty 
assignments; motif ordering; consecutive-correct threshold from two to three; initial level from 1 to 2; 
and deterministic fallback ordering. Block boundaries, core reservations, queue cap/expiry/eviction 
and no-grace rule are fixed outside this whitelist. Changes require written feasibility or measurement 
reasons and a dated log. Review due dates, eight-motif syllabus, primary contrast, session

change to those elements requires an explicitly revised protocol and fresh review before registration, 
not an undisclosed pilot adjustment.

Appendix C Proposed Assessment Blueprint And Pretrial Validation Rules 
Status and Scope 
Every threshold below is a proposed design decision, not a validated educational standard or a 
calibration result. No participants have been recruited and no assessment has passed these criteria. 
Materials, scoring rules, the adaptive algorithm and the statistical plan must be frozen and publicly 
dated before main-trial randomization. The primary construct is unaided tactical decision-making and 
explanation on unfamiliar chess positions, including near transfer within chess. It does not encompass 
general intelligence, tournament strength or all forms of independent learning. 
C1 Separate feasibility and calibration stages 
The feasibility pilot comprises 40 participants, ten per condition. It examines instructions, 
accessibility, session logistics, software operation and process recording. Its purpose is neither 
estimating treatment efficacy nor selecting the arm that appears most successful. Its participants will 
not enter the calibration study or confirmatory trial. 
A separate assessment-calibration sample will provide 180 complete three-visit records from adults 
spanning the intended eligibility range. Recruit up to 225 to allow approximately 20% incomplete

records; report all recruited participants and missing assessments. No participant receives the 
experimental training. Each completes all three assessment forms, seven days apart, without item 
feedback, assigned equally to the six possible form orders: 30 complete records per order. This yields 
180 observations per item and 540 form administrations. At an item success probability of 0.50, the 
elementary independent-person standard error is approximately 0.037. That calculation motivates

Incomplete records will inform feasibility reporting and sensitivity checks; they will not be silently 
replaced in the analysis. Failure to obtain the planned complete records triggers a documented 
feasibility review before proceeding. 
C2 Item allocation and administration 
Prepare three disjoint forms, each containing 30 positions, for baseline, immediate and delayed 
assessment. Each form contains 20 single-motif items across eight prospectively named syllabus 
motifs: four motifs receive three items each and four receive two each. Freeze the same allocation 
across forms before calibration. The remaining ten combine motifs, using the same prespecified 
pairings and approximate difficulty bands across forms. A motif-by-form blueprint will identify the 
decision, explanation target, intended difficulty, accepted alternatives and training-bank separation of 
every item. No assessment position may occur in training, and superficial rotations or color reversals 
of training positions are excluded. 
All forms use paper diagrams and a physical board, the same instructions and a 60-minute limit, 
subject to accessibility arrangements fixed before randomization. The feasibility pilot must establish 
that this administration is practicable. Record completion time and unanswered items. An unanswered 
item on an otherwise administered test receives zero; a wholly absent visit is missing, not zero. No 
hints, engines, internet or item-level feedback are permitted. Balance the six form orders within 
treatment and center. The calibration data must support the proposed order counterbalancing rather 
than assuming that different forms are interchangeable. 
C3 Binary rubric and provisional examples 
Each item contributes two separately recorded binary scores. Decision = 1 for a legal move on the 
frozen accepted-move list; otherwise 0. Explanation = 1 only when the response correctly states the 
item-specific tactical reason and, where required, the critical continuation or excluded defense; 
otherwise 0. Scoring is independent, not conditional on decision credit: an incorrect selected move 
may coexist with explanation = 1 only if the explanation itself correctly identifies the required tactic 
and continuation for an accepted move. An explanation falsely claiming that the incorrect selected 
move executes that tactic receives 0. Contradictory claims that negate the required reason fail that 
component. Accept equivalent notation, verbal square descriptions and semantically equivalent 
explanations; spelling and prose quality are not targets. Total scores range from 0 to 60. Decision and 
explanation subtotals will also be reported.

C4 Proposed acceptance gates and failure decisions 
Require each form's mean to lie between 15 and 45 points, with no more than 10% of participants 
scoring 0-6 or 54-60. Review every component with success below 0.10 or above 0.95, and every item 
with a negative corrected item-total association. Such flags require documented content review rather 
than automatic deletion solely to increase reliability. 
For the 30 item totals, require coefficient alpha at least 0.75, with a person-bootstrap 95% lower 
confidence bound of at least 0.65, separately for each form. These are screening gates, not proof of 
unidimensionality or validity. Estimate form differences using a participant-intercept model with form 
and visit effects. Require the 90% confidence interval for each adjusted pairwise form difference to lie 
within plus or minus 0.20 pooled calibration standard deviations. Freeze that pooled standard 
deviation for subsequent planning and sensitivity analyses. 
Independently double-score a random 20% of complete forms, stratified by form, visit and ability 
band, before adjudication. Require at least 90% exact agreement on each component, explanation 
kappa at least 0.70 with lower 95% bound at least 0.60, and total-score absolute-agreement ICC at 
least 0.90 with lower bound at least 0.80. Use participant-level resampling for uncertainty. Preserve 
pre-adjudication ratings; adjudication cannot manufacture agreement. 
If any gate fails or its interval is too imprecise, pause. Diagnose the failure, revise the bank, rubric or 
calibration design, and publish a dated amendment. Changed forms require fresh independent 
calibration under the same acceptance rules. Do not repeatedly tune thresholds against the same 
participants. Main-trial randomization begins only after all gates pass; any changed outcome variance, 
cohort dependence or eligibility range also requires a revised power calculation.

times sqrt(2/175) = 0.210 SD. Thus even an estimate exactly at zero would not exclude a 0.20-SD 
benefit under that approximation. Baseline adjustment may narrow intervals and cohort dependence
