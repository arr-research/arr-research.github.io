## Mathematical examination

### Model and Theorem 1 (pages 1–3)

The uniform-probability convention is consistent. Direct multiplication verifies that `A_u sqrt(pi)=sqrt(pi)` and that multiplication by `sqrt(pi)` conjugates the actual kernel to `A_u`. The Boolean noise matrix is strictly positive and positive definite for fixed `0<rho<1`; this supplies a simple positive top eigenvector and nonnegative spectrum. Thus `lambda1(T_r)=1/N` has the stated normalization, rather than being assumed close to one.

For the rigidity proof, the high-degree projection is used on an L2 vector before applying hypercontractivity. There is no unjustified Lp contraction of a Walsh projection. If `p'>2`, the factorization gives `beta^2=rho/(p-1)<1`; if `p'<=2`, norm monotonicity supplies the other branch. Matching removes exactly the nonconstant terms of degree at most k. Combining `1<=a E_pi g` with the tail estimate gives `v<=a^2 epsilon^2`, hence the displayed lower bound on a and upper bound on v.

The sign of the normalization inequality is important and correct: `QM_uQ` is positive semidefinite with top eigenvalue one, so its Rayleigh quotient on the constant vector is `N<=1`. Together with `a^2<=N`, this proves both the Perron and Hellinger bounds. The L1 statements follow by Cauchy–Schwarz. The compression estimate needs `2m<=k`; its factor `D_(L,m)` is harmless only for fixed m at the stated square-root scale. The manuscript explicitly stops at a compression. The cases `k=0` and `k=L` do not cause a contradiction; in the latter the actual tail vanishes and h is one. At the critical exponent, `rho/(s-1)=(1-rho)/2`, as required by (7).

### Lemma 2 and Theorem 3 (pages 4–5)

Positive diagonal terms make the Hessian strictly positive even if the reversible matrix has other zero entries. The two lower bounds on J bound its largest and smallest coordinates on each sublevel. This pays existence as well as uniqueness; strict convexity alone would not have sufficed. The proof does not require an unavailable dimension-uniform conditioning estimate.

In the construction, each odd block majority is balanced. A Fourier monomial in their product must use at least one coordinate from every block, so the density has exact matching to degree k when there are k+1 blocks. The choices of t give a strictly positive floor and the critical cap, uniformly in dimension. With block size asymptotic to `sqrt(L)/C`, the Chernoff error decays faster than the number of blocks grows. The argument at the all-plus point and the `delta<1/2` split yield precisely the claimed positive lower bound. This is an admissible failure of supremum convergence, not an admissible family of collapsing gaps. The text respects that distinction. The statement about uniform `r/pi` convergence also follows from `N->1` and its exact identity with `1/(Ng^2)`.

Its cross-block bound is paid by first fixing the low-degree cutoff n, then increasing the amplitude cutoff M, then increasing dimension. The intermediate low block is controlled by exact matching and positivity. The fixed-degree bound is dimension-free at fixed n. Min-max applies to the full block operator after the off-diagonal norm tends to zero. In source324 the dual exponents and noise convention agree with the external input. The bounded part of the truncation gives `rho^(m/4)`, and the small-tail alternative gives `exp(-c m)`. ### Theorem 5 (pages 6–7)

The ground-state identity has the correct one-sided flux/half-directed-sum normalization. The stronger norm `||g-1||_(2s')<=epsilon` follows from `g-1=Q(Qh-1)` and endpoint hypercontractivity, not from incorrectly identifying p' and 2s'. The indicator-energy comparison is absolute, and its lack of relative control for very small q is acknowledged.

The auxiliary operator is used correctly. The proof does not identify its Perron vector w with `sqrt(pi)`. The Rayleigh lower bound, the top eigenvalue excess d1, and the rank-one projector estimate give the stated operator bound. With `q<=1/2`, the uniform eigenvalue estimates leave at least `G/4` in the auxiliary cut-energy comparison. The triangle inequality then produces the actual flux estimate.

For the explicit threshold, `q>=64 B epsilon^2/c^2` bounds the cross error by c/4 and the squared error by c^2/64. Since `c=G/4<1`, the lower bound `47c/64>=c/2` is valid. Substitution gives `1024B/G^2`, and inserting `epsilon^2=B^2 eta^(2k+2)` gives the stated B-cubed factor. The finite level-set proof gives `Phi<=sqrt(2 gamma)` with these exact Dirichlet conventions. Thus `gamma<G^2/128` indeed yields a cut below `G/8`, to which the contrapositive applies. No missing factor of two was found.

### Theorem 6 (pages 7–9)

The maximal noise entry equals its diagonal value d. Consequently `h(y)<=sqrt(pi(y)/d)`, and finite-sum Hölder gives the displayed inside contribution. The outside contribution uses the stationary floor and weighted Cauchy–Schwarz; omitting this denominator estimate would invalidate a transition bound, but it is present. The semigroup identity is `P^2 h=P_(rho^(3/2))Qh`, and the squared evaluation norm is `(1+rho^3)^L`. Combining these terms produces exactly the stated kappa and the exponent `theta=1-1/(2s)`.

The row-sum bound applies to every x and therefore to the killed spectral radius and the cut escape ratio. In the explicit noise range, the logarithmic estimate follows from `log(1+rho)<=rho`, `log(1+rho^3)<=rho^3`, and `2/(1+rho)>=8/5`. The constants check: `17/16-(8/5)(2/3)=-1/240`; half this decay minus the support growth `1/1920` gives `1/640`. Also `md<=(2/3)^L`, so the squared reciprocal denominator is at most nine. The singleton endpoint and the example `rho=1/2` are consistent. The bound is restricted to fixed rho and b and does not cover every set of small stationary mass.

## Literature position, significance and outstanding findings

The finite positive scaling background is genuinely classical; the manuscript's specialized convex proof is useful exposition. The relevant [Georgiou–Pavon theorem and Hilbert-metric argument](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.6650v2) support that context, without implying dimension-uniform conditioning here. [Duan–Lyu–Powell's stability statements](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23461v1) distinguish coupling control from discrete potential control; the latter uses relative entrywise margin closeness and a conditioning quantity. These hypotheses cannot be replaced by low-order matching alone. I inspected those relevant statements/passages, not all proofs of either paper.

The strongest novelty candidates in the checked scope are the rigidity estimate under exact stationary matching and the explicit transfer of an auxiliary weighting gap to actual cut flux above a rare-mass threshold. The block-majority construction usefully shows why a superficially stronger route fails. The support estimate is a concrete combination of standard tools. None is certified as historically new.
