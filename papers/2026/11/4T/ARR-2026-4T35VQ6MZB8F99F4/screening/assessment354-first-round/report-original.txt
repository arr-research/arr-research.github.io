MATHEMATICAL FINDINGS

1. Definitions and spectral setup, page 1.

The stationary law and symmetrization are correct. Reversibility follows from pi(x)K(x,y) proportional to h(x)P(x,y)h(y); conjugation gives M_sqrt(h/g) P M_sqrt(h/g). Product noise at fixed 0<rho<1 has strictly positive eigenvalues, so this symmetric matrix is positive definite. Strict positivity of the transition entries supplies the simple top eigenvalue. The use of the second eigenvalue, without an absolute-value gap convention, is consequently consistent. The potential is arbitrary positive, not assumed to be a P-eigenfunction; the explicit kernel definition removes any ambiguity about the term Doob kernel.

2. Exact critical-cap failure, Theorem 2.1, pages 2-3.

The example uses the actual stationary and weighting densities of one potential. I checked the Walsh expansion H=1+c(x-y)+dxy with c=1/32 and d=13/16; its four values, mean, v=1/512, and w=169/256 agree. Differentiating Z_theta and the normalization of H/G_theta gives opposite density derivatives H(q-v) and -H(q-v). The displayed Ct numerator follows from these four values. Its sign argument is sound: the first difference decreases for t>=1, the remaining positive power increases, and equality occurs at t=1. The exact second and third moments bracket two. Convexity makes M strictly increasing for t>1, so t_* is unique and fixes rho_* in (1/3,1/2), with no varying-exponent argument.

The disjoint parity embedding has exactly the effective noise theta=rho_*^m. Closure of the quotient algebra under products and positive inverses justifies transporting both actual densities, not merely similar functions. The support supplies exact stationary matching through m-1. Eventually m exceeds the specified square-root cutoff, the floor follows from convergence to minimum 1/8, and the finitely many remaining dimensions can indeed use h=1. Both moments converge to two; the strict violation is a local separation across the numerical cap. It does not prove unbounded critical moments, a fixed-size asymptotic violation, or a collapsing gap. The manuscript makes these limits explicit, correctly.

Proposition 2.2 is also valid. Under energy normalization, a<=1 and a^2>=b; Cauchy-Schwarz under pi yields N>=a^2. In the two-level proof, the implicit step E(g 1_A)<=Eg=a<=1 validates m>=bq. The two pointwise estimates give g>=b/2. For product factors the displayed one-bit coefficients have |B|<=|A|, and tensorization proves the moment comparison. These are useful exclusions of simple routes, not classifications of all potentials.

3. Nonlinear floor and inverse-moment iteration, Theorem 3.1, pages 4-5.

Applying P to h>=b/g and Jensen to its positive transition rows gives g>=b P(1/g)>=b/(Pg). This is the essential nonlinear floor consequence. The ordinary hypercontractive uses have the correct parameters: Pg=P_(rho^(3/2)) Qh yields D0=1+rho^(-3), and g=P_sqrt(rho) Qh yields R=1+rho^(-1), since ||Qh||_2=1. The floor then gives ||h||_(-R)>=b. With negative exponents, the reverse-hypercontractive orientation and condition are correct: beta=-D_*<alpha=-R and rho^2=(1+R)/(1+D_*). Thus ||g^(-1)||_(D_*)<=b^(-1).

The Holder step 1/p=1/s+1/q and Pg=P_(rho^2)h produces exactly F(q)=1+(p-1)/rho^4. I checked the rational expression for F(q)-q, the fixed-point formula, and the two inequalities locating D_* strictly between q_- and q_+. Since D_*>R=2s/(s-1), the relevant Holder exponents remain above one. Monotonicity supplies q_n increasing to q_+, and the constants A_n and K_n follow by induction and r=pi/(Ng^2). No dimension-dependent constant is hidden in these steps.

The proof does not reach q_+, t_+, or s. In particular A_n grows with n, and a pointwise-in-n family of bounds cannot be passed to the limiting exponent. The manuscript expressly acknowledges this. I found no endpoint claim accidentally used in a later proof. The inequality gPg>=b is called an 'identity' in Section 7; this is a minor terminology error, not a change to the actual valid argument.

4. Exact matching and strong subcritical convergence, Lemma 3.2 and completion of Theorem 3.1, pages 5-6.

The high-degree bound is correctly obtained by factoring Q and applying its multiplier to Qh-a; there is no unjustified Lp bound for a sharp Fourier projection. Matching annihilates the nonconstant low-degree pairing, so E_pi Pi_(<=k)g=a. Weighted Cauchy-Schwarz gives 1<=a E_pi g, hence v<=a e sqrt(v) and a^2>=1/(1+e^2). The asserted N<=1 is justified by the positive eigenvector of A=M_sqrt(u) P M_sqrt(u), its spectral radius one, and the common spectrum of XX* and X*X, followed by testing Q M_u Q at the constant unit vector. This step is not a generic inequality for arbitrary unrelated densities.

The Hellinger affinity equals a/sqrt(N), giving the L1 estimate with the stated factor two. Total variation is correctly identified as half the L1 norm. The interpolation exponent (t_n-t)/(t(t_n-1)) is correct; choosing one finite n before the dimension limit proves convergence for each fixed 1<t<t_+. The case t=1 follows from the lemma. This does not itself imply spectral convergence for unrestricted potentials, and no such implication is asserted.

5. Uniform full-chain code estimate, Theorem 4.1, pages 6-7.

The quotient kernel b0 is the correct Fourier expansion, is positive, and lies in [1-delta,1+delta]. Its Doob reweighting minorizes alpha H/a; the quotient non-Perron eigenvalues are bounded by oscillation contraction. Crucially, the proof then treats all affine Fourier cosets, whose spaces are invariant and orthogonal under pi because pi belongs to the same code algebra. They span the full space, so no hidden sector is discarded.

For a minimum-weight nonzero coset representative of weight d, the minimum code distance gives weight at least D-d to every other coset element. Splitting d<=D/2 and d>D/2 proves the stated absolute Fourier sum bound rho+eta. Pointwise domination |b_t|<=B_C b0 survives multiplication by an arbitrary positive H and division by G. The resulting sup-norm bound on each sector is independent of the amplitude or minimum of H. This explains why the full-chain conclusion is stronger than a quotient-only mixing assertion.

For a singleton sector, precisely the codewords containing that coordinate contribute to b_ei-rho b0. The coefficient (1-rho^2)rho^(|c|-1) gives epsilon_gap. The original singleton character is pi-mean-zero and has norm one, and its Rayleigh quotient supplies the lower bound on lambda2. Positive definiteness permits clipping at zero. The pi/r ratio identity uses the pi-weighted mean of G^(-2); the bound alpha^2<=r/pi<=alpha^(-2) follows without a floor or moment cap. Algebra closure proves matching of both densities. The hypothesis delta<1 is essential for the finite bounds, and eta->0 guarantees it eventually. The limits and uniformity stated in the theorem follow.

6. Direct block regime and strict-class witness, Propositions 5.1-5.2, pages 7-9.

The block-sector factorization is correct, including residual coordinates. For beta_b<1 the product of one or more interior-factor bounds is at most beta_b; for beta_b>=1 the proof correctly uses the global Markov bound instead. The singleton comparison cancels the untouched block factors and has the stated amplitude-independent error. The asymptotic regime n rho^m->0 is genuinely sharper for these codes than the coarse code-cardinality condition 2^n rho^(m/2)->0. Structural inclusion alone would not imply this improvement, and the manuscript preserves the distinction.

The simplex parameters are classical and correctly computed. Full Fourier support, pairwise coordinate separation, and activity at every coordinate rule out inclusion in any algebra generated by disjoint blocks of size at least two, including an enlarged algebra and permutations. This is the correct stronger obstruction. The failed three-block construction described in Section 7 would not establish it; that claim is explicitly rejected and is not used. Constants and partial-support potentials remain allowed in both classes, so the strict-class statement is appropriately existential. General invertible changes of Boolean coordinates are properly excluded because they need not preserve noise/Hamming weight.

7. Positive rate at all sufficiently large lengths, Theorem 6.1, pages 9-11.

For each nonzero row vector a, aG is uniform, so the distance union bound does not require independence between different aG. The entropy estimate handles the strict integer event |aG|<dL and gives distance at least ceil(dL). Avoiding zero words also enforces full row rank. The separate column union bound tends to zero exponentially despite the quadratic number of pairs. The intersection has positive probability at every sufficiently large integer L. This proves the asserted existence, without an efficient construction or nested sequence.

The rate restriction gives eta_L<=exp(-a_* L) and delta_L<=eta_L. The finite upper and lower eigenvalue bounds then give equation (8) with the displayed C_rho; the auxiliary requirement E_L<=min(1/2,rho/2) removes the quotient sector from being the limiting upper obstruction. All constants are independent of H. For the witnesses, choosing fixed small tau controls h0 and Ph0 in [1-tau,1+tau], and normalization gives the stated floor and moment cap. Full nonzero Fourier coefficients survive normalization. The same nonzero/distinct column property supplies coordinate activity/separation and the strict-class conclusion. These witnesses are deliberately small perturbations of a constant; they demonstrate simultaneous feasibility, while the stronger spectral statement applies to arbitrary positive amplitudes. Neither assertion extends beyond the code hypothesis.
