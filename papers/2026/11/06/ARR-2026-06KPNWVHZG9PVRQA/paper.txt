Abstract
We present INT-ROOTS-NEAROPT-CERT-1.0, an indepen-
dently reproducible, zero-floating-point verification en-
gine and constructive Lean 4.35.0 kernel auditing the
finite-dimensional algebraic, leading-truncation, Landau–
Mahler, Gaussian-shift, Pellet–Graeffe bisection, and

1 Introduction and Scope
Given f(x) = Pn
i=0 aixi ∈Z [x] of degree n with |ai|< 2b,
computing all integer roots deterministically in time nearly
linear in the input bit size nb has been a long-standing
problem since Cucker–Koiran–Smale [2]. In their Novem-
ber 3, 2026 unrefereed preprint [ 4], Feng and Nir achieve
eO(nb + d3b2) time via an elementary Pellet–Graeffe bi-
section search (Theorem 1.1(a), Appendix A, [ 1]) and
eO(nb) time via Pan’s approximate factorization [ 7] (Theo-
rem 1.1(b)), whered= min(n, b).
The algorithm rests on four exact algebraic pillars:
(1) Lemma 2.2’s leading truncationg(x) = anxd+··· +an−d
forces every integer root r with |r| ≥2 within distance
< 2 of a complex root of g (while failing sharply at
r = ±1); (2) Landau’s inequality M(g) ≤ ∥g∥2 [6]
(Lemma 2.1) and Ostrowski-style perturbation bounds
(Lemma 2.3, Prop. 3.4) bound the total candidate bit
length S = P
c∈C⌈log2(2 + |c|)⌉ = O(b); (3) Lemma 3.3’s
Gaussian-integer shift q (R = 10d2) ensures |g(q)| ≥2

and zero roots in |z−q| ≤1, while Appendix A’s Pellet–
Graeffe test bisects [−2b, 2b] keeping ≤ 7d centers per scale;
and (4) Lemma 3.5’s power-of-two bit-length bucketing
and block split f(x) = P

eO(nb+nS) = eO(nb) time [5].
Honest Scope Boundary.This companion artifact

tion
