1 Introduction
A positive integern isabundantif its divisor-sum function σ(n) = ∑
d|nd satisfies σ(n) > 2n,
equivalently if its abundancy indexI(n) = σ(n)/n exceeds2. Let A= {n≥ 1 : σ(n) >2n}. In a
recent breakthrough [3], Shunia proved that membership inAand complete integer factorization
are equivalent under deterministic polynomial-time Turing reductions ([3, Theorem 1.1]), combining
two deterministic ingredients:
1. Abundance-to-comparison reduction ([3, Theorem 1.2, Algorithm 1]):Sieving primes up
to U = 2H2 with H≥ (⌈log2 x⌉+ 2)8 (hence U = O((logx)16)) and reserving primes via
Baker–Harman–Pintz [1] constructs a coprime multiplierm deciding σ(x) ≥h via a single
abundance queryz= mx0 with logz = O((logx)2)and least prime factorP−(z) ≥c√logz
for any fixedc∈(0,1)([3, Theorem 5.2]).
2. Nonadaptive power-sum reconstruction ([3, Theorem 3.1, Algorithm 2]):Setting M =
⌈log2 n⌉and J = 48(1 + M)2, the2 M divisor sumsσ(nJ+s)(0 ≤s< 2M) recoverφ(w) =
⌊wJ+1 /σ(wJ)⌋([3, Lemma 3.2], proved forJ≥ 16M) and isolate a cluster of full prime
powers via a gap in[q1,2q1]([3, Lemma 3.3]) and anℓ×ℓHankel system ([3, Lemma 3.4]).
For c >1, Shunia showed ([3, Theorem 5.2]) that only finitely many abundant integers satisfy
P−(z) ≥c√logz, and explicitly left the critical endpointc= 1open:“The endpoint c= 1is not
settled by the preceding arguments”[3, Section 5.2].
