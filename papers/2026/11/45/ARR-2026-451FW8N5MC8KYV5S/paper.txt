    Coverage and coexactness in checkerboard slice observations
                                           Lluis Eriksson
                                  THE ERIKSSON PROGRAMME

                                         12 November 2026


                                               Abstract
          We study original-channel rectangular means for a finite three-dimensional checkerboard
      gradient energy with weak coefficient c/48 and strong coefficient cn2 . Each partition has
      at most 36n rows and is specified by one cut per slice. If the cut diagonal is a permutation,
      actual singleton observations give an explicit logarithmic coercivity bound and a finite
      quadratic penalty, valid on all fields and therefore on the actual curl-adjoint image. For
      arbitrary cuts, the full means exclude every nonzero field of exactly zero strong energy;
      this qualitative fact does not restore the target coefficient c. Conversely, at an explicit,
      extremely large dyadic threshold, every cut diagonal with image of size at most n/32 admits
      a real, nonzero, genuinely coexact invisible field of energy below c/2 times its squared
      norm. We also prove a contrast-uniform factor-four energy bound for the true coexact
      projection, give an explicit counterexample to its preservation of these mean kernels, and
      quantify a necessary correction cost. The last estimate concerns the correction alone, not
      the energy of the corrected field. The permutation case at the original target for the
      nonlinear example F (i) = i + 2i2 , historical priority, and a matching logarithmic rate
      remain open; some affine permutation subfamilies already have counterexamples at that
      target. These are finite analytical results, not formal verification or a physical Yang–Mills
      spectral-gap theorem.


1    Problem, scope and principal conclusions
Low-dimensional observations of a high-contrast energy depend both on the observed functionals
and on the domain on which a restoring inequality is required. Here the domain distinction
is concrete: a field in the actual curl-adjoint image need not remain there after rectangular
averaging, while projection into that image need not preserve rectangular mean zeros. We
keep these two operations separate throughout.
    The purpose is a consolidated account of the programme’s finite analytical sources 266–273.
Their complete proofs and source-specific reports accompany the reproducibility package.
The preceding checkerboard working paper [3] treats broader observation-count and tagged-
window questions. We do not claim a new general theory of capacity or Hodge decomposition.
Logarithmic point estimates in two dimensions are classical [1]; stability of projections in
differential complexes is also an established subject [2]. Every estimate needed for this particular
coefficient family is derived below. A bounded comparison with these primary sources does
not establish historical originality or wider practical relevance.
    The results describe three distinct situations. Permutation diagonals yield a paid lower
bound of order c/(1 + log n). Arbitrary diagonals still exclude exactly strong-null invisible
fields. Low-coverage diagonals nevertheless fail at target c through fields of positive, small
strong energy. Thus qualitative injection on the strong kernel is insufficient, and coverage
alone is not proved sufficient. We establish no condition-number or solver guarantee.

4    Full means exclude exactly strong-null invisible fields
Lemma 4.1. If every adjacent midpoint of a finite real cyclic sequence A0 , . . . , AM −1 belongs
to its range, the sequence is constant.

Proof. The case M = 1 is immediate. Otherwise choose σ(j) with 2Aσ(j) − Aj − Aj+1 = 0,
cyclically. The integer coefficient matrix has row sums zero. Modulo 2 it is I + T , where T is
the next-coordinate shift. Keep rows 0, . . . , M − 2 and columns 1, . . . , M − 1: the resulting
matrix modulo 2 is lower bidiagonal with diagonal entries 1. Its integer minor is odd and
nonzero, so real rank is at least M − 1. Constants already belong to its kernel, so that kernel
consists only of constants. This does not require rational values or an injective selection σ.

8    Remaining questions and review context
The original coefficient c for the nonlinear permutation F (i) = i + 2i2 remains undecided.
Theorem 3.1 is a logarithmic lower bound; Theorem 5.1 is a counterexample for a different
coverage regime. Neither establishes necessity of a logarithmic observation count or a matching
logarithmic kernel rate for permutation diagonals. The projection and repair results explain
why an ambient construction cannot simply be transferred into the actual domain, but they
do not close that gap.
    All results concern finite counting models. There is no continuum limit, physical spectral-
gap assertion, practical threshold, optimal coefficient, solver guarantee or claim that the finite
estimates solve Yang–Mills. The programme’s separate PR29 record remains 20/41 with
TermSource = 0.
