CLAIM-BY-CLAIM FINDINGS

1. Model and quantifiers (pp. 1-2). The counting-measure convention, once-counted two-form pairs, actual image im(d1*) and componentwise original rows fix the operator and observation normalizations. The two channels are a direct sum; exchanging parity changes a block coupling sign without changing its spectrum. The quadratic-cut permutation argument correctly uses the invertibility of an odd number modulo a power of two; it does not claim this for all even n. The all-even-n response statements assume a permutation cut explicitly.

2. Lemma 3.1 (p. 3). The adjoint symbol uses conjugated forward symbols. The displayed skew preimage gives the orthogonal projector onto g(k) perpendicular, and the zero-frequency image vanishes. Thus 2(n^3-1) is the real dimension, not twice that number. The conjugation symmetry also handles self-conjugate pairs. No additional regularity assumption exists in this finite model.

3. Theorem 3.2 (pp. 3-4). The source multiplier shifts by q; its off-diagonal scalar is Gamma=g*h. The two plane-overlap singular values are 1 and |Gamma|/sqrt(DD'), yielding precisely the two stated root pairs. The {0,q} pair has only the q plane and contributes two eigenvalues 12A0. Degenerate Gamma, coincident planes and repeated eigenvalues do not introduce omitted dimensions. The formula also has the expected uncoupled limit H=a. The multiplicity count exhausts the actual domain.

4. Proposition 3.3 (p. 4). At a=0, the attained first-polarization minimum H(3-|S|) vanishes exactly on the diagonal phase fibre. For a non-diagonal residue at least two phase differences are nonzero; the sine identity gives the claimed 16H/(3n^2) gap. The added weak form is positive. On the nonexceptional diagonal pairs the roots satisfy 0<mu_minus<=6a and mu_plus>=6H, including sin^2(theta)=1. Excluding {0,q} leaves n/2-1 pairs and n-2 low dimensions. The n=4 endpoint and the exceptional pair do not contradict the gap or dimension count.

5. Theorem 4.1 (pp. 4-5). Original row adjoints are constant along the long third-coordinate cell. Their curls are constant along its interior; this remains true after projection to U because the complement lies in ker(d1). If the off-diagonal coexact projection vanishes, diagonal dependence on x1+x2+x3 and equality at x3=1,2 force the curl to be constant and hence zero. This proves the range equality by annihilators, including dependent rows. The slab estimate remains nonempty at n=4. Its constants combine as [48n/(n-3)]/[768/n^4]=n^5/[16(n-3)], establishing the stated squared lift norm. Boundary cuts tau=0 or tau=n-1 do not alter the long third-coordinate cell. No full-row-rank assumption is needed.

6. Theorem 5.1 and Lemma 5.2 (pp. 5-6). B is positive by the high gap, and the preceding range equality makes every low observation feasible. The displayed minimizer satisfies the constraint and is B-orthogonal to every homogeneous feasible displacement. The energy splitting therefore gives both directions of (10), with all row dependencies handled by the pseudoinverse. For a diagonal field there are n^2 vertices per value of s, whereas singleton observations carry n^-3 in squared norm; permutation sampling gives the exact n^-5 frame constant. 7. Theorem 6.1 (pp. 6-7). The unnormalized singleton adjoint has unitary Fourier coefficients n^-3 times the sampled phase sum. Orthogonality in t gives n^-5 per transverse fibre. No missing polarization factor or extra volume factor was found. The embedding of singleton rows into C proves the corresponding full-row obstruction.

9. Lemma 7.2 (pp. 8-9). The strong scalar graph consists of n/2 two-layer components, each of size 2n^2. The displayed two-by-two transverse matrix has only one zero scalar eigenvalue per component. Translations and layer reflection give a constant Green diagonal.
