                                              Abstract
         We determine the asymptotic order of the minimum energy invisible to a fixed canonical
     partition in a finite coexact vector-field model. On an even three-dimensional torus with
     counting norm and checkerboard source weights H = cn2 , a = c/48, the full endpoint cell
     observations leave a nonzero kernel whose minimum Rayleigh quotient has order c/ log n.
     Explicit finite upper and lower bounds are proved. The upper witness is real, lies in the
     actual image of the forward curl adjoint, and annihilates every original cell mean. It follows
     that finite penalties on these observations cannot restore a size-uniform target c. The proof
     combines the complete restricted Fourier spectrum, an original-row range theorem, finite
     Green estimates, optimization over complementary singleton coefficients, and frequency-
     band removal localized to the lowest nonzero component sector. We also give an exact
     conditional-cost identity for the remaining rows of moving partitions. The best canonical
     partition, including a quadratic moving cut, remains unresolved. All arguments are finite
     and analytical. Green-function logarithms and Schur minimization are classical; external
     priority for the precise model-specific theorem is unestablished. No physical Yang–Mills
     gap or continuum conclusion is asserted.


1    Main result and relation to earlier work
Our main conclusion is a two-sided c/ log n estimate for the full observation kernel of one
admissible fixed partition. The distinction between a fixed partition and the best partition
is essential. A weak lower bound alone would not refute a uniform gap; here a real invisible
field supplies the matching upper order. Conversely, failure of this fixed partition does not
establish failure for moving partitions.
    This manuscript is a proposed linked revision of the programme working paper Spectral
Reduction and Logarithmic Observation Response in a Discrete Coexact Model, AIRR ver-
sion 1 [2]. That version established the spectrum, range, and direct-response bounds but
explicitly left optimized cancellation open. The present revision retains their proofs and adds
the full endpoint-kernel theorem and conditional residual cost. The earlier version remains a
distinct immutable artifact; its assessments are not assessments of this revision.
    Checkerboard weights couple two Fourier frequencies, while the actual curl-adjoint image
imposes two transverse polarizations and removes the harmonic zero mode. Neither a scalar
block alone nor injectivity on low modes decides high-mode cancellation. Sections 3–5 retain
all those restrictions and the original rows. The response estimates in Sections 6–7 supply

finite constants. Section 8 then handles complementary coefficients and constructs a full-cell
witness; Section 9 isolates the remaining problem for moving cuts.
    Two-dimensional logarithmic Green functions are classical. Kenyon’s Theorem 7.3 treats
critical planar graphs [4]; Lawler and Limic develop continuous-time walks and the two-
dimensional potential kernel, including Theorem 4.4.4 [5]. Their graph, volume, and operator
normalizations are not identified with our finite coexact compression. Schur minimization is
also classical; the primary abstract of Corach, Maestripieri, and Stojanoff explicitly relates
shorted operators and projections [3]. Every finite comparison used here is proved below.
These bounded primary-source comparisons do not establish priority for the precise endpoint
theorem; neither the logarithmic mechanism nor the general minimization algebra is claimed
as new.
    The earlier programme working paper Coverage and coexactness in checkerboard slice ob-
servations [1] concerns coverage, projection issues and an ambient-field lower bound. Those
results are not presented as new here. Remark 6.3 distinguishes its domain from the present
actual-coexact lower bound.

10     Limits and the remaining optimization question
The endpoint theorem proves a matching logarithmic order for the full original observation
kernel, not merely a large dual compression. It therefore closes the fixed-cut question left
open in version 1. The quadratic moving cut and the optimization over canonical partitions
remain open: their genuine planar prefixes may raise the conditional cost in (32). A generic
large direct response cannot decide that cost, and nominal row counts do not establish a rank
defect.
