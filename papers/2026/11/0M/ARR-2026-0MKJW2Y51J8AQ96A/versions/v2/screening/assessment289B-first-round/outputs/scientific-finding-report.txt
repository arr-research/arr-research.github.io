FINDINGS AND EVIDENCE

F1. Domain and conventions are explicit and consistent (pp. 2-4, Lemma 3.1).
Counting norms, forward differences, and a once-per-pair two-form norm fix the adjoint. The displayed adjoint uses conjugate Fourier symbols. The explicit skew preimage maps any vector in g(k)-perpendicular to that vector, and zero frequency is excluded. Consequently the real dimension 2(n^3-1) is correct; complexification does not double the real multiplicities. There is no silent addition of harmonic constants. No ambient invariance of the weighted operator is assumed.

F2. The complete compressed spectrum has the required multiplicities (p. 4, Theorem 3.2).
The source checkerboard couples exactly k and k+q. The singular values of the overlap of the two transverse planes are 1 and |Gamma|/sqrt(DD'), yielding the two pairs of roots in (8). The exceptional {0,q} pair has only the two-dimensional q plane. Counting gives 4(n^3/2-1)+2=2(n^3-1). Changing source parity changes the coupling sign but not these eigenvalues. Degenerate overlap and repeated roots are covered rather than omitted.

F3. Low/high separation is sufficient for every shifted inverse used later (p. 5, Proposition 3.3).
The strong minimum H(3-|S|) vanishes only on diagonal frequencies. A non-diagonal triple has at least two nonzero phase differences, giving the stated 16H/(3n^2) bound. The positive weak form cannot reduce it. On nonexceptional diagonal pairs the two low polarizations have 0<mu_-<=6a=c/8, with the other branches at least 6H. Thus dim L=n-2, A_F>=16c/3 and B=A_F-cI>=13c/3 hold, including the exceptional pair. The later inverse bounds do not invert the strong zero modes.

F4. The row normalization is correct in the actual PDF (pp. 2, 5, 7-8; (2), (9), (16)).
Equation (9) has denominator n^3 sqrt(|I||J|), as required by combining the n^(-3/2) unitary Fourier convention and the observation normalization. Text extraction can visually misplace this radical; page 5 and TeX lines 304-309 resolve it. Singleton evaluation T carries n^(-3/2), its adjoint Fourier data carry n^(-3), and permutation orthogonality gives n^(-5). No normalization error is retained as an objection.

F5. Range equality is actually proved with dependent rows (pp. 5-6, Theorem 4.1).
Original adjoint row fields are constant along the long third-coordinate interval. Curl then has a slab constancy incompatible with a nonzero diagonal coexact field, yielding equality of annihilators. The quantitative slab comparison gives n^5/[16(n-3)] from 48n/(n-3) divided by 768n^(-4). The adjoint factorization defines an actual bounded lift without requiring full row rank. This works for arbitrary cuts and n>=4, when the needed slices and slab exist.

F6. The shifted criterion and duality use the right sign and range (pp. 6-7, Theorem 5.1).
The feasible minimizing high field is -B^(-1)C_F^*M^dagger C_L l. Range equality ensures feasibility for every low datum. Completing the B-square gives (11), and the spectral decomposition gives the exact criterion C_L^*M^dagger C_L-D_L>=0. Dependent rows are treated by inversion on the range. The explicitly large lift estimate is correctly identified as inadequate to settle that criterion.

F7. The whole-response estimates and kernel lower bound have the necessary scaling (pp. 7-9, Theorem 6.1 and Corollary 6.2).
Each transverse fibre has n/2 checkerboard pairs; only (0,0) is diagonal. The shell bound and retained triangular set supply the upper and lower harmonic sums. In the lower trace argument the balanced singleton parities give tr M_T=n^(-5) tr_F B^(-1); the rank divisor is 3n, not n. The low datum frame is n^(-5)I. Optimizing the dual quadratic in the singleton coefficients gives a cancellation cost at least c||l||^2/K_n. Combining with c||f||^2 yields the actual invisible-field lower bound c/K_n, including both channels. The lower bound alone is not used to assert failure of target c.

F8. The Green estimate and coexact embedding address a genuine possible gap (pp. 9-11, Lemma 7.2 and Section 7.2).
The degree-three two-layer graph has eigenvalues 3+-|S|, with a unique zero mode in each component. Vertex transitivity supports the constant Green diagonal. The finite heat-kernel integral, short-time negative bound and exponentially summable tail justify G(x,y)>=-1 without an unjustified positivity assertion for G. The common polarization {1,z}-perpendicular lies in both actual coexact planes and is independent of the component Fourier variable t. It therefore defines a componentwise selected inverse R_0. On non-diagonal blocks B<=A_H, so B^(-1)>=A_H^(-1)>=R_0. The omitted transverse optical mode pairs to zero with the pair-plus data. These facts make (23)-(24) actual coexact response statements rather than ambient scalar surrogates.

F9. Rotation from the strong kernel to the positive-weight low space is paid for (pp. 11-12, Theorem 7.1).
The weak norm bound c/4 and high strong eigenvalue 6H imply the angle bound 2/(48n^2-1). The singleton frame is an isometry after multiplication by n^(5/2), so the same angle bound transfers to useful row data. The rank and trace-norm estimate yields the correction in (21). The theorem correctly identifies a large direct dual response only, and explicitly avoids treating it as a physical Rayleigh quotient.

F10. The polarization floor and cross-block estimate support optimization (pp. 12-13, Lemma 8.1).
Averaging the real, permutation-invariant polarization matrix over all nonzero transverse frequencies gives (1-n^(-2))P_V/2. The strong component inverse is at least I/6, giving the stated floor. The pair-plus trace uses the scalar Green bound with only one retained spatial polarization. The skew cross block is controlled by the imaginary part of the polarization projector, whose small-angle factor removes the logarithmic singularity in that estimate. The residue-shell estimate is summable and gives the claimed 2pi bound independently of sample separation. The proof does not assume an entrywise positive matrix or infer a floor from a trace.

F11. Band removal preserves the true domain and provides enough cancellations (pp. 13-14, Lemma 8.2).
The removed bands k_2 or k_3 in {0,N} are unions of whole checkerboard pairs, so their projections preserve U and commute with the compressed A and selected R_0. No diagonal low pair is removed. The selected support covariance at the sample line is (1-n^(-2))I_W/2, and each band evaluation covariance is 2I/n. Subtraction gives (1-8/n-n^(-2))/12>=1/32 for n>=16. Thus the floor survives removal. Every retained field has zero full ordinary line sums in coordinates 2 and 3, which are the facts used for the original endpoint cells.

F12. The bounded sector loss is localized where a sufficiently cheap low vector exists (pp. 14-15, Lemma 8.3 and (27)).
In sector H_1, the full Fourier evaluation data have a/n and b/n on the two k_1 planes, with exactly one representative k_1=1 per pair. Removed pairs lie on four coordinate lines and are never diagonal. Their transverse distance has a coordinate of centered magnitude at least one, producing a bounded one-dimensional reciprocal sum and the bound 8. This is not a claim of bounded removal loss uniformly on every sector. The low eigenvalue in this sector is at most c*pi^2/(2n^2), by rationalization with denominator at least 2H.

F13. The optimized response is distinct from direct compression, and the comparison is valid (pp. 15-16, Section 8.4).
For a positive form G, fixing P_E alpha=y has attained minimum (P_E G^(-1)P_E|E)^(-1). The endpoint component blocks are identical; the nonconstant character makes the global mean constraint automatic. The pair-minus Schur cost uses the surviving I/32 floor, bounded cross block and logarithmic plus trace. Rotating the useful space costs the stated 256 K_n^2/(48n^2-1). Extending to all singleton coefficients, including W-perpendicular, is justified by the floor and the pointwise form inequality Mhat'>=K'. The range inclusion E subset ran Mhat' follows because ker Mhat' subset W-perpendicular. Thus the minimum over complementary coefficients is controlled rather than ignored. Translation sectors prevent an unaccounted cross-sector reduction of cost.

F14. The witness is a real actual coexact field invisible to every original endpoint row (p. 16, Section 8.5).
For a top sector eigenvalue beta>=b_end>0, J=n^(5/2)T_L is an isometry, so l=J^*y has unit norm. The shifted cancellation cost scales to c/beta, not cn^5/beta: T_L l=n^(-5/2)y cancels the response scaling. B>=13c/3 bounds ||f||^2 by 3/(13beta), giving the 16/(13beta) energy term after adding c||f||^2. The low and high parts are spectrally orthogonal. Sectors 1 and N-1 are distinct for n>=16, so sqrt(2)Re(l+f) preserves the complex field's norm and energy and remains nonzero. Zero singleton values plus zero full line sums imply both point-by-long cell sums vanish; plane inclusion-exclusion then kills the long-by-long cell. All components are treated, and row normalization multiplies zeros. The argument therefore supports the main theorem for the full C_end, not just T.

F15. Quantifiers, asymptotics and penalties are appropriately limited (pp. 3, 16-18).
The explicit upper bound is conditional on b_end(n)>0 and n even >=16. Its leading harmonic coefficient is positive and the K_n^2/n^2 correction tends to zero, so this condition eventually holds along even sizes. The stated liminf and limsup constants follow from K_n/log n ->48/13 and b_end/log n ->1/(16pi^2). The theorem is linear in c>0. It is not an exact leading constant or a small-n claim. A finite penalty depending quadratically on the same zero observations vanishes on the constructed witness even if its coefficient is very large, so it cannot restore target c at sufficiently large sizes. This conclusion does not apply to extra observations or a different partition. The direct sum lower bound and one-channel upper witness give the claimed two-channel bounds.

F16. Conditional remaining-row cost is exact but does not solve moving cuts (p. 17, Proposition 9.1).
Whitening with B^(1/2) turns the singleton residual projector into the orthogonal projector onto ker(T_F B^(-1/2)). The range theorem supplies a fully feasible field and hence r(l) in ran K_P. The identity R_T B R_T=R_T and orthogonality to the singleton minimizer make the two costs add. On ran Xi, the normalized long-row relation has the factor -1/sqrt(n-1), and moving cuts retain genuine prefix constraints. No claim of negligible residual cost, rank defect or optimized-partition failure is proved or asserted. The dyadic permutation observation for the quadratic cut is correct but does not remove these constraints.

NONMATERIAL WEAKNESSES AND REQUESTED MINOR REVISIONS

1. Add a short domain/scaling dictionary before Section 8.4: full physical fields, L/F, singleton row space, W, E, H_1, and the relation between M', Mhat', K' and J. The present formulas are consistent, but the number of spaces and reused symbols makes this crucial argument difficult to audit.
2. Make the practical strength of the constants more concrete. The fixed subtraction 32(2pi+8)^2 means positivity of the explicit b_end requires a harmonic number of roughly million scale; the resulting sufficient n is fantastically large. This is an analytical existence result, not a feasible numerical witness at a presently specified size. A simple stated sufficient threshold or a rough scale explanation would improve interpretation without being a new theorem premise.
3. Explain in one additional sentence why the nonconstant component character makes the mean-zero constraint automatic and why the full complementary minimization separates by sector. These statements are correct in the translation-invariant endpoint setting and materially help a reader distinguish that setting from moving cuts.
4. Keep external priority and significance provisional. A focused comparison with finite-torus point-capacity or vector-constrained observation results would be needed before making a stronger originality claim. This review did not perform that search. No stronger novelty wording is warranted by the bounded contextual references alone.

WITNESS REALISM AND SCIENTIFIC LIMITS

The witness is rigorous as a finite-dimensional existence construction: a spectral top eigenvector followed by a constrained inverse, with a specified realification. It is not supplied as concrete component data at a practical n and was not numerically instantiated here. The logarithmic order is a meaningful obstruction for this fixed observation geometry, with constants far from a usable threshold. The model's weights vary with n; there is no demonstrated physical scaling limit, no optimized-cut theorem and no Millennium Problem solution. These limits are reflected in the score and significance criterion, and should remain visible in any later summary.
