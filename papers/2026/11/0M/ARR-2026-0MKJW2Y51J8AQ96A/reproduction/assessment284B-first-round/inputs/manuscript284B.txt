    Spectral Reduction and Logarithmic Observation Response
                  in a Discrete Coexact Model
                                          Lluis Eriksson

                                        12 November 2026
                                        Working paper draft


                                              Abstract
         We study a finite counting-norm vector-field model on an even three-dimensional torus,
     with checkerboard source weights and componentwise rectangular cell means. On the ac-
     tual image of the forward curl adjoint, we derive the complete two-polarization Fourier
     spectrum, including the missing zero mode and the exceptional frequency pair. For weights
     H = cn2 and a = c/48, the entire subtarget spectral space has dimension n−2 per channel.
     The original high modes realize every observation realized by the full coexact space; an
     explicit polynomial lifting estimate and an exact shifted cancellation-cost criterion follow.
     For permutation singleton cuts, we prove finite logarithmic upper and lower bounds for the
     high-response operator, and show that the logarithmic growth persists after compression
     to useful positive-weight low data. This rules out two uniform response estimates, with-
     out deciding the optimized cancellation problem. A logarithmic lower bound for invisible
     coexact fields is also proved. All arguments are finite and analytical. Two-dimensional
     Green-function logarithms are classical; originality of the precise model-specific compar-
     isons is unestablished. No continuum or physical Yang–Mills spectral-gap conclusion is
     asserted.


1    Scope and relation to classical logarithms
Checkerboard weights couple two Fourier frequencies, while a curl-adjoint domain imposes two
transverse polarizations and removes the harmonic zero mode. Keeping these restrictions and
the original observation rows together is essential: neither a scalar block alone nor injectivity
of observations on low modes settles cancellation by high modes.
    The argument below proceeds from the complete restricted spectrum to an original-row
range theorem, then to a finite cancellation criterion and quantitative response estimates. The
final obstruction concerns direct response bounds, including their compression to useful low
data. It does not determine the inverse problem after correlated dual directions are optimized.
    Logarithmic Green functions in two dimensions are established mathematics. Kenyon’s
Theorem 7.3 gives logarithmic Green-function asymptotics in the critical planar graph set-
ting [2]; Lawler and Limic develop continuous-time walks and the two-dimensional potential
kernel, including Theorem 4.4.4 [3]. Their graph, volume and operator normalizations are
not identified with the finite component graph or the coexact compression used here. We
derive every finite normalization and tail estimate needed below. These bounded primary-
source comparisons do not establish priority for the precise finite statements, and the classical
logarithmic mechanism is not claimed as new.

8    What the obstructions leave open
Theorems 6.1 and 7.1 exclude uniform scalar response estimates on the complete singleton
rowspace and on its direct useful low-data subspace. Adding original prefix rows does not
repair a blanket full-row norm estimate, since it retains the singleton compression. It can
nevertheless change an optimized dual problem.
    In fact, for useful data an added singleton coefficient in E ⊥ has zero low pairing but may
lower response through cross terms. Correlated prefix or long-cell coefficients may do the
same. The corresponding optimized response involves an inverse or Schur complement, which
is not determined by a large direct compression. No theorem here asserts that all singleton
methods fail, that the best full cancellation cost must lose a logarithm, or that an invisible
field has quotient below c. The sign in (10) remains unresolved for the quadratic cut.
    The polynomial lift and the logarithmic kernel bound are paid finite estimates, with their
inadequate target-c strength explicitly retained. No selected numerical finite-size result is
used as a proof premise, and no numerical rank, formal verification or continuum limit is
asserted. These statements concern this discrete counting-norm family; they are not a physical
Yang–Mills spectral gap or a resolution of the continuum problem. The classical logarithmic
mechanism and the model-specific finite comparisons should be distinguished from any future
claim of external originality, which remains unestablished here.
