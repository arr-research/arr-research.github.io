THE 2-PEBBLING PROPERTY, SHARPt-PEBBLING BOUNDS, AND GRAHAM’S
CONJECTURE FOR SPLIT GRAPHS
LLUIS ERIKSSON
Abstract. A connected graph G satisfies Chung’s 2-pebbling property ( 2PP) if, for every target vertex r∈V (G)
and every pebble configuration D satisfying p(D) + q(D) > 2π(G) (where p(D) is the total number of pebbles and
q(D) is the number of occupied vertices), two pebbles can be moved to r. Despite the structural simplicity of split
graphs—chordal graphs of diameter at most 3 whose pebbling numbers were characterized by Alc´ on, Gutierrez, and
Hurlbert (2014)—the validity of 2PP, the tight behavior of the t-pebbling number πt(G), and Graham’s product
conjectureπ(G□H)≤π(G)π(H) for split graphs have remained open.
We resolve all three questions completely. Our central structural tool is anIntra-Clique Move Elimination Lemma:
any minimum-length pebbling sequence delivering t pebbles to a target r in a split graph G = (C∪I, E ) avoids all
moves entering I\ {r}, all moves inside C\ {r}when r∈C , and all moves C1 →C and C0 →C 0 when r∈I , thereby
compressing optimal pebbling into a layered directed acyclic graph of path length at most 3 (and at most 2 when r∈C ).
Combining this compression with causal binary-tree extraction yields the sharp bound πt(G) ≤π (G) + 2diam(G)(t− 1)
for all connected split graphs and all t≥ 1, attained across every diameter d∈ {0, 1, 2, 3} in both non-Class-0 ( δ = 1)
and Class-0 (δ = 3) regimes, while exhibiting a strict sub-maximal frontier πt(G4,4) = 8t < π1(G4,4)+8( t− 1) for t≥ 2.
Coupling the same DAG compression with an upward even-to-odd parity boost on I\ {r}, a conservation identity, and
an optimal parity-absorption routing analysis proves that every connected split graph satisfies 2PP (and hence O2PP; in
particular, no split graph is a Lemke graph). Finally, we establish Graham’s conjecture whenever G is a connected split
graph and H is any tree, complete graph, hypercube Qd, complete bipartite graph, or complete multipartite graph,
including a self-contained lifting lemma for the odd 2-pebbling property that bypasses strict product drops such as
π((K3 + leaf)□K 2) = 9<10 = 2π(K 3 + leaf).
1.Introduction
Graph pebbling, introduced by Fan Chung [ 2] following a number-theoretic question of Lagarias and Saks (see also
Lemke and Kleitman [9]), models resource transport across a connected finite simple graph G = (V, E). Given an
initial pebble configuration D:V→Z ≥0, apebbling move( u→v ) along an edge uv∈E removes two pebbles from
u and places one pebble on v. For a target vertex r∈V and integer t≥ 1, a configuration D is t-solvable for r if
there exists a finite sequence of valid pebbling moves transforming D into a configuration D′ with D′(r) ≥t . The
t-pebbling number πt(G, r) is the smallest integer m such that every configuration D of size p(D) := P
v∈V D(v) ≥m
is t-solvable for r, and πt(G) := maxr∈V πt(G, r). When t = 1, we write π(G) = π1(G) for the classical pebbling
number. Clearly π(G) ≥max{n, 2diam(G)} for every connected graph on n = |V| vertices; graphs satisfying π(G) = n
are calledClass0.
To attack product bounds via induction, Chung [ 2] introduced a fundamental strengthening of π(G) based on the
support size q(D) := |{v∈V : D(v) ≥ 1}|. A connected graph G satisfies the 2-pebbling property( 2PP) if, for every
targetr∈Vand every configurationDsatisfying
p(D) +q(D)>2π(G),(1)
D is 2-solvable for r. Graphs that fail (1) are known asLemke graphs. A closely related weakening tracks the number
of vertices with an odd number of pebbles, o(D) := |{v∈V : D(v) ≡ 1 ( mod 2)}|: a graph satisfies theodd2-pebbling
property( O2PP) if p(D) +o(D) > 2π(G) guarantees 2-solvability for every target r∈V . Since o(D) ≤q (D) pointwise,
2PP implies O2PP. Both properties lie at the heart ofGraham’s pebbling conjecture[ 2], which asserts that for all
connected graphsGandH,
π(G□H)≤π(G)π(H),(2)
where G□H denotes the Cartesian product. Over the past three decades, 2PP has been proved to force (2) whenever
H is a complete graph or hypercube under tight lifting [ 2], a tree [10, 11], a complete bipartite graph [4], or a complete
multipartite graph [12]; see also the surveys of Hurlbert [7, 8].

A graph G is asplit graphif V (G) admits a partition V (G) = C⊔I into a clique C and an independent set I.
Split graphs form a central subclass of chordal graphs, are recognizable in linear time from their degree sequences [ 5],
and satisfy diam(G) ≤ 3. In a landmark paper, Alc´ on, Gutierrez, and Hurlbert [1] derived a closed-form formula and
an O(nβ) algorithm (β <1.41) for π(G) on split graphs, and proved that every connected split graph with minimum
degree δ(G) ≥ 3 is Class 0. Nevertheless, three natural questions left open since [ 1] and the product literature [ 2, 12]
remained unresolved: whether split graphs can ever produce Lemke graphs (i.e., fail 2PP), whether their t-pebbling
numbers obey the sharp diameter scaling πt(G) ≤π (G) + 2diam(G)(t− 1), and whether split graphs satisfy Graham’s
conjecture against the standard2PPproduct families.
In this paper, we answer all three questions affirmatively with completely self-contained combinatorial proofs.
Theorem 1.1(Full 2-Pebbling Property for Split Graphs).Every connected split graph G satisfies the2-pebbling
property ( 2PP): for every target r∈V (G)and every configuration D with p(D) + q(D) > 2π(G), two pebbles can be
moved to r. Consequently, no split graph is a Lemke graph, and every connected split graph satisfies the odd2-pebbling
property (O2PP).
Theorem 1.2(Sharp t-Pebbling Bound for Split Graphs).For every connected split graph G and every integer t≥ 1,
πt(G)≤π(G) + 2 diam(G)(t−1).(3)
Moreover, equality holds for every diameter d∈ {0, 1, 2, 3} and every t≥ 1; in diameter3, equality is attained both
by the infinite non-Class-0( δ = 1) family Km + {r, u}(m≥ 2, πt = n + 8t− 4) and by the Class-0( δ = 3) graph
G8 (πt(G8) = 8t). By contrast, the Class-0( δ = 4) diameter-3split graph G4,4 exhibits a strict sub-maximal jump
π1(G4,4) = 10whileπ t(G4,4) = 8t < π1(G4,4) + 8(t−1)for allt≥2.
Theorem 1.3(Graham’s Pebbling Conjecture for Split Graphs).Let G be any connected split graph, and let H be
any tree, complete graphK m, hypercubeQ d, complete bipartite graph, or complete multipartite graph. Then
π(G□H)≤π(G)π(H).
A subtle point arises in the hypercube case H = Qd (d≥ 2) of Theorem 1.3. Chung’s classical K2-product
theorem [2, Theorem 5] asserts that if G satisfies 2PP, then π(G□K 2) ≤ 2π(G), andif equality π(G□K 2) = 2π(G)
holds, thenG□K 2 again satisfies2PP. However, strict dropsπ(G□K 2)<2π(G) genuinely occur among connected
split graphs: as we prove and verify computationally in Section 5, the diameter-2 split graph K3 + leaf has π = 5
yet π((K3 + leaf) □K 2) = 9 < 10. To keep Theorem 1.3 completely rigorous for all Qd (d≥ 1), we prove a clean
Cube Lifting Lemma(Lemma 5.1) showing that the relaxed threshold property O2PP relative to an upper bound
M≥max{π (G),|V (G)|} propagates unconditionally to ( G□K 2, 2M) because removing pairs from vertices preserves
their pebble parity.
The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 establishes the Intra-Clique Move Elimination Lemma,
the upward even-to-odd boost on I\{r} , and our two structural lower bounds (Lemmas 2.3 and 2.4). Section 3 proves
Theorem 1.2 via causal binary-tree extraction and establishes the exact extremal and frontier properties of Km +{r, u},
G8, and G4,4. Section 4 presents the complete proof of Theorem 1.1 via conservation identities and parity-absorption
routing. Section 5 proves Lemma 5.1 and Theorem 1.3. Section 6 summarizes independent exhaustive computational
checks across all 164 connected split graphs onn≤7 vertices and closes with open problems.

3.Sharpt-Pebbling Bounds and the2t-Pebbling Frontier
To convert the bounded-depth DAG compression of Lemma 2.1 into an inductive bound on πt(G), we extract a
bounded-weight causal sub-configuration for each delivered pebble.
Lemma 3.1(Causal Sub-Configuration Extraction).Let ⃗H = (V, ⃗E)be a directed acyclic graph with target r∈V
such that every directed path in ⃗H ending at r has length at most L. If a pebbling sequence σ = (m1, . . . , mℓ)on ⃗H
from configuration D places at least1pebble on r, then there exists a sub-configuration Dused ≤D (pointwise) with
p(Dused)≤2 L that alone moves1pebble tor.
Proof. Treat the initial p(D) pebbles as distinct physical tokens on their starting vertices. Whenever move ms =
(x→y ) executes, it consumes two distinct tokens currently at x and creates one new token at y whose two children
in an ancestry tree are the two consumed tokens. At termination, choose one token located at r and let T be its
causal binary ancestry tree.
Because ⃗H is a DAG and time s strictly increases along moves toward the root of T, every token produced by a
movem s can appear at most once inT, and every root-to-leaf path inTtraces a directed path in ⃗Hending atrin
reverse. Hence T is a binary tree of depth at most L whose leaves are pairwise distinct initial tokens of D. Since
a binary tree of depth at most L has at most 2 L leaves, letting Dused(v) count the leaves of T starting at v∈V
gives Dused ≤D with p(Dused) ≤ 2L. Executing the moves corresponding to the internal nodes of T in their exact
chronological order fromσconsumes only the leaves ofTand places the root token onr.□

5.Graham’s Pebbling Conjecture for Split Graphs
By Theorem 1.1, every connected split graph G satisfies both 2PP and O2PP. Combining Theorem 1.1 with the
classical2PPproduct theorems already resolves four of the five target families in Theorem 1.3:
•IfH ∼= Km is a complete graph,π(G□K m)≤mπ(G) =π(G)π(K m) by [2, Theorem 5(i)].
•IfH=Tis a tree,π(G□T)≤π(G)π(T) by Moews [10] and Snevily–Foster [11].
•IfHis a complete bipartite graph,π(G□H)≤π(G)π(H) by Feng–Kim [4].
•IfHis a complete multipartite graph,π(G□H)≤π(G)π(H) by Wang [12].
For hypercubes H = Qd (d≥ 1), caution is required when invoking [ 2, Theorem 5(ii)], which states that G□K 2
inherits 2PP provided π(G□K 2) = 2π(G). As verified in Section 6 (and shown in Figure 3(b)), connected split graphs
can exhibit a strict product drop π(G□K 2) < 2π(G), such as G = K3 + leaf (π(G) = 5 yet π(G□K 2) = 9 < 10). To
establish π(G□Q d) ≤ 2dπ(G) without assuming equality at intermediate steps, we lift O2PP relative to an explicit
