## Hostile checks of the claimed distinctions

### `Q_R` is not merely the classical projection-density constant

For a spherical law, the score density at zero is `c_d E R^{-1}`, whereas the transverse-energy-weighted score density is `c_d E R/(d-1)`. Their ratio is `Q_R`. Cambanis-type representation results supply the ingredients, but the manuscript's sigmoid-Hessian conclusion requires combining two different weighted marginal limits. The novelty is the combination and its role in anisotropy, not either marginal identity in isolation.

### The phase transition is not implied by Chardon's exponent statement

Chardon's regular-design result supplies non-Gaussian reference scales and an empirical lower-Hessian theorem under margin regularity. It does not state the negative-radius-moment boundary, the logarithmic critical regime, or the `r^{a+1}` anisotropy law for a spherical radial mixture. Conversely, the manuscript should not claim that no non-Gaussian `r^{-1}`/`r^{-3}` scale was known; it correctly avoids that claim.

### The shell theorem is narrower in one axis and stronger in another

Chardon v3 Theorem 6 is uniform over a local Fisher ellipsoid and one-sided at constant factor for `p=1`; Ostrovskii–Bach gives a local bilateral constant-factor sandwich. The present theorem is uniform over a full dyadic Euclidean shell and tunable to `(1+-epsilon)` for all `p>0`, but pays a covering logarithm and makes no estimator claim. These are genuinely different guarantees. Calling the manuscript theorem “the first uniform Hessian bound” would be false; the present prose explicitly says it is not first.

### Generic covariance lower bounds do not erase the `r d` theorem

Classical covariance theory establishes sharp dimension/effective-rank spectral rates for Gaussian sample covariance. Here the normalized weighted observation is neither a fixed Gaussian vector nor an `r`-independent covariance model. The manuscript proves that its conditional tangential off-diagonal variance is of order `r/n`, which is the missing mechanism. The result is defensible only as a lower bound for the stated fixed-oracle weighted Gram and bilateral relative-Loewner event; the limitations section already enforces this boundary.
