1    Introduction and scope
For an isolated hypersurface germ h, the Tjurina number

                       d    . The purpose of this note is to record a stronger finite-jet count and
use it to settle the corresponding sharp floor for point-span absorbed Gauss fibres in dimension
two.
    The key observation is small but consequential. In characteristic zero, the Euler combination
removes the degree-(s + 1) part of h while leaving the Tjurina ideal unchanged. A truncated
coefficient count then remembers that the ideal has d generators of order at least s and one
further generator of order at least s + 2. Optimizing the truncation produces the number Ed,s
above.

In the plane, E2,s agrees with the classical sharp minimum in the ordinary multiplicity-
(s + 1) equisingularity class computed through the work of Briançon–Granger–Maisonobe [2];
explicit attaining families are also discussed by Canino–Gimigliano–Idà [3, 4]. Their results
supply sharp examples; the elementary argument below supplies the lower bound for arbitrary
isolated plane germs of the prescribed order. We state this scope explicitly and make no claim
that the abstract generator count was absent from all earlier commutative-algebra literature.
    The geometric application continues the scheme-theoretic Gauss-fibre framework of [5]. That
paper identifies the local Gauss-fibre algebra with the Tjurina algebra and proves the earlier
fat-point floor. The exact reduced-support bound used here is from [6, 7]. We restate the needed
definitions and arguments so that the new numerical step is transparent.
    Everything is over C = C. In particular, division by s+1 in the Euler reduction is legitimate.
We do not assert the same theorem in positive characteristic.

6    Comparisons, reproducibility, and limitations
Liu proved µ/τ ≤ d for isolated hypersurface singularities [9]; together with µ ≥ sd , this gives
τ ≥ ⌈sd /d⌉. For plane curves, Almirón proved the strict inequality µ/τ < 4/3 [1]. These are
important comparisons, but they are not used in the proof of Theorem 2.2. The plane bound
(3.1) retains an additional linear term that the ratio estimate alone does not recover.
    A recent preprint of Ma–Zuo [10] announces sharper universal dimension-dependent asymp-
totic ratios in several dimensions. We do not use that preprint, and we do not treat it as
externally consolidated. The present estimate is instead a direct, finite-jet generator count and
gives an exact geometric answer in dimension two.
    The accompanying replays use exact integer combinatorics, exact Q-Gröbner bases, and
sparse modular linear algebra. They check the optimized formula for 1 ≤ d ≤ 8, 1 ≤ s ≤ 20,
verify strict improvement over the former fat-point floor on that grid, and evaluate local quotient
fixtures over two large prime fields. For the sharp plane family, exact Gröbner bases over Q
verify 1 ≤ s ≤ 12. These computations certify only the displayed fixtures; the proofs above
establish the general statements.
    There are three deliberate limitations. First, the exact global realization is proved only
for surfaces; the higher-dimensional floor need not be sharp. Second, the degree in (5.1) is
a transparent separator bound, not an optimized interpolation threshold. Third, no positive-
characteristic version is claimed because Euler reduction divides by s + 1.


7    Conclusion
Euler cancellation exposes one further vanishing order in the Tjurina ideal. Optimized after
truncation, that single order raises the local and global Gauss-fibre floors substantially. In the
plane it yields the exact value          $              %
                                           3s2 + 4s − 3
                                                 4
per support, and prescribed jets realize equality simultaneously at the smallest support allowed
by point-span absorption. The higher-dimensional quantity Ed,s , its possible sharpness, and
lower degree realizations remain open.
