\begin{abstract}
Let \(h\in\m^{s+1}\subset\kk[[x_1,\ldots,x_d]]\) have a finite
Tjurina algebra.  Euler cancellation replaces \(h\), without changing its
Tjurina ideal, by a generator of order at least \(s+2\).  Counting coefficient
jets after this replacement gives the self-contained bound
\[
 \tau(h)\ge E_{d,s}:=\max_{K\ge0}
 \left\{
 \binom{d+K}{d}
 -d\binom{d+K-s}{d}
 -\binom{d+K-s-2}{d}
 \right\},
\]
with out-of-range binomial coefficients interpreted as zero.  For plane
germs the maximum is explicit:
\[
 E_{2,s}=\left\lfloor\frac{3s^2+4s-3}{4}\right\rfloor.
\]
This is exact, as follows from classical ordinary multiple-point families.

We apply the count to scheme-theoretic Gauss fibres.  If the complete
\(\cO_X(m)\) point span of the reduced fibre of a smooth hypersurface
absorbs order \(s\), then every tangent-section germ belongs to
\(\m^{s+1}\).  Consequently
\[
 \length(\Gamma_\eta)\ge
 E_{d,s}|Z|\ge E_{d,s}\binom{d+m}{d}.
\]
For surfaces, a prescribed-jet construction realizes equality in the last
bound at the explicit sufficient degree
\(D\ge(s^2+2)\binom{m+2}{2}+1\).  Thus the plane Gauss-fibre floor is sharp
under the stated absorption and extremal-support conditions.  The local
count is elementary and may be folklore; priority is claimed only for the
specific proved synthesis and application, not for every ingredient.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and scope}

For an isolated hypersurface germ \(h\), the Tjurina number
\[
 \tau(h)=\dim_\kk
 \frac{\kk[[x_1,\ldots,x_d]]}
 {(h,\partial_1h,\ldots,\partial_dh)}
\]
measures the length of its Tjurina algebra.  If
\(h\in\m^{s+1}\), the immediate containment
\((h,\partial h)\subseteq\m^s\) gives only
\(\tau(h)\ge\binom{d+s-1}{d}\).  The purpose of this note is to record a
stronger finite-jet count and use it to settle the corresponding sharp
floor for point-span absorbed Gauss fibres in dimension two.

The key observation is small but consequential.  In characteristic zero,
the Euler combination removes the degree-\((s+1)\) part of \(h\) while leaving
the Tjurina ideal unchanged.  A truncated coefficient count then remembers
that the ideal has \(d\) generators of order at least \(s\) and one further
generator of order at least \(s+2\).  Optimizing the truncation produces
the number \(E_{d,s}\) above.

In the plane, \(E_{2,s}\) agrees with the classical sharp minimum in the
ordinary multiplicity-\((s+1)\) equisingularity class computed through the
work of Brian\c{c}on--Granger--Maisonobe~\cite{BGM1988}; explicit attaining
families are also discussed by Canino--Gimigliano--Id\`a
\cite{CaninoEtAl2025,CaninoEtAlArxiv}.  Their results supply sharp examples;
the elementary argument below supplies the lower bound for arbitrary
isolated plane germs of the prescribed order.  We state this scope
explicitly and make no claim that the abstract generator count was absent
from all earlier commutative-algebra literature.

The geometric application continues the scheme-theoretic Gauss-fibre
framework of~\cite{ErikssonGauss2026}.  That paper identifies the local
Gauss-fibre algebra with the Tjurina algebra and proves the earlier
fat-point floor.  The exact reduced-support bound used here is from
\cite{ErikssonDual2026,ErikssonTangent2026}.  We restate the needed
definitions and arguments so that the new numerical step is transparent.

Everything is over \(\kk=\mathbb C\).  In particular, division by \(s+1\)
in the Euler reduction is legitimate.  We do not assert the same theorem in
positive characteristic.

\section{The Euler-reduced local bound}

We use the convention
\[
 \binom nd=0\qquad\text{if }n<d.
\]
For integers \(d,s\ge1\) and \(K\ge0\), set
\begin{equation}
 E_{d,s}(K)=
 \binom{d+K}{d}
 -d\binom{d+K-s}{d}
 -\binom{d+K-s-2}{d},
\end{equation}
and \(E_{d,s}=\max_{K\ge0}E_{d,s}(K)\).

\begin{lemma}[Euler-reduced generator]\label{lem:euler}
Let \(R=\kk[[x_1,\ldots,x_d]]\), let
\(\m=(x_1,\ldots,x_d)\), and let \(h\in\m^{s+1}\).  Then
\[
 r=h-\frac1{s+1}\sum_{i=1}^d x_i\partial_i h
 \in\m^{s+2}
\]
and
\[
 (h,\partial_1h,\ldots,\partial_dh)
 =(r,\partial_1h,\ldots,\partial_dh).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Writing \(h=\sum_{q\ge s+1}h_q\) with \(h_q\) homogeneous of degree \(q\),
Euler's identity gives
\[
 r=\sum_{q\ge s+2}\left(1-\frac{q}{s+1}\right)h_q,
\]
so \(r\in\m^{s+2}\).  Both ideal containments in (2.2) follow directly
from the displayed definition of \(r\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Truncated-generator Tjurina floor]\label{thm:localfloor}
Let \(h\in\m^{s+1}\subset R=\kk[[x_1,\ldots,x_d]]\), and assume that
\(R/(h,\partial h)\) has finite length.  Then
\[
 \tau(h)\ge E_{d,s}.
\]
In particular,
\[
 \tau(h)\ge\binom{d+s}{d}-d.
\]
For \(d,s\ge2\), (2.4) strictly improves
\(\binom{d+s-1}{d}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(K\ge0\) and write \(A_K=R/\m^{K+1}\).  Its dimension is
\(\binom{d+K}{d}\).  By Lemma~\ref{lem:euler}, the Tjurina ideal is
generated by \(d\) elements \(\partial_i h\in\m^s\) and one element
\(r\in\m^{s+2}\).

In \(A_K\), the multiples of a fixed element of order at least \(s\) are
spanned by coefficients of degree at most \(K-s\), a space of dimension
\(\binom{d+K-s}{d}\).  Similarly, the multiples of \(r\) are spanned by a
space of dimension at most \(\binom{d+K-s-2}{d}\).  We only need an upper
bound on the sum of these images; possible syzygies can make it smaller,
never larger.  Hence
\[
 \dim_\kk A_K/(h,\partial h)A_K\ge E_{d,s}(K).
\]
The Tjurina algebra surjects onto this truncated quotient, proving the
inequality for every \(K\), and therefore (2.3).

For large \(K\), the degree-\(d\) leading coefficient of \(E_{d,s}(K)\) is
negative, so the maximum is finite.  Taking \(K=s\) gives (2.4).  Its gain
over the fat-point count is
\[
 \binom{d+s-1}{d-1}-d>0
\]
when \(d,s\ge2\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The proof uses only generator orders after the Euler cancellation.  It does
not require a Milnor-number inequality, weighted homogeneity, or an
assumption on the tangent cone.  The finite-colength hypothesis is needed
only so that \(\tau(h)\) is finite.
\end{remark}

For context, without the lower-order correction caused by Euler reduction,
the same argument would subtract
\(\binom{d+K-s-1}{d}\) instead of the last term in (2.1).  The extra order
is essential for the exact plane answer.

\begin{proposition}[Asymptotic form]\label{prop:asymptotic}
For fixed \(d\ge2\), as \(s\to\infty\),
\[
 E_{d,s}=c_d^{\mathrm{gen}}s^d+O_d(s^{d-1}),
 \qquad
 c_d^{\mathrm{gen}}=
 \frac{d+1}{d!\left((d+1)^{1/(d-1)}-1\right)^{d-1}}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(K<s\),
\[
 E_{d,s}(K)=\binom{d+K}{d}
 \leq \frac{s^d}{d!}+O_d(s^{d-1}).
\]
For \(K=s+\alpha s\), division of (2.1) by \(s^d\) gives, uniformly for
\(\alpha\) in a bounded interval, the limiting function
\[
 \phi_d(\alpha)=\frac{(1+\alpha)^d-(d+1)\alpha^d}{d!},
 \qquad \alpha\ge0.
\]
Its derivative at zero is positive, so its maximum is strictly greater
than \(1/d!\).  Thus the range \(K<s\) is not maximizing for all sufficiently
large \(s\).  If \(K/s\to\infty\), the normalized expression is eventually
negative, whereas \(K=s\) gives a positive multiple of \(s^d\); hence every
maximizing sequence has \(K=O_d(s)\).  Uniform binomial expansion on the
remaining bounded range gives the upper estimate by maximizing
\(\phi_d\).  Conversely, choosing the nearest integer to
\(s(1+\alpha_d)\) gives the matching lower estimate, where the unique
maximizer is
\[
 \alpha_d=\left((d+1)^{1/(d-1)}-1\right)^{-1}.
\]
Substitution and integer rounding give (2.5), with an error of order
\(s^{d-1}\).
\end{proof}

\section{The exact plane floor}

\begin{theorem}[Universal plane bound]\label{thm:plane}
Let \(h\in\kk[[x,y]]\) define an isolated plane curve singularity and
assume \(h\in\m^{s+1}\).  Then
\begin{equation}
 \tau(h)\ge
 \left\lfloor\frac{3s^2+4s-3}{4}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
The bound is sharp for every \(s\ge1\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(K<s\), (2.1) is \(\binom{K+2}{2}\).  For \(K=s+t\), \(t\ge0\), it is
\[
 E_{2,s}(s+t)
 =\frac{s^2+2st-2t^2+3s-2t-2}{2}.
\]
The concave quadratic is maximized at the integer or integers nearest
\((s-1)/2\).  Comparing with the range \(K<s\) yields
\[
 E_{2,s}=\left\lfloor\frac{3s^2+4s-3}{4}\right\rfloor,
\]
so Theorem~\ref{thm:localfloor} proves (3.1).

Set \(a=s+1\).  If \(a=2k\), take
\[
 h=x^{2k}+y^{2k}+x^{k+1}y^k;
\]
if \(a=2k+1\), take
\[
 h=x^{2k+1}+y^{2k+1}+x^{k+1}y^{k+1}.
\]
These are ordinary multiplicity-\(a\) germs.  The classical computation
\cite{BGM1988,CaninoEtAlArxiv} gives respectively
\[
 \tau=3k^2-k-1,
 \qquad
 \tau=3k^2+2k-1,
\]
which is exactly the right-hand side of (3.1).  The case \(s=1\) is also
included, with \(\tau=1\).
\end{proof}

\medskip
\begin{remark}[Literature boundary]
Writing \(a=s+1\), the number in (3.1) is
\[
 \left\lfloor\frac{3a^2-2a-4}{4}\right\rfloor.
\]
It is already classical as the minimum in the ordinary multiplicity-\(a\)
class.  The role of the families above is therefore sharpness, not novelty.
The lower bound here is obtained for arbitrary isolated plane germs by the
Euler-reduced coefficient count.  We use conservative attribution because
the elementary local estimate may admit formulations elsewhere.
\end{remark}

\section{Osculating absorption and Gauss fibres}

Let \(X^d\subset\PP^{d+1}_\kk\) be a smooth non-linear hypersurface and
let
\[
 \gamma_X:X\longrightarrow X^\vee
\]
be its Gauss morphism.  For a tangent hyperplane
\(\eta=[W]\in X^\vee\), let \(\Gamma_\eta\) be the scheme-theoretic fibre
and \(Z=(\Gamma_\eta)_{\mathrm{red}}\).  Fix \(m\ge1\), put
\(H=\cO_X(1)\), and define
\[
 S_Z=\operatorname{Im}\left(
 H^0(Z,H^m|_Z)^*\longrightarrow H^0(X,H^m)^*\right).
\]
The affine order-\(s\) osculating block is
\[
 \widehat\Osc^s_p(H^m)=\operatorname{Im}\left(
 H^0((s+1)p,H^m|_{(s+1)p})^*
 \longrightarrow H^0(X,H^m)^*\right).
\]

\begin{definition}
For \(1\le s\le m\), the fibre support \(Z\) is
\emph{point-span \(s\)-osculating-absorbing} if
\[
 \widehat\Osc^s_p(H^m)\subseteq S_Z
 \qquad(p\in Z).
\]
\end{definition}

We use two established facts from
\cite{ErikssonGauss2026,ErikssonDual2026}.  First, if \(h_p\) is a local
equation of the tangent section \(X\cap W\) at \(p\), then
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\cO}_{\Gamma_\eta,p}
 \cong\kk[[z_1,\ldots,z_d]]/(h_p,\partial h_p).
\end{equation}
Second, point-span order-\(s\) absorption implies
\begin{equation}
 h_p\in\m_p^{s+1}\quad(p\in Z),
 \qquad |Z|\ge N_{d,m}:=\binom{d+m}{d}.
\end{equation}
For completeness, the first assertion in (4.2) follows by multiplying a
linear equation of \(W\) by a section of \(H^{m-1}\) that is a unit at \(p\),
and then reversing the osculating-span containment under annihilators.

\begin{theorem}[Euler-reduced Gauss-fibre floor]\label{thm:gauss}
Assume that \(Z\) is point-span \(s\)-osculating-absorbing in the complete
\(H^m\) embedding, where \(1\le s\le m\).  Then
\begin{equation}
 \length(\Gamma_\eta)
 \ge E_{d,s}|Z|
 \ge E_{d,s}\binom{d+m}{d}.
\end{equation}
For \(d=2\), this becomes
\begin{equation}
 \length(\Gamma_\eta)\ge
 \left\lfloor\frac{3s^2+4s-3}{4}\right\rfloor
 \binom{m+2}{2}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equations (4.1)--(4.2) and Theorem~\ref{thm:localfloor} give
\(\length(\Gamma_\eta)=\sum_{p\in Z}\tau(h_p)\ge E_{d,s}|Z|\).
The support floor in (4.2) proves the second inequality.  Formula (4.4)
follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:plane}.
\end{proof}

Already \(K=s\) in the local theorem replaces the former factor
\(\binom{d+s-1}{d}\) by \(\binom{d+s}{d}-d\).  Thus (4.3) is strictly
stronger than the earlier fat-point floor whenever \(d,s\ge2\).

\section{Sharp global realization for surfaces}

We now show that (4.4) is an exact geometric floor at a conservative degree.
The interpolation architecture follows~\cite{ErikssonGauss2026}; the new
choice is to prescribe the sharp germs from Theorem~\ref{thm:plane}.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp absorbed surface fibres]\label{thm:realization}
Let \(m,s\) be integers with \(1\le s\le m\), put
\[
 N=\binom{m+2}{2},
 \qquad T_s=\left\lfloor\frac{3s^2+4s-3}{4}\right\rfloor,
\]
and suppose
\begin{equation}
 D\ge(s^2+2)N+1.
\end{equation}
Then there exist a smooth integral surface
\(X\subset\PP^3\) of degree \(D\), a plane \(W\), and
\(\eta=[W]\in X^\vee\) such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(Z=(\gamma_X^{-1}(\eta))_{\mathrm{red}}\) consists of exactly \(N\)
      points;
\item \(\dim S_Z=N<h^0(X,H^m)\), and \(Z\) is point-span
      \(s\)-osculating-absorbing;
\item every local tangent-section germ has multiplicity \(s+1\) and
      Tjurina number \(T_s\);
\item
\[
 \length(\Gamma_\eta)=T_sN.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the bound (4.4) is sharp.  The degree threshold (5.1) is
sufficient and is not asserted to be minimal.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose \(N\) distinct points \(Z\subset W\cong\PP^2\) for which evaluation
is an isomorphism
\begin{equation}
 H^0(W,\cO_W(m))\xrightarrow{\sim}H^0(Z,\cO_Z(m)).
\end{equation}
Such a set exists by choosing a basis among point-evaluation functionals.

At every point prescribe the germ from Theorem~\ref{thm:plane} with the
appropriate parity of \(s+1\).  It has Milnor number \(s^2\), because it is
an ordinary multiplicity-\((s+1)\) plane singularity, and it has Tjurina
number \(T_s\).  An isolated hypersurface germ is contact
\((\tau+1)\)-determined~\cite[Corollary~2.24]{GLS2007}.  It is therefore
enough to prescribe jets through order
\[
 a=s^2+1.
\]

For distinct points \(p_1,\ldots,p_N\) and nonnegative jet orders \(a_i\),
the elementary separator product gives surjectivity
\[
 H^0(\PP^2,\cO(k))\longrightarrow
 \bigoplus_i\cO_{\PP^2,p_i}/\m_{p_i}^{a_i+1}
\]
whenever \(k\ge\sum_i(a_i+1)-1\): multiply an order-\(a_i\) local
interpolant by
\(\prod_{j\ne i}\ell_{ij}^{a_j+1}\), where \(\ell_{ij}\) vanishes at
\(p_j\) and is nonzero at \(p_i\).  Hence a degree-\(D\) plane equation
with all prescribed \(a\)-jets exists under (5.1).

Let \(f_*\) be one such equation and
\[
 V=H^0(W,\cI_{(a+1)Z}(D)),
 \qquad \mathcal A=f_*+V.
\]
For \(u\in W\setminus Z\), the product
\[
 A_u=\prod_{p\in Z}\ell_{p,u}^{a+1}
\]
has degree \((a+1)N=(s^2+2)N\), vanishes to the required orders at \(Z\),
and is a unit at \(u\).  Since (5.1) leaves at least one further degree,
\(V\) generates first jets at \(u\).  The incidence of members singular at
some \(u\in W\setminus Z\) has fibre codimension \(3>2=\dim W\).
Consequently the closure of its image in \(\mathcal A\) is proper.  Thus a
general \(f\in\mathcal A\) is smooth away from \(Z\), and finite contact
determinacy gives exactly the prescribed germs at \(Z\).

Embed \(W=V(y)\) in \(\PP^3\), lift \(f\) independently of \(y\), and put
\[
 F_G=f+yG,
 \qquad G\in H^0(\PP^3,\cO(D-1)).
\]
A general \(G\), chosen nonzero at every point of \(Z\), makes
\(X=V(F_G)\) smooth: on \(W\setminus Z\) this follows from the choice of
\(f\); at \(Z\), \(dF_G=G(p)\,dy\); and off \(W\) the first-jet incidence
has fibre codimension \(4>3=\dim\PP^3\), so the closure of its image in the
space of \(G\)'s is proper.  The smooth hypersurface is connected and hence
integral.

The section \(X\cap W=V(f)\) has singular locus exactly \(Z\).  A point of
\(X\) maps to \([W]\) precisely when it lies on \(W\) and is singular on
that section, so the reduced Gauss fibre is exactly \(Z\).  Locally on \(X\),
\(y=-f/G\), which is contact equivalent to the prescribed germ.

Because (5.1) implies \(D>m\), restriction identifies degree-\(m\) ambient
forms with \(H^0(X,H^m)\).  If such a form vanishes on \(Z\), (5.2) says its
restriction to \(W\) is zero, hence it is divisible by \(y\).  Since
\(y|_X\) has order \(s+1\) at every support, the reverse-annihilator argument
gives point-span order-\(s\) absorption.  Equation (5.2) also gives
\(\dim S_Z=N\), and this span is proper because
\(h^0(X,H^m)=\binom{m+3}{3}>N\).  Finally, (4.1) and the prescribed
Tjurina numbers yield \(\length(\Gamma_\eta)=T_sN\).
\end{proof}

\section{Comparisons, reproducibility, and limitations}

Liu proved \(\mu/\tau\le d\) for isolated hypersurface singularities
\cite{Liu2018}; together with \(\mu\ge s^d\), this gives
\(\tau\ge\lceil s^d/d\rceil\).  For plane curves, Almir\'on proved the
strict inequality \(\mu/\tau<4/3\)~\cite{Almiron2022}.  These are important
comparisons, but they are not used in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:localfloor}.  The plane bound (3.1) retains an additional
linear term that the ratio estimate alone does not recover.

A recent preprint of Ma--Zuo~\cite{MaZuo2026} announces sharper universal
dimension-dependent asymptotic ratios in several dimensions.  We do not
use that preprint, and we do not treat it as externally consolidated.  The
present estimate is instead a direct, finite-jet generator count and gives
an exact geometric answer in dimension two.

The accompanying replays use exact integer combinatorics, exact
\(\mathbb Q\)-Gröbner bases, and sparse modular linear algebra.  They check
the optimized formula for
\(1\le d\le8\), \(1\le s\le20\), verify strict improvement over the former
fat-point floor on that grid, and evaluate local quotient fixtures over two
large prime fields.  For the sharp plane family, exact Gröbner bases over
\(\mathbb Q\) verify \(1\le s\le12\).  These computations certify only the
displayed fixtures; the proofs above establish the general statements.

There are three deliberate limitations.  First, the exact global
realization is proved only for surfaces; the higher-dimensional floor
need not be sharp.  Second, the degree in (5.1) is a transparent separator
bound, not an optimized interpolation threshold.  Third, no
positive-characteristic version is claimed because Euler reduction divides
by \(s+1\).

\section{Conclusion}

Euler cancellation exposes one further vanishing order in the Tjurina
ideal.  Optimized after truncation, that single order raises the local and
global Gauss-fibre floors substantially.  In the plane it yields the exact
value
\[
 \left\lfloor\frac{3s^2+4s-3}{4}\right\rfloor
\]
per support, and prescribed jets realize equality simultaneously at the
smallest support allowed by point-span absorption.  The higher-dimensional
quantity \(E_{d,s}\), its possible sharpness, and lower degree realizations
remain open.
