# Adversarial research audit — working version 0.7

Date: 2026-08-24

## Claim under audit

Let `X` be a smooth projective integral variety of dimension `d >= 1` over an
algebraically closed field, let `L` be very ample, let `m >= 1`, and let `Z`
be a nonempty finite reduced support. If the span of the `L^m` value
evaluations at `Z` contains the first-jet space at every point of `Z`, the
candidate claims

```text
h_Z(m), |Z| >= binom(d+m,d)
```

in arbitrary characteristic. Proper-span equality examples are claimed only
over the complex numbers.

## Proof audit

1. **Kernel formulation.** The annihilator of the value span is
   `H^0(I_Z tensor L^m)`. The annihilator of the sum of the affine tangent
   spaces is `H^0(I_2Z tensor L^m)`. Since every value line belongs to its own
   tangent space, absorption is equivalent to equality of these kernels.

2. **Adapted projection.** In the complete `L`-embedding, a general center of
   dimension `M-d-1` avoids `X`, the finitely many tangent spaces at `Z`, and
   the finitely many secants joining pairs of supports. A positive-dimensional
   fibre would be a projective subset of a fibre `P^(M-d)` disjoint from its
   hyperplane center, which is impossible. Thus the projection is finite.
   Tangent-space avoidance makes the differential an isomorphism at `Z`; with
   smooth equidimensional source and target, the finite local map is etale
   there. Secant avoidance makes the restriction to `Z` injective.

3. **Transfer to projective space.** For `Y=pi(Z)`, a degree-`m` equation of
   `Y` pulls back to an `L^m` section vanishing on `Z`. Absorption makes the
   pullback second-order at every support. The cotangent isomorphism transfers
   this to second-order vanishing on `Y`, hence
   `(I_Y)_m=(I_Y^(2))_m`.

4. **Perfect-field gap.** Let `a=alpha(I_Y)` and suppose a nonzero degree-`a`
   form `F` belongs to `I_Y^(2)`. Every first partial lies in `I_Y`. If one is
   nonzero, it contradicts minimality in degree `a-1`. If all vanish in
   characteristic `p`, every monomial exponent is divisible by `p`; perfection
   gives `F=G^p`, and radicality gives `G in I_Y` in degree `a/p<a`. Therefore
   `alpha(I_Y^(2)) >= alpha(I_Y)+1`.

5. **Fixed-degree consequence.** If `(I_Y)_m` were nonzero, take a minimal
   form `F` of degree `a<=m`. For each support choose a degree `m-a` multiplier
   nonzero there. Equality of the degree-`m` kernels makes the product
   second-order; the multiplier is a local unit, so `F` is second-order at
   every support, contradicting the gap. Hence `(I_Y)_m=0`.

6. **Rank floor.** All `binom(d+m,d)` degree-`m` forms on `P^d` then evaluate
   independently on `Y`. Their pullbacks give the same independent value data
   on `Z`, so `h_Z(m)` has at least that dimension; a span generated by `|Z|`
   lines has dimension at most `|Z|`.

7. **Sharpness.** Over `C`, the simplex lattice is unisolvent by a triangular
   falling-factorial basis. A characteristic-zero Bertini argument produces a
   smooth high-degree hypersurface having the lattice hyperplane as tangent
   hyperplane at every support. Its normal coordinate is second-order in each
   local ring, so the value and tangent spans coincide at exact binomial rank.
   The ambient `L^m` space is strictly larger. No field-uniform Bertini claim
   is used.

## Counterexample and quantifier search

- Algebraic closure implies perfection, supplies rational residue fields, and
  makes the relevant Grassmannian opens nonempty. The paper does not descend
  the projection theorem to finite or imperfect base fields.
- Reducedness is essential to the displayed Frobenius-root proof: from
  `G^p in I` it uses `G in I`. No nonreduced extension is claimed.
- Smoothness is used for tangent/first-neighbourhood identification and the
  cotangent transfer. No singular version is claimed.
- Completeness of the `L^m` embedding and very ampleness of `L` are explicit.
  No subsystem result is claimed.
- The all-partials-zero phenomenon is not treated as a counterexample: it is
  exactly the Frobenius branch that lowers degree through radicality.
- The sharpness lattice uses factorial denominators and integer nodes and is
  deliberately retained over `C`; it is not silently transported to small
  characteristic.
- The secondary squared-hyperplane and Gauss-fibre refinements require
  `m>=3`; the exact binomial floor itself includes `m=1,2`.

No counterexample was found within the stated hypotheses.
