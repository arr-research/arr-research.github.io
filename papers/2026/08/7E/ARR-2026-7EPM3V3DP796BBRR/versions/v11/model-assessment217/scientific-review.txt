3. Mathematical audit by result family
3.1 Actual finite form and Fourier/rank obstructions (Sections 2-6, pages 3-7).
The unscaled unit-spacing convention is explicit. Periodic curl/divergence cancellation with unordered plaquettes counted once gives the component-gradient energy. The two real internal coordinates store the real and imaginary parts of a complex field injectively; thus the complex band with B frequencies really has real dimension 2B. It does not incorrectly double a single real scalar sine/cosine space. The no-alias condition 2R<n ensures character orthogonality. Differentiating each character preserves the same orthogonality, which is needed to bound every vector in the band, not just the generators.

The exact line-map witness is independent of its nonzero field component direction, so divergence vanishes; its block sum vanishes by a nontrivial root-of-unity sum. Its actual quotient 4 sin^2(pi/ell) and the resulting ell^2-order necessary Poincare growth have the stated normalization. The uniform-rank result correctly fixes output dimension, repair rank and c before choosing a sufficiently large lattice. Restricting (C,V) or (C,P) to the band makes the rank-nullity argument valid even for nonsymmetric V and arbitrary zero-vanishing penalties. These are classical dimensional mechanisms with a useful concrete dictionary. Removing a quotient or gauge constraint cannot be inferred unless its actual domain and norm match the theorem.

3.2 Differentiable observations, regular nonlinear sectors and mean-weight budgets (Sections 7-11).
On a derivative-kernel ray, C(tv)=o(t); a locally upper-quadratic penalty has a negligible t^2 contribution. Shrinking is done at each fixed lattice, so no uniform differentiability modulus, penalty constant or radius is needed. Section 8 correctly uses a submersion and the implicit-function theorem to obtain an exact-sector curve tv+o(t). Its curve can leave the Fourier subspace; the quadratic form expansion controls that error. Mere C1 energy regularity would not suffice, and the supplied norm-power and singular-sector counterexamples explain both retained hypotheses.

The directional strip trace only requires constant pointwise amplitude of each selected character, not invariance under the weighted operator. Positivity is explicitly used for complementary trace terms. For signed weights, passing to the pointwise positive part is an upward energy comparison, not taking a positive part after averaging. The constructed zero-signed-mean example and the cycle cancellations show why ordinary signed means are insufficient and why the positive-part condition is only sufficient. The all-off-diagonal finite sum retains the distinction from the single-cycle formal endpoint.

The full-volume coexact packet uses two field components but all D derivative directions. Its nonzero first frequency prevents a zero normalization denominator. The total envelope max(1+w,0) is appropriate: adding the flat 1 twice would be wrong. Integer R=floor(n/k) estimates and the trace/rank inequality give a positive density without taking limits of local neighborhoods. The averaged sine-squared formula and its sinc limit are consistent with the displayed asymptotic density. These are necessary information bounds, not constructions or optimal rank assertions.
