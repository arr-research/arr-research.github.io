PROOF AUDIT AND ADVERSARIAL CHECKS

A. Parallel benchmark, including arbitrary ancillas and a perfectly known carrier.
For the fixed-weight kernel, the all-ones vector is an eigenvector with eigenvalue r_w, and positivity on its orthogonal complement gives K_w >= (r_w/N_w)11^T. The stated bound is therefore valid, including singular endpoint cases. In a purified parallel probe, orthogonality comes from the word register, not from any unsupported orthogonality of ancilla vectors. The projector expression really contains t_x t_y. An acceptance effect between zero and identity that accepts every frozen output with certainty dominates the projector onto their span. Known carrier information can change the probe and measurement but does not change this argument. It can also simulate any finite noiseless carrier calibration, so the comparison is conservative and covers coherent calibration assistance to the parallel side.

For n=5, putting u=eta^2 gives F_1-F_2=(1-u)(1+3u)/10 and F_0-F_2=3(1-u)(3+u)/10. Thus weights 2 and 3 minimize on the claimed interval; at eta=1 other weights tie. The manuscript does not claim uniqueness.

B. No-calibration minimax theorem.
The history vectors of an arbitrary purified causal circuit need not be orthogonal. The manuscript correctly uses Fourier integration of the normalized frozen state instead: sum_w ||psi_w||^2=1. Averaging the renewed carrier removes only unequal-weight terms. Congruencing the positive matrix inequalities for K_w with the history-vector maps gives the stated operator bound. Zero rejection on every frozen state forces the rejection square root to annihilate every Fourier coefficient psi_w, hence the acceptance effect is identity on these coefficients. The lower bound on average failure is therefore valid; worst-case failure is at least average failure. The uniform fixed-weight parallel test attains it for every carrier. There is no illicit exchange of minimization and maximization and no assumption that a causal test refrains from estimating the carrier internally.

C. Calibration and zero false positives.
The continuous POVM integrates to identity and gives exactly the displayed moments. The finite POVM also sums to identity. For any five forward calls, s(delta) has Fourier degree at most five; multiplying by the calibration likelihood produces degree at most six. A seven-point grid annihilates every nonconstant frequency in that range for every shift of the carrier. This proves worst-case, not merely prior-averaged, success. Independently, the exact certificate has only frequencies 0, +/-1, +/-2, +/-3, with
  c_0 = 52978072648546953/100000000000000000,
  c_1 = 1770640130338863/400000000000000000,
  c_2 = -2577780972501/50000000000000000,
  c_3 = -3016901538987/250000000000000000,
and c_-k=c_k. The success is c_0+c_1, agreeing with the theorem. The seven-outcome scheme is sufficient even though it is not an optimized outcome count.

Under H0 the compensated phase is still a frozen phase, for each calibration outcome and each independent pair (theta,phi). Therefore averaging calibration outcomes cannot conceal a false positive. The calibration oracle alone carries no hypothesis label. The proof does rely on the stated ideal controls and free laboratory reference; it does not establish a resource advantage when those physical costs are included.

D. From Gram constraints to an actual causal test.
At each level, opposite last-symbol sectors are orthogonal. Their two child-vector sums have the parent Gram matrix because both cross terms vanish and the recursion sums the same-symbol terms. Equality of Gram matrices makes the parent-to-sum linear map well defined even for dependent vectors and isometric; extending it to a unitary in a common 32-dimensional space supplies a legitimate next control. No bypass or inverse query is needed. Each last-symbol block has dimension at most 16, justifying the signal memory bound. For terminal vector matrix V, V^*V=Q and the effect M=(V^+)^* R V^+ realizes R; here Q is actually full rank. R<=Q ensures M<=I, and R annihilates every weight indicator, ensuring exact null rejection. The published sufficiency argument is concise but sound. The 16-dimensional memory statement concerns this signal-circuit construction; classical storage and control of the calibration outcome are ideal external resources in the stated model.

E. All noise histories, total cost, and exponent.
The independent history expansion agrees with the kernel eta^d and the objective. The all-I and all-Z rejection values are exactly zero. Under arbitrary coherent and interleaved noiseless calibration, those histories still coincide with allowed null experiments at theta=phi and theta=phi+pi. Their positive probabilities therefore give beta>=p^n+q^n for n>=1. For the upper exponent bound, positivity of eta makes the fixed-weight row sum a sub-sum of (1+eta)^n, and a largest binomial coefficient is at least 2^n/(n+1). The two exponential bounds coincide. The total-query comparison follows from n<=N; the case of zero signal queries is trivially beta=1. These arguments cover eta=0 and eta=1 without a problematic limiting interchange. The six-signal countercomparison is explicitly restricted to the displayed construction and does not falsely prove global causal inferiority at total budget six.

F. What was not established.
I did not optimize the L=1 causal value, prove the minimum signal count, characterize coherent-reference optima, test experimental noise, optimize a positive-false-positive tradeoff, or independently regenerate the numerical search that found the rational matrices. Those are not stated theorems. Exact verifier success is a proof of this certificate's feasibility, not optimality. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes these points.

SIGNIFICANCE AND NONBLOCKING IMPROVEMENTS

The strongest novelty candidate is exact activation by the smallest positive integer calibration budget, at the specified signal budget and noise parameter. The L=0 impossibility plus L=1 feasible certificate establishes that threshold. It is stronger than merely showing that sufficiently accurate calibration preserves a known advantage, but it is a small finite construction, not a broad new framework. No checked source provided the identical claim; that is bounded evidence, not a priority certificate.

The practical significance remains low to moderate. The absolute gain is tiny, five signal uses are fixed, noiseless calibration and ideal controls are assumed, an extra signal use wins under fungible costs, and the error exponent is unchanged. The paper has no experimental demonstration or robust advantage analysis with both classes reoptimized at positive false positives. These are genuine limits, accurately disclosed rather than hidden proof defects.

For presentation, a direct citation to query-decomposition or quantum-network realization, a short expanded formula for the rejection effect, and an explicit supplement URL next to Q.json/R.json would make the note easier to use alone. None is an unresolved material objection to the supplied version. I do not require a stronger optimum theorem or an experiment as a condition for this narrow claim.
